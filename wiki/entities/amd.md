## Top observations

- AMD64/x86-64 became standard `[ps=5]` — [[study-2004-03-intelitaniummfewp11.doc-618d4a]]
- The 64-bit market is dominated by high-end proprietary systems. This will change dramatically over the remainder of the decade. Industry-standard 64-bit architectures will move down to the low-end and up into the mid-range and high-end. `[ps=5]` — [[study-amd-industry-analyst-visionaries-64bit-2-7a191f]]
- Transition for pervasive 64-bit computing is going to take some time. The server side is well familiar with this technology; the trick is on a desktop, where this is going to take longer. `[ps=5]` — [[study-amd-industry-analyst-visionaries-64bit-2-7a191f]]
- AMD dropped out of BAPco non-profit in June 2011 'with much sturm und drang' `[ps=5]` — [[study-bapco-sysmark-2012-benchmark-post-a40084]]
- AMD Athlon 64 desktop processor (ClawHammer) launched September 23, 2003 `[ps=5]` — [[study-dct-memoir-athlon-64-prescience-2003]]
- AMD launched the Sempron CPU brand July 2004 (one month after this article's publication), placing Sempron in the low-end segment previously held by Athlon XP and freeing the Athlon brand to compete in mainstream/premium alongside Athlon 64. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-chips-amd-s-semprons-spa-21b184]]
- AMD x86 server share peaked near 25% in late 2006 with Opteron Rev F / Barcelona positioning, driven by HP/IBM/Sun/Dell (Dell adopted Opteron May 2006) — validating Kastner's 'sweet spot' call. AMD lost ground to Intel Core / Nehalem 2008-2017, then recovered with EPYC 2017+. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-s-opteron-a-ecf5a9]]
- In the next two to three years, one will witness a large set of enterprises going in for 64-bit systems as it offers superior performance in terms of scalability, power and reliability `[ps=4]` — [[study-amd-industry-analyst-visionaries-64bit-2-7a191f]]
- I believe that 64-bit technology will bring new and exciting opportunities to the mainstream market. Once you give millions [of users 64-bit, the ecosystem follows]. `[ps=4]` — [[study-amd-industry-analyst-visionaries-64bit-2-7a191f]]
- AMD endorses only benchmarks 'based on real-world computing models and software applications' `[ps=4]` — [[study-bapco-sysmark-2012-benchmark-post-a40084]]
- AMD gradually retired the Sempron brand through 2013, replaced by Athlon low-end and later Ryzen 3; the Sempron-vs-Celeron low-end duopoly Kastner described ended as the overall low-end-desktop segment shrank. `[ps=4]` — [[study-technology-news-chips-amd-s-semprons-spa-21b184]]
- AMD Llano (A-series) shipped June 2011 as the first AMD Fusion APU product, integrating Phenom II K10 CPU cores with Radeon HD 6000-series GPU on the same die — approximately 3 years after AMD's original late-2007/early-2008 target `[ps=3]` — [[study-amd-fusion-outlook-newsfactor-garrett-5859d3]]
- AMD started with dual-core chip designs (Hammer/POWER4 style); initial 130nm Opteron had room for only one core but slots for second already present. Replication of CPU0+L2 cache boxes was trivially simple compared to Sun/Intel retrofits. `[ps=3]` — [[study-intel-longlivedualcore-revised-5-fa8298]]
- Intel trying to get more support for Itanium to compete with AMD's Opteron architecture `[ps=2]` — [[study-internetnews-veritas-itanium-believer-20-2ff4eb]]
