# Compaq Computer Corporation

> Acquiring Digital Equipment; sees Digital as systems integration and professional services provider; combined with Tandem and Digital becomes major EC force


## Summary




## Top observations

- HP completed acquisition of Compaq in May 2002 for ~$25B `[ps=5]` — [[study-2001-aberdeen-group-recent-publications-psk-2be54d]]
- Compaq's worldwide #1 PC title through 2000 was a lagging indicator propped up by geography and DEC/Tandem revenue bulk; US share, growth, and inventory velocity had already flipped to Dell. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- Aberdeen's 1997 call that Dell would surpass Compaq was a hit (scorer 5/5), two years before the crossover. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- Compaq's 1998-99 revenue growth was DEC/Tandem arithmetic, not organic vigor; the quality of earnings deteriorated underneath the headline. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- Pfeiffer answered the wrong question ('how do we become IBM?') when the right one was 'how do we build to order before Dell buries us?' `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- Kastner underweighted how completely a product advantage — Compaq often had the better-engineered machine — could be rendered irrelevant by a business-model disadvantage. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- Compaq's story is acquisition used as a substitute for business-model transformation; it bought what it lacked but could not integrate it into a coherent operating entity able to defend the PC franchise. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- Compaq iPAQ identified as leading Pocket PC hardware driving enterprise Pocket PC adoption `[ps=4]` — [[study-2001-winning-the-enterprise-pocket-pc-will-he-pervasive-7686b2]]
- Compaq was major PC vendor contributing volume to the merged entity `[ps=4]` — [[study-2002-hp-s-consumer-pc-business-will-shrink-psk-ef3cd5]]
- Compaq lost the PC business to Dell on business-model grounds — build-to-order direct beat the dealer channel — before and independent of the Tandem/DEC acquisitions. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- The Tandem/DEC acquisitions were Pfeiffer's flight FROM a PC race already being lost, not ambition for its own sake; an escape, not a strategy. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- Pfeiffer's 1991 low-cost pivot doubled down on the dealer channel (more resellers, consumer outlets) instead of breaking the dealer dependency — the hinge error. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- The dealer channel that WAS Compaq's business model was itself collapsing under margin compression, so Compaq was chained to a dying distribution layer Dell bypassed entirely. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- Compaq's half-in/half-out 'direct and indirect model transition' was the worst place to stand while a competitor was all-in on direct. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- The 1997 white paper's HP-would-lead-NT-by-late-1998 call was wrong; the right prediction on Dell sat next to the wrong emphasis on the high-end server fight. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- The tracked-SKU asymmetry itself tells the story: Dell's tight 15-config catalog vs Compaq's 68-SKU sprawl = disciplined direct vs channel-driven proliferation. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-dell-pc-clones-split]]
- Compaq's Tandem/DEC acquisitions were not the disease but the flight from it — a lunge for the enterprise because the PC low ground was turning to sand under Dell. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- Roel Pieper's five-month tenure is the thesis in miniature: the man hired to build the direct channel could not survive a company constitutionally wedded to its 40,000 dealers. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- The DEC integration failed on culture: Digital's engineering-autonomy/consensus culture was the near-perfect inverse of Compaq's cost-disciplined Houston machine. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
- The dealer loyalty that made Compaq great in the 1980s forced it to absorb its own forecasting-mistake losses rather than let the channel bleed — a wound Dell's near-zero inventory never carried. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-compaq-long-fall-demise]]
