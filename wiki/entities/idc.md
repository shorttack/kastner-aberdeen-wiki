# IDC (International Data Corporation)

> Founded 1964. Dominated market sizing and forecasting; owned quantitative shipment/revenue/market-share data. Tom Willmott came from IDC.


## Summary




## Top observations

- There is no place in the datacenter for lower availability at the application level simply because the storage uses capacity-oriented technology — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Most receptive applications are (1) mirrors and snapshots for data protection and (2) repositories for fixed content — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Capacity-oriented technology will be embedded within arrays as a class of capacity managed through current tools — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Over time capacity-oriented storage shipments will reach a quarter or more of the storage array terabytes shipped — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- [UNVERIFIED] — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- There is not currently enough space for multiple classes/tiers of storage products based on SATA — market should be analyzed as single tier — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Capacity-oriented disk storage poses little near or medium term threat to tape; in fact larger disk capacity means more data to transfer to tape for archiving — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Lack of effective information management software and concurrent lack of information policies will be the major inhibitor for aggressive adoption of tiered storage — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- 50 North American in-depth one-hour interviews conducted October-November 2003 across 10 vertical markets (banking/energy/financial/health/insurance/manufacturing/media/pharmaceutical/transportation/wholesale retail) — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Spending on IT in general and storage in specific expected to increase in 2004 — majority expect storage growth — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Raw survey results suggest 14% of all storage dollars in 2004 would be on SATA/high-capacity low-cost storage — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Number one SATA concern mentioned 30% of time: less reliable than SCSI or FC; performance second; 'too new' tied for second — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- HP most preferred SATA storage supplier among 19 interviewees surveyed; 10 of 19 mentions; IBM and EMC also mentioned — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Manufacturing has highest TAM for capacity-oriented storage ($802M); Financial ($733M) second; Transportation ($477M) third — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- By percent of installed base OK for capacity-oriented storage: Financial 51.9% (1st); Transportation 33.1% (2nd); Media 32.7% (3rd) — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Industries with compliance requirements (Financial/Health/Pharmaceutical) have highest ratio of near-line tape to disk — highest long-term conversion opportunity for SATA — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Data protection (mirroring/snapshots/rapid restore) is most popular use for SATA; 29% of total installed base TBs targeted; many already engaged — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- 12% of total installed base TBs estimated as appropriate for fixed-content management applications — IDC believes this is low and will grow with compliance — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- 60% of all reported installed terabytes (4563 TB total sample) are considered over-served — appropriate for capacity-oriented storage — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- 4563 TB total enterprise storage across 49 qualifying interviews (one disqualified); grand total by vertical market — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
