## Top observations

- $3 billion revenue; third-largest US PC distributor behind Ingram Micro and Tech Data — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- Speed of IT implementation was critical — IE needed to transform IT within 12-18 months to survive competitive pressure — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE positioned itself as leading-edge technology company — a reseller not just a distributor — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE lacked internal IT competency to execute a major systems transformation without external help — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE's fundamental strategic error was pursuing a reseller/value-added model rather than low-cost distributor model as industry consolidated — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE owned the IE/2000 project — IE management made all key decisions including scope, timing, and technology choices — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE's long-term viability was questionable regardless of IT outcome — the PC distribution industry was consolidating to 2-3 major players — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE filed for bankruptcy in 1996 and was liquidated by 1997 — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE repeatedly changed project scope and requirements — a key factor in IE/2000 difficulties — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE revenue approximately $3 billion in 1993 — peak before decline — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- PC distribution industry consolidating from 10+ players to 2-3 major full-line distributors; mid-tier players being squeezed by margin compression — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE experienced significant management turnover during the IE/2000 project — destabilizing project governance and decision-making — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE's IT spending was below industry averages for companies of its size — underinvestment contributed to competitive disadvantage — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- IE's market share was declining as Ingram Micro and Tech Data gained share through scale advantages and lower margins — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- Even with successful IT implementation IE would likely have been acquired or dissolved due to structural industry dynamics — IT was necessary but not sufficient for survival — [[study-kastner-ie-v-andersen-expert-report-3de98a]]
- Kastner retained by Montgomery McCracken for Andersen Consulting as defendant's fact expert witness; IE accused Andersen of failing $17M ERP project (order management, inventory, warehouse, financial accounting); Kastner produced 129-page expert witness report; case settled day before trial — [[study-psk-litigation-b452e6]]
- Key issue: whether Andersen should have recommended replacing IE's existing HP 3000 computer system; technology maturity and fitness for purpose in mid-1990s context — [[study-psk-litigation-b452e6]]
- Kastner involved in the IE v. Andersen Consulting litigation as part of his expert witness practice (referenced in chapter task brief; not detailed in chapter text). — [[study-volume-1-ch09-expert-witness-technology-on-trial-1992-2015]]
