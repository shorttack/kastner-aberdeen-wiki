# SAP SE

> ERP vendor; subject of the longitudinal study


## Top observations

- SAP remains #1 ERP vendor globally with >400,000 customers in 2025 `[ps=5]` — [[study-2001-aberdeen-group-recent-publications-psk-2be54d]]
- Across the whole arc, R/3's failure mode was never the box or the database — it was organizations that never honestly assessed their desire for change before throwing the 10,000 switches. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- The systems-integrator layer (Andersen's 300 SAP/Siebel technicians as the tell) became a structural power center in the R/3 economy that the vendor-and-platform framing under-modeled at the time. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- SAP's own self-description as a 'server Switzerland, neutral to all comers' could hold for hardware but never for the database, because its database vendor was simultaneously its applications rival — neutrality had a structural limit. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- Kastner read SAP's database dependency on Oracle as a convenience to optimize; SAP read the same dependency as a strategic vulnerability to eliminate — he underweighted how badly SAP wanted independence. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- SAP is 'Once and Future King' — not ready to cede ERP market hegemony `[ps=4]` — [[study-2001-aberdeen-group-recent-publications-psk-2be54d]]
- Oracle and SAP shared enterprise ERP/Applications duopoly by 2000; Oracle stronger in financials; SAP stronger in manufacturing `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-oracle-longitudinal]]
- The arc closes where the archive itself did not expect: SAP walked away from every third-party database to build HANA — the one move Kastner explicitly advised against in 2001. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- Every 'whose server?' R/3 decision from 1993-2004 was really a bet on a RISC/CISC architecture and an RDBMS at once; the platform question was a proxy war for the database and the OS. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- Coopetition of this kind is inherently unstable and resolves toward vertical integration; SAP could not indefinitely depend on a database vendor that also sold competing applications, so HANA was the inevitable resolution. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- The 2001 Compaq Tru64/Oracle9i recommendation — that SAP should embrace and not resist the Compaq/Oracle partnership — sat exactly backwards to what SAP actually did; scored low, the most instructive miss in the archive. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- 2026 rebuttal: Kastner knew in-memory DBs ran 10-100x faster than disk-bound RDBMS even in 2001, but at ~$1/MB ($16,000 for 16GB on a Compaq 4-way) there wasn't enough affordable memory to run Fortune 1000 workloads in-memory yet. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- The real mistake was not the in-memory cost math itself but failing to anticipate the competitive/frictional advantage SAP would gain by replacing Oracle's cost with margin flowing to SAP's own HANA — SAP was not usually early to innovate, but it was with HANA. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- Microsoft itself uses AS/400 to run its own business operations; its migration to SAP R/3 on BackOffice will be closely watched `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-as400-vs-microsoft-nt-server]]
- Implementing SAP R/3 throughout an enterprise is a once-in-twenty-year project transforming operational infrastructure `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-taming-sap-r3-consultant-led-introspection]]
- SAP customers tell Aberdeen R/3 has more functionality than any other client-server integrated business process application `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-taming-sap-r3-consultant-led-introspection]]
- Barriers to entry for Financials, HR, and high-end manufacturing (SAP) are huge `[ps=4]` — [[study-software-market-safegu~1-ea7453]]
- Distribute SAP R/3 to $100M manufacturers; high barrier but large opportunity `[ps=4]` — [[study-software-market-safegu~1-ea7453]]
- SAP promoted SOA platforms with full-page trade press campaigns by 2004-2005 `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- SAP positioned as ERP vendor beginning to integrate internet procurement capabilities; partnered with Commerce One December 1997 `[ps=3]` — [[study-1998-internet-procurement-cdc4a6]]
