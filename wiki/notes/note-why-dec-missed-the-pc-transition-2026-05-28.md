# Why DEC missed the PC transition

> **Question (2026-05-28):** Why did DEC miss the PC transition?

## Answer

Based on the provided sources, there is no explicit explanation of *why* DEC missed the PC transition. The sources do not contain a direct analysis of DEC's strategic failure regarding the personal computer market.

However, the sources provide the following contextual observations regarding DEC's trajectory and eventual decline:

*   **Strategic Focus on Mainframe Displacement:** In 1987–1988, DEC was focused on displacing IBM mainframes using VAX architecture and transaction processing (DECtp), believing that "mainframe displacement was 'not a matter of whether but when — and when was the early 1990s'" [[study-volume-1-ch06-dec-mainframes-last-stand-1987-1988]].
*   **Failure to Predict Market Shifts:** By 1994, analysts criticized DEC's "inability to predict its own results," leading to a $183M loss and plummeting stock [[study-boston-globe-dec-steul-cfo-resigns-1994-4013c6]].
*   **Displacement by x86:** The sources note that DEC, having beaten the mainframe on price-performance, was "soon to be surpassed by x86 servers using the very same TPC benchmarks" [[study-volume-1-ch06-dec-mainframes-last-1987-1988]].
*   **Acquisition and Discontinuation:** DEC was acquired by Compaq for $9.6B in 1998, after which the Alpha platform was discontinued in favor of Intel Itanium [[study-1993-hercules-sap-28344a]][[study-1997-digital-ramps-up-for-the-leadership-fb5854]].
*   **Specific Product Failures:** Aberdeen research noted that DEC's Alpha/OSF1 was a "5th Unix attempt in 5 years" and recommended against it for specific enterprise needs as early as 1993 due to lack of production readiness and application support [[study-1993-hercules-sap-28344a]].

The sources do not explicitly state that DEC missed the "PC transition" specifically, but rather describe a broader failure to maintain leadership against x86 servers and eventual acquisition.
