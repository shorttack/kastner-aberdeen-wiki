# Computerworld — 1992-02-17

**Headline**: Compaq cleans up its act in battery disposal

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> is not just hype. There is a need, and I believe over time, corporations will mandate that they recycle these products.

## Rationale

Within three years of 1992, concern about nickel-cadmium battery waste did grow and regulations like the EPA’s 1995 Universal Waste Rule and emerging state laws, followed shortly by the 1996 Mercury-Containing and Rechargeable Battery Management Act, pushed manufacturers and recyclers to create collection and recycling programs. However, by roughly 1995 most ordinary corporations had not yet broadly ‘mandated’ internal recycling of these batteries; adoption of formal corporate e-waste and battery recycling policies became common only in the 2000s and 2010s, well beyond the stated three-year horizon. The claim correctly anticipated a real need and eventual institutionalization of battery recycling, but its implied pace and locus of action (corporate mandates rather than primarily regulation and industry stewardship) were off for the early‑ to mid‑1990s.
