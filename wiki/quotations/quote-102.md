# Computerworld — 1992-08-24

**Headline**: DEC to give PC modular upgrades

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “Modular PCs are somewhat attractive, but users also become locked into a vendor's PC line because you can only buy the upgrade from them,'' added Peter Kastner, an analyst at the Aberdeen Group, a market research firm in Boston.

## Rationale

In the early-to-mid 1990s, most proprietary "modular" PC schemes (such as vendor-specific CPU daughtercards, docking modules, or upgrade chassis from vendors like DEC, IBM, Compaq, and major notebook OEMs) did in fact require buying upgrade modules from the original vendor, creating exactly the kind of lock-in Kastner described. At the same time, the broader desktop PC market rapidly commoditized around industry-standard components and clone systems, allowing buyers to avoid lock-in entirely by choosing non-modular, standards-based PCs, so his observation was accurate for modular product lines but not a defining property of PCs in general.
