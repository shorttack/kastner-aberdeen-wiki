## Rationale

From the late 1980s into the early 1990s, GUI environments such as DECwindows (X11/Motif), OS/2 Presentation Manager, and Windows 3.0/3.1 did in practice require substantially more CPU and memory than character-mode systems, with "usable" setups typically needing several megabytes of RAM on 386-class PCs or VAXstations. Over the subsequent three-year horizon, the industry broadly validated this view: GUI rollouts went hand-in-hand with upgrades to higher-MIPS desktops and workstations, and GUI stacks continued to be regarded as resource-hungry rather than becoming lightweight. However, falling hardware prices and rapid adoption of 4–8 MB RAM PCs meant that while the technical assessment of GUIs as MIPS- and memory-intensive was accurate, the notion of this being a lasting *problem* diminished as such configurations quickly became mainstream.
