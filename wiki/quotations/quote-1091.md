By the mid-1990s, low-cost PC databases and tools such as Microsoft Access, Borland Paradox, FoxPro, Visual Basic, and later Delphi had normalized sub-$500–$1000 pricing for powerful development environments, putting clear competitive pressure on higher-priced 4GL/database development suites. Vendors of products like Gupta SQLWindows and PowerBuilder faced intense competition from these shrink‑wrapped tools and from Microsoft’s broader platform strategy, leading to more aggressive pricing, bundling, and discounting of higher-end development products within a few years, even if specific list prices did not always drop to the exact levels implied. Over the longer term, the trend intensified as development tools became cheaper or free (e.g., Visual Studio tiers, open-source stacks), validating the directional prediction that PC databases would push bigger database development products down in price.
