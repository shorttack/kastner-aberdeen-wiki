## Rationale

In the mid-1990s Informix was indeed regarded as technologically advanced, with its Universal Server and parallel architecture seen as competitive with Oracle and suitable for high-end workloads, including some SAP deployments, so the old knock that its products were not "world-class" was becoming outdated. However, within about three years of the article (1997–1999) Informix was hit by an accounting scandal, leadership turnover, revenue shortfalls, and steep market-share losses to Oracle and Microsoft SQL Server, which damaged its credibility and commercial momentum despite its technical strengths. By 2001 its database business was sold to IBM, so Kastner’s implication of a durable, credibility-driven upswing—anchored in being the database in frequent SAP wins—only partially held up over the stated horizon.
