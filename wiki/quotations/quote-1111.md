## Rationale

The Power Mac G5, introduced in 2003, delivered significant performance gains for creative professionals, with its 64-bit PowerPC 970, higher clocks, large caches, and much larger RAM ceilings enabling markedly faster rendering and filter application in tools like Adobe Photoshop compared to both prior G4 systems and contemporary Pentium 4 workstations. However, the full potential of 64-bit optimization was constrained because macOS user applications and many key creative suites remained largely 32-bit for much of this three‑year window, and the G5 line itself was short‑lived as Apple began transitioning to Intel in 2005–2006, so the benefits were real but not as transformative or enduring as the rhetoric implied.
