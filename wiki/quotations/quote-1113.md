## Rationale

The predicted “urban WiFi crash of 2004” did not materialize as a widely observed systemic failure; instead, Wi-Fi usage in cities grew rapidly through 2004–2006 as 802.11b/g home routers and public hotspots proliferated and remained usable despite congestion. While dense 2.4GHz environments did experience interference and performance degradation in apartment buildings and offices, the industry mitigated this with better channel planning, the use of 5GHz (802.11a, later 802.11n dual‑band), and improved radios rather than suffering a crash. The claim correctly anticipated congestion as an issue in urban Wi-Fi, but its framing as a near-term crash in 2004 substantially overstated the severity and timing of the problem.
