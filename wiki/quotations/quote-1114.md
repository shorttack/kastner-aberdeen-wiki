## Rationale

Dense 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi environments in cities did experience increasing interference and performance degradation as 802.11b/g home routers and hotspots proliferated from 2003–2006, but there was no widely recognized “urban WiFi crash” in 2004 that materially disrupted consumer or enterprise usage. Instead, Wi-Fi adoption accelerated in homes, enterprises, and public hotspots, and vendors mitigated congestion with better channel management and migration to 5 GHz and 802.11n, so the predicted dramatic systemic breakdown never occurred even though the underlying concern about spectrum crowding was technically valid.
