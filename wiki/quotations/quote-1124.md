## Rationale

Kastner’s characterization that manufacturers and retailers rely on low rebate redemption rates to advertise discounted prices while mostly selling at full price held up: studies through the 2000s consistently found many consumer rebate programs with redemption rates well under 50%, often in the teens for complex mail‑in offers, making breakage a core economic driver. Within roughly three years, backlash over confusing, delayed, and denied rebates led several major U.S. retailers and PC/electronics vendors to scale back or eliminate mail‑in rebates in favor of instant discounts or simpler promotions, explicitly citing consumer dissatisfaction, which aligns with his warning that the practice could alienate customers. The only reason this is not a perfect score is that subsequent industry analysis emphasized additional major motives for rebates (price discrimination, data capture, and channel incentives), so “primary reason” was somewhat overstated.
