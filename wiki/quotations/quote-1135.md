## Rationale

Within a few years of the statement, national competition around high-end supercomputers (e.g., U.S. DOE systems and Japan’s Earth Simulator) was explicitly framed in terms of economic competitiveness and scientific leadership, validating the link between supercomputing and national innovation agendas. Over the same period, military and defense programs increasingly relied on large-scale simulation for tasks like nuclear stockpile stewardship and weapons design, reducing but not fully eliminating the need for certain categories of real-world tests, while commercial sectors such as automotive and aerospace deepened their use of HPC for virtual crash tests and design optimization. The only overstatement is the implication that real-world testing could be eliminated rather than substantially reduced, which is why the assessment is largely but not perfectly accurate.
