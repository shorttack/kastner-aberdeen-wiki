## Rationale

The forecast that Microsoft’s EU antitrust appeal would galvanize many ‘silent friends’ into vocal allies did not really materialize; the 2007 judgment largely upheld the Commission’s 2004 decision and most prominent technology firms (Sun, Oracle, RealNetworks, IBM) were complainants or on the opposing side rather than public allies. However, the broader warning that the EU was articulating a competition standard meaningfully different from the U.S. did prove directionally correct, as EU competition law became a more aggressive constraint on dominant firms and later investigations extended to other global companies like Intel and, in the following decade, Google and other large platforms beyond the initial three‑year horizon.
