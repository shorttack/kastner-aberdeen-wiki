## Rationale

Within three years of the EU’s 2004 antitrust decision against Microsoft, the European Court of First Instance largely upheld the ruling in 2007 and Microsoft paid fines and offered remedies, but there was no broad wave of public corporate allies rallying to Microsoft as “silent friends” becoming vocal. The EU did apply a somewhat different and more aggressive competition standard than the post-settlement U.S. approach in United States v. Microsoft, yet this did not quickly translate into a generalized threat to “every other global company,” instead evolving into targeted cases against specific dominant firms over a longer period (e.g., Intel and later Google) rather than a wholesale challenge to global business models.
