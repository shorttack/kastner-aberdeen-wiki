## Rationale

Between 2000 and 2002 the dot-com bubble burst, the NASDAQ collapsed, and high-burn, hype-driven e-commerce firms like Pets.com and Webvan failed, forcing investors and boards to insist on basic business metrics such as unit economics, profitability paths, and sustainable customer acquisition costs. From 2001–2004, enterprise and online retailers sharply tightened IT and e-commerce spending, demanded clearer ROI and disciplined planning, and the surviving players like Amazon and eBay shifted focus from land-grab marketing to operating efficiency and profits, matching Kastner’s depiction of the post–“gold rush” environment. While later waves of Internet exuberance appeared, in the three-year horizon the sector clearly transitioned from buzz-fueled growth to more stringent, fundamentals-driven planning and investment.
