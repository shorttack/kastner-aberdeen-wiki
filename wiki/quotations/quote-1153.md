## Quote

> ew processors right away. And an important question looms: Do businesses really want to make the switch to 64-bit computing? In the short term, the answer is probably no. "The imminent arrival of 64-bit computing has been wildly exaggerated," Peter Kastner, executive vice president at Aberdeen Group, told the E-Commerce Times.

## Rationale

Over the three years after this claim, 64-bit processors like Intel’s Itanium (launched 2001) saw very limited business uptake, and AMD’s Opteron/Athlon 64 (launched 2003) were only beginning to penetrate server and enthusiast niches rather than the mainstream enterprise desktop. Most business PCs and standard Windows deployments stayed on 32-bit x86 through the early 2000s, with broad 64-bit operating system and application adoption not arriving until later in the decade. This slow, niche-heavy trajectory means the hyped, near-term ‘arrival’ of 64-bit business computing was indeed exaggerated in the short term, matching Kastner’s assessment for the 3-year horizon.
