## Rationale

Within about three years of AMD64 and Intel’s 64-bit x86 introductions (Opteron in 2003 and Intel’s EM64T in 2004), the primary real-world beneficiaries were exactly the kinds of workloads Kastner named: database and middleware servers, high-performance and scientific computing, and other memory-intensive applications such as knowledge management and analytics. Most client and line-of-business applications remained 32-bit through the mid-2000s, typical PCs still shipped with 1–2 GB of RAM, and enterprises continued to run substantial investments on established 32-bit and Unix systems, so 64-bit did not yet justify a wholesale abandonment of 32-bit architectures within that three-year window.
