## Quote

> "They never had a really strong case to begin with, particularly from the consumer's standpoint," Peter Kastner, chief research officer at Aberdeen Group, told the E-Commerce Times. "At this point, it can only be for the political visibility gain for the attorneys general and the potential for class-action litigation fees by the supporting council." Fundamen

## Rationale

Within three years of the quote, the federal and state antitrust actions against Microsoft were widely regarded as legally solid, with the D.C. Circuit’s 2001 decision upholding key monopoly and tying findings and forming an important modern antitrust precedent. Multiple state cases led to sizeable consumer voucher and refund settlements in places like California and New York and to additional behavioral remedies from Microsoft, indicating there was more than just political visibility and fee-seeking at stake. Kastner’s claim that the attorneys general “never had a really strong case” and were acting only for politics and class-action fees is therefore largely contradicted by the legal outcomes, settlements, and conduct changes that followed in the early 2000s.
