## Quote

> close to the vest, only hinting here and there about how the case might play out. Analysts said they expect the states will not get exactly what they want. "I don't believe the states will get Windows chopped up the way they have suggested," Peter Kastner, chief research officer at the Aberdeen Group, told the E-Commerce Times. "But I do believe the judge will [do more than] simply reaffirm the agreement the Justice Department and Microsoft reached last November," he added. June 2004 Step Up to 64-

## Rationale

Kastner correctly anticipated that the U.S. state plaintiffs would not get the structural remedy they wanted, such as having Windows ‘chopped up’ or made modular, which Judge Kollar-Kotelly ultimately rejected in the final U.S. v. Microsoft remedies decision in late 2002 and which remained off the table through the mid‑2000s.[2] However, his expectation that the judge would go meaningfully beyond simply reaffirming the November 2001 DOJ–Microsoft settlement did not really materialize: the final judgment largely adopted the federal settlement terms, added only limited refinements, and withstood appeals without being materially strengthened between 2002 and 2005.[2] Thus the forecast was partly accurate on the outcome for the states but overstated how far the court would go beyond the existing DOJ agreement.
