# Computerworld — 1993-04-19

**Headline**: IMRS TO PUSH CLIENT/SERVER

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> A lot of companies haven't delivered products yet, and the reality is that purchases won't be made until 1994,'' said Peter Kastner, a vice president at Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

Kastner’s timing that most client/server financial accounting purchases would not occur until 1994 aligns with the broader pattern that, while client/server architectures had existed since the 1980s, packaged client/server business applications (including financials and early ERP suites) did not see broad enterprise buying until the mid‑1990s. In 1993 many vendors were still finishing client/server rewrites of their GL/AP/AR products, and large organizations continued to run mainframe financials while planning or piloting client/server migrations that translated into significant purchase waves starting around 1994–1995. The claim slightly overstates how few purchases would occur before 1994, but it correctly characterizes that the real market ramp for client/server financial packages was just beginning and would not materialize in earnest until the following year.
