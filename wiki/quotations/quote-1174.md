## Rationale

Within three years of the article, mainstream consumer desktops did not have broad 64‑bit OS support; AMD’s Athlon 64 only appeared in 2003 and most shipped systems still ran 32‑bit Windows and applications. The prediction that the industry would avoid a long lag between 64‑bit hardware and software also failed, as Windows XP Professional x64 Edition did not arrive until 2005 and 64‑bit Windows and application adoption remained minority well into the Windows 7 era. While 64‑bit did eventually become the norm, both the near‑term timing and the implied short hardware–software lag were substantially overoptimistic.
