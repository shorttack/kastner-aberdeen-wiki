## Rationale

Between 2003 and 2006, benchmarks consistently showed that Hyper-Threading on Pentium 4 delivered its largest gains in multithreaded or compute-heavy workloads like video encoding and image processing, while offering little to no benefit—and sometimes minor regressions—in lightly threaded applications such as Microsoft Office, aligning well with his Word vs. Photoshop/video distinction.[1][3][7] Hyper-Threading was indeed already shipping and validated in Xeon processors before being brought to Pentium 4 desktops, so calling it a “proven technology” was reasonable.[2][7] However, Intel did not end up including Hyper-Threading in all future Pentium 4-branded processors (several Prescott-based and low-end variants lacked HT), so that part of the prediction was overstated even within the 3-year window.[2]
