## Rationale

Within three years of the Aberdeen report (early‑2000s), AMD not only did *not* retreat from its gigahertz‑equivalency/model‑number scheme, it extended it across Athlon XP and Athlon 64 (e.g., 2500+, 3200+, 3800+) and Opteron parts, and consumers and reviewers routinely used those PR‑style numbers as the primary way to distinguish SKUs. Intel itself moved away from clock‑speed branding after the NetBurst era, shifting to Core 2 and then Core i3/i5/i7 naming where GHz was de‑emphasized, which undercut the report’s premise that AMD’s approach was untenable and would “soon” have to be abandoned. AMD only gradually transitioned away from the “+” performance ratings later in the decade, well beyond the stated short horizon, so the specific short‑term prediction that AMD ‘must soon retreat’ from gigahertz equivalency was contradicted by what actually happened.
