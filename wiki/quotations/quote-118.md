# Computerworld — 1993-04-19

**Headline**: CA gets on Microsoft bandwagon

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> NT test drive The rapid growth of interest in downsizing by CA customers during the past year leads Aberdeen Group to conclude that more than 50% of CA's Unicenter mainframe clients “would at least want to kick the tires of Unicenter on NT,'' said Peter Kastner, a vice president at the Boston consultancy.

## Rationale

Between 1993 and 1996, Windows NT rapidly gained traction in enterprise IT as a server platform, prompting many mainframe-centric organizations to at least evaluate NT-based management tools such as CA’s Unicenter ports and later Unicenter TNG. CA did in fact invest heavily in Unicenter for NT and entered co-marketing arrangements with Microsoft, indicating real demand from its large-enterprise customer base, but there is no solid evidence that a majority of existing MVS Unicenter mainframe clients specifically wanted to trial or adopt Unicenter on NT within that three-year window. Given the lack of quantitative data on CA’s installed base behavior, the claim appears directionally consistent with the era’s downsizing and client/server experimentation trend but cannot be validated at the stated “more than 50%” magnitude.
