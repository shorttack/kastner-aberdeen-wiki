## Quote

> These tests also show that "normal users" will see a considerably better user experience with a dual core microprocessor. The hour glass may not be a thing of the past, but it will take over your cursor much less often.

## Rationale

Within about three years of the 2005 console and CPU transition window, dual-core processors such as AMD’s Athlon 64 X2 and Intel’s Core Duo/Core 2 Duo had become mainstream in consumer PCs, delivering noticeable responsiveness gains and smoother multitasking versus single-core Pentium 4–class systems.[13][5][9] As operating systems and everyday applications increasingly ran background tasks (antivirus, indexing, media players, and more complex browsers), the second core reduced UI stalls and the frequency of visible wait cursors for typical home and office users, aligning with the prediction of a considerably better user experience.[5][7] However, many individual applications remained lightly threaded, so performance improvements were workload-dependent rather than universally dramatic, making the claim directionally and largely correct rather than perfectly so.[7][9]
