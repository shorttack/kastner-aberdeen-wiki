## Rationale

Dual-core CPUs such as AMD’s Athlon 64 X2 (2005) and Intel’s Pentium D/Core Duo (2005–2006) quickly proved that everyday multitasking workloads felt significantly smoother because background tasks could move to the second core, even though many single-threaded applications saw little gain or ran slower due to lower clock speeds. By roughly three years out, dual-core had become the mainstream client configuration and was widely regarded as the inflection point where the industry shifted from frequency scaling to multicore designs, confirming the characterization of dual-core’s introduction as a landmark in personal computing history.
