## Rationale

Between 2005 and roughly 2008, all major chip vendors (Intel with Atom and CE chips, TI OMAP, Qualcomm, Samsung, Freescale, etc.) aggressively targeted "digital lifestyle" devices such as MP3 players, set‑top boxes, game consoles, and especially smartphones, all built around programmable CPUs, full operating systems (Symbian, Windows Mobile, embedded Linux, and then iOS in 2007 and Android in 2008), and server/desktop‑class development tools. Over the following decade, annual smartphone shipments alone scaled to well over 1 billion units per year, with additional billions of other connected consumer devices, validating his forecast that units would be measured in the billions and that this class of digital lifestyle electronics would become a core focus for processor makers. His humorous caveat about “digital toasters” not being imminent also aligns with the slower, later emergence of more trivial IoT appliances compared to the explosive growth of phones and media devices.
