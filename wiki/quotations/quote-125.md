# Computerworld — 1993-06-14

**Headline**: USL makes microkernel move

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “It is quite likely that the engineers at Novell and USL are struggling to merge two highly different operating system cores,'' said Peter Kastner, a vice president at Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

Novell’s attempt to combine its NetWare network operating system with USL’s Unix System V codebase via the UnixWare/SuperNOS microkernel strategy quickly proved very difficult in practice, with officials later acknowledging that full integration of the two environments on a microkernel might not be achievable. By 1995, Novell effectively abandoned the unified-kernel vision, assigning most of its Unix business to the Santa Cruz Operation, confirming that merging the two very different cores had indeed been a serious struggle rather than a smooth engineering effort. The quote accurately characterizes the complexity and difficulty of the kernel-merging effort, but it is more an informed present-tense inference than a bold forward-looking prediction, so it rates as partially prescient rather than remarkable.
