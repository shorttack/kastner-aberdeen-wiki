## Quote

> But over the long term, Microsoft's stated direction is to make this [connection] nearly transparent with Chicago,'' said Peter Kastner, a vice president at Aberdeen Group, a Boston consulting firm.

## Rationale

Windows 95 (Chicago) shipped in August 1995 and significantly integrated DOS and Windows into a single user experience, with most DOS applications running in virtual DOS machines under the Windows GUI, making the DOS/Windows boundary far less visible than in Windows 3.x.[1] This direction matched Microsoft’s stated aim and did make the connection between DOS and Windows ‘nearly transparent’ for many mainstream users, though low-level DOS utilities and some games still exposed underlying DOS quirks and configuration, so the transparency was not complete.[1]
