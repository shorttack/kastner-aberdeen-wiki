## Quote

> I think we'll be seeing people use their machines for only a few years,

## Rationale

Between 1989 and the mid-1990s, workstation and PC hardware rapidly followed Moore’s law, driving steep price/performance improvements and making it economical to replace systems rather than upgrade them. By the early–mid 1990s, three- to five-year corporate refresh cycles for Unix workstations and Wintel PCs had become common, and organizations increasingly treated desktop systems as disposable assets superseded by newer generations. This “throwaway” pattern persisted into the 2000s and 2010s with commodity x86 servers and PCs, validating the idea that users would only keep machines a few years before technology leapfrogged what they owned and lower costs justified replacement.
