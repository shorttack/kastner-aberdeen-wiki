## Quote

> As client/server technology matures, the number of end-user query tools is proliferating, noted Peter Kastner, a vice president at Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

Through the mid‑ to late‑1990s, client/server architectures became a dominant model for business applications, prompting numerous independent software vendors to deliver GUI-based end‑user query and reporting tools on top of relational databases, which created a crowded, overlapping tools market.[2] Industry accounts from that period also describe IT organizations trying to rationalize and standardize their client/server application stacks, mirroring Kastner’s observation that IS groups were imposing controls to limit the number of different end‑user tools they had to support.[9][2]
