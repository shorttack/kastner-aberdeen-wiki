# Computerworld — 1993-11-29

**Headline**: TECHNOLOGY ON TRIAL

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Like Lotus Development Corp.s 1-2-3, dBase applications are so entrenched in the corporate world that “it's not going to disappear anytime soon,'' says Peter Kastner at Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

By the late 1990s, dBase was clearly in steep decline versus Microsoft Access, client–server relational databases, and emerging web architectures, but it had not “disappeared” and many departmental and line-of-business applications still ran on dBase code. dBASE for Windows 5.0 shipped in 1994 and the product line continued under Borland and later dBASE LLC, with updates well into the 2000s and 2010s, while a long tail of legacy installations persisted through the 2000s and beyond. The prediction correctly captured the persistence of entrenched dBase applications over the ensuing five years, but understated the speed at which it would lose strategic importance, hence a “largely prescient” rather than “remarkably prescient” score.
