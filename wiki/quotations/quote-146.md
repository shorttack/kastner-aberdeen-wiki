# Computerworld — 1993-11-29

**Headline**: TECHNOLOGY ON TRIAL

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> As a result, “You're going to see the PC databases pushing the bigger database development products down in price,'' Kastner says.

## Rationale

Within five years of 1993, low-cost PC databases such as Microsoft Access (often bundled with Office and priced in the low hundreds) and desktop products like Paradox and FoxPro had set developer expectations that serious database tools should cost a few hundred dollars rather than a few thousand, putting clear downward price pressure on 4GL and client/server development environments such as Gupta SQLWindows, PowerBuilder, and similar RAD database tools. At the same time, vendors responded by bundling more capabilities into moderately priced IDEs (e.g., Visual Basic, Delphi) and by introducing cheaper workgroup or developer editions of enterprise databases, making it much harder to sustain stand-alone $2,000+ desktop-oriented database development products. However, core enterprise DBMS server licenses (Oracle, DB2, Sybase) remained high-priced at the upper tiers, so the prediction was more accurate for the tooling and mid-tier market than for the entire database stack, warranting a "largely prescient" rather than perfect score.
