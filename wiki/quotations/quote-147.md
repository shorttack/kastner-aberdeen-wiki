## Quote

> But users should be aware of the consequences of pricing wars on customer support for high-end development tools, according to Peter Kastner, vice president at the Aberdeen Group, a Boston-based consulting firm.

## Rationale

From 1994–1997, low-priced, shrink‑wrapped development tools such as Microsoft Visual Basic, Borland Delphi, and similar IDEs did in fact come with limited vendor hand‑holding, relying heavily on documentation and emerging community support rather than high-touch consulting or training. In parallel, higher-priced enterprise tools and 4GL environments like Powersoft PowerBuilder, Oracle, and IBM offerings maintained premium pricing structures that explicitly bundled substantial support, training, and services, so buyers did not get that level of service at $199. However, the forecast that price wars would materially undermine support for high-end tools was not clearly borne out within three years; the market instead stratified into low-cost, lower-touch tools and high-cost, high-touch platforms, which makes the claim directionally right but somewhat generic and overstated.
