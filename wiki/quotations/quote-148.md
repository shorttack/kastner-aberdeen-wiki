# Computerworld — 1994-01-03

**Headline**: Price wars pressure low end Forecast

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> hould be aware of the consequences of pricing wars on customer support for high-end development tools, according to Peter Kastner, vice president at the Aberdeen Group, a Boston-based consulting firm.

## Rationale

From 1994–1997, mainstream shrink‑wrapped development tools such as Microsoft Visual Basic, Visual C++, Borland Delphi, and PowerBuilder were indeed sold at a few hundred dollars per seat and generally came with limited, largely self-service support compared with high-end CASE and 4GL tools that were bundled with expensive maintenance and consulting contracts. At the same time, vendors of higher-end enterprise tools (e.g., IBM, Oracle, and later Rational) continued to rely on high license and support fees to cover substantial R&D and provide hands-on services, while cheaper tools and, increasingly, open-source toolchains shifted users toward community forums and lower-touch vendor support. However, this was more an extension of existing software economics than a surprising new development, and the quote does not anticipate how acceptable minimal vendor support for low-cost tools would become, which tempers its prescience.
