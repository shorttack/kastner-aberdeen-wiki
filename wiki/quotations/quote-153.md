# Computerworld — 1994-07-18

**Headline**: IBM and CA enhance database features

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Enhancements brought to the fore by the Unix-oriented database vendors “are forcing the mainframe database bunch to extend and enhance their products,'' said Peter Kastner, a vice president at Aberdeen Group, a Boston consultancy.

## Rationale

From the early to late 1990s, Oracle, Sybase, and Informix on Unix platforms grew rapidly and differentiated with features such as stored procedures, triggers, and advanced client/server capabilities, putting clear competitive pressure on mainframe-centric databases. In that same period IBM’s DB2 for MVS/ESA introduced and then significantly enhanced stored procedures and related programmability and performance features (e.g., in DB2 V4 and subsequent releases), and CA likewise evolved IDMS and other mainframe data products to maintain parity with Unix-oriented SQL databases. This matches Kastner’s characterization that Unix database innovation was forcing the ‘mainframe database bunch’ to extend and enhance their products over the ensuing years, though it described a trend already underway rather than predicting a surprising new turn.
