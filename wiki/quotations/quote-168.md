## Quote

> “They will continue to whip their companies [and] continue to innovate so that IBM or anybody else can never catch up,'' Kastner said.

## Rationale

From 1995 through the mid‑2000s, Microsoft remained dominant in PC operating systems and office suites, while IBM never caught up in those categories and ultimately exited the PC business by selling it to Lenovo in 2004–2005.[3] Over roughly the same period Intel’s x86 processors under Andy Grove’s leadership stayed at the center of the Wintel ecosystem, though AMD gained periods of technical leadership with Athlon and Opteron and Grove left the CEO role in 1998, undercutting the idea of an indefinitely unassailable lead. After the early 2000s, both firms faced strong new competitors (Linux, Google, Apple, ARM-based chips), so the “never catch up” claim and the focus on Grove and Gates personally proved overstated even though IBM specifically did not regain parity in the core PC platform arena.
