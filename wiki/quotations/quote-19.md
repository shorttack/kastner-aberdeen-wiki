# Computerworld — 1989-07-24

**Headline**: Tesseract, Walker to be financial software allies

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> it becomes a no-brainer for software houses to begin to blend their applications using DB2.

## Rationale

Between 1989 and the mid‑1990s, DB2 did become the default relational database on IBM mainframes, and most major enterprise financial and ERP vendors selling into large IBM accounts (such as SAP, PeopleSoft, and core banking/insurance application providers) added or prioritized DB2 support, effectively “blending their applications using DB2” for that segment. However, the database market as a whole remained fragmented—with Oracle and others dominant on Unix and emerging client‑server platforms—so DB2’s pull was very strong in large IBM shops but not as universal across all software houses as the quote implies. Over the longer term, DB2 stayed a key platform in the mainframe and high‑end enterprise segment, which validates the direction of the claim even if its breadth was somewhat overstated.
