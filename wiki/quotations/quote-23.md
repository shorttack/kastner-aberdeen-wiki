# Computerworld — 1989-10-09

**Headline**: DEC challenges IBM CASE strategy

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P1_tiebreak

## Quote

> This is a horse race, and DEC is literally lengths ahead,

## Rationale

In late 1989 DEC did have a more complete and shipping suite of CASE tools (e.g., Decdesign, CDD/Plus, VAXset) and a real installed base, while IBM’s AD/Cycle was still largely announced but undelivered, so the snapshot claim of being ahead at that moment had some truth. However, over the next three years neither DEC nor IBM emerged as a clear winner in CASE: repository standards like ATIS did not take hold, the market fragmented toward specialist vendors and emerging IDEs, and DEC’s broader business began to weaken, culminating in major restructuring and an eventual sale later in the decade. Given that DEC’s apparent lead in 1989 did not translate into durable advantage or recognized leadership even by the early 1990s, the characterization of being “lengths ahead” does not hold up well when judged over the stated three-year horizon.
