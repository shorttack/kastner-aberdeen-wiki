# Computerworld — 1989-12-25

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=LH, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> I just don't see all the various players getting together in 1990, or possibly ever,

## Rationale

Kastner’s skepticism that “all the various players” would come together in 1990 was accurate: the Unix wars between AT&T/Unix International and the Open Software Foundation continued into the early 1990s, and no grand unification materialized that year. Through the 1990s, standards like POSIX, the X/Open specifications, and later the Single UNIX Specification under The Open Group improved source-level compatibility, but vendors maintained distinct System V, BSD, and vendor-specific Unix flavors while Linux emerged as yet another, eventually dominant, Unix-like platform rather than a unifying vendor coalition. His stronger suggestion that they might never truly “get together” was directionally right—there was convergence on standards but never a single unified Unix or fully aligned vendor front—so the timing and basic thrust were prescient even if the absolute “ever” overstates the case.
