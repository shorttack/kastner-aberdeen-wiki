## Rationale

Between 1989 and roughly 1992, the huge installed base of MS‑DOS PCs remained dominant, and DOS applications continued to run as the primary workload even as Windows 3.0 (1990) and 3.1 (1992) emerged as graphical environments layered on top of DOS rather than replacing it.[10] DOS extenders such as Phar Lap and DOS/4GW enabled 32‑bit protected‑mode applications and games to keep using DOS hardware and software well into the early 1990s, effectively extending the useful life of existing DOS systems. Over the same period, OS/2 saw only modest adoption and never provided the expected mass‑market successor to DOS, while Unix remained relatively niche on desktops, so Kastner’s core claim that the DOS base and extenders would prolong DOS systems’ life even without a broad OS/2 “awakening” was borne out by subsequent history.
