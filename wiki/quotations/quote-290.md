# CNET News.com — 2003-04-29

**Headline**: IBM's Itanium Server Goes on Sale

**Verdict**: LOW prescience — score=1, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> There's no doubt that Itanium machines will begin to take material market share away from other architectures going forward this year.

## Rationale

By 2003–2008, Itanium remained a niche platform and did not take "material" market share from other architectures; x86 (Xeon/Opteron) and IBM Power dominated volume and growth while Itanium shipments stayed relatively small.[2][4] IBM itself exited the Itanium server business after gaining only about 10% of the already tiny Itanium segment, underscoring that Itanium was not materially displacing other architectures in the broader server market.[1] Analyst and Gartner data show that by 2007–2008, HP accounted for the overwhelming majority of the modest Itanium server shipments, with overall volumes and share far below initial expectations and not materially eroding x86 or RISC markets.[3][6]
