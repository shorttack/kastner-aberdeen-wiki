# Forbes — 2004-01-12

**Headline**: Apple's Supercomputing Scientists

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> I don't see it putting many pennies on the bottom line,

## Rationale

From 2004–2007, Apple’s revenue and profit growth came overwhelmingly from consumer products like the iPod, iTunes Store, and later the iPhone, while Xserve and G5-based high-performance clusters (including showcase installations like Virginia Tech’s) remained a tiny, niche business line. High-performance computing and life-sciences clusters never became a material revenue or profit driver for Apple, and the eventual discontinuation of Xserve in 2011 underscores that these offerings contributed little to the company’s long-term bottom line. Kastner’s view that Apple’s supercomputing push would not add many “pennies on the bottom line” was therefore strongly borne out within the 3-year horizon and beyond.
