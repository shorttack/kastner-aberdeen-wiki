# Computerworld — 1991-08-05

**Headline**: How low can mail-order firms go? Watch'em

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Kastner pointed to Intel Corp.'s slashing $300 from the price of an I486 processor and its need to compete in the 80386 market with clone chips from Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.

## Rationale

By 1991–1992, Intel had in fact implemented steep price cuts on 486 parts and introduced lower-cost variants such as the 486SX, confirming the pressure to move prices down. Over the same period, AMD’s Am386 line ramped quickly, taking a significant share of the 80386 market and forcing Intel to compete directly on both pricing and positioning in that segment. Kastner’s characterization of Intel’s need to respond to clone competition and cut flagship CPU prices was directionally accurate, though it largely described an unfolding reality rather than anticipating an unexpected shift.
