> The relatively low cost of ramp-up - combined with the improving automation of server management functions - will attract customers,

## Rationale

By roughly 2007, blade servers had moved from niche to meaningful share, with Gartner estimating blades at about 10% of server shipments and vendors explicitly emphasizing easier deployment and management as adoption drivers.[6] Industry analyses later reinforced that lower hardware and deployment costs, along with centralized chassis-based management and improving automation tools, were key reasons organizations adopted blades.[3][6] Although density and virtualization were also major factors, Kastner’s specific claim that relatively low ramp‑up cost plus improving server management automation would attract customers was largely borne out in the mid‑2000s enterprise market.
