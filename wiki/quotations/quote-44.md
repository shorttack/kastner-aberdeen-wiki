# Computerworld — 1991-09-02

**Headline**: Bull forms systems integration unit

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Referring to CICS/VSAM users as the "bread and butter" of the IBM mainframe world, Kastner pointed out that it is also the most vulnerable to the lower costs of downsizing strategies, due in part to the costliness of migrating to DB2.

## Rationale

CICS/VSAM shops did come under strong pressure from early-1990s downsizing and client/server migrations, and the effort and licensing cost of moving legacy VSAM applications to DB2 on z/OS was a real concern in many enterprises. However, rather than making these customers the "most vulnerable" segment that abandoned mainframes en masse by the mid-1990s, most large CICS/VSAM workloads stayed on IBM platforms while IBM aggressively evolved DB2, introduced migration aids such as CICS VSAM Transparency, and kept CICS as the dominant transaction monitor. By the late 1990s and beyond, CICS with DB2 or VSAM remained central to banking, insurance, airlines, and retail, so the vulnerability was real but far less decisive than implied.
