# Computerworld — 1991-09-30

**Headline**: Sun seeks Intel Unix

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “The acquisition is more than hedging Sun's bets; it will make Sun's schedule possible,'' said Peter Kastner, vice president of Aberdeen Group, a technology and market research firm in Boston.

## Rationale

Sun’s 1991 acquisition of Interactive Systems did quickly give it a shipping Intel-based Unix (Interactive Unix) by 1992 and contributed engineering and code that underpinned Solaris for x86, which appeared in the 1992–1993 timeframe. This supports the idea that the deal was more than a hedge and materially helped Sun deliver Intel Unix on an aggressive early-1990s schedule. However, the claim is about making an internal schedule “possible,” which is hard to verify directly, and Sun’s Intel Unix line remained strategically secondary and commercially modest within a few years, so the statement cannot be scored as strongly prescient beyond the narrow scheduling dimension.
