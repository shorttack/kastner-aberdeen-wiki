# Computerworld — 1991-10-14

**Headline**: Unix System Labs signs on with ACE

**Verdict**: LOW prescience — score=1, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “ They seem to have come up with the best of both worlds,'' said Peter Kastner, vice president at Aberdeen Group, a consultancy in Boston, Mass.

## Rationale

Kastner’s comment assumes that the ACE/USL effort to run Ultrix, OSF/1, and SVR4 applications on a common platform would meaningfully standardize Unix open systems features for buyers, but the ACE consortium effectively collapsed by 1993 and never became a unifying force in the Unix market. Through the early-to-mid 1990s the “Unix wars” and fragmentation between System V, OSF/1, and vendor Unix variants persisted, with real convergence and de facto standardization only arriving later via POSIX/Single UNIX Specification and then Linux—developments largely unrelated to the specific initiative he was praising. Thus his characterization of this particular move as delivering “the best of both worlds” and long-run standardization was contradicted by what actually happened in the stated timeframe.
