# CNET News.com — 2003-04-29

**Headline**: IBM's Itanium Server Goes on Sale

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> IBM had to have a strong Intel 32-bit and 64-bit product line in order not to confuse customers into thinking that there's some unannounced transition to AMD in the cards,

## Rationale

Within three years, IBM did maintain a broad Intel-based 32- and 64-bit server portfolio (chiefly Xeon with EM64T/Intel 64) alongside its emerging AMD Opteron systems, so customers never saw a wholesale shift to AMD as the exclusive x86 supplier. However, the specific idea that IBM needed a "strong" Itanium-based 64-bit line to avoid perceptions of an AMD transition aged poorly, as IBM quickly backed away from Itanium and focused its Intel commitment on mainstream x86 Xeon rather than IA-64, making the Intel-vs-AMD signaling logic only partially accurate.
