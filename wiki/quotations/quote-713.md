# Computerworld — 1991-08-05

**Headline**: How low can mail-order firms go? Watch'em

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> a 386DX/ 25-MHz-based PC could sell for less than $1,000 before long

## Rationale

By 1992–1993, the U.S. PC price war and intense competition among mail-order vendors such as Dell, Gateway 2000, and Zeos pushed complete 386-based desktop systems, especially older 386DX/25 configurations, below the $1,000 mark (often excluding monitor). Given that 486SX systems were approaching or dipping under $1,000 in this period, it is highly likely that 386DX/25 machines crossed that threshold within roughly a year or so of the 1991 article, matching the direction and rough timing of the prediction even if exact dates and configurations are hard to pin down from surviving price lists.
