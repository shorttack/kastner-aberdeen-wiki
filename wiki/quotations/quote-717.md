## Quote

> Compaq's release was . . . great PR, but eventually, it's quite possible that corporations will have to sort out their own toxic waste from ordinary office trash.

## Rationale

Within a few years of 1992, regulations like the U.S. RCRA universal waste rules and emerging state battery laws pushed companies to segregate items such as nickel-cadmium batteries and other hazardous office wastes from general trash, and many large firms introduced separate collection streams for batteries, toner cartridges, and electronics. However, the dominant pattern was manufacturer take-back and specialized recyclers handling hazardous materials rather than corporations broadly being legally compelled to sort all of their own toxic waste at the trash-can level, so the direction toward greater corporate responsibility was right but the implied universality and manner were overstated for the 3‑year horizon.
