## Quote

> The 66-MHz DX2 vs. a 50-MHz chip should not be that much faster,

## Rationale

In practice, the 486DX2-66 delivered on the order of a 25–35% performance gain over a 486DX-50 in many real-world benchmarks, which most users and reviewers regarded as a substantial, clearly noticeable improvement rather than a marginal one. By 1993–1994 the DX2-66 became the de facto high-performance mainstream 486 configuration, prized specifically for its speed advantage over 25/33 MHz and 50 MHz 486 systems. Kastner’s implication that users would not see much additional performance thus understated both the benchmarked gains and the market’s strong preference for the DX2-66 within a year or two of launch.
