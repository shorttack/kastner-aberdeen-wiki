## Quote

> If users want to enter into enterprisewide development projects, version control is mandatory,

## Rationale

By the mid‑1990s, large-scale client/server and other enterprise software projects were rapidly standardizing on source-code control systems such as PVCS, ClearCase, and later Microsoft Visual SourceSafe, which was acquired and pushed aggressively into the Visual Studio toolchain. Within roughly three years of 1993, multi-developer, enterprise application teams were widely expected—by both vendors and methodologists—to use version control as basic infrastructure, and major RAD tools (including PowerBuilder and Visual Basic) commonly integrated with SCC-compliant repositories. However, adoption was not literally universal, as some enterprise teams still operated with ad hoc file-sharing practices, so "mandatory" slightly overstates the reality even though it correctly captured the direction and emerging norm.
