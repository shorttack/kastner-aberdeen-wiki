# Computerworld — 1993-06-14

**Headline**: USL makes microkernel move

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> A microkernel approach may in fact allow a lot of differences to be papered over.

## Rationale

Novell’s attempt to use a UnixWare/microkernel basis to reconcile NetWare and USL Unix never succeeded; the ambitious “SuperNOS” integration was abandoned, and Novell sold its Unix business to SCO in 1995 without having effectively ‘papered over’ the architectural differences between the two cores. More broadly, 1990s multi-personality microkernel efforts such as IBM’s Workplace OS and Mach-based systems struggled with complexity and performance, and were largely dropped, so the idea that microkernels would conveniently hide major OS-core differences did not play out in mainstream enterprise platforms. While microkernels later proved valuable in niches like QNX, L4, and seL4 for modularity and isolation, those uses did not validate the specific hope that they would smooth over deep incompatibilities between large, existing general-purpose operating systems within the 2–3 year horizon.
