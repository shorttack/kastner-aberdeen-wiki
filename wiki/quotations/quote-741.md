## Quote

## Rationale

Within three years of the 1993 article, NCR’s software assets such as Teradata and its retail/financial systems software were increasingly organized and marketed as part of integrated solution portfolios, and the company was spun off from AT&T in 1996 with a clearer focus on data warehousing and industry-specific systems. However, during this 1993–1996 window AT&T’s NCR/Global Information Solutions unit significantly underperformed expectations, prompted write‑downs, and did not show the kind of strong software-led growth that would clearly validate the idea that a new software division had unlocked major expansion. The prediction that NCR would be better positioned to expand and develop its product portfolio was directionally correct in organizational terms, but the business results and market impact in the stated horizon were mixed.
