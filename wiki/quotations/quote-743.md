## Rationale

Kastner argued in 1993 that entrenched dBase applications were not going to disappear anytime soon; in fact, dBase remained in use well past the five-year horizon, even as it became obsolete for most new development and was increasingly displaced by client/server RDBMS and tools like Microsoft Access.[1][7] The product line itself continued with new commercial releases into the 2000s and 2010s,[5][7] and as of the mid-2020s hundreds of companies still run dBase in legacy scenarios, confirming that these applications persisted far longer than five years.[1][8]
