## Quote

> Reading between the lines of CA's white papers, these are not maintenance releases CA has planned. They're planning to integrate ASK technologies into [their] broad product set and into the CA '90s architecture,

## Rationale

CA did acquire ASK in 1994 and publicly positioned the ASK portfolio, including Ingres and MANMAN, as part of its broader CA 90s architecture, but over the subsequent five years these products largely remained aging, standalone lines with modest enhancements rather than being deeply woven into a unified platform. CA became known for running acquired software as maintenance cash cows, and Ingres in particular stagnated relative to Oracle, DB2, and SQL Server before ultimately being spun out in the 2000s, indicating that genuine, strategic integration of ASK technologies into CA’s broader product set was limited. Detailed public records of CA’s internal architectural efforts are sparse, but the product trajectories and market perception by the late 1990s contradict the claim that these were anything more than lightly-modernized legacy offerings.
