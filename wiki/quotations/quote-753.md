## Quote

> And it's not for someone with a staff of a hundred Cobol programmers who wouldn't know a PC if it bit them. With those caveats, the three-tier architecture is now becoming the de facto topology of choice for serious [on-line transaction processing]-based applications.

## Rationale

By the late 1990s, three-tier and broader n‑tier architectures had become the mainstream pattern for new large-scale business and transactional applications, especially web-based banking, e‑commerce, and enterprise portals, matching his claim that three-tier would become the de facto topology for serious OLTP-style systems.[3][5][7] Industry practice shifted from two-tier client/server toward a presentation–application–data split, with dedicated application servers such as WebLogic, WebSphere, and later J2EE/.NET adopting this model as the standard enterprise approach.[2][5][7] Kastner slightly overstated how immediate and universal this was in 1994—mainframe COBOL OLTP remained dominant for many incumbents—but within the five-year horizon his directional call about three‑tier becoming the preferred architecture for serious new transactional workloads was largely borne out.
