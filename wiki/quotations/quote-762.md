## Rationale

AT&T’s 1995 decision to break itself into separate companies for services (AT&T), equipment (Lucent, including Bell Labs), and computing (NCR) did create new vendor relationships and contractual complexity for large enterprise customers, but it did not become a widely cited or unusually severe systems integration crisis compared with other mid‑1990s integration challenges such as client–server migration, Internet adoption, and multi-vendor networking.[1] By the late 1990s, enterprises were already operating in a heterogeneous environment with Cisco, Nortel, IBM, HP, and others, so dealing with AT&T, Lucent, and NCR as distinct vendors largely fit the prevailing multi-vendor pattern rather than creating a uniquely large integration headache. Given available historical accounts, the breakup is remembered more for strategic repositioning and Lucent’s subsequent rise and fall than for substantial systems integration disruption at customer sites, so the claim’s implied magnitude appears overstated.[1]
