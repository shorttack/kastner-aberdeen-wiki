# The Christian Science Monitor — 2002-10-21

**Headline**: A move to build PCs without Windows

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> 5 percent of a 100 million PC market is huge.

## Rationale

Arithmetically and strategically, the characterization is correct: even in the early 2000s, annual PC shipments were on the order of 150–180 million units, so a 5% share would have meant many millions of systems and a substantial business in absolute terms. However, within the 3-year horizon, Linux desktop distributions such as Lindows/Linspire never approached anything like 5% of the PC market, and Windows remained overwhelmingly dominant on new PCs. The framing that a small percentage share of a large commodity market can be a meaningful opportunity was sound, but the implied near-term scale for Linux desktops did not materialize.
