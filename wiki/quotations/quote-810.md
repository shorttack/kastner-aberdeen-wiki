# MarketWatch — 2003-04-26

**Headline**: Apple to answer rumors with facts

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P1_tiebreak

## Quote

> Chip technology is small enough that Apple might be close to developing a new IPod that is as thick as just two or three credit cards and capable of carrying more information than any of its current devices.

## Rationale

The claim correctly anticipated that advances in chip technology would enable significantly thinner iPods, and within three years Apple introduced the flash-based iPod nano (2005) and slimmer hard-drive iPods, confirming the general direction. However, by 2006 no iPod was anywhere near as thin as two or three credit cards, and the highest-capacity models were still relatively thick hard-drive iPods (up to roughly 40–60GB) while the very thin nano topped out at only a few gigabytes, so the specific prediction of a device that was both ultra-thin and higher-capacity than all existing iPods did not materialize. Overall, the forecast captured the form-factor trend but missed the capacity trade-offs and exact form-factor extremity in the stated horizon.
