# Computerworld — 1989-02-20

**Headline**: Users finding ESA alternatives

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> There are tens of thousands of companies that can only afford a $250,000 to $500,000 machine. The 4381s will be around for a long time.

## Rationale

IBM’s 4300 series, including the 4381, remained marketed and supported through the early 1990s, with the family being sold up to around 1992, so 4381 systems were indeed still widely deployed within the three-year horizon after 1989. Many smaller and mid-sized organizations continued using older System/370-class machines rather than moving immediately to ESA-based high-end systems, because ESA/370 and later ESA/390 platforms (e.g., 3090, early ES/9000/System/390) were significantly more expensive and targeted at larger enterprises. However, although the 4381s persisted into the early 1990s as cost-conscious choices, they were clearly on a migration path toward ESA-era hardware and did not remain a major strategic platform much beyond that, so describing them as being around “for a long time” slightly overstates their enduring centrality.
