# Computerworld — 1989-03-13

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> I think we'll be seeing people use their machines for only a few years,

## Rationale

Between 1989 and roughly 1992, the economics and pace of improvement in client systems (PCs and Unix workstations) were already pushing organizations toward 3–4 year refresh cycles, a sharp contrast with the decade-long lifetimes of many minicomputers and mainframes. Rapid performance jumps from 286 to 386 to 486 processors, falling hardware prices, and the rise of relatively low-cost Unix workstations from vendors like Sun, HP, and DEC made keeping older machines unattractive and normalized the idea that desktop and departmental systems would be replaced after only a few years. The prediction slightly overstates the immediacy and universality of “throwaway” behavior in that period, especially for servers, but it captured the emerging replacement-cycle dynamic for workstations and PCs quite well.
