# Computerworld — 1989-03-13

**Headline**: Mini vendors adapt in order to survive

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> By then the technology will have leapfrogged anything you have and you'll get another. And the cost will allow that.

## Rationale

From 1989 to the early 1990s, CPU and graphics performance in both PCs and Unix workstations advanced rapidly (for example, the move from 80386 to 80486 PCs and the rise of affordable RISC workstations), making three- to four-year replacement cycles economically rational. Hardware prices declined while price/performance improved sharply, and by the early 1990s many enterprises had adopted short depreciation and refresh cycles for client systems, treating them as relatively disposable compared with earlier minicomputers. Kastner slightly overstates how universal and immediate this “throwaway” behavior was within three years, but the core idea that technology would quickly leapfrog installed systems and that lower cost would enable frequent replacement was largely borne out.
