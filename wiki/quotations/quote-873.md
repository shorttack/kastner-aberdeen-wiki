**Headline**: Tesseract, Walker to be financial software allies

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P1_tiebreak

## Quote

> it becomes a no-brainer for software houses to begin to blend their applications using DB2.

## Rationale

By the early 1990s, DB2 had in fact become the default relational database for large IBM mainframe shops, and many enterprise ISVs targeting Fortune 1000 customers (particularly in financials, HR, and ERP on IBM platforms) did add DB2-backed versions and integrate their applications around it. Within that IBM-centric segment, supporting and "blending" applications on DB2 was close to a no-brainer, but across the broader enterprise software market the rapid rise of Unix client–server systems and alternative databases like Oracle, Sybase, and Informix meant that DB2 was far from the universal integration choice Kastner’s wording implies.
