# Computerworld — 1989-08-28

**Headline**: PRODUCT SPOTLIGHT; The DEC market

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> DEC can already claim they have [transparent computing] from the desk to the data center. SAA won't have that for many years,

## Rationale

From 1989 through the early 1990s, DEC’s tightly integrated VAX/VMS, DECnet, terminal servers, and DECwindows stack did provide a comparatively cohesive “desk to data center” environment within DEC-centric shops, especially relative to most multivendor or IBM PC/mainframe combinations of the time. IBM’s SAA vision, centered on common interfaces and Presentation Manager across MVS, AS/400, and OS/2, remained slow, complex, and only partially realized by the 1992 timeframe, and never delivered the seamless transparency originally promised, so the claim that SAA would lag for many years was directionally accurate. The forecast overstates DEC’s long-term strategic advantage, since both DEC’s NAS/DECwindows and IBM’s SAA were soon overshadowed by Windows, TCP/IP networking, and web-centric client/server models, but that played out beyond the stated three‑year horizon.
