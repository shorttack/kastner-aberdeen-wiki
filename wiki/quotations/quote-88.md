# Computerworld — 1992-06-01

**Headline**: Users leery of multiprocessing

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-5y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “We now have enough other architectural and topological ways to put systems together because of networking software so that multiprocessing is no longer the only logical growth path,'' said Peter Kastner, vice president at Aberdeen Group, a consulting firm in Boston.

## Rationale

By the mid-1990s, enterprise computing had decisively shifted toward client-server architectures, LAN-connected PCs, and departmental servers, so scaling via networking and distributed systems was a mainstream alternative to buying bigger multiprocessor boxes. Products such as DEC VAXcluster and later Microsoft clustering, distributed databases, and web server farms all exemplified ‘scale-out’ via networks, while SMP/MPP systems continued in parallel, confirming that multiprocessing was no longer the only logical growth path. The longer arc into the 2000s and 2010s—with web-scale architectures, cloud computing, and microservices—further validated the idea that networking-based topologies became at least as important as traditional multiprocessing for growth.
