# Computerworld — 1989-12-11

**Headline**: Workstations drawn more toward

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> The ability to write code, format it and do some checkout down on the PC has an enormous attractiveness and offers self-evident benefit.

## Rationale

From 1989 to the early 1990s, software development rapidly shifted from mainframe- and minicomputer-hosted toolchains toward PC and Unix workstation-based IDEs such as Borland Turbo C/C++, Microsoft C/QuickC, and later Visual Basic, validating that local coding, formatting, and checkout on a personal machine were indeed highly attractive to developers. By roughly 1992, doing most editing, compiling, and unit testing on a developer’s own PC or workstation was becoming the norm in client–server and departmental computing, reducing dependence on central mainframe resources. That pattern has persisted through 2026, with standard practice centered on powerful local or laptop environments (e.g., Visual Studio, Eclipse, IntelliJ, VS Code) backed by remote build and integration systems, confirming the long-run self-evident benefits of local development capabilities.
