# Computerworld — 1989-12-25

**Headline**: Support for open systems builds piece by piece

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> I just don't see all the various players getting together in 1990, or possibly ever,

## Rationale

Kastner’s skepticism that “all the various players” would come together around open systems in 1990 proved accurate: the UNIX community remained split between AT&T/Sun’s Unix International and the Open Software Foundation, and no unified USO-style consolidation materialized by the early 1990s. Even beyond the 3‑year horizon, attempts such as COSE (1993) and later X/Open/Single UNIX Specification created standards but did not produce a single unified UNIX or truly harmonious vendor coalition, while fragmentation persisted across Solaris, AIX, HP‑UX, and others. Over the longer term, the commercial UNIX vendors never fully “got together” before being strategically eclipsed by Windows NT and then Linux, aligning with his “possibly ever” qualifier as well as the near-term 1990 timeframe.
