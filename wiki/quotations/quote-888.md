# Computerworld — 1990-05-07

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Major application suppliers are using 4GLs and CASE technology to allow users to maintain and customize their base-level applications,

## Rationale

By the early 1990s, major enterprise application vendors such as SAP, Oracle, and PeopleSoft broadly used proprietary 4GL-style environments (e.g., ABAP/4, Oracle Forms, PeopleTools) to let customers modify and extend packaged applications without working directly in low-level languages. CASE-style repositories and model-driven tools were also integrated into many package development and customization workflows, although in practice they were used more by IT professionals and implementers than by true end-users. The direction of the claim—that packaged application suppliers would embed 4GL/CASE technology to support ongoing maintenance and customization—held up well through roughly 1993, even if the ease-of-use and genuine “end-user” empowerment were more limited than the wording implies.
