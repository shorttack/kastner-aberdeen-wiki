## Rationale

From 1990–1993, large multi-division firms increasingly adopted integrated, general-purpose financial and ERP-style suites (e.g., SAP R/2 and early R/3, Oracle Financials, and emerging PeopleSoft modules) specifically because they could consolidate multiple subsidiaries and business units. At the same time, organizations with many lines of business found that stitching together disparate vertical packages created costly integration and reporting headaches, which directly reduced the perceived advantage of those siloed vertical systems and set up the broader ERP consolidation wave of the mid-1990s. The statement slightly overgeneralizes the loss of value from vertical packages, but its core point about integration and roll-up needs driving preference for general packages in multi-business firms was directionally and operationally correct in that early-1990s window.
