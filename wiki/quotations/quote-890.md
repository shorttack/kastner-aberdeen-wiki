# Computerworld — 1990-05-07

**Headline**: Integration vs. specialization

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> you start to lose the edge of vertical packages that don't talk to each other too well. General packages are designed from the beginning to roll up subsidiaries.

## Rationale

From the early 1990s onward, multi-division firms increasingly standardized on general-purpose, integrated financial/ERP suites such as SAP (R/2, then R/3), Oracle Financials, and later PeopleSoft and Baan specifically because they supported multi-entity consolidation and subsidiary roll‑up better than disparate vertical accounting packages. By the mid‑ to late‑1990s and into the 2000s, these general suites dominated large enterprises while many verticals survived only by integrating into or coexisting with these systems rather than displacing them, confirming the direction of Kastner’s argument even if specialized verticals never fully “lost the edge” in all contexts.
