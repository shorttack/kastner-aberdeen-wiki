# Computerworld — 1991-10-14

**Headline**: Unix System Labs signs on with ACE

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Over the long run, it will be most beneficial to buyers because it will standardize the features and functions of an open systems environment.

## Rationale

Kastner tied buyer benefits and standardization of open-systems features to the USL/ACE Unix consolidation, but ACE rapidly lost momentum and Unix remained fragmented among multiple incompatible commercial variants (Solaris/SVR4, HP-UX, AIX, OSF/1 and others) through the early–mid 1990s, with vendors continuing to differentiate their systems. Over this period, the main convergence came instead from generic standards such as POSIX and the emerging X/Open Single UNIX Specification, which defined a common API baseline but did not fully standardize higher-level features or eliminate behavioral differences across Unix systems.[2][4] As a result, the specific initiative he was praising did not, within about three years, deliver the broad, buyer-visible standardization of open systems features that his comment implies.
