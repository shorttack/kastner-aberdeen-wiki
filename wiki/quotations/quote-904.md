## Quote

> Compaq's release was . . . great PR, but eventually, it's quite possible that corporations will have to sort out their own toxic waste from ordinary office trash,

## Rationale

Within three years of 1992, U.S. regulators moved to tighten controls on hazardous batteries, culminating in the EPA’s 1995 Universal Waste Rule and, shortly after the horizon, the 1996 Battery Act, which pushed organizations to keep nickel–cadmium cells and similar items out of ordinary trash streams. Large enterprises increasingly implemented internal recycling and take‑back programs for items like NiCd batteries and toner cartridges in the mid‑1990s, effectively requiring some separation of hazardous or recyclable office waste from general garbage. However, the obligation was not universally enforced at the individual corporate level within that three‑year window, and much of the operational burden was shared with manufacturers, retailers, and specialized recyclers rather than corporations being solely responsible for sorting all their toxic waste from office trash.
