# Computerworld — 1992-06-01

**Headline**: Users leery of multiprocessing

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> We now have enough other architectural and topological ways to put systems together because of networking software so that multiprocessing is no longer the only logical growth path,

## Rationale

Between 1992 and 1995, LAN-based client-server computing, powered by Novell NetWare, Microsoft Windows NT Advanced Server, and Unix TCP/IP networks, became the dominant way enterprises scaled capacity, validating networking as a major alternative to buying ever-larger SMP machines. Distributed and clustered architectures such as VAXcluster systems, network-of-workstations setups using Sun SPARCstations, and early Unix and NT application servers allowed organizations to scale horizontally across multiple networked systems instead of relying solely on symmetric or asymmetric multiprocessing. Although SMP servers from vendors like Compaq, Sun, HP, and IBM continued to grow in capability and adoption, they shared the stage with rapidly expanding network-based architectures, so multiprocessing clearly was no longer the only logical growth path within the 3-year horizon.
