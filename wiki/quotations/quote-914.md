# Computerworld — 1992-06-15

**Headline**: Alpha-ready VAX line expected in July

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> They've basically taken their customers into the DECworld candy shop and said, `Here's all the Alpha goodies, but you can't have them for one to two years,' which makes it exceedingly difficult to justify why you should go out and purchase a MicroVAX today, which is what DEC desperately needs,

## Rationale

Kastner’s critique that DEC’s early Alpha marketing would make it hard to justify new MicroVAX purchases was largely borne out: the 1992 Alpha announcement significantly preceded broad, affordable system availability, contributing to an Osborne-effect dynamic in which customers hesitated on fresh VAX/MicroVAX buys while they evaluated migration paths. Over the 1992–1995 window, VAX and MicroVAX hardware sales declined sharply while Alpha adoption ramped more slowly than hoped, and DEC’s financial condition worsened, reinforcing later historical analyses that faulted DEC’s muddled VAX-to-Alpha transition and pre-announcement strategy for undermining its legacy minicomputer line. The statement is somewhat overstated because the collapse of VAX/MicroVAX demand also reflected broader RISC competition, client/server shifts, and macroeconomic pressures, not just Alpha marketing timing, so it is best scored as largely rather than perfectly prescient.
