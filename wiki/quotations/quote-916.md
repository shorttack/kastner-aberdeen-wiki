# Computerworld — 1992-07-13

**Headline**: Intel to unleash DX2, claims strongest chip yet

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> You'll get the delta between a 50 MHz and a 66 MHz. However, as soon as you go to memory, all bets are off.

## Rationale

The 486DX2-66’s doubled core clock but 33 MHz external bus meant that while it outperformed a 50 MHz 486 in compute-heavy benchmarks, the gains were often modest once workloads became memory- or I/O-bound, and in some memory-intensive cases the 50 MHz bus of a 486DX-50 could keep pace or even edge ahead. This early observation that memory and bus speed could negate much of the benefit of a higher internal clock foreshadowed the well-documented CPU–memory bottleneck that drove the industry toward larger on-chip caches, faster memory buses, and later technologies like SDRAM and L2/L3 caches in the Pentium era and beyond. However, he slightly understated the typical real-world advantage DX2/66 systems delivered in mainstream applications, which is why the score is not a 5.
