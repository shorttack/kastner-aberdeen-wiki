## Quote

> “Oracle's basic business model is changing,'' said Peter Kastner, vice president of Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

Within five years of 1992, Oracle had aggressively expanded its Oracle Applications product line and built a sizable consulting and services organization under Ray Lane, shifting emphasis beyond its core database license business[3][4]. However, through the late 1990s, software licenses and related maintenance still made up the bulk of Oracle’s revenue, so the business model evolved toward being more applications- and services-driven rather than fully transforming in that period[4]. This makes the forecast directionally strong but somewhat overstated in terms of the speed and depth of change over the five-year horizon.
