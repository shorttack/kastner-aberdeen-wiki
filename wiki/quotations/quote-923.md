## Quote

> Modular PCs are somewhat attractive, but users also become locked into a vendor's PC line because you can only buy the upgrade from them,

## Rationale

In practice, proprietary modular PC schemes such as DEC and IBM upgrade modules remained niche and short-lived, while by the mid-1990s enterprises mostly bought commodity 386/486 and early Pentium PCs built around standard ISA/PCI buses and interchangeable components from many vendors. As a result, modular hardware did not significantly lock customers into a single vendor’s PC line over the next few years; hardware lock-in weakened while software and OS ecosystems (notably Windows and Office) became the primary sources of dependency instead.
