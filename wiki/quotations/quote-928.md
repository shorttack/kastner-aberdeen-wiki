# Computerworld — 1992-11-02

**Headline**: Stratus still rock solid as it moves CISC users to RISC

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Where fault tolerance is absolutely required, Stratus is a standout leader, but the company will be experiencing greater difficulty in reaching a wide section of the market.

## Rationale

Through the mid‑1990s Stratus remained a leading supplier for ultra‑high‑availability transaction systems in telecom and financial switching, but shipped comparatively modest volumes next to fast‑growing Unix/RISC and PC server vendors. Between 1992 and 1995, clustering, RAID storage, and software‑based replication on commodity servers spread rapidly, allowing most enterprises to achieve acceptable uptime without specialized fault‑tolerant hardware and confining Stratus largely to a high‑end niche. Later developments, including Stratus’s focus on specialized fault‑tolerant x86 servers and its sequence of acquisitions, further demonstrate that it stayed a standout in strict fault‑tolerance use cases while failing to capture a broad share of the general server market.
