# Computerworld — 1992-06-15

**Headline**: Alpha-ready VAX line expected in July

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “They've basically taken their customers into the DECworld candy shop and said, `Here's all the Alpha goodies, but you can't have them for one to two years,' which makes it exceedingly difficult to justify why you should go out and purchase a MicroVAX today, which is what DEC desperately needs,'' said Peter Kastner, vice president at Aberdeen Group, a market research firm based in Boston.

## Rationale

DEC introduced the 64-bit Alpha architecture in 1992 explicitly as the successor to its 32-bit VAX and MicroVAX systems, signaling that the older line was technologically superseded within the 1–2 year window Kastner referenced. Over the next few years, VAX and MicroVAX sales eroded while DEC shifted marketing, engineering, and customer roadmaps toward Alpha-based systems, contributing to declining demand for new VAX-class purchases even though DEC still needed that revenue. This pattern matches his warning that preannouncing “Alpha goodies” would undercut short-term MicroVAX buying incentives, although macro factors like the early-1990s recession and competition from Unix/RISC vendors also played major roles in DEC’s struggles.
