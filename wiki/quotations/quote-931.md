## Quote

> The bad news is that Access alone should not be viewed as a tool that can craft all the applications in an enterprise.

## Rationale

Access launched in 1992 as a user-friendly desktop DBMS and quickly became popular for small workgroup and departmental applications, but it was not adopted as the primary platform for building all enterprise applications.[4][5] Through the mid-1990s, larger organizations continued to rely on more scalable platforms like SQL Server, Oracle, and mainframe or client–server tools for mission-critical and high-concurrency systems, with Access often serving only as a front end or for smaller solutions.[4] Over time, concerns about scalability, multi-user limits, and advanced database features reinforced that Access alone was insufficient for an enterprise-wide application portfolio, validating the analyst’s caution even beyond the 3-year horizon.[6][8]
