# Computerworld — 1992-12-21

**Headline**: Middleware speeds client connection

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> We're moving into a new generation of smart front-end tools that know how to spit out the relevant SQL.

## Rationale

Within three years of 1992, mainstream client/server development heavily adopted front-end tools that automatically generated SQL, such as Microsoft Access (1992) with its graphical query designer, Visual Basic 3.0 (1993) data controls, and Sybase PowerBuilder’s DataWindow technology (1993–94), all of which emitted vendor-specific SQL under the hood. Fourth-generation languages and report/query tools like Oracle Forms, Gupta SQLWindows, Business Objects, and Crystal Reports similarly insulated developers and business users from writing raw SQL while producing dialect-appropriate queries. This pattern not only matched his short-term horizon but continued into the late 1990s and 2000s with ORMs and BI tools further entrenching the idea that smart front ends would handle SQL generation automatically.
