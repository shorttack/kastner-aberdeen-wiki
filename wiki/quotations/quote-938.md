# Computerworld — 1993-01-11

**Headline**: Apple exec jumps to Microsoft

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Microsoft is admitting that their database strategy is not nearly as far along as they had hoped and that they needed a world-class software manager with vision to come in and shake things up,

## Rationale

The comment correctly reflects that, circa 1993, Microsoft’s database strategy was still behind entrenched enterprise rivals like Oracle, Informix, and Sybase, and was in a period of rethinking and reorganization, including the unwinding of its co-development relationship with Sybase and a pivot to owning SQL Server on Windows NT. Over the following three years, however, Microsoft rapidly advanced its database offerings with popular desktop products such as Microsoft Access 1.1/2.0 and major server releases like SQL Server 6.0 (1995) and 6.5 (1996), establishing a credible and growing position rather than merely “getting its database market off the ground.” In hindsight the claim that Microsoft needed a visionary manager to shake up a lagging strategy captures part of the truth about its transitional state, but overstates how underdeveloped the strategy was given how quickly its database portfolio matured by 1996.
