## Quote

> Our research shows that enterprise buyers who are looking at distributed systems view Unix as a difficult and expensive operating system. They want the same quality of tools they're used to using in the corporate data center,

## Rationale

From 1993 to 1996, enterprise Unix remained concentrated on proprietary platforms like Solaris, HP-UX, and AIX, which were widely perceived as requiring scarce, specialized administrators and expensive hardware compared with emerging Windows NT on commodity x86. During this period, the rapid growth of systems management suites such as CA Unicenter, IBM Tivoli (acquired in 1996), HP OpenView, and BMC Patrol specifically targeted the gap in Unix and distributed-systems management tooling, reflecting buyers’ desire for mainframe-style operations tools on Unix. Although Unix adoption continued to grow in servers and technical workstations, the characterization of it as difficult and costly to manage, and of enterprises wanting data-center-grade management tools for distributed Unix environments, held up well over the three-year horizon.
