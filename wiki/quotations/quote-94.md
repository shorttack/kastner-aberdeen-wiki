# Computerworld — 1992-07-13

**Headline**: Price war to hurt PC industry

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> “Clearly, by the time this is all over, the industry is going to wish this had been the summer that never happened,'' said Peter Kastner, an analyst at Aberdeen Group in Boston.

## Rationale

Compaq’s 1992 price cuts triggered a sustained PC price war into 1993 that sharply reduced average selling prices and margins, pressuring many vendors’ profits and accelerating the shakeout of weaker players such as Zenith Data Systems, CompuAdd, and DEC’s PC line. At the same time, overall PC unit shipments continued to grow in 1992–1994, and leaders like Compaq and other major Wintel OEMs gained market share and benefited strategically from the expanded market. The episode was painful but not a catastrophe the entire industry ‘wished had never happened,’ so the warning about broad industry regret was overstated even though the call on severe short‑term pain for many suppliers was directionally right.
