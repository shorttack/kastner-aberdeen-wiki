# Computerworld — 1993-04-19

**Headline**: CA gets on Microsoft bandwagon

**Verdict**: LOW prescience — score=1, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> financial suicide. If CA has Unicenter on a mainframe for $150,000 to $250,000, why in God's name would they shrink-wrap it on NT for a fraction of the cost?

## Rationale

Within three years of 1993, CA did in fact bring Unicenter to Windows NT at much lower per-server prices and entered a co-marketing relationship with Microsoft, and this move became a core part of Unicenter’s growth rather than causing serious financial harm. Through the mid- to late 1990s CA remained profitable with strong systems management revenues as Windows NT and distributed systems management suites (including Unicenter, Tivoli, and HP OpenView) expanded rapidly, so the prediction that offering a cheaper NT version would be “financial suicide” was clearly contradicted by events.
