# Computerworld — 1993-05-03

**Headline**: Sybase sets its own direction

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Clearly, they can differentiate themselves by finding a better model for distributed enterprise computing, and that's what they believe they've got with System 10.

## Rationale

System 10 did introduce a more fully featured client/server and distributed database platform for Sybase, but it did not become a clearly superior or market-defining model for distributed enterprise computing relative to Oracle7 or emerging competitors. Within three years, Sybase was losing share and momentum to Oracle and to Microsoft SQL Server, and System 10’s technical and quality problems hurt rather than strengthened Sybase’s differentiation, leading to subsequent corrective releases (System 11/ASE) and a long-term position as a niche rather than leading enterprise database vendor. Thus the idea that System 10 gave Sybase a sustainably differentiating and better model for distributed enterprise computing mostly failed to materialize.
