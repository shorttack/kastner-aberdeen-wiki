## Quote

> With the Pentium processor and added symmetric processing head room, NCR has clearly pushed its midrange performance target well into the mainframe class with the 3575,

## Rationale

By the mid‑1990s, Pentium-based symmetric multiprocessing Unix servers from vendors like NCR, HP, and Sun were indeed taking on some online transaction processing workloads that had previously required smaller mainframes, so in raw performance terms the direction of Kastner’s remark was partially borne out. However, IBM’s ES/9000 and later System/390 mainframes retained a clear lead in scalability, I/O bandwidth, and reliability, and most core banking and large enterprise transaction systems remained on traditional mainframes through at least the late 1990s, so NCR’s 3575 was not broadly regarded as truly “mainframe-class.” Thus the claim captured an emerging performance trend but overstated how far and how quickly midrange Pentium servers had moved “well into the mainframe class” over the three-year horizon.
