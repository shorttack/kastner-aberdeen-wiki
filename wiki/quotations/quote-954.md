## Quote

> We are seeing an increasing interest in the marketplace for high-availability systems for people who are not willing to pay the substantial price penalty for fault-tolerant systems,

## Rationale

From 1993 to about 1996, enterprise buyers increasingly adopted lower-cost high-availability Unix and emerging Windows NT clusters (e.g., IBM HACMP, HP MC/ServiceGuard, early Microsoft Wolfpack) instead of classic lockstep fault-tolerant systems from Tandem, Stratus, and Sequoia for many workloads. These HA offerings, built on commodity or midrange servers, provided "good enough" resilience at far lower prices and became the volume segment, while fully fault-tolerant platforms remained specialized and relatively niche. Kastner’s characterization of rising market interest in high-availability over premium fault-tolerance thus captured the direction and economics of the mid-1990s server market accurately, even if it was more a sharp observation of an unfolding trend than a bold, contrarian forecast.
