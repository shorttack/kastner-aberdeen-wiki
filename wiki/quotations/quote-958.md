# Computerworld — 1993-06-14

**Headline**: USL makes microkernel move

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P1_tiebreak

## Quote

> A microkernel approach may in fact allow a lot of differences to be papered over.

## Rationale

Within three years, Novell's attempt to use a Chorus-based microkernel (the "SuperNOS" effort) to unify NetWare and Unix System V Release 4 had largely failed, and company officials were already conceding by 1995 that wholesale integration of the two environments on a microkernel might not be achievable. Novell's acquisition of USL is now widely regarded as unsuccessful, and the unified microkernel-based product never became a significant, shipping platform, indicating that the architectural differences between NetWare and Unix were not effectively "papered over" in practice. While microkernels such as Mach and the NT-style hybrid did enable multiple OS personalities, they did not, in this case or timeframe, deliver the seamless masking of deep architectural differences that the quote cautiously suggested might be possible.
