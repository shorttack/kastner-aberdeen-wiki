## Quote

> There's no doubt that if you have a DOS-to-Windows problem today, Wingate sure looks like it beats rolling your own code. But over the long term, Microsoft's stated direction is to make this [connection] nearly transparent with Chicago,

## Rationale

Windows 95 (code-named Chicago) shipped in August 1995 and tightly integrated the DOS and Windows environments, allowing most DOS applications to run in virtual DOS sessions and making day-to-day DOS/Windows interoperability far more seamless than under Windows 3.x. Within about a year it had very large market share on business and consumer PCs, reducing the need for specialized DOS-to-Windows migration shims like Wingate, although some edge cases and power users still depended on lower-level DOS access and tools, so the connection was not entirely transparent for all workloads.
