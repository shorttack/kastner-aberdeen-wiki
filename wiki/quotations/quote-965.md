## Quote

> Our research shows that there's a strong interest in NT by Fortune 1,000 companies, especially where OS/2 was the underlying operating system for a specific database or as an application preparation package for something like Lotus Notes,

## Rationale

Between 1993 and 1996, Windows NT 3.1/3.5/3.51 saw rapidly growing adoption in Fortune 1000 data centers, particularly as a server OS for databases and groupware, while OS/2’s presence in those roles stagnated and then declined. Many early Lotus Notes and database deployments that had been built on OS/2 were indeed re-platformed onto NT as enterprises standardized on Microsoft’s stack and moved away from OS/2, though NT was still sharing space with NetWare and Unix rather than fully displacing them within three years.
