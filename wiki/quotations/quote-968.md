# Computerworld — 1993-11-01

**Headline**: Start-ups bring heavyweight tools

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> And business objects will allow businesses to create units, such as a purchasing function, and then snap together applications from templates.

## Rationale

By the 1996 horizon, object-oriented tools and client/server RAD environments had improved reuse, but most enterprises were still building bespoke applications rather than snapping together domain-level business objects like a complete purchasing function from templates. Componentized, template-based business-object frameworks for line-of-business domains emerged more meaningfully in the later 1990s and 2000s, as reflected by work on business-object–centric platforms and patents for building business objects and applications from templates filed toward the end of the decade.[6] The quote anticipated a longer-term direction toward componentized, higher-level business constructs, but its implied near-term ease and modularity were not realized within three years of 1993.
