# Computerworld — 1993-11-29

**Headline**: Software vendors converge on database market

## Quote

> The database vendor focus for these tools has a lot of merit because IS needs to keep control of the implementation, as opposed to letting individual departments go out and buy all their own query tools,

## Rationale

By the mid-1990s, while major DBMS vendors like Oracle, Sybase, IBM, and Informix did ship their own query and reporting tools, organizations increasingly adopted independent BI/query products such as Business Objects, Cognos Impromptu/PowerPlay, Brio, and Crystal Reports, often purchased and driven by departments rather than centralized IS. ODBC and later JDBC made it easy for generic desktop tools—including Microsoft Access and Excel (with pivot tables)—to connect to enterprise databases, which strongly enabled departmental and end‑user query tool proliferation rather than tight IS control over a single database-vendor tool stack. Within the 3-year horizon (through ~1996), the dominant trajectory was toward a mix of central IT–sanctioned tools plus rapidly growing departmental and end‑user tools, so the implied direction—that database-vendor-focused tools would align with IS’s need to keep control and head off departmental tool buying—was mostly undercut by how quickly best‑of‑breed and desktop query tools spread.
