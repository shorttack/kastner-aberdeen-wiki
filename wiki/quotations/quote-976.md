## Quote

> Users see no reason to buy from a tiny, unknown object company if they can wait, safe and warm, for Oracle to get [them] to where they want to go,

## Rationale

Through the mid-1990s, most enterprise buyers standardized on major relational vendors such as Oracle, Sybase, and Informix and were reluctant to bet core systems on small object database specialists like Object Design, Versant, GemStone, or O2, which remained niche despite heavy buzz. By the three-year mark, Oracle8 (1997) and other object-relational releases from the big vendors had shipped, giving conservative customers a way to add object-like features while staying with their incumbent supplier, while many pure OODBMS vendors stayed confined to specialized CAD/CAM, telecom, and finance use cases. The quote slightly overstates things (“no reason”) because some organizations did adopt products from those smaller object vendors, but its core characterization of mainstream buyer behavior and the safety appeal of waiting for Oracle largely held true.
