# Computerworld — 1994-01-03

**Headline**: Price wars pressure low end Forecast

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=5, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> The [high-end] suppliers have to cover their very high R&D costs [up to 25% of sales] and marketing and support, as they help hand-hold users in learning how to use the more powerful tools. If you think that you're going to get that for $199, wish again.

## Rationale

From 1994–1997, high-end enterprise systems such as SAP R/3, Oracle databases and applications, PeopleSoft, and Lotus Notes sold for thousands to millions of dollars per deployment, with customers paying separately for extensive consulting, training, and premium support rather than getting it bundled into low-cost licenses. Over the same period, shrink-wrapped packages like Microsoft Office, consumer databases, and development tools were priced in the low hundreds of dollars and came with only limited phone support or manuals, not the intensive hand-holding characteristic of enterprise vendors. This structural separation between low-priced boxed software and high-cost, high-touch enterprise offerings persisted well beyond the three-year horizon, validating the claim that users would not get high-end tools plus heavy support for around $199.
