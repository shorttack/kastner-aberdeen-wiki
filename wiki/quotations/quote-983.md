## Quote

> The open systems environment is forced with so many different suppliers and so many different products that the costs and complexity have gotten totally out of hand,

## Rationale

By the mid‑1990s, enterprises were indeed struggling with highly fragmented 'open systems' environments, with multiple incompatible Unix variants (Solaris, HP‑UX, AIX, Digital UNIX, SCO) alongside NetWare and emerging Windows NT, which drove up integration and administration costs. Systems management suites such as HP OpenView, IBM Tivoli, and CA Unicenter grew rapidly precisely to tame the operational complexity of heterogeneous multi‑vendor networks and servers, and high total cost of ownership for these environments became a major theme in mid‑ to late‑1990s IT strategy. Over the longer term, the continued proliferation of Unix and then Linux distributions, middleware stacks, and open‑source components kept integration complexity and indirect costs as central concerns into the 2000s and 2010s, confirming that his characterization captured a real and persistent problem rather than a transient issue.
