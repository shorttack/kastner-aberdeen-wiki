# Computerworld — 1994-09-12

**Headline**: High-end tools gain steam

**Verdict**: HIGH prescience — score=4, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> This is not your 20-user workgroup problem. Dynasty, Seer, Forte, along with many other industry players are heading upscale into enterprise client/server both to meet market demand and to avoid the bottom-clearing juggernaut that is Microsoft's tools division,

## Rationale

By 1997, Microsoft's Visual Basic, Visual C++, and then Visual Studio had largely captured the mass-market Windows client/server and departmental development tools segment, putting strong price and volume pressure on low-end rivals such as Borland and Gupta. In response, high-end client/server and 4GL vendors including Forte Software, Seer Technologies, and Dynasty focused their offerings on large-scale, multi-tier enterprise applications and major relational databases rather than small 20-user workgroup deployments. Although many of these upscale vendors later struggled as web and Java architectures rose, within the three-year window the pattern Kastner described—Microsoft as a bottom-clearing tools juggernaut and niche tools vendors moving upmarket into enterprise client/server—reflected how the market actually evolved.
