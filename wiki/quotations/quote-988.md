## Quote

> They are trying to give people more bang for the buck, and they're more open than they ever have been.

## Rationale

Between 1994 and 1997, Tandem did in fact move its NonStop/Himalaya line toward greater openness and better price/performance relative to its own earlier systems, adding the POSIX-based Open System Services environment to the NonStop Kernel in 1995 and emphasizing parallel, scalable 'open' Himalaya servers in marketing and alliances.[1][2] The 1996 strategic alliance with Microsoft to bring NonStop-style capabilities to Windows NT Server further reinforced a shift toward industry-standard platforms and interfaces, even though Tandem systems remained premium-priced and the company was acquired by Compaq in 1997, showing that improved openness and value did not translate into long-term independence.[1][2][4]
