## Quote

> But until AT&T's announcement ... customers had to select and buy multiple technologies and then build the applications, which are not trivial.

## Rationale

Kastner’s characterization that data warehouse customers had to assemble multiple technologies and build their own applications captures the well-documented early-1990s reality, when enterprises typically integrated separate databases, ETL tools, and front-end query/reporting systems with significant custom development effort.[9] Over the next few years, vendors including AT&T GIS with Teradata-based solutions, and later integrated warehouse and BI stacks from major suppliers, explicitly targeted this integration pain point with more packaged offerings, validating that the prior do‑it‑yourself approach had indeed been the norm.[1][9] The claim is not bold, but it accurately anticipated the recognized complexity of first-generation data warehousing and the ensuing move toward more integrated solutions within the 3-year horizon.
