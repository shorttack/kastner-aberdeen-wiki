> And it's not for someone with a staff of a hundred Cobol programmers who wouldn't know a PC if it bit them. With those caveats, the three-tier architecture is now becoming the de facto topology of choice for serious [on-line transaction processing]-based applications.

## Rationale

Within roughly three years of 1994, three-tier (presentation, application, data) client/server and early web architectures had become the preferred design pattern for new distributed and OLTP-style enterprise systems, as vendors such as Microsoft and others formalized three-tier models and promoted COM+/application-server middle tiers for mission-critical business logic.[3][6][7] While legacy mainframe COBOL OLTP systems remained dominant in installed base, industry guidance and major new projects in banking, e-commerce, and large transactional systems increasingly standardized on three-tier or n-tier approaches for scalability and maintainability, making it effectively the topology of choice for serious new OLTP applications even if not yet universal.[2][3][8] The claim slightly overstates the speed and breadth of displacement of existing mainframe OLTP, but its direction and architectural emphasis proved largely accurate over the period.
