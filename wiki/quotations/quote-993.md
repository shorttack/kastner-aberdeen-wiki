# Computerworld — 1995-06-19

**Headline**: IBM, Lotus iron out agreement

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=3, confidence=3, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> execute nearly perfectly because they are so far behind,

## Rationale

Kastner correctly characterized IBM as far behind Microsoft in desktop platforms and 32-bit enterprise networking in 1995, as Windows 95/NT and Office rapidly consolidated their dominance through the late 1990s while OS/2 and IBM’s PowerPC desktop initiatives faded. Over the 1995–1998 horizon, the Lotus Notes/Domino groupware franchise became a substantial but niche enterprise product relative to Exchange/Outlook, and IBM’s broader success came from pivoting to services and server-side software rather than from near-perfect execution of the original PowerPC-plus-Lotus desktop strategy he described. Thus his emphasis on the severity of IBM’s competitive gap and the execution risk was directionally right, but it did not anticipate the strategic shift that reduced the relevance of that particular “must execute nearly perfectly” path.
