# Computerworld — 1995-12-11

**Headline**: DBMS tool meets demand

**Verdict**: MEDIUM prescience — score=2, confidence=2, horizon=SH-3y, pipeline=P2

## Quote

> Enterprise Manager will be the [database administrator] Relief Act of 1996, a boon to [the administrators] and their bosses,

## Rationale

Oracle Enterprise Manager and similar GUI consoles did make routine administration tasks easier for Oracle and Rdb DBAs in the mid‑ to late‑1990s, so the general idea that such a tool would be welcomed by administrators was directionally correct. However, Enterprise Manager did not become a transformative “Relief Act” for DBAs across the industry, and within three years competitors such as IBM’s DB2 Control Center and Microsoft SQL Server Enterprise Manager offered comparable centralized management, contradicting the claim that no other vendor had anything close. DBAs also remained in high demand and heavily burdened through the late 1990s, indicating the tool was an incremental aid rather than the sweeping boon implied.
