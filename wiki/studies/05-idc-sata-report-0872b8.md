# Tired Storage and Capacity-Oriented Disk Drives (SATA) — Another Small Step Toward Data and Information Consolidation

> IDC report based on 50 in-depth executive interviews across 10 North American vertical markets examining enterprise acceptability of SATA/capacity-oriented disk drives in storage arrays. The study provides quantitative forecasts for SATA storage system revenues and HDD shipments 2003-2007 forecasting SATA will reach a quarter or more of storage array terabytes over time. Key findings include that compliance requirements are the primary driver for fixed-content adoption and that software management tools are the primary inhibitor for tiered-storage deployment.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — IDC core belief — application-level availability not negotiable: There is no place in the datacenter for lower availability at the application level simply because the storage uses capacity-oriented technology
- **2003** — Top two SATA applications: Most receptive applications are (1) mirrors and snapshots for data protection and (2) repositories for fixed content
- **2003** — SATA embedded in arrays prediction: Capacity-oriented technology will be embedded within arrays as a class of capacity managed through current tools
- **2003** — SATA market share prediction — TB shipments: Over time capacity-oriented storage shipments will reach a quarter or more of the storage array terabytes shipped
- **2010** — SATA TB market share outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2003** — Multiple SATA product tiers not viable: There is not currently enough space for multiple classes/tiers of storage products based on SATA — market should be analyzed as single tier
- **2003** — SATA threat to tape — minimal near term: Capacity-oriented disk storage poses little near or medium term threat to tape; in fact larger disk capacity means more data to transfer to tape for archiving
- **2003** — Software management as primary inhibitor: Lack of effective information management software and concurrent lack of information policies will be the major inhibitor for aggressive adoption of tiered storage
- **2003** — Survey scope: 50 North American in-depth one-hour interviews conducted October-November 2003 across 10 vertical markets (banking/energy/financial/health/insurance/manufacturing/media/pharmaceutical/transportation/wholesale retail)
- **2003** — Storage budget growth 2004: Spending on IT in general and storage in specific expected to increase in 2004 — majority expect storage growth
- **2003** — Aggregate 2004 SATA spending forecast (raw survey): Raw survey results suggest 14% of all storage dollars in 2004 would be on SATA/high-capacity low-cost storage
- **2003** — SATA top concern — reliability: Number one SATA concern mentioned 30% of time: less reliable than SCSI or FC; performance second; 'too new' tied for second
- **2003** — Preferred SATA supplier — HP: HP most preferred SATA storage supplier among 19 interviewees surveyed; 10 of 19 mentions; IBM and EMC also mentioned
- **2003** — Vertical market TAM ranking — manufacturing first: Manufacturing has highest TAM for capacity-oriented storage ($802M); Financial ($733M) second; Transportation ($477M) third
- **2003** — Vertical market adoption density — financial first: By percent of installed base OK for capacity-oriented storage: Financial 51.9% (1st); Transportation 33.1% (2nd); Media 32.7% (3rd)
- **2003** — Compliance verticals as long-term tape conversion targets: Industries with compliance requirements (Financial/Health/Pharmaceutical) have highest ratio of near-line tape to disk — highest long-term conversion opportunity for SATA
- **2003** — Data protection — most popular SATA application: Data protection (mirroring/snapshots/rapid restore) is most popular use for SATA; 29% of total installed base TBs targeted; many already engaged
- **2003** — Fixed content SATA adoption — 12% of installed base: 12% of total installed base TBs estimated as appropriate for fixed-content management applications — IDC believes this is low and will grow with compliance
- **2003** — Overserved installed base: 60% of all reported installed terabytes (4563 TB total sample) are considered over-served — appropriate for capacity-oriented storage
- **2003** — Total installed base — interviewed organizations: 4563 TB total enterprise storage across 49 qualifying interviews (one disqualified); grand total by vertical market
- **2003** — WW storage system revenue forecast 2003-2007 (internal + external): Total internal storage revenue: $6.8B in 2003 flat through 2007 (0.4% CAGR); TB growth: 364K TB (2003) to 1.29M TB (2007) at 37.2% CAGR; $/GB declining from $19 to $5
- **2003** — Internal ATA storage revenue forecast 2003-2007: Internal ATA storage revenue: $283M (2003) growing to $313M (2007) at 2.5% CAGR; share 4.2% to 4.5%
- **2003** — SATA market growth toward 50% of new capacity: Market is moving now and can grow toward 50% of new capacity (per executive summary heading)
- **2010** — SATA 50% of new storage capacity outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2003** — IT inertia as adoption inhibitor: Strong tendency in corporate IT to lean toward status quo rather than adopt fundamentally different technology; ATA in enterprise is fundamental departure from SCSI/FC status quo
