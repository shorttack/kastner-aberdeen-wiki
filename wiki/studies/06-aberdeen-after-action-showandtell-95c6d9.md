# Maxtor Pools of Storage — After-Action Review (Show and Tell)

> Internal Aberdeen Group presentation reviewing the completed Maxtor Pools of Storage engagement. The 11-slide deck covers project background ($128K fixed price / 60-day schedule that took 150 days starting December 2003) client issues Aberdeen selection rationale client satisfaction challenges and lessons learned. Key findings: the project funded $75K in Aberdeen tool R&D; produced the first Flash-based research-delivery simulation tool; developed 50 maturity grids; and attracted Macromedia EVP Al Ramadan's interest.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — Project contract value: $128000 fixed price
- **2004** — Planned project duration: 60 days
- **2004** — Actual project duration: 150 days (2.5x planned)
- **2004** — Project start date: December 1 2003
- **2004** — Sales team composition: Sales: Kastner and Tubbs
- **2004** — Project team members: Team: Beganny Boyne Conrad Conant Creese Elbaum Erickson Hill C. Jones Kastner Kernochan Lane McLaughlin Ryan Veystel
- **2004** — Client strategic problem: Maxtor makes disk drives — a commodity; needs to move up the storage value chain; influence end-user buyers instead of purchasing agents at computer companies
- **2004** — Client selection rationale: Client said 'Aberdeen created Unix-RDBMS and CRM markets' and selected us; sought Aberdeen principals not the analyst
- **2004** — First proposal date and initial engagement value: First project proposal November 2002; started spring 2003 ($60K): research and white paper
- **2004** — Sales cycle duration: This project took three months to scope propose get buy-in and budget
- **2004** — Client internal challenges: Could Aberdeen influence internal storage engineers who love IDC's storage analyst? Could we simplify positioning and messages? Maxtor sales reps not solutions-oriented; Maxtor enterprise marketing not Aberdeen's friend; senior executives skeptical a…
- **2004** — Client success measurement: Market influence without pandering measured by pick-up on Aberdeen's terms and thought leadership
- **2004** — Tool satisfaction challenge: In spite of specs and pilot one tool failed to impress; by re-doing the disliked tool Aberdeen showed commitment
- **2004** — Workshop effectiveness assessment: Positioning workshop did not work on the spot but definitely influenced the outcome our way
- **2004** — Client engagement difficulty: Difficult to pin client down due to changing political climate absorption rate and quality time
- **2004** — Tool R&D value to Aberdeen: $75000 in tool R&D funded by Maxtor engagement helped TTCM site come up in weeks on budget
- **2004** — First Flash-based research simulation: Funded development of Pools of Storage Flash-based web browser simulation — Aberdeen's first research-delivery-by-simulation next-generation tool
- **2004** — Maturity grid development: Funded development of 50 maturity grids and training/process for doing same — received applause
- **2004** — Maturity grids as top-tier sales tool: Allows Aberdeen to sell into top-tier storage and computer companies with actionable compelling research and a simple yet sexy tool
- **2004** — Macromedia EVP Al Ramadan inquiry: Macromedia EVP Al Ramadan saw PoS tool demo and immediately asked if Aberdeen would work with them on a case study on Aberdeen's use of Macromedia development tools — to be turned into a sales opportunity
- **2004** — Lessons learned — tool development specs: Tool development is like programming; requires complete specs and user sign-off
- **2004** — Lessons learned — schedules advisory: Schedules are advisory not deadlines for tool development projects
- **2004** — Lessons learned — sales pressure negative: Hard sales press for end-Q1 extension was a client turn-off
- **2004** — Lessons learned — client absorption rate: Must factor in client organization's ability to absorb and efficiently use our research
- **2004** — Lessons learned — deliverable completion metrics needed: It's not hours charged but gaining and maintaining client satisfaction while getting deliverable done; 'Deliverable % completion' metrics needed
