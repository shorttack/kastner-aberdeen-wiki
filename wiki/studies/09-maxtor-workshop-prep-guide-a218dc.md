# Midline Messaging Workshop Prep Guide

> Pre-workshop preparation guide distributed to Maxtor executives and core team before the February 13 2004 midline category creation workshop in Shrewsbury MA. Provides session goals and pre-read questions for three workshop sessions covering category definition, go-to-market strategy, and messaging finalization. Includes appendices defining Pools of Storage concepts and messaging foundations.

**Author:** Peter Kastner, David Hill, John Boyne · **Date:** 2004-02-06 · **Type:** employer-record
**Importance:** high — *Rare internal document showing the structured methodology Aberdeen used to drive executive alignment before a category-creation milestone event; illustrates professional facilitation approach for market engineering.*
**Prescience:** medium — *Predicted Maxtor would gain executive consensus on midline messaging; workshop did occur on Feb 13 and messaging was finalized, but Maxtor's midline category never achieved full independent standing before Seagate acquisition in 2006.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — Workshop goal: Create corporate agreement about midline category value and messaging to support Maxtor's aggressive foundation of the category to ensure continued profitability
- **2004** — Workshop logistics: February 13 2004; Maxtor Corporation 333 South Street Shrewsbury MA
- **2004** — Executive Team participants: Stephen DiFranco, Mike Cordano, Joseph DeRosa, Linda Burchik
- **2004** — Midline storage definition: High capacity disk storage that has lower price per GB as well as relatively lower performance than higher performance disk storage; suitable for low I/O duty applications
- **2004** — ILM definition: Manages 'content' through different stages of its life using policy-driven software automation; assigns data to least expensive storage device without compromising service
- **2004** — Pools of Storage definition: A pool is a homogeneous group of storage hardware that meets service level requirements for access to data with a certain level of capital and management intensity; target of ILM process
- **2004** — Session 1 go-to-market question: What is Maxtor's go-to-market strategy for the midline category?
- **2004** — Category neutrality challenge: How can Maxtor influence creation of midline category without looking self-interested in pushing drives?
- **2004** — Seagate midline threat: Seagate wants to protect its dominance in FC disk market; will be reluctant to compete aggressively in midline market
- **2004** — Western Digital midline positioning: WD does not have extensive experience selling to 'business'; sells products not solutions
- **2004** — Hitachi midline positioning: Hitachi Global Storage focuses on 'right product at right time'; product and technology focus rather than business solutions
- **2004** — Storage buying decision hierarchy: TCO (budget fit); ROI (net positive IT savings); Business value to end users; Competitive advantage
- **2004** — Optimal messaging strategy: Take market share from competitors by being externally focused; establish basis for sustainable competitive advantage beyond price
- **2004** — Literal vs experiential messaging: Literal messaging: functions and features; Experiential messaging: emotional responses and problem solutions
- **2004** — Credible threat identification: What is the most credible threat to the emergence of the midline category?
- **2004** — Competitor classification: Which competitors are friends and which are enemies regarding creation of midline category?
- **2004** — Session 2 influencer targeting: Focus on maximizing time-to-market and ROI in Q2-Q3 2004 for target audience segments
- **2004** — Alternative marketing channels considered: Academic journals; advertising; analyst white papers; conferences; decision tools; industry associations; trade shows; trade press; Wall Street; webinars; web site
- **2004** — Business value of storage decisions: Reducing capital costs; improving return on capital; aligning to business policy; synchronized expansion with storage growth requirements
- **2004** — Session 3 goal: Refining the message; reaching agreement; giving the go-ahead
