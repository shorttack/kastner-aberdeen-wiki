# Front-Ends and Apple Computer in the 1990s

> This January 1991 Aberdeen Group report for Apple Computer USA examines the market for frontware (non-invasive front-ends to mainframe applications) and client-server computing based on in-person interviews at eleven major mainframe sites across banking, brokerage, government, insurance, retail, transportation, and university sectors. Aberdeen finds that over 80% of corporate MIS is deeply committed to IBM PC architecture and largely unaware of Apple's connectivity capabilities, while the frontware market is under $50M (1990) and client-server maturity is projected for the mid-1990s. The report recommends Apple pursue aggressive direct marketing to enterprise MIS, combine frontware and client-server messaging, and increase the pilot program well beyond 25 sites.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1991** — Frontware software market size 1990: Under $50 million in 1990; growing at 20%-25% in 1991
- **1991** — Client-server market maturity projection: Most buyers believe 1994 is when organizations will be fully up to speed with client-server; technology barriers until early 1992 or later
- **1996** — Client-server maturity actual outcome: Client-server matured by mid-1990s as predicted; World Wide Web arrival 1993-1995 accelerated and disrupted transition
- **1991** — IBM PC MIS commitment level: Over 80% of corporate MIS committed to IBM PC architecture; unwilling to consider Apple for future client-server and front-end projects
- **1991** — Apple enterprise market share prediction: Apple's market share in this decade unlikely to exceed 15% of business PC market; large MIS shops putting up largest resistance
- **2024** — Apple enterprise market share actual outcome: macOS desktop market share ~16% in 2025 (all PCs); Apple enterprise presence grew significantly post-iPhone but fragmented MIS pattern persisted
- **1991** — Delta Airlines frontware implementation: Only large mainframe site to have implemented frontware application using standard PC; used for flight status display
- **1991** — IBM 3270 installed base decline: ~5 million IBM 3270 terminals declining at 15%-20% per year through 1993; PC replacement of choice
- **1991** — Microsoft Windows 3.0 enterprise positioning: Most frequently mentioned as likely target for client applications; has established itself as a standard
- **1991** — IBM OS/2 Presentation Manager enterprise reception: Late and slow; used for multithreading or IBM OfficeVision/2 commitments; SAA still 2 years away
- **1991** — University of Texas Apple installed base: Largest Mac site in US: 18,000 sold; 2,000 in administration alone; using SAG Natural Connection for frontware
- **1991** — Novell NetWare dominance and Apple connectivity gap: Novell NetWare is dominant LAN OS; Mac-NetWare 286 support unknown to MIS; Netware 386 Mac connectivity delayed
- **1991** — Frontware pilot program recommendation: Aberdeen concerned Apple planning fewer than 25 frontware pilots in 1991; Fortune 1000 needs 1991 wake-up call
- **1991** — Apple quality reputation as enterprise asset: Apple has favorable reputation as quality company; buyers seeking flight to safety with highest quality, financially strong, reputable suppliers
- **1991** — Merrill Lynch client-server investment plans: $175M S&P budget; planning native APPC applications in 1991; productivity tools 1992; common use 1994
- **1991** — USAA client-server application rewrite plan: By 1994, USAA plans $5M+ rewrite of core customer service application using 5,000 client workstations; PS/2 Model 55SX with OS/2
- **1991** — Prudential-Bache broker workstation replacement plans: Planning to replace 8,000 broker workstations; three recommended platforms under budgetary consideration; Unisys/CTOS expected to lose contract
- **1991** — Frontware vs. client-server mutual exclusivity: Frontware and client-server are mutually exclusive; MIS will either retrofit mainframe app with frontware or rearchitect using client-server
- **1991** — Apple polarized enterprise buyer pattern: At 10%-20% of sites, Apple is established supplier with healthy installed base; other 80% have virtually zero Apple visibility
- **1991** — Software AG Natural Connection joint marketing recommendation: Aberdeen recommends evaluation of expanded joint marketing with Software AG of Natural Connection for Macintosh for client CASE tools and 4GL
- **1991** — Dealer channel limitation for Apple enterprise sales: Dealers taking orders for IBM PCs rather than educating market about Apple; dealers have few incentives to sell Apple when customer says IBM PC
- **1991** — SNA SDLC departmental gateway topology emergence: Aberdeen anticipates rapid growth in market for departmental communications gateways running SNA SDLC to IBM mainframe; potential cost-per-seat advantage for Apple
- **1991** — Sabre Group (Federated) client-server plans: No specific client-server plans; 20,000+ 3270s; database checking requirement makes frontware inappropriate; IBM mandatory for AD/Cycle
- **1991** — Unix workstations for corporate MIS desktops: Unix workstations are non-starters for corporate MIS desktops; Unix servers attracting increasing interest for price-performance advantages particularly for RDBMS decision support
- **2007** — Apple iPhone and enterprise market disruption: Apple launched iPhone 2007 and iPad 2010; consumer device success fundamentally changed enterprise adoption patterns; BYOD eliminated historical barriers
