# Aberdeen Group Recommendation: HP 9000 Series 800 for SAP R3 Implementation at Hercules, Inc.

> Aberdeen Group evaluates three RISC/Unix platform suppliers — Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC), IBM, and Hewlett-Packard — for Hercules Inc.'s planned SAP R3 implementation and recommends HP 9000 Series 800 as the sole supplier based on five criteria: breadth of product line, supplier experience, Unix stability, upgradeable RISC processor, and ISV support. The report advises against Hercules' planned two-tier architecture and recommends a three-tier-plus topology for distributed enterprise computing. Aberdeen also warns that the platform decision will be 'winner-take-all' for 3-5 years, making the selection consequential.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner / Aberdeen Group (signed by John R. Logan, VP) · **Date:** 1993-06-16 · **Type:** consulting-report
**Importance:** high — *Rare pre-SAP-dominance consulting artifact documenting the hardware platform selection process for a major Fortune 500 chemical company's ERP implementation; reflects the critical 1993 juncture when SAP R3 was first gaining traction on Unix and displacing mainframes.*
**Prescience:** high — *HP won and dominated commercial RISC/Unix market through the 1990s as predicted; DEC was acquired by Compaq in 1998 (confirming Aberdeen's concerns); IBM RS/6000 AIX instability was later resolved with AIX/6000; SAP R3 became the dominant ERP worldwide.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1993** — Aberdeen's primary recommendation: Hercules should implement SAP R3 on HP 9000 Series 800 as sole supplier
- **1993** — Architecture recommendation: Implement three-tier-plus topology instead of planned two-tier; two-tier has 'fatal flaws' for distributed enterprises
- **1993** — Platform selection commitment horizon: Decision will be winner-take-all; Hercules committed to one hardware supplier for next 3-5 years if selection made June 1993
- **1993** — HP RISC/Unix market share (1992): 45%+ market share in commercial RISC/Unix systems
- **1992** — IBM RISC/Unix market share (1992): 15% market share in commercial RISC/Unix systems
- **1993** — SAP R3 original hardware target: HP 9000 Series 800 was original targeted hardware platform for SAP R3 redevelopment
- **1993** — SAP R3 completion timeline: Original target: all R3 modules by mid-1994; rewriting proprietary mainframe database to Unix required Oracle adoption
- **1993** — Post-SAP-R3 throughput growth pattern: Applications grow at 30-40% per year for first three years after initial SAP R3 installation
- **1993** — DEC Alpha/OSF1 maturity assessment: Still beta — not ready for production; ISVs and early adopters confirmed; no TPC-A results available
- **1993** — DEC Alpha processor speed status: Production yields mostly 133-200 MHz; unable to produce 200 MHz in quantities; first-generation chip with no proven next-gen
- **1993** — DEC Alpha/OSF1 ISV support count: 550 applications stated by ISVs as planned ports as of March 1993; multiuser support not a priority without demand
- **1993** — Aberdeen conclusion on DEC: Strongly recommends Hercules NOT select DEC as supplier of choice for SAP R3
- **1993** — IBM AIX 3.2 stability assessment: Not highly stable according to user reports; major reason few large enterprises deployed large RS/6000 counts; new Toronto AIX development center expected to produce more stable AIX/6000 before end 1993
- **1993** — IBM RS/6000 RIOS processor trajectory: End of RIOS processor visible; entire IBM product line (PC + RS/6000 + AS/400 + mainframes) migrating to PowerPC 600
- **1993** — Aberdeen conclusion on IBM: RS/6000 is viable but relatively poor choice vs HP 9000 Series 800; multiple deficiencies outweigh advantages
- **1993** — HP PA-RISC technology maturity: 7 generations of PA-RISC implementations; Hitachi developing high-end next-gen; Samsung developing low-end
- **1993** — HP 890-400 TPC-A benchmark vs IBM 3090-200: HP 890-400 has more throughput capability than Hercules' current IBM 3090-200 mainframe
- **1993** — HP as SAP and Oracle reference platform: HP is RISC/Unix reference port for both SAP and Oracle; Oracle's most important multiuser platform in revenue
- **1993** — HP evaluation criterion 1 — Breadth of product line: Only supplier with both high-end and midrange SMP; broadest product line; granular midrange by processor speed
- **1993** — HP evaluation criterion 2 — Supplier experience: Longest history of Unix commitment; HP PSO claimed world's largest Unix services organization
- **1993** — HP evaluation criterion 3 — Stable Unix OS: HP-UX in top tier for Unix stability; OpenView extensions shared with industry accelerated stable Unix acceptance
- **1993** — HP evaluation criterion 4 — Upgradeable RISC processor: 7 generations of PA-RISC; proven track record; Hitachi and Samsung developing next-gen implementations
- **1993** — HP evaluation criterion 5 — ISV support: SAP and Oracle reference port; majority of ISVs for multiuser Unix marketplace
- **1993** — Hercules mainframe elimination timeline: Hercules goal to remove mainframe by Q1 1996; achievable with HP 9000 Series 800 and SAP R3
- **2000** — HP RISC/Unix dominance trajectory: HP dominated commercial RISC/Unix through late 1990s; acquired Compaq (including DEC/Tandem) in 2002 for $25B
