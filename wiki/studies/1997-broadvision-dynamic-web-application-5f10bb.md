# BroadVision Dynamic Web Applications Enhance Employees, Clinch Customers, and Pamper Partners

> Aberdeen Group's Product Viewpoint (Volume 10, Number 11, July 7, 1997) evaluates BroadVision's One-to-One Application System as a high-end dynamic Web application platform for corporate relationship management. Aberdeen defines a three-tier hierarchy of dynamic web servers (low-end CGI, mid-range ODBC, high-end CORBA-based object-oriented) and positions One-to-One as the industry leader in the high-end tier for personalized content delivery across Internet, intranet, and extranet channels. The study profiles One-to-One's capabilities for customer management, employee knowledge management, and partner/supplier extranets, citing customers including Liberty Financial, Banco Santander, USWest, and Kodak Picture Network.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Dynamic web server tier 1 - Low-end: CGI scripts; poor scaling with large users/transactions/databases; little flexibility or system integration
- **1997** — Dynamic web server tier 2 - Mid-range: ODBC/native drivers with SQL-like query; basic cross-sell/up-sell business rules; improved database access
- **1997** — Dynamic web server tier 3 - High-end: Object-oriented middleware foundation with TP-monitor technology; maximum scalability, performance, system integration
- **1997** — Dynamic page generation performance - Custom CGI baseline: 4,737 pages per hour (custom CGI on single-processor Sun UltraSPARC)
- **1997** — Dynamic page generation performance - Custom NSAPI: 5,455 pages per hour (custom NSAPI on single-processor Sun UltraSPARC)
- **1997** — Dynamic page generation performance - One-to-One with CGI: 21,176 pages per hour (One-to-One with CGI on single-processor Sun UltraSPARC)
- **1997** — Dynamic page generation performance - One-to-One with NSAPI: 51,428 pages per hour (One-to-One with NSAPI on single-processor Sun UltraSPARC); ~9,990% improvement vs. Custom CGI
- **1997** — USWest One-to-One extranet scale: Interfaces with approximately 30 mainframe systems, customer/job scheduling apps, inventory management, telephone and billing systems
- **1997** — USWest development time savings from One-to-One: USWest bought 'at least six months of development time as well as linear scalability' vs. building in-house
- **1997** — Internet customers as % of total sales in conventional catalog industries: 1-2% of total sales from new online customers in most large conventional catalog industries
- **1997** — Web-based customer service cost vs. call center: Web-based customer service costs an order of magnitude less than call center at $2 per call
- **1997** — Customer acquisition cost vs. retention cost: Cost of securing new customer is ten times as great as selling to existing customer
- **1997** — BroadVision market leadership prediction: Aberdeen strongly recommends all Fortune-1000 corporate web site buyers evaluate BroadVision; predicts rest of industry will choose similar relationship management strategy
- **2005** — BroadVision market leadership actual outcome: BroadVision stock peaked at $26B market cap in 2000; crashed in dot-com bust; nearly delisted; Salesforce.com (founded 1999) captured CRM market; BroadVision became marginal player; acquired by ESW Capital (private equity) May 2020
- **1997** — Smart card adoption for retail personalization: Smart cards will allow financial institutions to bridge internet storefronts and brick-and-mortar stores; enable cross-pollination of promotions
- **2015** — Smart card actual adoption outcome: Smart card (EMV chip) became universal by 2015 for payment security but did not bridge online/offline personalization as Aberdeen predicted; that role was filled by loyalty programs, cookies, and mobile apps
- **1997** — Push technology limitations assessment: Push technologies merely shift where information resides and do not significantly improve worker productivity or personalize end-user interaction
- **2000** — Push technology actual market outcome: PointCast declined and was sold to entrypoint.com in 1999 for approximately $7M after failing to achieve $400M expected IPO; push technology category disappeared by 2000
- **1997** — Customer data as competitive advantage prediction: Consumer (end-user) data is the competitive advantage; serious internet businesses in financial services and computer sales already recognize this
- **2015** — Customer data competitive advantage actual outcome: Customer data became the defining competitive advantage of the internet era; data-driven personalization (Netflix, Amazon, Google) confirmed Aberdeen's prediction; market for CRM and CDP reached $50B+ by 2020
- **1997** — BroadVision One-to-One differentiation strategy: Three differentiators: (1) integrated multi-tech system; (2) management/dev tools matching user skills; (3) industry-leading user profile management for custom interaction
- **1997** — BroadVision CORBA architecture scalability: CORBA-based system scales by replacing or adding servers; communicates with existing apps via screen scraping or other CORBA systems
- **1997** — Internet ROI timeline for large catalog vendors: Return on investment achievable in as little as six months with third-party solutions per large internet catalog vendors
- **1997** — Aberdeen timing assessment for BroadVision: Aberdeen believes BroadVision is NOT too early for the market; serious internet businesses already recognize customer data as competitive advantage
- **1997** — BroadVision database connection optimization: Interaction manager maintains persistent connections; caches user profiles, content, categories; amortizes database cost over multiple sessions
