# Managing Customers with Next-Generation Software Applications: 1997 Edition

> Aberdeen Group's third annual market research report on Customer Interaction Software (CIS) — a class of front-line applications covering customer service, customer support, sales force automation, telemarketing/telesales, sales configuration, internal help desk, and quality assurance. Published in late 1997 at $895 per copy and 246 pages, the report profiles over 60 CIS software suppliers, seven systems integrators, and three systems suppliers. Key themes include: the transition to Web-based CIS architectures, diverging paths of customer support vs. customer service vs. help desk, CIS-to-ERP integration trends, and computer-telephony integration (CTI). The report names Siebel Systems, Clarify, Vantive, Remedy, Scopus, and over 50 other vendors as major market participants, and forecasts substantial market growth through the late 1990s.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — CIS module: External Customer Service: Reactive customer service; handling complaints, returns, inquiries; key CIS function
- **1997** — CIS module: Sales Force Automation: Lead tracking, opportunity management, account management, forecasting; most mature CIS category
- **1997** — CIS module: Telemarketing/Telesales: Outbound calling, campaign management, lead qualification via telephone channels
- **1997** — CIS module: Sales Configuration: Complex product quoting and configuration; enables accurate pricing for multi-option products
- **1997** — CIS module: Internal Help Desk: IT service desk for employee support; tracking incidents and resolutions
- **1997** — Web-based CIS architecture status: Fundamental shift in how CIS applications are architected and deployed; transition underway
- **1997** — CTI importance for CIS: Critical enabling technology; integrates phone systems with CIS to pass caller data to agent screens
- **1997** — CIS-ERP integration trend status: Rapidly emerging trend; enabling 360-degree customer view by connecting front-office to back-office data
- **1997** — Siebel Systems market position: Leading SFA vendor; profiled as dominant supplier of sales force automation software
- **1997** — Clarify market position: ~22% CRM market share in call center/customer service segment; major market player
- **1997** — Vantive Corporation market position: CRM suite vendor covering SFA, call center, and field service; publicly traded
- **1997** — Remedy Corporation product focus: Action Request System; leading internal help desk platform; strong workflow capabilities
- **1997** — Scopus Technology market position: Customer service software focused on call center and case management
- **1997** — Aurum Software market position: SFA and sales management software vendor; already in acquisition process at time of report
- **1997** — CIS market size (1997): Multi-hundred million dollar market; report priced at $895 for 246-page analysis
- **1997** — Number of CIS suppliers profiled: Over 60 CIS software suppliers profiled; 7 systems integrators; 3 systems suppliers
- **1997** — CIS strategic value proposition: CIS clearly demonstrated ability to raise revenues; user organizations using CIS pulling ahead of rivals in customer satisfaction, retention, new business development, and profitability
- **1997** — Siebel market leadership sustainability: Siebel positioned as top-tier CIS vendor; expected to remain dominant SFA supplier
- **2005** — Siebel ultimate fate: Siebel peaked at ~45% CRM market share in 2002; acquired by Oracle for $5.8B in 2005; Oracle Siebel persists as legacy on-premises CRM
- **1997** — Vantive independence and growth: Vantive positioned as integrated CRM vendor; expected to grow as market consolidates
- **1999** — Vantive actual fate: Acquired by PeopleSoft in $433M stock-for-stock deal (December 1999); PeopleSoft then acquired by Oracle ($10.3B, 2004); Vantive brand dissolved
- **1997** — Clarify growth as independent CRM vendor: Clarify positioned as ~22% market share leader in customer service segment; expected to grow
- **2001** — Clarify actual fate: Acquired by Nortel Networks for $2.1B (2000) — a networking company buying CRM; Nortel sold Clarify to Amdocs for $200M (2001), a 90% value destruction in 2 years
- **1997** — Scopus independence and growth: Scopus positioned as customer service/call center specialist; expected to compete in maturing CIS market
- **1998** — Scopus actual fate: Acquired by Siebel Systems for $460M in stock (March 1998) — just 6 months after this report; Siebel gained call-center/customer-service capabilities to complement its SFA strength
