# Cisco's Core Products Business Integrates Routing and Switching

> Aberdeen Group Company Profile (January 1997) of Cisco Systems, Inc., examining how the undisputed leader in enterprise internetworking has evolved from a router-only vendor to a comprehensive end-to-end networking solutions provider. The report documents Cisco's acquisition strategy including the 1996 StrataCom acquisition, its IOS software as the de facto internetworking standard, and two key new technologies: NetFlow Switching (enhancing router performance with per-flow statistics and QoS) and Tag Switching (combining Layer 2/3 switching submitted to IETF). Aberdeen analyzes Cisco's positioning for ISPs and the emerging Gigabit Switch Router (GSR/12000 Series). The report correctly identifies Cisco's strengths (IOS investment protection, comprehensive product line, channel execution) and key challenge (can Cisco maintain leadership if switching replaces routing?). Aberdeen's answer: yes, through technology assimilation and Tag Switching.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Cisco trailing four-quarter revenue: $4 billion in sales during last four quarters
- **1997** — Cisco market capitalization: $40 billion market capitalization
- **1997** — Cisco LAN switching market share: Over 40% of the LAN switching market
- **1997** — Cisco's share of Internet backbone traffic: More than 80% of Internet backbone traffic carried by Cisco routers
- **1997** — IOS as de facto networking standard: Cisco IOS described as de facto standard in internetworking; unifying thread across disparate networks; embedded in hardware, server software, client OS
- **1997** — NetFlow Switching implementation scope: Implemented in 7000/7500 series routers; per-port enabling; flow cache expires inactive flows; 10% overhead on router processing
- **1997** — NetFlow Switching value proposition: Establishes flow between source/destination on first packet; subsequent packets switched not routed; exports per-conversation statistics; enables per-flow QoS and security
- **1997** — Tag Switching status at time of report: Not yet available for field trials in early 1997; submitted to IETF; endorsed by numerous third parties
- **1997** — Tag Switching architectural benefits: Combines Layer 2 switching performance with Layer 3 routing scalability; eliminates connection set-up congestion; pre-establishes accelerated paths; enables ATM switch participation in Layer 3 routing
- **1997** — GSR 12000 technical specifications: Multigigabit crossbar switching fabric sustaining 80 Gbps; high port density; interfaces OC-3, OC-12, OC-48; Gigabit Ethernet, Packet over SONET, ATM; fully distributed forwarding; topology-driven route-table switching
- **1997** — Stratm Technology (ST) capabilities: High-performance ASICs for ATM switching; complete ABR support; virtual path trunking; weighted fair queuing; multicast; higher density lower cost ATM switching
- **1997** — 7500 series capacity improvement: Four-fold increase in system capacity from new high-performance system processors and high-speed interfaces
- **1997** — Cisco's core competitive strategy: End-to-end internetworking solutions provider; assimilating switching into historically router-centric portfolio; acquiring promising technologies ($40B market cap as acquisition currency)
- **1997** — Cisco market leadership sustainability: Aberdeen predicts Cisco will maintain dominant position by assimilating switching into its portfolio; IOS investment protection retains installed base; management committed to full-service provider status
- **1997** — Tag Switching industry-standard potential: Tag Switching promises significant features for internet and enterprise scaling; submitted to IETF; uncertainty about whether it will achieve IOS-level industry-standard enthusiasm
- **1997** — GSR as appropriate and necessary product: Aberdeen views GSR addition as both appropriate and necessary for ISPs and large enterprise; will complement the 7500 router addressing different needs and markets
- **2002** — Cisco market leadership outcome: Cisco maintained dominant enterprise networking market position through 2000s and beyond; peaked at $555B market cap (March 2000); retained IOS as de facto standard; still dominant in enterprise networking as of 2026
- **2001** — Tag Switching outcome: Tag Switching evolved into MPLS (RFC 3031, January 2001); MPLS became fundamental to carrier backbone networks and VPNs; still essential to carrier infrastructure as of 2026; Tag Switching itself obsolete but its successor dominant
- **1998** — GSR 12000 commercial outcome: Cisco sold 1,000 GSR 12000 units within first year (by November 1998); became major commercial success; backbone routers for major ISPs and carriers; still referenced in carrier networks
- **2013** — NetFlow actual evolution outcome: NetFlow v5 became widely deployed standard for network flow monitoring; NetFlow v9 (Flexible NetFlow) became basis for IETF IPFIX standard (RFC 7011, 2013); still fundamental to network monitoring and security analytics as of 2026
- **2010** — Cisco IOS de facto standard persistence: Cisco IOS remained the de facto internetworking standard through the 2000s; evolved into IOS XE and IOS XR for modern routing platforms; still the basis of Cisco's networking OS as of 2026
- **1997** — The network is the business thesis: Once a mission-critical distributed client-server application is deployed, high-speed network performance is required to sustain it; CEOs recognize network investments create strategic competitive and shareholder value
- **1996** — StrataCom acquisition value: Cisco acquired StrataCom for $4.67B (June 1996); first true integration was BPX+Stratm Technology+Tag Switching; StrataCom technology became foundation for Cisco's ISP/carrier product line
- **1997** — ISP market requirements in 1997: Increased throughput, improved traffic engineering, bandwidth management, QoS support, differentiated Level 3 services, platform and transport flexibility, cost advantage
- **1997** — Enterprise network traffic pattern shift: 80% of LAN traffic formerly local; now 80% crosses backbone with only 20% remaining in subnet; imbalance demanding correction
