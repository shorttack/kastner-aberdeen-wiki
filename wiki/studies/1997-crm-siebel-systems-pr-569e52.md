# Siebel Systems: Customer Interaction Software Vendor Profile

> Aberdeen Group strategy profile evaluating Siebel Systems as a major and rapidly growing force in the Customer Interaction Software (CIS) market. Covers the N-tiered architecture of Siebel Enterprise Applications, the company's partner-driven deployment model (Andersen Consulting, KPMG, Cambridge Technology Partners, Price Waterhouse), and Microsoft alliance. Aberdeen predicts 1997 will be another successful year for Siebel as CRM market grows.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1997-06-04 · **Type:** Vendor Strategy Profile
**Importance:** high — *Siebel became the dominant CRM vendor of the late 1990s peaking at 45% market share; this profile captures a critical early inflection point before the CRM explosion; the 214-employee company described here grew to $1B+ revenue by 2000*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen's prediction of Siebel's success was spectacularly accurate: 1997 revenue $120M; 1998 revenue $391.5M (+89%); named fastest-growing US company by Fortune in 1999; $1B revenue by 2000; acquired by Oracle for $5.85B in 2006 at 45% CRM market share peak*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — employee_count: Siebel Systems has over 214 employees
- **1997** — growth_drivers: Three elements driving rapid growth: robust N-tiered architecture; partner-leveraged business model; rapid application development methodology
- **1997** — company_trajectory: Aberdeen expects 1997 will be another successful year for Siebel; company positioned as major and rapidly growing force in CIS market
- **1997** — revenue_1997: Siebel 1997 revenue rose to $120 million
- **1998** — revenue_1998: Siebel 1998 revenue rose 89% to $391.5 million; net income rose 135% to $55.7 million
- **2000** — revenue_milestone: Siebel revenue surpassed $1 billion mark in 2000; named fastest-growing US company by Fortune magazine in 1999
- **2006** — acquisition: Oracle acquired Siebel Systems January 2006 for $5.85 billion; Siebel achieved peak CRM market share of 45% circa 2002
- **1997** — product_architecture: Siebel Enterprise Applications built on N-tiered client-server architecture; supports Windows and Windows NT clients; Unix and Windows NT servers; Oracle, Sybase, Informix and MS SQL Server databases
- **1997** — development_platform: Siebel Enterprise Applications written in Microsoft Visual C++ and MFC; object-oriented design
- **1997** — version_3_release: Siebel announced Version 3.0 in January 1997 with enhanced customization via Siebel Tools; remote software distribution; Application Upgrader; CTI integration
- **1997** — pen_computing_anticipation: Siebel anticipated need for pen-based computing as example of GUI advantage and field sales use case
- **2000** — mobile_crm: Pen computing prediction proved prescient: mobile CRM on smartphones and tablets became standard by 2010s; Siebel Mobile was released but Salesforce.com led cloud/mobile CRM
- **1997** — partner_strategy: Siebel uses a virtual professional services organization through partners (Andersen Consulting, KPMG, Cambridge Technology Partners, Price Waterhouse) rather than building its own services capacity
- **1997** — compaq_alliance: In March 1997, Siebel and Compaq announced alliance under which they mutually recognize each other as preferred provider in CIS market
- **1997** — microsoft_alliance: Siebel's Microsoft alliance is described as most significant; Siebel provides full support for MS Application Development Tools, MS Office and MS BackOffice
- **1997** — customization_capability: Siebel Tools allows graphical configuration of application to unique requirements without altering source code; modular N-tiered architecture using configurable business objects (BusObjects)
- **1997** — internet_deployment: Siebel's N-tiered architecture allows flexible deployment: personal computing (client-centric); client-server; or virtual computing (network/server-centric) using Internet-based technologies
- **1997** — market_segment: Siebel's solutions appropriate for firms selling directly or through indirect channels; ideal for large multi-national firms with multiple distribution channels
- **1997** — cti_integration: Siebel 3.0 integrates with CTI middleware from Prospect and Genesys; supports automatic call distributors from Aspect, AT&T and Nortel
- **1997** — microsoft_relationship: Aberdeen expects Siebel-Microsoft relationship will increase in value for both companies throughout 1997
- **2000** — microsoft_relationship_outcome: Siebel-Microsoft relationship remained important through late 1990s; but Microsoft eventually developed its own CRM (Microsoft Dynamics CRM in 2003); relationship became competitive
- **1997** — upgrade_capability: Siebel Application Upgrader is unique: enables new software releases to be seamlessly merged with previous versions and user customizations by a single command
- **1997** — crm_market_growth: Aberdeen expects Siebel to capitalize on ability to help forward-looking organizations increase revenues while driving down costs in growing CIS market
- **2003** — crm_market_size: CRM market projected to reach $16.8 billion by 2003; Siebel dominated with ~45% market share at peak circa 2002
- **1997** — sfa_competitors: Siebel's SFA competitors named as Vantive Corp., Aurum Corp. (Baan subsidiary), and Clarify Corp.
