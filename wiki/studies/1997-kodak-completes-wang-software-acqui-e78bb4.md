# Kodak Completes Wang Software Acquisition

> Aberdeen Group analyzes Kodak's $260M acquisition of Wang Software to form Eastman Software Inc. The study examines synergies in workflow imaging COLD document management and HSM technology and forecasts dominant market position for the combined entity in document-intensive business solutions.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1997-03-17 · **Type:** Announcement Profile
**Importance:** high — *Significant M&A analysis capturing major document management industry consolidation; important case study in enterprise software acquisition failure*
**Prescience:** low — *Aberdeen's bullish assessment of Eastman Software as 'extremely well-positioned' proved wrong; Kodak sold the troubled unit in 2000 at significant loss*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Kodak revenue: $16 billion multi-national corporation
- **1997** — Acquisition price: Kodak paid $260 million in cash for Wang's software business
- **1997** — Eastman Software employee count: 700 employees worldwide post-acquisition
- **1997** — Kodak BIS installed base: 20000 customers in Kodak BIS installed base
- **1997** — Wang Laboratories projected revenue: $1.4 billion annual revenue in fiscal year 1997
- **1997** — Wang Software revenue growth: More than 100% revenue growth for last two years in Wang Software business unit
- **1997** — Eastman Software market position: Aberdeen believes Eastman Software is extremely well-positioned as independent Kodak subsidiary to expand leadership in document-intensive businesses
- **2000** — Eastman Software market position: Kodak sold troubled Eastman Software unit in August 2000 to private investment group; financial terms undisclosed but widely reported as significant loss on $260M investment
- **1997** — Workflow technology expansion: Eastman Software's workflow capabilities will position it as dominant force in desktop and departmental server market
- **2001** — Workflow technology expansion: Eastman Software was sold before achieving dominant position; workflow market moved to SharePoint Lotus Notes and later BPM platforms; Eastman Software technology did not achieve predicted dominance
- **1997** — Microsoft-Wang imaging integration: Wang imaging technology embedded in Windows 95 and NT 4.0; HSM joint development continued with Eastman Software
- **2001** — Microsoft-Wang imaging integration: Windows XP/2000 did not further develop Wang/Eastman imaging integration; Microsoft shifted focus to SharePoint for document management rather than continuing Wang-era imaging integration
- **1997** — COLD technology market opportunity: COLD dramatically improves distribution options for service bureaus and supplements customers' existing COM capabilities
- **2005** — COLD technology market outcome: COLD technology became obsolete by mid-2000s as internet-based document delivery and digital archiving replaced optical disk output; market largely gone by 2005
- **1997** — HSM data warehouse integration: Aberdeen forecasts HSM technology will play key role as organizations need access to digital images in multi-terabyte decision support systems
- **2005** — HSM data warehouse integration: HSM evolved into Information Lifecycle Management (ILM) and later cloud storage tiering; Eastman Software HSM did not achieve predicted role; Veritas StorageExec and IBM Tivoli became market leaders
- **1997** — Kodak organizational structure: Kodak has nine customer-focused business units; customer-centric rather than product-oriented view of market
- **2012** — Kodak organizational structure: Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2012; digital photography disrupted core film business; emerged in 2013 as much smaller commercial printing company
- **1997** — Eastman Software IDAR architecture: IDAR provides backbone of complete enterprise document life cycle solution combining imaging workflow COLD DM and HSM
- **1997** — Document management market evolution: Document imaging and work management entering new stage of explosive growth; production-level capabilities now deliverable at lower price points to distributed departmental environments
- **1997** — Wang enterprise acquisition strategy: Wang acquired Sigma Imaging Avail Systems and Vantage Technologies to enhance market position and accelerate NT platform transition
- **1997** — Aberdeen M&A assessment: Aberdeen field interviews indicate considerable enthusiasm from Wang Software customers resellers and partners about Kodak's document-imaging focus and additional R&D funding
