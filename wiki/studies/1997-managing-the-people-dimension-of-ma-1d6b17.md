# Managing the People Dimension of Major Technology Transitions

> In September 1996, Hewlett-Packard engaged Aberdeen Group to conduct one-on-one interviews with senior IT executives and project leaders undergoing major technology transitions. The study identifies education and training as the critical enabler of successful technology adoption, documents barriers including time pressure and ROI measurement challenges, and finds that on-demand, customized training is preferred over classroom instruction. Author Susan Irving concludes that people and process challenges consistently exceed technical challenges in large-scale technology transitions.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Primary research sponsor: HP commissioned Aberdeen interviews with IT executives undergoing major technology transitions, Sep 1996
- **1997** — Critical enabler finding: Education (communication, change management, process training, skills training) consistently named as critical enabler for technology adoption
- **1997** — Training failure consequence: Without training: best case = underutilization; worst case = outright rejection of new system
- **1997** — Time pressure barrier: Education consistently short-shifted due to cost containment, competitive pressure, and day-to-day operating demands
- **1997** — Training delivery preference shift: Traditional classroom training demand supplanted by universal appeal for on-demand, on-line, real-time, continuous access learning
- **1997** — Classroom training criticisms: Classroom training seen as: too long, not specific enough, overwhelming detail unlikely to be retained
- **1997** — Training customization requirement: Respondents demand training tailored to unique project requirements: 'I don't want a least-common-denominator one-size-fits-all approach'
- **1997** — Trainer qualification standard: Decision-makers require trainers with both educational credentials AND implementation/operational expertise
- **1997** — Training provider geographic paradox: Prefer local training provider but want consistent global/national/regional delivery capability
- **1997** — ROI measurement gap: Project managers strongly believe in education value but rarely use ROI measures to demonstrate this to senior management
- **1997** — Education budget cut pattern: Educational investments cut because: difficult to communicate effect of smaller education budget on project outcome
- **1997** — People vs. technology challenge ratio: Most executives found people and process issues far more challenging than technology issues during transitions
- **1997** — Success pattern: Most successful projects implemented disciplined project plans that fully embraced and incorporated education tracks
- **1997** — Failure pattern: Leaders who deferred or eliminated education are 'playing catch up' on the actual technology roll-out
- **1997** — HP strategic positioning: HP engaged Aberdeen to produce research validating HP's training and education services offerings for enterprise technology transitions
- **1997** — E-learning market prediction: Aberdeen implicitly predicts on-demand, customized, online training will supplant classroom instruction for enterprise technology transitions
- **2010** — E-learning market actual outcome: E-learning market grew to $35B+ by 2011; corporate LMS market (Cornerstone, SuccessFactors, Saba) became mainstream; LinkedIn Learning, Coursera launched; on-demand fully replaced classroom as primary modality
- **1997** — Change management as discipline prediction: Implicit prediction: change management (people+process dimension) will become a recognized discipline alongside technical project management
- **2006** — Change management discipline actual outcome: Prosci ADKAR model (1999), Kotter 8-step model (1996 book), ACMP founded 2011; change management became recognized profession with dedicated certifications and consulting practices worth $10B+
- **1997** — Study methodology: One-on-one interviews with key project leaders and decision-makers, September 1996; qualitative primary research
- **1997** — Human anxiety factor: 'Humans are built to avoid anxiety so our goal was to get people through the transition with grace and poise' — executive respondent
