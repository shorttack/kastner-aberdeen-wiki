# The Business Value of Applications Hosting

> Aberdeen Group profiles Interliant, a Lotus Notes Net Service Provider Alliance Partner (NSPAP), articulating the economic and operational case for outsourcing groupware hosting to specialist providers. The study documents how enterprises can eliminate the complexity of wide-area network build-out by delegating Lotus Notes and Domino hosting to Interliant, which manages over 20,000 Notes databases for 2,000+ customers globally. Aberdeen concludes Interliant is best-positioned to manage hosted application infrastructure and recommends enterprise CIOs evaluate outsourcing options before building internal network empires for groupware deployments.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Interliant market position: One of three global Lotus NSPAP with unique combination of specialization and customization; acknowledged as innovative leader in global NSPAP business by competitors and customers
- **1997** — Customer scale: Over 2000 public and private customer organizations; over 20000 Lotus Notes databases hosted
- **1997** — Competitive differentiation vs other NSPAPs: Low up-front cost of connection; willingness to meet customer-specific requirements; network independence via multiple public carriers; global reach
- **1997** — NSP value proposition factor 1: Economies of scale providing more robust/timely/complete services than most companies can provide internally
- **1997** — NSP value proposition factor 2: Technology leadership — Service Provider maintains expertise in latest software/hardware versions eliminating enterprise technology obsolescence
- **1997** — NSP value proposition factor 3: Time-to-market advantage — outsourcing notes hosting enables months of increased productivity without network provisioning headaches
- **1997** — NSP value proposition factor 4: Focus on core competencies — frees CIOs and MIS directors from network build-out complexity to focus on productivity benefits of Notes
- **1997** — Lotus Domino client independence feature: Domino only web server application allowing user to add/delete files from web server via browser; enables Notes functionality for users without proprietary client
- **1997** — Notes Release 5 roadmap: Lotus Notes Release 5 expected end of 1997 to further blur distinction between Notes and Domino; anticipated as significant enhancement
- **1997** — Connection options supported: Dedicated: X.25; TDM/T/E carrier; Frame Relay. Dial-up: PPP; X.PC. Mobile: RAM/ARDIS wireless messaging — multi-protocol network independence
- **1997** — Business partner channel strategy: Conducts business through Notes integrator reseller program; win-win: integrators get complete service offering plus recurring revenue; Interliant gets distribution
- **1997** — Interliant long-term viability as Notes hosting pioneer: Aberdeen concludes Interliant best-positioned to manage and develop virtual infrastructure integral to hosted application; recommends enterprise CIOs evaluate Interliant before building network empires
- **2002** — Interliant bankruptcy: Interliant filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy August 5 2002; CEO/COO resigned; total debts $109.9M vs $5.77M cash; revenue fell 45% to $19.2M in final reported quarter
- **2003** — Interliant assets acquired: NaviSite acquired Interliant assets in May 2003 for approximately $7M cash/credits; included managed messaging/collaboration/hosting/security businesses and UK operations
- **2019** — Lotus Notes/Domino platform fate: IBM sold Notes/Domino to HCL Technologies in 2019; HCL Notes 14 released 2024; platform survives in niche legacy installed base but lost mainstream enterprise relevance
- **1997** — Hosted application outsourcing as standard enterprise practice: Aberdeen Group believes all organizations should consider outsourced solutions for some component of their IT and network activities
- **2010** — Application hosting outsourcing vindicated: Cloud computing (AWS launched 2006; Azure 2010; GCP 2011) fulfilled and vastly exceeded Aberdeen's 1997 prediction that all organizations should consider IT outsourcing
- **1997** — Domino hosting service scope: 24x7 customer support for network connectivity; Notes administration; security; maintenance; server maintenance; ISP-level infrastructure
- **1997** — Aberdeen competitive positioning of Interliant: Interliant offers better value than other NSPAPs; has application management capabilities that non-specialized ISPs cannot match; succeeds where larger telecoms have failed
- **1997** — Total cost of ownership argument: Decision models often fail to factor in intangible services cost and technology investment required to remain technology leader; Interliant customers avoid technology obsolescence costs
