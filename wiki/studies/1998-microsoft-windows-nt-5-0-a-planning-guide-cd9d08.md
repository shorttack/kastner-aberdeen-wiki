# Microsoft Windows NT 5.0: A Planning Guide

> This comprehensive multi-disciplinary Aberdeen Group report — compiled by 12 analysts — analyzes Microsoft Windows NT 5.0 (renamed Windows 2000) across ten dimensions including scalability, reliability, development, administration, security, directory services, the Internet, desktop, and storage management. Published October 1998, it predicts NT 5.0 will ship late 1999, warns of uneven benefits and slow arrival, and counsels careful enterprise planning before deployment. The report identifies NT 5.0 as potentially the greatest OS-upgrade impact since the mainframe era.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group (Wayne Kernochan lead; 11 contributing analysts) · **Date:** 1998-10-01 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *One of the most comprehensive pre-release analyses of Windows NT 5.0/Windows 2000 from a major independent research firm; a 40-page multi-analyst study covering 10 technical domains that shaped enterprise planning decisions across thousands of IS organizations.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen's key predictions proved remarkably accurate: NT 5.0 did not ship until late 1999 (RTM Dec 15 1999); benefits were slow and unevenly distributed; early adopters faced significant challenges; Active Directory required major effort from large enterprises.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — NT market penetration at time of study: NT market penetration at 25%; new-market share at 40% and growing by some estimates
- **1998** — NT 5.0 release timeline: NT 5.0 will not ship until late 1999 at earliest; may be delayed by 6-12 months
- **1999** — NT 5.0 actual release date: Windows 2000 RTM December 15 1999; GA February 17 2000 — consistent with Aberdeen's late-1999 prediction
- **1998** — Overall OS upgrade impact assessment: Should have greatest OS-upgrade impact on all types of enterprises at least since the heyday of the mainframe; and perhaps the greatest impact ever
- **1998** — NT 5.0 codebase size: NT 5.0 has as much as 40 million lines of code
- **1998** — NT 4.0 Service Pack 4 significance: Many immediately-needed robustness improvements folded into Service Pack 4 for NT 4.0 due October 1998; NT 5.0 will offer less added bang for some customers' bucks
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Scalability: Enterprise Edition with enhanced scalability projected within a year of NT 5.0 FCS; 64-bit version planned for Intel IA-64 when Merced reaches mass market
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Reliability: NT 5.0 improvements will make it comparable to some midrange environments; but will not fully arrive until 1 year (best case) or 2 years (Aberdeen worst-case) post-FCS
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Development: Short-term developer productivity will go down sharply; long-term benefits from integrated directory services and enhanced file services
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Administration: ZAW reaches full stride with NT 5.0 FCS; but will not deliver zero administration without ISV tools that won't arrive until 1-2 years post-FCS
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Security: Kerberos; PKI; and SSO added to NT 5.0; but Microsoft has not shown signs of resolving heterogeneous interoperability — Aberdeen recommends other suppliers for mixed environments
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Directory Services: Smaller orgs unaffected; mid- and large-sized organizations will need major effort to deploy NT beyond workgroup and across the enterprise via Active Directory
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Internet: Microsoft best game in town for Internet/Intranet scaling applications; NT 5.0 will strengthen but not fully deliver on Internet infrastructure promise
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Desktop: NT 5.0 Workstation requires 64 MB RAM; users upgrading desktops will need to rebuild file systems — major inconvenience multiplied across enterprise's thousands of desktops
- **1998** — Planning dimension — Storage Management: NT 5.0 storage management capabilities likely to be a pleasant surprise; one-stop storage shopping and reduced labor burden
- **1998** — Benefits timeline prediction: Benefits will be slow to arrive and unevenly distributed across enterprise types; careful planning is mandatory
- **2001** — NT 5.0/Win2000 adoption reality: Windows 2000 achieved significant enterprise adoption but was largely superseded by Windows XP (Oct 2001) and Server 2003 within 3 years; confirming Aberdeen's prediction of uneven and slow benefits
- **1998** — 64-bit NT 5.0 on IA-64 Merced platform: Microsoft plans 64-bit NT 5.0 for Intel IA-64 when Merced reaches mass market; potentially dramatic performance improvements
- **2021** — Itanium (Merced) mass market outcome: IA-64 Itanium never achieved mass market; Intel discontinued Itanium in July 2021; Aberdeen's conditional 64-bit prediction never triggered
- **1998** — Active Directory deployment complexity assessment: Active Directory problems mean mid- and large-sized organizations will need major effort to deploy NT beyond the workgroup
- **1998** — Microsoft NT 5.0 market strategy: NT 5.0 with 40M lines of code and CE integration positions as platform for future of all Windows releases — corporate server to embedded
- **1998** — Y2K and Internet distraction risk: NT 5.0 release will occur in frantic atmosphere of Year 2000 concerns and Internet hype; IS should not allow these issues to distract from NT 5.0 migration planning
- **1998** — NT 5.0 as NT 3.5.1/4.0 successor risk: Early implementers who have not preplanned and pretested will face major enterprise-implementation problems similar to NT 3.5.1 and NT 4.0
- **1998** — SMS 2.0 as ZAW enabler: Systems Management Server 2.0 part of ZAW — requires large ISV customization before yielding value
- **1998** — Microsoft Management Console new admin paradigm: MMC and Snap-ins represent new administrative paradigm requiring ISV tool development; tools not available at NT 5.0 FCS
