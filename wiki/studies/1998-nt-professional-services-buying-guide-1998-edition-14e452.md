# NT Professional Services Buying Guide: 1998 Edition

> Aberdeen Group analyst Joe Clabby's 116-page guide examines the NT Professional Services market in 1998, defining service categories (design/integration, project management, lifecycle management, training), profiling 18 major suppliers including OEMs, ISVs, IPSPs, and resellers, and providing IS executives with comparative evaluation criteria for selecting an NT services partner. The report identifies four classes of service providers and maps buyer needs to supplier types.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — NT market position: Windows NT growing rapidly in market share on PC desktop and entry-level server, workgroup, and midrange server markets
- **1998** — NT limitations requiring professional services: NT complexities and shortcomings in scalability; stability; systems management; storage management; security; and directory/domain services drive professional services demand
- **1998** — NT services category 1 — Systems Design/Integration: Initial assessment followed by architectural design and deployment; includes NT/Unix integration
- **1998** — NT services category 2 — Project Management: Scheduling; tracking; troubleshooting related to NT systems/application implementation
- **1998** — NT services category 3 — Lifecycle Management: Ongoing maintenance and management of installed NT environment
- **1998** — NT services category 4 — Training: Training of IS staff and user community in NT-based systems and applications
- **1998** — Provider type 1 — OEMs: Computer makers providing NT platforms and NT-related services/support
- **1998** — Provider type 2 — NT ISVs: Software suppliers with professional services orgs for NT software deployment; typically subcontract to IPSPs
- **1998** — Provider type 3 — Independent Professional Services Providers (IPSPs): Systems integrators ranging from small focused consultancies to large strategic planning and implementation firms
- **1998** — Provider type 4 — Computer Systems Resellers: VARs and PC/server distributors that have entered NT design/deployment business
- **1998** — Key IS resource competition from ERP and Y2K: Best IS resources frequently occupied with Y2K issues; enterprise intranet/extranet; and ERP/Supply Chain initiatives — driving external NT services demand
- **1998** — Buyer selection criterion — strategic direction alignment: If IS needs strategic business planning + implementation: choose IPSP; if strategic direction set and seeking design/deployment: choose OEM
- **1998** — Buyer selection criterion — specific application deployment: If deploying specific NT-based applications (SAP/Baan/Oracle/CA): choose ISV professional services or their IPSP subcontractors
- **1998** — Two consultant archetypes identified: (1) Desktop-grown NT consultants; (2) Production-environment veterans from mainframes/AS400/Unix — the latter commands higher pay
- **1998** — Digital Equipment Corporation fate: DEC acquired by Compaq January 26 1998 for $9.6B — the largest computer merger at the time; DEC's NT Professional Services became Compaq Professional Services
- **1997** — Tandem Computer fate: Tandem acquired by Compaq June 1997 for ~$3B; report already notes 'Tandem's NT Professional Services (Now Known as Compaq Professional Services)'
- **1999** — Data General fate: Data General acquired by EMC Corporation October 1999 for $1.1B
- **2001** — Cambridge Technology Partners fate: Acquired by Novell July 10 2001 for ~$266M; Jack Messman (CTP CEO) became Novell CEO
- **1998** — Coopers & Lybrand fate: Coopers & Lybrand merged with Price Waterhouse July 1998 to form PricewaterhouseCoopers — same year as this report
- **2000** — Entex Information Services fate: Acquired by Siemens AG March 2000 for approximately $105M; became Siemens IT Service
- **1998** — NT Professional Services market growth forecast: NT Professional Services revenues projected to grow through 2001
- **1998** — NT Professional Services report scope: 116-page report priced at $1495; includes 12 explanatory illustrations
