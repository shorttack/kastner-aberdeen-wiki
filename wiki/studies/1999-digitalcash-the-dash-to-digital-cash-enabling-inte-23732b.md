# The Dash to Digital Cash: Enabling Internet Commerce Microtransactions

> This Aberdeen Group market review and competitive analysis examines the emerging digital cash and micropayment market of October 1999, identifying 21 vendors offering diverse payment approaches — including credit-card aggregation, ISP/telco billing, smart cards, hardware-embedded chips, and electronic wallets. The report provides a SWOT analysis and competitive positioning map, profiles vendors such as CyberGold, eCash Technologies, Qpass, MasterCard/Mondex, Microsoft, and Visa, and predicts that well-funded players with large global installed bases (particularly credit card companies and ISPs/telcos) will most likely succeed while underfunded pure-play digital cash vendors will struggle.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1999** — Online payment method dominance: Credit cards used for 90% of online purchases
- **1999** — Credit card suitability for micropayments: Not well suited for numerous small purchases ($0.05-$5); high processing costs disincentivize merchants
- **1999** — U.S. smart card adoption status: Failed miserably in U.S.; no viable business case for issuers
- **1999** — Smart card individual cost range: $5 to $16 depending on microchip memory size
- **1999** — International smart card adoption: Adopted in France/Germany/UK/South Africa/South America due to high credit card fraud and expensive telecom
- **2015** — U.S. smart card adoption — actual outcome: EMV chip cards adopted in U.S. from 2015 (liability shift); stored-value smart cards remain niche
- **1999** — ISP/telco billing digital cash deployment: ISPs and telcos expected to be particularly proactive; will deploy digital cash into billing infrastructure within next two years
- **2010** — ISP/telco billing — actual outcome: Mobile carrier billing for digital content (app stores/ring tones) succeeded; ISP billing did not materialize broadly
- **1999** — Bank participation in digital cash market: Banks showed typically conservative approach; lack of viable business models prevented bank buy-in
- **2010** — Bank digital cash participation — actual outcome: U.S. banks did not spearhead digital cash; PayPal/Stripe/Square succeeded outside traditional banking
- **1999** — CyberGold consumer incentive scheme users: 1 million users claimed by CyberGold
- **2001** — CyberGold fate: Closed August 31 2001; part of wave of digital currency closures (Flooz/Beenz/CyberGold)
- **1999** — Digital cash market acceptance timeline: Will challenge traditional means of exchanging value but challenge will take time to unfold; 2-3 year momentum build
- **1999** — Credit card companies long-term digital cash success: Greater chance of success for credit card companies with large global installed bases
- **2020** — Visa digital payments — actual outcome: Visa (NYSE: V) remains dominant; Visa Direct enables real-time payments globally; market cap ~$500B in 2024
- **2020** — MasterCard digital payments — actual outcome: MasterCard (NYSE: MA) remains dominant global payments network; market cap ~$400B in 2024
- **1999** — eCash Technologies position: Holder of DigiCash patents after DigiCash bankruptcy 1998; digital cash software and service provider
- **2002** — eCash Technologies fate: Went bankrupt; acquired by InfoSpace (now Blucora) in 2002
- **1999** — Qpass digital commerce strategy: Digital content and commerce enablement for content providers
- **2006** — Qpass fate: Acquired by Amdocs for $275M in 2006; over 6x annual revenues
- **1999** — InterTrust Technologies position: One of few vendors providing comprehensive digital rights management solutions; ~9 years in business
- **2002** — InterTrust Technologies fate: Acquired by Sony/Philips joint venture for $453M in 2002
- **1999** — Trusted PC hardware chip model: Hardware-based chip creates market for trusted PCs; ready-made network for security vendors
- **1999** — DRM content distribution models: Pay-per-use and rent-to-own models for digital content enabled by trusted hardware
- **1999** — Electronic wallet evolution: Heavy-wallet approaches not working; lighter versions emerging to maximize user friendliness
