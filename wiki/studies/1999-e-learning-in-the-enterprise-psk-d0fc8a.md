# e-Learning in the Enterprise

> This Aberdeen Group study by Peter S. Kastner examines the emerging e-Learning market in November 1999, defining e-Learning as a knowledge and information infrastructure leveraging Internet power to provide timely training. The report forecasts that the worldwide e-Learning market will grow from near zero in 1996 to over $10 billion by 2002, that classroom training will reach parity with technology-based training (TBT) by 2002, and that e-Learning will capture approximately one-third of the total training market. Eleven vendor profiles are provided including SmartForce, click2learn.com, Lotus Development, Macromedia, Arthur Andersen Virtual Learning Network, and others.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — e-Learning market size 1996: Near zero in revenues
- **2002** — e-Learning market size forecast 2002: Well over $10 billion worldwide (software + services + content)
- **1996** — Classroom training share of enterprise training 1996: ~90% of enterprise training opportunities used classroom method
- **1998** — Classroom training share 1998: ~70% classroom; ~25% TBT
- **1999** — Classroom vs TBT parity forecast: Classroom training and TBT will reach approximately equal share of training opportunities by 2002
- **1999** — e-Learning market share forecast 2002: e-Learning will have approximately one-third of total training market by 2002
- **1999** — Corporate WBT/CBT adoption rate: More than 70% of corporations offer employees computer- or web-based training options
- **1999** — Higher education online course offering rate: 60% of higher education institutions offer online learning courses
- **1999** — Higher education online learning penetration forecast: Online learning penetration in corporate/government/higher education will reach ~90% within a few years
- **1999** — Travel cost share of corporate training budget: Travel costs consume up to 40% of average corporate training budget
- **1999** — e-Learning benefit: Elimination of travel costs: Virtual learning delivery eliminates travel expenses and leaves students in place
- **1999** — e-Learning benefit: Personalized learning curricula: e-Learning enables personalized curricula vs generalized classroom training
- **1999** — e-Learning benefit: Rapid deployment and continuous evolution: e-Learning enables rapid deployment and continuous course updates vs slow classroom refresh
- **1999** — e-Learning benefit: Just-in-time modular training: Modular e-learning provides only necessary information in smaller blocks vs just-in-case classroom
- **1999** — WBT (Asynchronous) lifecycle assessment: Emerging; enabling technology for self-paced enterprise learning; clear advantages over classroom
- **1999** — WBT (Synchronous) lifecycle assessment: Emerging; instructor-led collaborative virtual classroom; addresses interaction gap
- **1999** — Learning Management Systems (LMS) assessment: Emerging infrastructure layer for enterprise e-learning administration and tracking
- **1999** — Computer-based Training (CBT) lifecycle assessment: Mature but declining; being superseded by web-based delivery
- **1999** — SmartForce market position: Leading e-learning content and courseware provider with library-based subscription model
- **1999** — click2learn.com market position: Dominant LMS and authoring platform provider (formerly Asymetrix)
- **1999** — Lotus LearningSpace market position: IBM Lotus providing enterprise LMS integrated with Lotus Notes groupware platform
- **1999** — Macromedia authoring tools position: Dominant e-learning authoring tool vendor (Authorware; Flash; Director)
- **1999** — Arthur Andersen Virtual Learning Network position: Major professional services firm deploying internal e-learning at enterprise scale
- **2002** — SmartForce merger outcome: SmartForce merged with SkillSoft in September 2002; combined company became leading enterprise e-learning platform
- **2004** — click2learn.com merger outcome: click2learn merged with Docent to form SumTotal Systems in March 2004
