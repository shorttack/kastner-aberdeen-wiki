# AMD's Gigahertz Equivalency: Inexperienced Buyers Accept Bad Science

> This Aberdeen White Paper by Peter S. Kastner, published March 2002, critiques AMD's methodology for naming its Athlon XP processors using Gigahertz Equivalency ratings pegged to Intel Pentium 4 clock speeds. Kastner argues the approach is fundamentally flawed: it assumes an unrealistic application usage model, is platform-specific while ignoring memory type differences, is inconsistent between mobile and desktop processors, and improperly uses system-level I/O benchmarks to measure processor-only performance.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner · **Date:** 2002-03-01 · **Type:** white-paper
**Importance:** high — *Represents an independent, credentialed analyst critique of AMD's marketing methodology at a pivotal competitive moment in the CPU market; Aberdeen's critique contributed to industry debate about benchmark transparency that still resonates in processor marketing.*
**Prescience:** high — *Kastner's prediction that the Gigahertz Equivalency methodology was flawed and unsustainable proved correct: AMD abandoned the PR numbering system with the Athlon 64 launch in 2003, and industry benchmarking practices subsequently moved toward workload-specific testing.*
