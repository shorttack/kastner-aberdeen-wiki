# Melding Financial Operations and Analytics: The New Financial Battleground

> Alex Veytsel of Aberdeen Group argues that the Sarbanes-Oxley Act is accelerating convergence between Financial Value Chain Management (FVCM) and financial analytics software, creating a corporate performance management imperative. The insight categorizes three supplier heritage types—financial analytics vendors, FVCM specialists, and ERP companies—evaluating each against SOX compliance needs, with ERP vendors holding a trump card via general ledger control. Aberdeen predicts that innovative FVCM suppliers will either add analytic capability or be displaced, and that SOX-driven transparency will prove beneficial beyond compliance.

**Author:** Alex Veytsel · **Date:** 2003-06-10 · **Type:** industry-insight
**Importance:** high — *Foundational Aberdeen analysis of the Sarbanes-Oxley compliance software market structure published within one year of SOX passage; first systematic vendor-type taxonomy for financial compliance technology.*
**Prescience:** high — *Correctly predicted FVCM-analytics convergence that became the CPM and GRC software markets; ERP general-ledger trump card prediction vindicated by Oracle, SAP, and Microsoft Dynamics dominance; FVCM specialists did add analytics capability as predicted.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — SOX core thesis: FVCM-analytics convergence: Sarbanes-Oxley Act is accelerating convergence between financial value chain management (FVCM) and financial analytics software
- **2003** — FVCM-analytics convergence prediction: SOX compliance requirements will force most public companies to join financial analytics and FVCM technologies sooner than expected
- **2010** — FVCM-analytics market convergence outcome: [DEFERRED]
- **2003** — SOX compliance data challenge: SEC-demanded information nuggets cannot be found by mining traditional financial sources; enterprises must discover knowledge in operational systems
- **2003** — Three supplier heritage types competing for SOX: Financial analytics vendors, FVCM specialists, and ERP companies each have strengths and weaknesses for SOX solutions
- **2003** — Financial analytics vendors: strengths for SOX: Deep accounting expertise, GAAP-conformant statements, strong with CFO relationships, best for high-level reporting; weakness: must extend to FVCM operational level
- **2003** — FVCM specialists: strengths and weaknesses for SOX: Great insight into operational metrics; weakness: must add executive-level aggregation and strong BI partner for analytical capability
- **2003** — ERP vendors: SOX trump card: ERP vendors control the general ledger — key data source for FVCM and financial analytics; enter as fast followers; breadth over depth; safe CIO choice
- **2003** — Top-down vs. bottom-up FVCM analytics strategy: Enterprises need both top-down (pull data through central analytics) and bottom-up (push facts from operational level to management) FVCM strategies
- **2003** — Data discovery and audit as key SOX prerequisite: Department-level FVCM purchases make data discovery and audit a key element in integration; data quality issues will surface during integration
- **2003** — ESM methodology for alert management: Event stream management (ESM) is methodology for routing and prioritizing events to manage flood of compliance alerts throughout the enterprise
- **2003** — SOX alert complexity: cry-wolf problem: Software will cry wolf at innocuous patterns without knowledge of customized business arrangements; risk of alert fatigue
- **2003** — Drill-down analytics as SOX differentiator: Drill-down and drill-across mechanisms make difference between well-oiled disclosure function and one that moves in fits and starts
- **2003** — ERP general ledger as trump card: ERP vendors control general ledger — key data source for much of FVCM and financial analytics; unique competitive advantage
- **2003** — FVCM specialist evolution prediction: If ERP/analytics vendors fail to deliver systematic compliance infrastructure, innovative FVCM suppliers will add analytic capability or re-forge financial value chain as compliance anchor
- **2010** — FVCM specialist analytics evolution: [DEFERRED]
- **2003** — SOX as good business beyond compliance: Many SOX demands also make for good business: timely alerts of cash crunch or materially significant events; financial transparency as business benefit beyond compliance
- **2003** — SOX execution risk for companies: Companies must avoid costs and notoriety of being a Sarbanes-Oxley test case; financial transparency will prove beneficial beyond compliance burden
- **2003** — Data quality as SOX compliance prerequisite: Data cleansing software needed to juxtapose and reconcile conflicting, redundant, and incomplete records before FVCM integration
- **2003** — CFO certification personal exposure under SOX: Current financial analytics and ERP vendors will have first shot at soothing fears of executives who must now certify filings personally
- **2003** — DSO as key FVCM metric example: Reporting can pinpoint metrics like breakdown of days sales outstanding (DSO) identifying time to collect and bottlenecks extending that process
