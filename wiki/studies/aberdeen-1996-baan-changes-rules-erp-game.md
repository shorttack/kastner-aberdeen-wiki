# BAAN Changes the Rules of the ERP Game with Dynamic Enterprise Modeling

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Baan Company's April 1996 release of BAAN IV, an enterprise resource planning suite featuring Dynamic Enterprise Modeling (DEM) and Orgware technology. The report argues that BAAN IV's process-oriented, dynamically configurable architecture fundamentally advances ERP software beyond the static, parameter-heavy systems of competitors, giving Baan at least a 12-month competitive lead. Aberdeen concludes that Baan should be on every serious ERP evaluation shortlist.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1996-04-24 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *Aberdeen's endorsement of BAAN IV as a category-redefining product coincided with Baan's peak market momentum (Boeing win, 1995 IPO, 91% revenue growth); the study introduced the 'Dynamic-ERP vs. Static-ERP' framework that shaped evaluations across the industry through the late 1990s.*
**Prescience:** low — *Aberdeen predicted Baan's 12-month competitive lead and rising market stature; Baan's 1998 revenue manipulation scandal, loss of customers to SAP, and 2000 sale to Invensys for $700M (against a $7B peak cap) proved these claims premature and the competitive advantage was short-lived.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — ERP paradigm classification: BAAN IV classified as 'Dynamic-ERP'; competitors classified as 'Static-ERP'
- **1996** — ERP evaluation criteria: Three key criteria: process-oriented, best-practices-based, supports continuous business improvement
- **1996** — Orgware decision factor: Orgware is an important decision factor for companies choosing Baan solutions
- **1996** — BAAN IV business objects generated: Up to 450 customer-specific production-ready business objects, functions and workflows
- **1996** — DEM architecture components: Four components: Business Function Model, Business Process Model, Business Organization Model, BAAN IV Configurator
- **1996** — Dynamic ERP requirement: changing business model: True Dynamic-ERP must encompass a continually changing business model (new, reconfigured or time-phased)
- **1996** — Dynamic ERP requirement: re-generate business objects: Must completely re-generate and link underlying business objects encompassing application logic flow
- **1996** — Dynamic ERP requirement: parameter-free execution: Must generate customized executable system without manually setting system parameters, auth codes or user interfaces
- **1996** — Static ERP implementation failure pattern: Static ERP customers invest in upfront modeling that does not carry through to implemented system due to serial, disconnected process
- **1996** — Baan competitive lead over rivals: Aberdeen believes Baan enjoys at least a 12-month advantage over major ERP competitors
- **1998** — Baan competitive lead reality: Baan's lead evaporated by 1998: revenue manipulation scandal, share price dropped 95% from peak, customers (Siemens, Carrier) switched to SAP
- **1996** — Baan ERP shortlist recommendation: Aberdeen recommends Baan be on every serious ERP evaluation shortlist
- **2000** — Baan company viability: Baan sold to Invensys for $700M in 2000 (peak market cap was ~$7B); then sold to SSA Global for $135M in 2003; absorbed into Infor LN by 2006
- **1996** — Baan competitive strategy: Technology leadership + marketing agility + strategic wins (Boeing) to displace incumbent ERP vendors
- **1996** — Business process modeling as key ERP differentiator: Aberdeen concludes that inherently dynamic BPM capability is 'the key value-added technology' for next-century manufacturing ERP
- **1996** — ERP internet/intranet enablement: Aberdeen asserts Static-ERP must give way to Dynamic-ERP with internet enablement capabilities
- **1996** — Orgware Reference Model types: Two model types: Reference Model (industry best practices) and Project Model (company-specific)
- **1996** — Static ERP failure modes: Three failure modes: elongated config/implementation times, rigidly imposed predefined practices, plethora of configuration parameters
- **1994** — Boeing ERP selection: Boeing selected Baan over SAP and Oracle in 1994 in landmark competitive win for Baan
- **1996** — BAAN IV application domains: Covers manufacturing, finance, distribution, transportation, services, and project management
