# Cisco's Access Business Unit Crystallizes Its Vision

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Cisco Systems' Access Business Unit (ABU) January 1996 product rollout, assessing the company's strategy to dominate all segments of the remote access market: ROBO, dial-up/access server, SOHO, and embedded/server-based systems. The study traces Cisco's 15-month journey through acquisitions (Combinet, Internet Junction, Network Translation) and product launches (AS5200, 750 series, CiscoRemote, CiscoPro), arguing that Cisco's IOS software is becoming the de facto standard for remote access. Aberdeen concludes that Cisco's end-to-end access strategy across carrier, enterprise, SOHO, and home segments has all the ingredients of a successful win strategy.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Cisco ABU revenue share: 50% of Cisco's $2 billion overall revenues derived from the Access Business Unit
- **1996** — Cisco ABU four-segment strategy: Multi-pronged targeting of four segments: ROBO (regional/branch office), dial-up/access server, SOHO/professional office, embedded and server-based systems
- **1996** — IOS as de facto standard: Cisco IOS has become the de facto standard in internetworking; provides cohesion to disparate networks; functions as a unifying thread
- **1996** — IOS service: routing: Reliable, adaptive routing services
- **1996** — IOS service: WAN optimization: WAN optimization services including compression, bandwidth-on-demand, dial-on-demand routing
- **1996** — IOS service: management and security: Management and security services including TACACS+, CiscoSecure AAA
- **1996** — IOS service: scalability: Scalability services enabling migration path for growth and change across enterprise and SMB
- **1995** — Combinet acquisition cost: Cisco acquired Combinet in August 1995; spawned 750 ISDN series for SOHO and home
- **1995** — CiscoPro channel strategy: Exclusively two-tiered distribution for SMB; premier channel partners (Tech Data, Ingram Micro) responsible for VAR/integrator sales
- **1996** — AS5200 capabilities: Integrates CSU, comms server, channel banks, modems, switches, routers in single chassis; 48 (T1) or 60 (E1) integrated modems; digital + analog dial-in
- **1996** — CiscoSecure security offering: Triple-A security: authorization, authentication, accounting + NAT via TACACS+; per-user access control at server level
- **1996** — CiscoRemote client components: Integrates Network TeleSystems TCP/IP, Farallon Timbuktu Pro, AirSoft Powerburst, Netscape Navigator 1.2, Qualcomm Eudora 1.5.2, Cisco IOS extensions
- **1996** — IOS ubiquity prediction: Cisco's goal is to make IOS ubiquitous throughout the industry, becoming a force to be contended with on all fronts of the remote access market
- **2000** — IOS ubiquity outcome: Cisco grew to $18.9B revenue by FY2000 (from $2B in 1996); IOS became definitively the de facto internetworking OS; Cisco dominated all four ABU segments
- **1996** — ISP/carrier market capture: Cisco's strategy to add software value-add for ISPs looking to offer more value will prove attractive; IOS software sell will prove compelling long-term
- **1999** — Ascend Communications fate: Ascend Communications acquired by Lucent Technologies in 1999 for $20B; Cisco defeated Ascend in ISP/access server market through IOS differentiation
- **1999** — Shiva Corporation fate: Shiva Corporation filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 1999; Cisco won the SOHO remote access market
- **1996** — Remote access market evolution: ISPs shifting from price-only to demanding intelligent software, managed modems, security, and single-vendor solutions
- **1996** — Cisco 3-phase ABU strategy: Phase 1: market engagement; Phase 2: end-to-end solutions rollout; Phase 3 (current): enhanced services and enterprise-wide scalability
- **1996** — Home-to-office convergence vision: 'Firmly believing that the home of today is the office of tomorrow' — Cisco's vision of networking from enterprise core to home
- **1995** — TACACS+ announcement: TACACS+ announced March 1995; supports independent user authentication, authorization, accounting, and encryption
- **1996** — Aberdeen 1994 vs 1996 assessment: 1994 assessment: Cisco blueprint carefully crafted but lacked dial-up depth and channel relationships. 1996: channel and dial-up gaps largely closed
- **1996** — 50% remote access market share: ABU has marched toward 50% market share in key remote access segments
