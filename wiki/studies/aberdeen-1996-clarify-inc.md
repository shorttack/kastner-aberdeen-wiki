# Clarify Inc.

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Clarify Inc., a San Jose-based leader in customer interaction software (CIS) covering customer service, field service, quality assurance, helpdesk, and sales force automation. The study documents Clarify's integrated best-of-breed strategy, 99% uptime reliability, 100%+ annual revenue growth, linchpin accounts at Microsoft, Cisco, HP, MCI, and Sprint, and the Metropolis Software acquisition providing SFA and mobile computing capabilities. Aberdeen concludes Clarify is 'a CIS winner' positioned to dominate the rapidly consolidating CIS market.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — CIS market growth rate: CIS industry experiencing growth nearing 50% per year; leading firms exceeding this
- **1996** — Clarify revenue growth: Revenues increasing over 100% on annualized basis; 1H 1996 revenues exceeded $20.1M; Aberdeen expects more than double in 2H 1996
- **1996** — Clarify uptime achievement: Aberdeen research indicates Clarify has achieved 99% uptime with its customer base
- **1996** — Clarify customer base: Over 200 customers; 43,000 individuals using Clarify solutions
- **1996** — Clarify R&D investment: 20% of Clarify's revenues invested in R&D
- **1996** — CIS evaluation criterion 1: Speed and Scalability: Response time paramount; rapid deployment; global scale for thousands of users
- **1996** — CIS evaluation criterion 2: Customizability: Must adapt to company's unique policies/processes; end-user modifiable without IS staff; upgrades preserve customizations
- **1996** — CIS evaluation criterion 3: Expertise and product richness: Supplier must understand best practices; each application must stand on its own right
- **1996** — CIS evaluation criterion 4: Integration: Three-level integration: (1) cross-CIS app data sharing, (2) enterprise back-office (SAP, Oracle), (3) cross-organizational boundaries
- **1996** — CIS evaluation criterion 5: Communications: Remote/mobile access, Internet availability, security, data replication and synchronization for mobile users and customers
- **1996** — Metadata customization architecture: Metadata layer sits atop application logic; ClearCustomize toolset; new version deployments don't corrupt customizations; contrast with source-code modification vendors
- **1996** — Three-tier architecture: Presentation, application logic, database layers partitioned into separate modules dynamically deployed on different hardware
- **1996** — Metropolis acquisition rationale: Clarify elected to acquire rather than build SFA; recognized that robust SFA requires deep sales methodology expertise and mobile computing — both from Metropolis
- **1996** — Clarify competitive strategy: Integrated best-of-breed CIS suite; refuse implementations unlikely to succeed; TAM assigned to every customer; linchpin account strategy in IT/telecom verticals
- **1996** — Clarify market leadership prediction: Aberdeen expects Clarify will maintain its rapid growth, particularly in IT and telecommunications markets; 'Clarify is a CIS winner and will continue to be so in the future'
- **1999** — Clarify acquisition outcome: Clarify acquired by Nortel Networks in 1999 for $2.1B in stock; validating Aberdeen's 'CIS winner' assessment
- **2001** — Clarify post-Nortel fate: Nortel sold Clarify to Amdocs for $200M in 2001 — a fraction of the $2.1B paid; CRM market shifted toward Siebel and Salesforce
- **1996** — CommCenter vertical product: ClearSupport CommCenter for telecom: simultaneous customer account + network infrastructure access; proactive customer notification; customers MCI, Sprint, TeleNordia
- **1996** — CIS market consolidation prediction: Market quickly consolidating from hundreds of software suppliers to a few leaders with resources for Fortune 1000 requirements
- **2000** — CIS market consolidation outcome: CIS/CRM market consolidated rapidly: Siebel Systems dominated by 2000, followed by SAP CRM, Oracle Siebel, and eventually Salesforce.com (2000 IPO)
- **1996** — Linchpin account strategy: Named linchpin accounts: Microsoft, Cisco, HP (IT), MCI, Sprint (telecom), Bay Networks (networking); these customers' partners naturally adopt Clarify
- **1996** — Microsoft relationship advantage: Clarify's relationship with Microsoft is a 'tremendous resource for helpdesk excellence' — large percentage of helpdesk inquiries are Microsoft application-related
- **1997** — ClearTelesales planned release: ClearTelesales expected mid-1997: closed-loop sales from marketing campaign management to lead generation, quotation, closure, and forecasting
- **1996** — Cross-company helpdesk connectivity vision: Aberdeen believes Clarify's vision of connecting helpdesks across company boundaries is on-track with market realities; endorsed via CSC and DMTF participation
