# Digital: Leading the NT-Across-the-Enterprise Charge

> This Aberdeen Group profile (July 1996) evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's comprehensive NT-across-the-enterprise strategy, encompassing three operating environments (OpenVMS, Digital Unix, Windows NT), a full product suite from commercial desktops to enterprise servers, and a deep strategic partnership with Microsoft. Aberdeen concludes that Digital is uniquely positioned to become the leading enterprise NT supplier because none of its major competitors — IBM, HP, or Sun — are strategically committed to NT, leaving Digital unchallenged at the high end. The study examines channels strategy, ISV application portfolio, systems management capabilities, and the critical Digital-Microsoft relationship.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Enterprise NT buyer criterion 1: Strategic commitment to NT: IT managers require vendors to have demonstrated, public strategic commitment to NT as primary enterprise platform
- **1996** — Enterprise NT buyer criterion 2: Product breadth and extensions: IT managers require vendors to offer full product breadth from desktop to server plus platform extensions (scalability, availability, management)
- **1996** — Enterprise NT buyer criterion 3: Integration and deployment services: IT managers require vendors with enterprise-experienced professional services organizations for NT deployment in mission-critical environments
- **1996** — Enterprise NT buyer criterion 4: Financial stability: IT managers require financial stability from NT platform vendors for long-term support commitments
- **1996** — NT applications available on Digital Alpha: 1,400+ client/server applications on Alpha platform; direct support for 300 PC and NOS applications
- **1996** — Digital Microsoft-certified personnel: 5,000+ Microsoft support personnel including 1,000 Microsoft Certified Professionals (ramping to 1,700 near-term)
- **1996** — Digital NT server ISV application partners: 1,400+ NT applications qualified on Alpha platforms including database, business, banking/financial, groupware, Internet, software tools
- **1996** — OpenVMS strategic positioning: Continuous computing server for back-end database/application services supporting NT clients; VMS migration path via Affinity program
- **1996** — Digital Unix strategic positioning: High-end 64-bit Unix for decision-support, data warehouses; 32-bit NT not expected to compete in 64-bit market for several years
- **1996** — Windows NT strategic positioning: Premier client/server environment: Intel for commercial desktop/low-end servers; Alpha for high-performance technical and commercial desktops and application servers
- **1996** — IBM NT strategic posture: No public strategic commitment to NT; OS/2 division actively competes with NT; ISSC division markets NT — conflicted strategy
- **1996** — HP NT strategic posture: Stated NT leadership commitment but protecting HP/UX Unix base; no plans for NT on PA-RISC; will await HP/Intel combined chip for enterprise NT
- **1996** — Sun NT strategic posture: Deploys NT clients; no strategic NT server commitment; Ultra positioned to directly compete with NT servers
- **1996** — Compaq NT enterprise gap assessment: Meets product breadth/depth requirements but lacks enterprise-level systems integration and deployment experience necessary for mission-critical NT
- **1996** — FX!32 capability assessment: Binary translation/emulation enables 32-bit Windows/NT x86 apps on Alpha with significant performance improvements over x86 architectures
- **1996** — Digital enterprise NT management architecture: Three-tier: ClientWORKS (desktop) -> ServerWORKS Manager (workgroup SNMP) -> POLYCENTER/CA Unicenter (enterprise)
- **1996** — Digital-Microsoft strategic partnership assessment: Aug 1995 alliance: Digital technology swap for Microsoft underwriting NT/Office/BackOffice training for thousands of Digital consultants; Digital has 1,000 MCPs — largest MS professional services org in world
- **1996** — 64-bit Windows NT development partnership: Microsoft to work with Digital on 64-bit NT addressing for next major post-4.0 NT release
- **1996** — Digital NT channels strategy: Dual-channel: direct sales to create demand, indirect (distributors/VARs/SIs) for order fulfillment; internal sales force compensated for VAR referrals eliminating channel conflict
- **1996** — Digital NT enterprise market leadership prediction: Digital is clearly in an NT leadership position today — this race is Digital's to win (or lose); Aberdeen sees positive indications that Digital can execute strategy successfully
- **1998** — Digital NT enterprise market leadership — actual outcome: Digital acquired by Compaq January 1998 for $9.6B; Digital's NT product line and expertise absorbed into Compaq; independent NT market leadership not achieved
- **1996** — NT enterprise adoption prediction: With Digital's enterprise services and Microsoft's NT, NT will become the dominant enterprise server platform; days of Digital's rapid growth will return
- **2000** — NT enterprise adoption — actual outcome: Windows NT and Windows 2000 Server did become dominant enterprise server platform by 2000-2002; Unix market share declined significantly; NT adoption prediction was accurate
- **1996** — IBM NT competitive gap viability: IBM's conflicted OS/2 vs NT strategy and lack of public NT commitment leaves door wide open for Digital in enterprise NT
- **2000** — IBM NT competitive posture — actual outcome: OS/2 was discontinued in 2001; IBM never achieved strategic NT server commitment; IBM shifted focus to Linux (2000) instead; prediction of IBM weakness in enterprise NT was accurate
