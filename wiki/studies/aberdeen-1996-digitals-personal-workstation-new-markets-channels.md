# Digital's Personal Workstation: New Markets, New Products, New Channels, New Company, New Challenges

> This Aberdeen Group profile (April 1996) evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's Personal Workstation line — Intel Celebris XL and Alpha XL — positioned as a low-cost alternative to Unix workstations for technical and commercial applications. Using Pro/Engineer, CADalyst, and Ansys benchmarks, Aberdeen demonstrates that the Alpha XL 366 MHz outperforms SGI and HP Unix workstations at approximately half the cost. The study examines Digital's indirect channel strategy through VARs and identifies five target markets: MDA, GIS, Digital Content and Creation, Financial, and Software Development.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Alpha XL 366 vs SGI Indigo 2 R440 — Pro/Engineer benchmark: Alpha XL 366 MHz outperforms SGI Indigo 2 R440 (250 MHz) by 69% in Pro/Engineer benchmark
- **1996** — Alpha XL 366 vs HP C110 — Pro/Engineer benchmark: Alpha XL 366 MHz outperforms HP C110 Unix workstation by 81% in Pro/Engineer benchmark
- **1996** — Digital Personal Workstation vs HP 712/100 — CADalyst benchmark: Digital Personal Workstation outperforms HP 712/100 Unix workstation by 27% in CADalyst benchmark
- **1996** — Digital Personal Workstation vs Sun UltraSparc 1 — CADalyst benchmark: Digital Personal Workstation outperforms Sun UltraSparc 1 by 60% in CADalyst benchmark
- **1996** — Alpha XL vs SGI Indigo — Ansys benchmark: Digital Alpha XL outperforms SGI Indigo by 42% in Ansys benchmark; HP J210 outperforms Alpha XL by 6%
- **1996** — Alpha XL vs Unix workstation price comparison: Alpha XL listed at $17,413 vs SGI Indigo at $33,000 and HP J210 at $34,980 — approximately half the cost of comparable Unix workstations
- **1996** — NT applications available on Digital Personal Workstations: 2,000+ NT native applications on Celebris XL Intel platform; 1,200+ on Alpha XL platform
- **1996** — Personal Workstation fully configured cost vs Unix workstations: Fully configured Personal Workstation with business/technical applications averages about half the cost of comparable Unix workstation products
- **1996** — Personal Workstation target market 1: MDA: Mechanical Design Automation (MDA): AutoDesk, Bentley, EDS, Parametric Technology, Matra-Datavision, SolidWorks
- **1996** — Personal Workstation target market 2: GIS: Geographical Information Systems (GIS): 70+ software packages including ESRI, Bentley MicroStation Mapper, Cadcorp, ESC CEM, Small World GIS
- **1996** — Personal Workstation target market 3: Digital Content and Creation: Digital Content and Creation (DCC): Adobe, Softimage, AutoDesk 3D Studio Max, Saros, Looking Glass — high-end publishing, web creation, animation
- **1996** — Personal Workstation target market 4: Financial: Financial applications requiring powerful number-crunching or highly graphical display capabilities
- **1996** — Personal Workstation target market 5: Software Development: Software Development: compilers, GUI Builders, 4GL, CASE, Client/Server Application Development, OO tools — including ParcPlace Visual Works, Nutcracker, Informix SE
- **1996** — Personal Workstation indirect channel strategy: Drive sales exclusively through VARs; internal sales force does NOT sell Personal Workstation but earns compensation for VAR referrals; no channel conflict
- **1996** — Personal Workstation common platform strategy: Single base chassis with three microprocessor families (Pentium, Pentium Pro, Alpha); economies of scale in manufacturing; field-upgradeable via daughter card
- **1996** — Personal Workstation competitive market entry strategy: Target low-end and mid-range of Unix workstation market with equivalent/superior performance at half the cost; use NT's growing ISV ecosystem as leverage
- **1996** — Alpha XL dual personality positioning: 1) Low-cost Unix workstation alternative; 2) Powerful NT-based commercial desktop; runs both PC apps and formerly Unix-only technical apps
- **1996** — 3Space Viewer bridging capability: 3Space Viewer allows 3D technical designs to be imported into Microsoft Word/PowerPoint via OLE 2.0; images remain manipulable within Office documents
- **1996** — 64-bit application market exclusion: Markets requiring 64-bit processing (GIS data warehousing, complex 3D EDA) will continue on Unix workstations; NT will not challenge 64-bit architectures for several years
- **1996** — Alpha ISV porting status: NT ISVs port to Intel first, then to platform with largest business opportunity; Digital secured second-key-platform porting status for Alpha ahead of PowerPC and MIPS
- **1996** — Personal Workstation Unix market disruption prediction: Digital Personal Workstation is positioned to explode into the low-end and mid-range of the Unix workstation marketplace; HP and Sun face significant market share loss at low-end
- **2000** — Unix workstation market disruption — actual outcome: Unix workstation market share collapsed through late 1990s-2000s as Windows NT workstations captured low-end and mid-range; SGI revenue declined from $2.5B in 1997 to bankruptcy filings in 2006 and 2009
- **1996** — Personal Workstation VAR channel program viability: Digital's VAR program will succeed as real differentiator: common base platform reduces VAR inventory/training costs; promotional margin programs incent VAR adoption
- **1998** — Personal Workstation program outcome — actual outcome: Digital acquired by Compaq January 1998; Personal Workstation product line absorbed; Alpha workstation line continued under Compaq/HP briefly; program did not achieve independent market leadership
- **1996** — Aberdeen assessment of Unix workstation competitive impact: Personal Workstations should halve the cost of current low-end Unix workstations while providing superior price/performance; Unix workstation suppliers must cut costs or increase functionality
