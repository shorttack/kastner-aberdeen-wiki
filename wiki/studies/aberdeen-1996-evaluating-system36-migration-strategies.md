# Evaluating System/36 Migration Strategies

> This Aberdeen Group White Paper (September 1996) evaluates four migration strategies for IBM System/36 owners: stay put, migrate to Advanced 36, migrate to AS/400, or migrate to Windows NT PC-LAN. Based on interviews with System/36 owners and a five-criteria decision framework (capital cost, operating cost, MIS transition cost, business disruption, supplier support), Aberdeen recommends migrating to the Advanced 36 as clearly the best choice for the vast majority of owners. The study documents the Year 2000 compliance imperative and provides detailed cost tables for 10-user and 20-user configurations across all options.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — US System/36 installed base 1992 vs 1996: Declined from approximately 87,000 sites (1992) to projected 42,000 sites (1996) — decline of more than 50% in four years
- **1995** — System/36 migration destination in 1995: Over 90% of System/36 sites that migrated in 1995 moved to AS/400 solutions — mainly the Advanced 36
- **1996** — System/36 installed base decline rate: Installed base declining at a rate in excess of 20% per year; even third-parties finding support less profitable
- **1996** — PC-LAN capital cost: 10-user system: Hardware: $24,125; Systems software: $9,690; Basic accounting package: ~$12,500 (additional)
- **1996** — PC-LAN capital cost: 20-user system: Hardware: $40,875; Systems software: $15,040; Basic accounting package: $22,500
- **1996** — PC-LAN operational cost rule of thumb: $10,000 per year to manage, maintain, and upgrade a Windows NT Server + $1,000 per year for each attached PC
- **1996** — Advanced 36 capital cost range: $9,800 to $21,000 depending on model and additional peripherals; all existing System/36 applications run without modification
- **1996** — Advanced 36 maintenance cost vs System/36: Maintenance reduced from ~$330/month for larger System/36s to ~$95/month for even larger Advanced 36s (including internal storage)
- **1996** — Advanced 36 performance vs System/36: Advanced 36 runs batch processing jobs 8-10 times faster than System/36 — described as System/36 on steroids
- **1996** — System/36 to Advanced 36 migration time: Average of one day to migrate from System/36 to Advanced 36 using IBM Transition Data Link (TDL); TDL transfers data at 45MB per hour
- **1996** — AS/400 entry-level price: Entry level AS/400 around $8,000; initially comparable to Advanced 36 but may need larger configuration due to SSP Environment performance degradation
- **1996** — AS/400 multi-function server capabilities by 1996: AS/400 can function as application server, PC server (with data auto-inserted into PC spreadsheets), printer server, fax server, Internet gateway, and data warehouse
- **1996** — OS/400 Year 2000 compliance status: OS/400 Version 3 Release 6 and Version 3 Release 7 are Year 2000 compliant; growing number of AS/400 applications also Year 2000 compliant
- **1996** — System/36 Year 2000 compliance status: Most System/36 applications will become dysfunctional well before January 1, 2000 — forecasting and planning use future dates starting years ahead; SSP Year 2000 compliant apps very hard to find
- **1996** — Migration criteria 1: Lowest capital cost: Priority #1 for System/36 owners; Advanced 36 rated Best, AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable
- **1996** — Migration criteria 2: Lowest operating cost: Priority #2; Advanced 36 rated Best, AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable
- **1996** — Migration criteria 3: Lowest MIS transition cost: Priority #3; Advanced 36 rated Best (1-day migration via TDL), AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable (cannot reuse any S/36 skills or data)
- **1996** — Migration criteria 4: Least disruptive to business operations: Priority #4; Advanced 36 rated Best (same apps, same users, max 3-day cutover), AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable (all new processes)
- **1996** — Migration criteria 5: Responsive supplier support: Priority #5; AS/400 rated Best (multi-billion dollar IBM division), Advanced 36 Acceptable (IBM direct support), PC-LAN Unacceptable (local dealers, hourly billing, no accountability)
- **1996** — Aberdeen migration recommendation: Advanced 36 is clearly the best choice for the vast majority of current System/36 owners; all former System/36 owners interviewed who chose Advanced 36 were extremely happy
- **1996** — Aberdeen PC-LAN assessment for legacy migration: PC-LAN migration may be appropriate for those automating for the first time; it is not practical for System/36 owners who cannot afford to build new IT infrastructure from scratch
- **1996** — System/36 owner satisfaction paradox: Majority of System/36 owners prefer NOT to migrate due to high satisfaction with stable platform; yet universally say 'it is time to move' due to external support ecosystem collapse
- **1996** — Advanced 36 as transitional bridge platform prediction: Aberdeen recommends migrating to Advanced 36 within 12 months then planning for eventual move to OS/400 for Year 2000 compliant applications
- **2000** — Advanced 36 transitional bridge platform — actual outcome: IBM withdrew Advanced 36 from marketing by 2000; Advanced/36 Machine support removed in OS/400 V4R5; System/36 owners who migrated to Advanced 36 then migrated to AS/400 as predicted; prediction proved accurate
- **1996** — AS/400 long-term viability prediction: AS/400 division is IBM's most profitable hardware division; IBM will be supporting AS/400 for a significant time to come; resellers and software developers still working on new products
