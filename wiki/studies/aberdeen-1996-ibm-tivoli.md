# Tivoli Systems: Unifying the Management of Network Computing Environments

> Aberdeen Group profiles Tivoli Systems (an IBM company) and its Tivoli Management Framework (TMF), arguing it provides IT executives the most comprehensive enterprise-grade management solution for unifying diverse client-server, mainframe, and midrange environments under a single policy-based management framework. The study explains IBM's strategic decision to acquire Tivoli instead of completing its own Karat project, and positions TMF as superior to HP OpenView's bottoms-up approach and CA Unicenter's all-or-nothing purchasing model.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1996-10-01 · **Type:** product-profile
**Importance:** high — *Published just after IBM's acquisition of Tivoli, this study assessed a pivotal strategic move in the enterprise management software market; IBM's Tivoli became one of the most significant IT management platforms for 20+ years and Aberdeen's endorsement at this inflection point was significant.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen accurately predicted Tivoli would succeed IBM's Karat strategy and that IBM's investment would validate Tivoli users; Tivoli grew into a billion-dollar IBM franchise spanning 20+ years and the brand remains active in IBM's product catalog in 2026.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — IBM decision to replace Karat with Tivoli: IBM chose to replace Karat project with Tivoli rather than augment Karat; positioned SystemView and NetView as Tivoli management applications
- **1996** — IBM Karat project shortcomings: Karat would have been delivered years after Tivoli; would manage only IBM platforms; production-critical app suppliers were already on Tivoli, not Karat
- **1996** — Tivoli TMF platform coverage breadth: TMF manages 18+ platform operating environments including Unix variants, Windows NT, NetWare, MVS, OS/400, OS/2, DOS
- **1996** — Tivoli ISV ecosystem approach: TMF framework allows plug-in of best-in-class ISV management applications; 45+ ISV partners in 10/Plus association
- **1996** — HP OpenView vs Tivoli comparison: HP OpenView provides bottoms-up view of enterprise; Tivoli provides top-down view based on IT management policies/procedures
- **1996** — CA Unicenter TNG vs Tivoli comparison: CA Unicenter TNG is comprehensive but must be purchased in entirety; less flexible for integrating other management applications
- **1996** — Tivoli success as IBM enterprise management strategy: IBM has done an about-face and charged ahead with Tivoli; business-line executives, IT executives and IS managers will find their needs realized
- **2010** — Tivoli Systems post-acquisition success: IBM Tivoli became a multi-billion dollar franchise; Tivoli brand active 20+ years after acquisition; IBM Tivoli Monitoring still referenced in IBM bulletins as of 2024-2026
- **1996** — IBM global consulting/services for Tivoli: IBM's acquisition provides global consulting and support team for Tivoli-based solutions; validates ISV and user investments
- **2000** — IBM Tivoli services ecosystem growth: IBM built extensive Tivoli professional services organization; Tivoli products became core to IBM Global Services; ISV ecosystem grew substantially
- **1996** — TME 10 Enterprise Console centralized management capability: TME 10 EC removes administrators from details of underlying technology; manages according to enterprise policy; provides integrated unified view of IT resources
- **1996** — TMF benefit 1: Deploy without holding back: Enables rapid deployment of client-server applications instead of holding back due to management cost concerns
- **1996** — TMF benefit 2: Reduce administration cost: Extends reach of centralized resources throughout enterprise; reduces cost of administration
- **1996** — TMF benefit 3: Unify management: Unifies management of applications, operating environments, platforms and networks from mainframe to desktop
- **1996** — TMF benefit 4: Policy-based management: Manages evolving IT environment using policies and procedures instead of administrative skill or whim
- **1996** — Client-server cost-of-ownership problem: IT executives have found managing client-server environments to be more costly than anticipated; standardizing on fewest application platforms possible
- **1996** — IBM Karat vs Tivoli user validation: IBM's largest users told IBM they were already deploying the real Karat — and it was Tivoli; validates IBM's acquisition rationale
- **1996** — Tivoli managers and agents design: TMF employs managers and agents as smart objects carrying out enterprise policies/procedures; well designed to scale as IT environment grows
- **1996** — TME 10 Software Distribution capability: Controls distribution of software patches, updates, upgrades from central location to any managed Tivoli system (from OS/390 to desktops)
- **1996** — TME 10 Distributed Monitoring capability: Monitors conditions on managed resources; provides alerts; enables thresholds for wide variety of conditions; takes automatic corrective actions
- **1996** — IBM SystemView repositioning as Tivoli application: IBM's SystemView positioned to become well-behaved Tivoli management application; validates existing SystemView user investments
- **1996** — IBM NetView repositioning as Tivoli application: IBM's NetView positioned to become well-behaved Tivoli management application; validates existing NetView user investments
- **1996** — Tivoli ISV ecosystem sustainability post-IBM acquisition: Tivoli has minds and hearts of ISVs, system platform suppliers, packaged application suppliers; where ISVs go, so goes the enterprise
- **2005** — Tivoli ISV ecosystem growth: IBM Tivoli grew extensive ISV/partner ecosystem; partners like BMC, CA, and Remedy continued integrating with Tivoli for years; 10/Plus ecosystem expanded significantly
- **1996** — CA Unicenter competitive strategy: CA users possess ability to visually integrate networked resources using pre-packaged solution vs Tivoli's ability to functionally integrate with best-in-class solutions
