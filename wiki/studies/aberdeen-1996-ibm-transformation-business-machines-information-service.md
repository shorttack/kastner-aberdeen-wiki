# IBM: The Transformation From Business Machines To Information-Service Solutions

> Aberdeen Group profiles IBM's strategic transformation under Lou Gerstner from a hardware-centric company to a service-focused information solutions provider, centered on IBM Global Services' $20.1 billion (1995) and 100,000 employees. The study documents IBM's Network Computing vision, 12 industry solution groups, Year 2000 opportunity, strategic acquisitions (Lotus/Notes, Tivoli), and identifies structural vulnerabilities including OS/2 weakness and partner ecosystem tensions.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1995** — IBM 1995 total revenue: $71.9 billion (12.1% increase over 1994)
- **1995** — IBM 1995 net earnings: $4.2 billion (38.3% earnings increase over 1994)
- **1995** — IBM Global Services 1995 revenues and backlog: $20.1B revenue; $29B backlog; 100,000 employees (of 225,000 total IBM)
- **1995** — IBM Global Services professional fees growth rate: 30% annual growth for past 4 years in customer-intimate professional services
- **1996** — IBM H1 1996 revenue trend: Revenue up 4.4% but hardware declined 0.6%; services (ex-maintenance) up 26.4%
- **1996** — IBM Global Services headcount projection: 100,000 at study; on-track for 110,000 by end 1996; 45% of IBM total employees
- **1996** — IBM 12 global industry solution groups functional area coverage: 140 functional areas replicable; planned increase to 170+
- **1996** — IBM primary strategic objective: Be first source for installed base's next-generation information solutions; Network Computing supplier of choice globally
- **1996** — IBM 'Just Say Yes Company' strategy: IBM commits to fulfilling any customer request for information-service solutions including using competitor products (HP Unix, Microsoft BackOffice)
- **1996** — IBM 'One Face to the Customer' strategy: Unified IBM presentation replacing former competing hardware divisions (S/390, AS/400, RS/6000, PC server) fighting in front of customers
- **1996** — IBM strategic advantage 1: Most complete IT product line of any company; 25% of R&D staff required annually to work with customers via Global Services
- **1996** — IBM strategic advantage 2: Closest to truly global organization: unified industry solution groups treating global customer operations as single IT project; local presence in smaller metropolitan areas
- **1996** — IBM strategic advantage 3: Employees hardened by 1990s layoffs; motivated to prove value; no entitlement culture remaining
- **1996** — IBM strategic advantage 4: Installed in almost every Global 500 datacenter; knows customers' infrastructure, staff, and business processes
- **1996** — OS/2 strategic liability assessment: Excellent for Global Services desktop deployments but lacks ISV support for Microsoft Office 95; perception of proprietary strategy haunts IBM
- **1996** — IBM DB2 competitive position: Follower in RDBMS functionality and performance vs Oracle, Sybase, Informix; IS decision makers value best-in-class
- **1996** — Lotus Notes acquisition value realization: IBM sold more Notes licenses in first 6 months of ownership than Lotus had in previous 6 years; Domino Notes (internet version) extends value
- **2000** — IBM services revenue growth projection: Replication of implementations combined with Network Computing migration will increase revenues by >$8B/year in foreseeable future
- **1999** — IBM Y2K services opportunity: Y2K may be bigger and more profitable than Network Computing opportunity; IBM ideally positioned for Y2K Global Services engagement
- **2000** — OS/2 market trajectory prediction: OS/2's lack of ISV support and proprietary perception will continue to limit IBM's desktop influence
- **2000** — IBM ISV/partner tension prediction: Independent professional service providers will downplay IBM offerings as Global Services competes against them; ISVs will view IBM as competitor
- **2005** — IBM services transformation actual outcome: By early 2000s IBM became leader in IT services and consulting; $7B+ cost avoidance 1996-1998; services dominant revenue driver
- **2006** — OS/2 actual outcome: OS/2 discontinued; IBM ended development support 2006; Aberdeen's prediction of strategic liability proved correct
- **2021** — IBM Global Services spin-off actual outcome: IBM spun off infrastructure services as Kyndryl (2021); consulting retained as IBM Consulting; Aberdeen had warned against spin-off
- **2005** — Network Computing vision realization: IBM's Network Computing vision prefigured cloud computing and service-oriented architecture; IBM led enterprise services market through 2000s
