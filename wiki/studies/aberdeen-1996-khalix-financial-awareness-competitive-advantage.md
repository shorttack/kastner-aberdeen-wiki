# Khalix: True Financial Awareness, True Competitive Advantage

> Aberdeen Group profiles Longview Solutions' Khalix 2.1 financial management software, arguing it represents a superior approach to integrated enterprise financial management by combining consolidation, budgeting, forecasting, and analysis in a single truly integrated system built on Relational OLAP technology. The study contends Khalix's object-oriented hierarchical engine eliminates the cracks between loosely integrated point solutions and guarantees Longview a position on enterprise technology buyers' must-RFP shortlist.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1996-07-01 · **Type:** product-profile
**Importance:** medium — *Published during the early corporate performance management (CPM) market formation, this study captured important architectural debates between Relational OLAP and proprietary multidimensional databases; Longview/Khalix eventually became a durable market player for 25+ years.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen accurately predicted Longview's durable market position and the superiority of its integrated approach; Longview grew through 25 years of acquisitions and is still active as insightsoftware's Longview product suite in 2026.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Khalix 2.1 LAN site license pricing: Basic LAN site license (up to 50 users): $145,000; additional $95,000 for data warehouse components on RDBMSs
- **1996** — Longview Solutions revenue target: Plans to double sales during coming year to more than $10 million U.S.
- **1995** — Longview venture funding: Received funding in October 1995 from Edison Venture Capital Fund; established first U.S. office
- **1996** — Longview go-to-market model: Direct sales force throughout Canada and US; independent agents worldwide; partnership programs including Cognos, Oracle, Pyramid
- **1996** — Relational OLAP vs proprietary MOLAP advantage: ROLAP leverages mainstream scalable-parallel RDBMSs; eliminates need for enterprise to maintain non-mainstream technologies; benefits from hardware parallelism innovations
- **1996** — Essbase competitive mindshare: Arbor Software Essbase has significant mindshare and marketing might; adopted as popular proprietary multidimensional engine by many financial software developers via APIs
- **1996** — Oracle Express 6.0 limitation: Oracle Express uses proprietary multidimensional database (not RDBMS) for financial applications; requires enterprise to factor in non-standard database type
- **1996** — Oracle competitive strategy vs Khalix: Oracle counters Khalix with parallelism, 64-bit enablement, and RDBMS across full platform spectrum; but Oracle Financial management uses non-RDBMS multidimensional backend
- **1996** — Hyperion Software competitive position: Strong loyal user base; numerous years of experience; layered architecture using proprietary multidimensional DB for reporting plus RDBMS for accounting/transactional
- **1996** — Khalix object-orientation advantages: Object-oriented hierarchical engine with drag-and-drop hierarchy editor; inheritance for selective recalculation; reduces IS coding burden; enables what-if analysis
- **1996** — Khalix 64-bit scalability readiness: Khalix prepared to exploit emerging 64-bit technology; benefits from scalable-parallel databases; can scale up and speed up with demand via affordable processor increments
- **1996** — Khalix sparse-matrix compression: Compression technique knocks out unused cells creating tighter multidimensional matrix; reduces disk and memory requirements
- **1996** — Khalix RDBMS support breadth: Khalix Financial Warehouse runs on Informix, Microsoft SQL Server, Oracle, or Sybase RDBMSs
- **1996** — Integrated financial management requirement 1: Consolidation: Must handle multi-entity multi-currency rollups within 12-hour window; eliminate manual intervention for accounts synchronization
- **1996** — Integrated financial management requirement 2: Budgeting and forecasting: Must support relational-budgeting and activities-based management; drill into costs to find activities influencing bottom line
- **1996** — Integrated financial management requirement 3: Reporting: Must deliver both appropriate level of detail and executive digest; fully integrated world-class reporting tools
- **1996** — Integrated financial management requirement 4: Scalability: Must scale from desktop to SMP/MPP; non-intrusive; adjustable from root calculation logic across all modules
- **1996** — Loosely integrated financial management systems prevalence: Most financial management software suppliers have delivered loosely integrated point solutions requiring multiple software versions, technologies, and maintenance routines
- **1996** — Khalix market position on must-RFP shortlist: With Khalix 2.1 Longview Solutions has guaranteed itself a worthy position on technology buyers' must-RFP shortlist of financial management/analysis software
- **2020** — Longview market position and longevity: Longview survived through multiple acquisitions: Exact Software (2007, $51.5M), Marlin Equity (2014), insightsoftware (2020); product still active as Longview by insightsoftware in 2026
- **1996** — Khalix Intranet/WWW-enabled version: Longview plans to introduce fully Web-enabled version of Khalix by third quarter 1997
- **1997** — Khalix Web-enabled version delivery: Khalix did evolve with web capabilities; company grew through reference accounts and reached enterprise customers globally; web roadmap delivery consistent with stated plans
- **1996** — User testimony: elimination of download-plan-upload cycle: One of largest Khalix installations uses it as cross-enterprise modeling tool in highly decentralized business; eliminated multi-business-unit download-plan-upload cycle
- **1996** — User testimony: 2-year search for integrated CPM: After two-year search for integrated consolidated financial management, household-name company chose Khalix; found competitive products missing crucial components or couldn't scale
- **1996** — OLAP market consolidation trend: Most financial management suppliers allied with decision-support/OLAP tools developers; delivered loosely integrated systems requiring manual synchronization and multiple maintenance routines
