# Managing Customers with Next-Generation Software Applications: 1996 Edition

> Aberdeen Group's second report on the Customer Interaction Software (CIS) market defines the category encompassing sales force automation, helpdesk, field service, telemarketing, and related technologies. The study profiles over 40 CIS suppliers, identifies market dynamics, and advises IS executives that CIS is the key to building long-term profitable customer relationships while warning that most of the hundreds of suppliers will not survive long-term.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1996-10-01 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *This study was an early and comprehensive market definition of what would become CRM software—a category that grew to $40+ billion annually. Aberdeen's 1996 CIS framework anticipated the consolidation and key functionality that shaped CRM platforms for the next three decades.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — CIS supplier count: Literally hundreds of CIS suppliers in the market in 1996; study profiles over 40
- **1996** — Study publication history: Second Aberdeen CIS report; first published May 1995; 1996 edition priced at US$695
- **1996** — CIS target audience classification: Three audiences: (1) business executives choosing CIS, (2) IS executives implementing CIS, (3) CIS suppliers analyzing market position
- **1996** — CIS module: Customer Support / Internal Helpdesk: Issue tracking, escalation, knowledge base for internal IT and external customer support
- **1996** — CIS module: Field Service Management: Dispatch, scheduling, parts management, on-site service tracking
- **1996** — CIS module: Sales Force Automation: Contact management, opportunity tracking, forecasting, sales territory management
- **1996** — CIS module: Telemarketing/Telesales: Outbound/inbound calling campaigns, lead management, scripting
- **1996** — CTI as key CIS enabling technology: Computer-Telephony Integration identified as important related technology for CIS; links customer phone interactions with data systems
- **1996** — Internet impact on CIS: Aberdeen explains 'unexpected implications' of Internet-enabled CIS; web-based customer interaction beginning to emerge
- **1996** — Siebel Systems market position 1996: Profiled CIS supplier; founded 1993 by Tom Siebel; focused on enterprise sales force automation; early-stage high-growth vendor
- **1996** — Remedy Corporation market position 1996: Profiled CIS supplier; strong in IT helpdesk/service management; Action Request System product
- **1996** — Clarify Inc. market position 1996: Profiled CIS supplier; focused on customer support and CRM; later acquired by Nortel for $2.1B in 1998
- **1996** — Aberdeen warning on CIS market consolidation: Companies rushing to install CIS systems cannot afford to make mistakes. Market filled with hundreds of suppliers — only a few will be around over the long term
- **1996** — Aberdeen value proposition for CIS: Growth and profit-oriented companies gaining market share through coordinated sales tactics and superior customer service via CIS
- **1996** — Prediction: CIS market will consolidate dramatically: Most of hundreds of CIS suppliers will not survive long-term; only a few will remain to support users; buyers must choose right the first time
- **1996** — Prediction: Internet will transform CIS delivery: Internet-enabled CIS will create unexpected implications; future CIS systems will leverage web technologies
- **2006** — Outcome: CRM market consolidation around Siebel/Oracle/SAP: Siebel Systems became dominant CRM vendor with ~$1.5B revenue before Oracle acquisition for $5.8B (2006); SAP CRM and Salesforce.com (2000) also emerged; hundreds of small vendors were consolidated or disappeared
- **2005** — Outcome: Internet transformation of CIS/CRM: Salesforce.com (founded 1999) pioneered SaaS/web-based CRM and reached $300M revenue by 2006; web-based CRM became standard; prediction proved highly accurate
- **1996** — CIS supplier profiles count in 1996 study: Over 40 CIS software suppliers profiled; 5 systems integrators profiled; 3 hardware/platform suppliers profiled
