# Remote Access: Cisco's Untold Story

> Aberdeen Group examines Cisco Systems' remote access strategy as of November 1996, revealing Cisco's 'untold story' of quietly dominating the enterprise branch-to-branch segment (60%+ market share) while making aggressive inroads into the high-end ISP and carrier markets with the AS5200 access server. The study evaluates Cisco's multi-product portfolio (AS5200, 3600, 1600 series), IOS software competitive advantages, and the Telebit MICA technology acquisition, predicting Cisco is better positioned than competitors to serve the rapidly evolving ISP/carrier market.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Remote access market size estimate 1996: $4 billion
- **2000** — Remote access market size projection for 2000: $20 billion
- **2000** — Dial-up market size projection for 2000: Over $5.5 billion
- **1996** — Cisco enterprise branch-to-branch market share: More than 60%; over 1 million installed units
- **1996** — AS5200 worldwide customer count (October 1996): Over 700 customers including 10 major telcos, 5 ISPs, several IXC services
- **1996** — AS5200 new order unit growth trend: Units per customer on new orders tripled since August 1996
- **1996** — IOS software competitive moat: Robustness and depth of IOS software is Cisco's primary value-add; cross-platform consistency
- **1996** — Cisco competitive weapons framework: Four weapons: IOS software, acquisition capability, large loyal customer base, excellent support
- **1996** — Cisco acquisition strategy: Timely acquisition of technologies for rapidly changing market; Combinet, Newport, Telebit MICA
- **1996** — MICA technology benefit: High-density digital modem concentration for next-gen ISP access servers; enables sub-T3 installations
- **1996** — AS5200 architecture: Integrates CSU, communications server, channel bank, modems, switch, router in single chassis; fully digital
- **1996** — 3600 series routing performance: 15,000 to 40,000 packets per second; supports ISDN BRI, T1/PRI, async/sync serial in one chassis
- **1996** — 1600 ClickStart configuration: Web browser-based configuration and monitoring; installs in minutes with any browser
- **1996** — QoS as ISP differentiation requirement: Security, availability, reliability, QoS, and management cited as five ISP differentiation factors
- **1996** — VPDN as ISP value-add: Virtual Private Dial-up network capabilities cited as key IOS feature for service differentiation
- **1996** — Cisco ISP/carrier market leadership prediction: Aberdeen believes Cisco better positioned long-term than major competitors for ISP/carrier market
- **1996** — IOS attractiveness to ISPs: IOS software will prove attractive to ISPs differentiating services with value-added capabilities
- **1996** — 1997 remote access market prediction: 1997 will be year of consolidation at upper levels; internet access becomes commodity
- **1996** — 1997 price-per-port prediction: 1997 will be year of 'price per port' as Service Providers drive infrastructure expansion
- **2000** — Cisco ISP/carrier market leadership actual: Cisco became dominant ISP/carrier infrastructure vendor by 1999-2000; backbone routers and access servers
- **1999** — Internet access commoditization actual: Internet access did commoditize rapidly 1997-2000; ISP consolidation followed (AOL, MindSpring mergers)
- **1999** — Ascend competitive fate: Ascend acquired by Lucent Technologies in 1999 for $24B; validated Aberdeen's consolidation prediction
- **1997** — US Robotics competitive fate: US Robotics acquired by 3Com in 1997 for $7.3B; removed from independent competition
- **1996** — Cisco SNA traffic WAN market share: 70% of SNA traffic over wide-area claimed by Cisco
- **1996** — ISP infrastructure upgrade direction: Majority of ISP connectivity upgrades will be sub-T3 installations over next 12 months
