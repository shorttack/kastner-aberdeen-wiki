# Sun Microsystems Computer Company: UltraComputing for Business

> Aberdeen Group profiles Sun Microsystems Computer Company's (SMCC) commercial market strategy and new UltraSPARC server line launched April 1996. The study analyzes Sun's Network Business Advantage program (BusinessWare, Customer Management Solutions, Decision Warehouse), the UltraSPARC architecture delivering twice the power and five times the bandwidth of prior SPARC, and Sun's Java-driven Internet strategy. Aberdeen projects continued growth in Sun's commercial market revenues and endorses the UltraSPARC platform as meeting enterprise IS buying criteria.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1995** — Sun Commercial Market Revenue: Over $2 billion in sales from commercial applications market in 1995
- **1996** — Sun Revenue Growth (9 months ending March 1996): $5.1 billion in revenues for 9 months ending March 1996, up nearly 20% over prior year
- **1996** — Sun Commercial Market Growth Forecast: Aberdeen forecasts another significant increase in Sun's revenues from commercial accounts for 1996
- **2000** — Sun Commercial Market Growth - Outcome: Sun achieved peak revenues of ~$15B by FY2001; commercial Unix server leadership maintained through late 1990s before internet bust
- **1996** — UltraSPARC vs SPARC Performance: 2x processing power, 5x bandwidth, 20% latency reduction vs prior SPARC architecture
- **1996** — Solaris SMP Scalability vs Competitors: Other Unix SMP implementations require 15-30% CPU overhead when adding processors; Solaris 2.5 finely tuned for near-linear SMP scaling
- **1996** — Java Market Impact Assessment: Java has taken the ISV community including industry giants by storm; many business-critical applications expected within following months
- **1996** — Java as Enterprise Platform: Aberdeen opinion: Sun has again set industry vision for important emerging IS opportunity with Java internet/intranet applications
- **2010** — Java as Enterprise Platform - Outcome: Java became the dominant enterprise application runtime; billions of devices; Oracle acquired Sun for $7.4B largely for Java IP
- **1996** — Network Business Advantage Strategy: Three-pillar commercial strategy: BusinessWare (ERP/transactional), Customer Management Solutions, Decision Warehouse (analytics)
- **1996** — IS Buying Criteria: Scalability: Ability to efficiently and cost-effectively scale system as needs grow
- **1996** — IS Buying Criteria: Availability: Ability to keep total system availability 7x24
- **1996** — IS Buying Criteria: Manageability: Ability to easily manage the system
- **1996** — IS Buying Criteria: ISV Software Compatibility: Ability of software packages to work well with total system solution
- **1996** — Sun Internet Commerce Positioning: New Internet-based electronic commerce programs announced March 1996; Netra servers for remote e-commerce deployment
- **1996** — Network is the Computer Vindication: Sun's 'network is the computer' concept derided in mid-1980s is now a de facto standard for distributed information systems design
- **1996** — Sun OS Strategy: Pure Unix play with Solaris 2.5 at center stage; no OS identity crisis unlike multi-platform competitors
- **1996** — Sun ISV Ecosystem Size: Sun has contributed to more than a dozen worldwide standards organizations; extensive ISV partner network
- **1996** — Java Applet Integration with Legacy Systems: Sun professional services specializes in building links between existing production applications and new distributed Java applets
- **1996** — UltraSPARC Architecture Design Generation: New Enterprise Server line is totally new design inside and out; not a simple update of older SPARCservers
