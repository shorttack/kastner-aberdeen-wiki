# Unisys Clears the Path Ahead for A & OS 2200 Series Customers

> Aberdeen Group profiles Unisys ClearPath HMP (Heterogeneous Multi-Processing) servers, which combine CMOS versions of MCP/AS and OS 2200 enterprise systems with Intel-based Unix/NT SMP servers in a single cabinet. Aberdeen examines how ClearPath gives Unisys's 8,500+ mainframe customers a migration path to open client-server architectures while preserving existing investments. The study notes that Unisys is transitioning from hardware to services, while hardware remains 40% of $6.2B in 1995 revenue.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1996-04-01 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *Unisys ClearPath was a pivotal product for preserving the installed base of 8,500+ mid-to-large-range A Series and 1100/2200 customers; Aberdeen's analysis helped these organizations evaluate their mainframe migration strategies at a moment of maximum uncertainty.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen predicted Unisys would successfully transition to services while maintaining mainframe customers; Unisys ClearPath OS 2200 is still actively supported and now runs on AWS (2025), validating the 'clear path' migration strategy Aberdeen endorsed in 1996.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Unisys ClearPath strategic positioning: Clear path for A Series and OS 2200 customers to migrate to multi-tier open architectures while preserving existing investments
- **1995** — Unisys total revenue 1995: $6.2 billion
- **1995** — Unisys hardware revenue share: 40% of $6.2 billion (approximately $2.48 billion) from large-scale computer systems
- **1996** — Unisys ClearPath target installed base: More than 8,500 mid- to large-range A Series and 1100/2200 customers worldwide
- **1996** — ClearPath CMOS processor performance: 0.5-micron CMOS technology (IBM-fabricated) expected to deliver 40-60 MIPS; replaces ECL systems
- **1996** — ClearPath ECL replacement scope: Unisys becomes first supplier of large-scale enterprise servers to replace entire line -- including high-end -- with CMOS alternatives
- **1996** — ClearPath benefit 1: Mainframe + open integration: Customers capitalize on combined benefits of robust mainframe OS environment and open Windows NT or Unix environment in single cabinet
- **1996** — ClearPath benefit 2: CMOS upgrades: CMOS delivers smaller footprint, power savings vs ECL; cost-effective upgrades preserve customer investments
- **1996** — ClearPath benefit 3: Investment preservation: New enterprise server models preserve customers' investments in mission-critical applications; natural evolution rather than forced migration
- **1996** — ClearPath OPUS MPP integration timeline: Unisys promises to integrate OPUS MPP system into ClearPath HMP in future releases
- **2003** — OPUS MPP integration outcome: Unisys discontinued OPUS MPP line by early 2000s; OPUS MPP not integrated into ClearPath as promised; prediction did not materialize
- **1996** — Unisys services transition viability: Unisys committed to transforming into services-based information management company while sustaining hardware revenue
- **2024** — Unisys services transformation outcome: Unisys successfully transitioned to services-dominant company; ClearPath OS 2200 and MCP still actively supported; OS 2200 now available on AWS (June 2025)
- **1996** — ClearPath long-term viability for A Series/OS 2200 customers: ClearPath HMP provides effective architecture for modernization; gives 8,500+ customers clear path to multi-tier applications of the future
- **2025** — ClearPath long-term viability actual outcome: ClearPath OS 2200 and MCP remain in active production; ClearPath Forward product line updated through 2024; OS 2200 deployed on AWS June 2025
- **1996** — Unisys business model transformation: Transitioning from large-scale computer hardware company to services-based information management company; hardware still 40% of revenue
- **1996** — ClearPath pre-integration value: Pre-integration by Unisys substantially enhances using HMP for sophisticated client/server applications; single-cabinet management
- **1996** — IBM-fabricated CMOS for Unisys: 0.5-micron CMOS technology designed by Unisys and fabricated by IBM; new high-end processors expected to deliver 40-60 MIPS
- **1996** — Aberdeen verdict on ClearPath announcement: ClearPath HMP announcement is important for Unisys and its customers; provides effective architecture for modernization and re-engineering
- **1996** — A Series / MCP migration path: ClearPath A Series (MCP) customers can implement client/server at sensible pace; path to new cost-effective CMOS upgrades
