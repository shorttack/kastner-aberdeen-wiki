# VPNs for Business: The MFS Success Strategy

> Aberdeen Group profiles MFS Communications Company's strategy for leadership in the emerging Virtual Private Network (VPN) and digital telecommunications market. The study documents MFS's five-operating-unit structure, its SONET/ATM fiber network spanning 45+ US metro centers and international markets, and its first-mover advantages in national ATM (1993) and international ATM (1994) services. Aberdeen examines MFS's WAVE integrated voice-data service, its accelerated deployment plan (65 US metro centers by end 1998 vs. original 2000 target), and its co-carrier strategy under the US Telecom Act of 1996, concluding MFS is well-positioned for world leadership in VPN and integrated network services.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1995** — MFS network scale (September 1995): 2,559,526 circuits; 165,999 miles of fiber; 4,458 buildings; 49 cities; 26 ATM switches in 25 cities
- **1996** — MFS US metro deployment plan acceleration: Original plan: 65 US metro centers by 2000; accelerated to end of 1998 due to success
- **1994** — MFS network uptime: 99.99% availability throughout 1994 and 1995
- **1996** — MFS Intelenet growth: 100 employees (end 1993) to 1,500+ employees and 20,000 business customers by 1996
- **1996** — Fiber optic cable installed by MFS Network Technologies: 125,000 miles of fiber optic cable installed
- **1996** — MFS market strategy classification: Metro-dense network aggregation + regulatory arbitrage + SONET/ATM first-mover + 5-unit integrated structure
- **1996** — MFS organizational structure: Five integrated operating companies: MFS Telecom, MFS Data Services, MFS Intelenet, MFS International, MFS Network Technologies
- **1995** — WAVE service cost savings vs. dual T-1: 20-25% savings vs. two separate T-1 lines in typical installation
- **1994** — Frame Relay vs. packet switched data speed advantage: 30% faster than conventional packet switched data; more cost effective
- **1994** — MFS ATM service milestones: First national ATM data service US (1993); first international ATM (1994); first FR over ATM (1994)
- **1995** — WAVE VBR voice quality: First to offer VBR (Variable Bit Rate) voice with toll quality fidelity over ATM
- **1996** — MFS network development phase 1: Market identification: Identify market opportunity → legal/regulatory assessment → network design → business plan → site/license acquisition
- **1996** — MFS network development phase 2: Construction: MFS Network Technologies handles engineering, project management, construction, and network support
- **1996** — MFS network development phase 3: Telemanagement and services: Completed network available to operating units for service delivery; integrated across telecom, data, international, and network services
- **1996** — VPN market trend assessment: VPNs are a trend on the rise, creating strong demand for skillful network integrators and global VPN providers
- **1996** — MFS regulatory advantage assessment: MFS history of network quality gives advantage with regulators; first non-government telecom licensee in Germany
- **1996** — Telecom Act 1996 impact on MFS: Telecom Act of 1996 provides significant co-carrier opportunities; MFS sent co-carrier letters to all 50 states immediately
- **1996** — MFS world VPN leadership prediction: MFS is well-positioned to achieve world leadership in VPN and integrated network communication solutions
- **1996** — MFS 65-metro deployment completion by end 1998: MFS will deploy in all 65 US metro centers by end of 1998 (accelerated from year 2000)
- **1996** — MFS international expansion prediction: MFS will expand into 25 financial centers internationally, primarily Western Europe (15 new metros beyond original 10)
- **1996** — MFS acquisition outcome: WorldCom acquired MFS for ~$14B in August 1996 stock deal; created first US bundled local+long-distance+internet company
- **2002** — WorldCom corporate outcome: WorldCom filed largest US bankruptcy ($107B assets) in July 2002; emerged as MCI; acquired by Verizon 2006
- **1996** — UUNET acquisition outcome: MFS acquired UUNET for $2B just before WorldCom deal; UUNET became core of WorldCom/MCI internet infrastructure
- **2005** — VPN market evolution outcome: VPN market became massive; MPLS VPNs dominated enterprise by early 2000s; IP-VPN/SD-WAN by 2015+
