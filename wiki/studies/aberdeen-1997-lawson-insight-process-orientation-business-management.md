# Lawson Insight: Where Process-Orientation Makes A Difference For Business Management

> This May 1997 Aberdeen Group profile assesses Lawson Software and its Lawson Insight Business Management System, positioning the company as a leader among enterprise business application (EBA) vendors in the transition to process-centric, web-deployable software. Aberdeen documents Lawson's four-cornerstone architecture (Process Orientation, Event-based Management, Continual Improvement, Ubiquitous Information Access) and its Web Service Centers, characterizing Lawson as ahead of SAP, Oracle, and PeopleSoft on process orientation and internet-readiness. The study further profiles Lawson's healthcare vertical strategy, $101.8M FY1996 revenues, and competitive positioning against midrange rivals J.D. Edwards, Ross, and System Software Associates.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — Lawson FY1996 revenues: $101.8 million (46% increase over prior year)
- **1997** — Lawson customer count: Over 2,300 customers
- **1997** — Lawson employee count: Over 800 employees
- **1997** — Lawson Insight cornerstone 1: Process Orientation — cross-functional business processes via Lawson Process Suites (Financials, HR, Procurement, Supply Chain)
- **1997** — Lawson Insight cornerstone 2: Event-based Management — Activity-Based Management with financial and process data for decision support
- **1997** — Lawson Insight cornerstone 3: Continual Improvement — multi-level cross-organizational workflow enabling process evolution
- **1997** — Lawson Insight cornerstone 4: Ubiquitous Information Access — Drill-Around analysis, OLAP, web-deployable WebDesktop and Web Service Centers
- **1997** — WebDesktop maturity: Second release; full BMS functionality from browser; Insight screens as JAVA forms; supports JavaScript and Microsoft Office integration
- **1997** — ABM architecture differentiation: Activity Repository as intelligent data warehouse: synchronized with GL yet unconstrained by GL metadata model; avoids GL performance degradation
- **1997** — Web Service Centers coverage: 6 pre-built web apps: Employee Service Center, Supervisor Service Center, Internal Procurement Center, Customer Information Center, Vendor Information Center, Financial Information Center
- **1997** — Active Object Repository architecture: Maintains business object methods shared across multiple application suites; combined with multi-level workflow for process models
- **1997** — Lawson market strategy: Go-up-market strategy: non-manufacturing process-oriented EBA + vertical market focus (healthcare, multi-tier retail, hospitality, publishing)
- **1997** — EBA market shift — best-of-breed redefinition: Best-of-breed EBA now horizontally defined as business process (e.g., Procurement) rather than vertically by function (e.g., Accounts Payable)
- **1997** — Internet as first business context for OO: The Internet will provide the first business context for widespread adoption of object-oriented line-of-business solutions
- **1997** — Aberdeen competitive assessment of Lawson vs. enterprise rivals: Lawson ahead of SAP, Oracle, PeopleSoft on process orientation, event-based management, continual improvement, and ubiquitous information access
- **1997** — Aberdeen on Lawson's self-reinvention capability: Lawson stands out as a company able to continually reinvent itself, its business solutions, its integration of leading-edge technology and its market value proposition
- **1997** — Aberdeen on Lawson healthcare vertical success: Healthcare initiative begun nearly three years ago is an excellent example of Lawson's vertical focus and drive-to-dominate
- **1996** — Lawson revenue growth vs. EBA peers: 46% YoY revenue growth to $101.8M in FY1996; crossed $100M threshold ahead of most midrange peers
- **1989** — Lawson open systems migration timing: Migrated to open systems in late 1980s; nearly a decade more experience than midrange rivals (JD Edwards, Ross, SSA) in advanced distributable multi-tier technologies
- **1997** — Lawson web leadership: Lawson is leading its competitors in anticipating the Internet watershed event and delivering products allowing enterprises to gain advantage through advanced technology + flexible process orientation
- **1997** — Lawson vertical diversification success: 1997 vertical strategy will diversify to multi-tier retail, hospitality and leisure, and publishing markets
- **1997** — Internet as ERP delivery platform: Internet will provide the first business context for widespread adoption of OO line-of-business solutions; enterprises will accelerate acquisitions of inherently Internet-enabled solutions
- **2012** — Lawson long-term viability: Acquired by Infor and Golden Gate Capital for $2B in 2012 after going public ~2001; had grown to significant healthcare ERP market share before acquisition
- **2005** — PeopleSoft viability: Acquired by Oracle in hostile takeover for $10.3B in 2005; PeopleSoft brand eventually discontinued
- **2003** — J.D. Edwards viability: Acquired by PeopleSoft for $1.7B in 2003; then absorbed into Oracle via 2005 hostile takeover; continues as Oracle JD Edwards EnterpriseOne
