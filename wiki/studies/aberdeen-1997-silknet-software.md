# Silknet Software: Changing the Rules for Next-Generation Customer Support Applications

> This July 1997 Aberdeen Group Product Viewpoint profiles Silknet Software's eService, characterizing it as the first viable internet-native customer support application built from the ground up for web deployment. Aberdeen identifies four trends driving next-generation customer support — problem resolution, customer self-service, enterprise integration, and multimedia — and asserts that Silknet's thin-client architecture uniquely satisfies all four. The study predicts Silknet will radically redefine the $1B+ customer interaction software (CIS) market, forcing client-server incumbent vendors (call-tracking specialists) to fundamentally rethink their product strategies.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1997-07-08 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *Silknet eService was the first true internet-native customer support application in 1997, predating Salesforce Service Cloud and other SaaS CRM by years; Aberdeen's assessment correctly identified the architectural discontinuity that would reshape the entire CIS market category.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen's prediction that Silknet would force market restructuring was confirmed: Kana Communications acquired Silknet for $4.2B in 2000 at dot-com peak; the internet-native CIS market Aberdeen described became the dominant delivery model (Salesforce, Zendesk, ServiceNow) within a decade.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — CIS software market total revenues 1996: $1 billion+ (68% increase over prior year)
- **1997** — Customer support/help desk share of CIS market: Over 60% of CIS market
- **1997** — Silknet venture capital raised: $7.3 million
- **1997** — CIS trend 1: Transition from call tracking to problem resolution and service delivery
- **1997** — CIS trend 2: Customer self-service gains prominence — bypassing call center, 24x7 access, reduced wait times
- **1997** — CIS trend 3: Integration with complementary enterprise-class applications (SFA, field service, manufacturing, logistics, billing)
- **1997** — CIS trend 4: Increasing use of multimedia — images, schematics, video, remote session control to enhance support interactions
- **1997** — eService architecture assessment: Thin-client; Netscape or IE browser; object-oriented ActiveX; call control server; firewall-secured; single central administration site
- **1997** — eService vs. incumbents — key differentiation: Built from ground up for customer (not support agent) as primary end-user; natural language problem description; personalized interface
- **1997** — Client-server CIS weakness for self-service: Not inherently designed for customer self-service; complex navigation; specialized training required; Web front-ends bolted on as afterthought
- **1997** — Thin-client administration advantage: eService administered from single centralized site; latest version auto-downloaded at each session; eliminates client-side upgrade coordination
- **1997** — Silknet market positioning strategy: Category creation: first-mover in pure internet-native CIS; distinct market segment vs. call tracking incumbents
- **1997** — Incumbent CIS vendor dilemma: Must maintain client-server for installed base while developing internet solution; hybrid web front-end strategy inadequate
- **1997** — Aberdeen on internet CIS advantage: Internet-based CIS self-service is only scratching the surface; virtual call centers, peer forums, universal access are the real value
- **1997** — Aberdeen on technology transition: As with all fundamental technology shifts, some suppliers will make transition to Internet, others will not
- **1997** — Aberdeen assessment of 3 successful ISV design tenets: Successful internet CIS ISVs must excel at: 1) Ease-of-Use, 2) Solution/Information Access, 3) Customer Communication
- **1997** — CIS market growth rate: 68% revenue increase from 1995 to 1996; $1B+ total market
- **1997** — eService launch date and first-mover status: Introduced March 10 1997; first viable internet-native CIS; caused 'desperate scramble among competitors to catch-up'
- **1997** — Silknet market leadership: Unless competitors move fast, leadership in this new internet CIS market is Silknet's for the taking
- **1997** — CIS market leader disruption: As software suppliers address new user requirements, new market leaders will emerge; incumbent call-tracking vendors at risk
- **1997** — CIS market size by 2000: Total CIS ISV market reaching $3.4 billion by year 2000
- **2000** — Silknet market leadership — actual outcome: Acquired by Kana Communications for $4.2B in Feb 2000; combined entity served ~450 enterprise clients including eBay
- **2005** — CIS market leader disruption — actual outcome: Salesforce.com (2000), RightNow Technologies (2000), Kana/Silknet established internet CIS dominance; traditional call-tracking vendors (Clarify, Vantive) absorbed or marginalized
- **2000** — CIS market size forecast accuracy: CIS/CRM market exceeded $3.4B estimate; Gartner reported CRM software market at ~$3.7B in 2000, exceeding Aberdeen's forecast
