# Accelerating the Process At Every Step — EMC-Aberdeen Workshop

> Aberdeen sales-acceleration deck prepared for EMC outlining a research-driven program to pull through storage solutions. Positions Pools of Storage and Information Lifecycle Management (ILM) as the CIO strategic agenda, argues the 'opening gun just went off' on ILM and predicts ILM will be the big storage-related market battle of the decade. Maps Q1 Storage Research findings on cost, consolidation, retention policies, regulation, and ILM.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group (Peter S. Kastner) · **Date:** 2004-06-01 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *Early, widely-circulated industry articulation of the ILM thesis and Pools of Storage concept; shaped EMC's 2004-2010 go-to-market and the broader storage industry's data-lifecycle narrative.*
**Prescience:** high — *ILM did become the big storage-related market battle of the decade (EMC, IBM Tivoli, HDS, Symantec/NetBackup, CommVault all framed around ILM through 2010s); vagueness-of-definition prediction also verified.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — emc-stated-goals: Three EMC goals: (a) align EMC with prospect business issues; (b) align prospect executives with EMC solutions; (c) pull through storage solutions via services.
- **2004** — emc-brand-gap: EMC has a strong technology-first brand but is not well known for either business insight or services.
- **2004** — emc-cio-access-gap: Strong brand recognition within CIO's team but limited access to the CIO; sales executives lack business-level insight to be credible with LOB executives.
- **2004** — policy-driver-1: Top policy driver: Mandate to lower overall costs.
- **2004** — policy-driver-2: Driver 2: Consolidation within the datacenter.
- **2004** — policy-driver-3: Driver 3: Improving business value of IT.
- **2004** — policy-driver-4: Driver 4: Consolidation of datacenters.
- **2004** — policy-driver-5: Driver 5: Changing data retention policies to retain more information.
- **2004** — policy-driver-6: Driver 6: Staging data on devices based on an information lifecycle plan.
- **2004** — policy-driver-7: Driver 7: Regulation or compliance.
- **2004** — acq-importance-top: Top acquisition-importance attributes: improved backup/restore times; better I/O throughput per drive; improved MTBF; lower cost per GB; improved TCO.
- **2004** — pools-familiarity: 3/4 of respondents moderately-to-very familiar with the concept of Pools of Storage.
- **2004** — pools-adoption-gap: But market is not yet buying in pools.
- **2004** — no-fixed-content-share: 1/3 of respondents believe they have no fixed-content or static data — a drag on the midline pool.
- **2004** — ilm-great-new-idea: ILM is a classic 'Great New Idea' with all the challenges and opportunities.
- **2004** — ilm-timeframe-decade: ILM Timeframe: the next decade (analogous to systems management in the 1990s).
- **2004** — ilm-timeframe-outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2004** — ilm-decade-battle-prediction: ILM will come to be the big storage-related market battle of the decade — whether called ILM or not.
- **2004** — ilm-decade-battle-outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2004** — ilm-no-winners-yet: Winners: None yet. Opening gun just went off. EMC making waves.
- **2004** — ilm-definition-vagueness: ILM definition is too vague, but vagueness gives ability to morph the concept over time as products mature.
- **2004** — ilm-product-gap: Big concept short on solutions — no one can deliver on the ILM vision anytime soon.
- **2004** — ilm-business-value-risk: ILM looks like 'more IT infrastructure' with few tangible business benefits to non-IT execs — likely cause of impeded growth in ILM market.
- **2004** — ilm-immediate-services-opportunity: Immediate services opportunity around ILM despite long product roadmap.
- **2004** — cio-info-access-storage-accelerate: Info access & storage requirements accelerate; new data capture & storage costs create significant budget trade-offs.
