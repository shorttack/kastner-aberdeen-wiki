# How To Represent Aberdeen and Yourself (Twelve Vital Hours, Session 2)

> Session 2 of Aberdeen Group's Twelve Vital Hours new-hire orientation program, delivered in August 2000 by the three founding partners: John Logan, Thomas Willmott, and Peter Kastner. Covers how analysts introduce Aberdeen to supplier executives, conduct supplier briefings, follow up with prospects, and give press interviews. The PDF and DOCX are equivalent versions of the same August 2000 training deck; both are preserved as twin source artifacts. Explicitly states Aberdeen ownership structure: primarily held by three partners (Logan, Willmott, Kastner) plus staff and 1998 private capital.

- **2000** — Core objective: describe Aberdeen in under 5 minutes: Describe Aberdeen in less than 5 minutes, without slides, to a relatively unknowledgeable audience
- **2000** — Aberdeen ownership structure (FAQ): Primarily held by three partners (Logan, Willmott, Kastner) and other members; private capital obtained in 1998
- **2000** — Aberdeen staff count and growth rate (FAQ): Over 50 analysts on staff; growing approximately 50% per year
- **2000** — Aberdeen founding rationale (FAQ): Founders wanted a name implying IT as tool for business advantage, not domain of high priests of mainframe computing
- **2000** — Aberdeen age as of 2000 (FAQ): Founded over 12 years ago (implies founding ~1988)
- **2000** — Aberdeen competitive differentiation vs. Gartner/Meta/Forrester/IDC: Unique by strength of 1-on-1 consulting and analytical capabilities; rarely compete directly against them for a client contract
- **2000** — Aberdeen brochure policy: Has never used brochures; business comes from word-of-mouth; web site is living brochure
- **2000** — Aberdeen matrix research model vs. stovepiped competitors: Multiple structured practice areas; analysts share findings at Friday staff meetings to build Aberdeen research consensus
- **2000** — Friday staff meeting purpose: Analysts share findings from practice areas at Friday staff meetings to build Aberdeen research consensus
- **2000** — Profile vs. Viewpoint vs. Impact distinction (introduction context): Profile: objective review of supplier go-to-market strategy; Viewpoint: analyst opinion; Impact: market-effect synopsis
- **2000** — Supplier audience definition: Target audience: supplier executives who do not yet know Aberdeen or whose knowledge comes from intermediaries (PR firms, press quotes)
- **2000** — Press interview style principle: vivid imagery: This is the start of a nuclear war between IBM and DEC
- **2000** — Trade weekly deadline pattern: Trade weeklies have a noon Thursday deadline; expect calls on Wednesday; product announcements are typically on Tuesdays
- **2000** — NDA ethics principle: Aberdeen honors all NDAs — written or verbal; do not break the trust of others even in your enthusiasm
- **2000** — Press bias concern: The reporters are frequently looking for Aberdeen bias — especially if your analysis differs from theirs
- **2000** — Pay-for-play avoidance principle: Never say or imply that Aberdeen will write positive comments if a supplier becomes a client — that's extortion
- **2000** — Press publications that rarely quote analysts: Investors Business Daily does not like to quote individual analysts; Business Week quotes analysts on a relatively rare basis
- **2000** — Most popular press story format: Report the announcement; quote analyst opinion; quote customer impact; summarize editor opinion
- **2000** — Being burned by the press: Experienced Aberdeen analysts have been burned: positive and negative analysis, only negative printed; quotes taken out of context; confidential info printed
- **2000** — Supplier briefing golden rule: analyst is in charge: The analyst is in charge of the meeting; the supplier gave them 1-2 hours of valuable analyst time
- **2000** — Supplier briefing golden rule: session interactivity: Keep the session interactive; base comments on 'Aberdeen research shows...' where possible
- **2000** — Supplier briefing: asking for three key takeaways: Ask at any time: What are the three major points you want me to take away from this meeting?
- **2000** — Kill the Message Not the Messenger principle: Do not assume the presenter prepared the presentation; politely but pointedly state the Aberdeen view; use company not 'you' for negative comments
- **2000** — Profile proposal follow-up process: Go to Aberdeen intranet; modify proposal; review with manager; send with copy to Aberdeen sales; follow up by phone for verbal acceptance/rejection
