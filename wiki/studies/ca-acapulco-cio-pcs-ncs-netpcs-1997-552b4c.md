# PCs, NCs, and NetPCs — Where Do We Go From Here? (CA Acapulco CIO Conference 1997)

> 'PCs, NCs, and NetPCs — Where Do We Go From Here?' presentation by Peter S. Kastner to the Computer Associates Acapulco CIO Conference in May 1997. Frames the late-1990s desktop dilemma between Microsoft/Intel (Wintel PC), Oracle/Sun (Network Computer / NC), and the Microsoft/Intel NetPC compromise. Analyzes desktop business drivers (intranets, extranets, JIT supply chain, globalization) and argues where each client architecture fits best.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner · **Date:** 1997-05-01 · **Type:** employer-record
**Importance:** high — *Captures the late-1990s desktop-architecture debate at its peak, delivered to the CA CIO audience the year Oracle/Sun first shipped the NC and Microsoft/Intel responded with NetPC.*
**Prescience:** high — *Kastner framed the NC/NetPC debate correctly: NC and NetPC did not displace the fat-client PC in general enterprise desktop use; the arguments he highlighted (TCO, centralization, thin-client economics) re-emerged successfully a decade later as VDI, Chromebooks, and web-SaaS.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — Presentation agenda: Desktop business drivers; PC/NC/NetPC choice dilemma; Microsoft/Intel approach; Network Computing approach; where each architecture is best suited.
- **1997** — Desktop business drivers: Expanded internal communications (intranets); expanded external communications (extranets); JIT supply chain; globalization eliminating distances.
- **1997** — End-user decision-making model: Analyze -> Decide -> Transact -> Report framework taught as the new desktop workflow model.
- **1997** — Wintel PC continuing dominance: Kastner's implicit framing: Wintel PC remains the default for general enterprise desktop use; NC/NetPC fit limited use cases.
- **1997** — NC limited enterprise uptake: Network Computer suited for narrow task-worker / kiosk use cases; unlikely to displace fat-client PC for knowledge workers.
- **1997** — NetPC limited uptake: NetPC as Microsoft/Intel compromise between PC and NC unlikely to establish a durable category.
- **2026** — Wintel PC outcome: Wintel PC remained the dominant enterprise desktop architecture through the 2000s; was later complemented (not replaced) by thin-client/VDI, Chromebooks, and web-SaaS — vindicating Kastner's 1997 framing over the decade.
- **2026** — NC outcome: Network Computer failed as a commercial category; Oracle/Sun's NC effort wound down by 2000. Kastner prediction verified.
- **2026** — NetPC outcome: NetPC failed to establish a durable commercial category; Microsoft and Intel quietly abandoned NetPC branding by 1999. Kastner prediction verified.
- **1997** — Intranet as desktop driver: Intranets framed as a primary desktop investment driver — consistent with actual enterprise intranet adoption 1997-2003.
