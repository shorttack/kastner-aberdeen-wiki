# Aberdeen Group Assessment of Unicenter and Jasmine for Computer Associates

> Letter-form report from Peter S. Kastner (Group Vice President, Aberdeen Group) to David M. Hood (Director of Analyst Relations, Computer Associates) following a two-day analyst roundtable evaluation of CA's Unicenter and Jasmine products. Kastner delivers pointed positioning recommendations to maximize 1997 revenue, including brand-led advertising, ISV instrumentation, a VAR program, non-traditional environments, a national fault-response center, and competitive collateral against Tivoli. The letter also proposes a broad Aberdeen-CA consulting engagement.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner · **Date:** 1996-12-01 · **Type:** white-paper
**Importance:** high — *First-person Aberdeen-to-CA strategy letter capturing a pivotal 1996 moment for Unicenter and Jasmine when CA still held dominant share in enterprise systems management and Tivoli was ascendant under new IBM ownership.*
**Prescience:** medium — *Strong prediction that Tivoli's 1996 product set would not be IBM's product three years later is partially verified — IBM Tivoli was repeatedly rearchitected and eventually subsumed into broader IBM portfolios.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1996** — market-share-claim: dominant market share, largest installed base, impressive/leading technology
- **1997** — branding-stage: 'Ask for the #1 product by name' branding stage
- **1997** — advertising-venues: business-executive ads in Business Week, Forbes
- **1997** — core-message-pillars: Measure, Secure, Adapt, Manage
- **1997** — isv-instrumentation-program: instrument name-brand first-tier business apps for Unicenter management
- **1997** — var-channel-build: aggressive worldwide VAR program for lower/middle-tier markets
- **1997** — national-fault-response-center: establish a partner-run national fault-response center for small companies
- **1997** — customer-success-stories: invest modestly to produce references and success stories
- **1997** — non-traditional-environments: target management of office buildings, shop-floor robots, PBX, routers
- **1996** — competitive-white-paper: CA has no competitive white paper to hand to analysts
- **1996** — tivoli-product-continuity: 'We are unconvinced that Tivoli's product as now constituted will be IBM's product of three years from now'
- **1999** — tivoli-product-continuity: Tivoli TME was repeatedly rearchitected post-1996 IBM acquisition; rebranded through multiple generations (TME 10, Tivoli Enterprise, etc.)
- **1996** — gerstner-statement: 'not to buy from a company whose initials are Computer Associates'
- **1997** — cost-of-ownership-study: commission a TCO study of Unicenter vs selected competitors
- **1996** — consulting-engagement-pitch: Aberdeen consulting proposals for high-level market messages, white papers, CEO/CIO speeches, partner training, go-to-market consulting, case studies
- **1996** — enterprise-scope: can tie computing resources together — past, present, and future
- **1997** — cio-audience: CIOs hold service contracts with user orgs; Unicenter can raise service levels, scale, cut costs
- **1997** — smb-support-model: new support model for unserved small companies via partners
