# What Have We Learned From the Tandem and Datapoint Billings and Bookings Blunders? Or, Did Wall Street Make Them Do It?

> Executive Viewpoint #2 examines the structural pressures that led to the billing and booking accounting irregularities at Tandem Computers and Datapoint Corporation. Casale argues that Wall Street's quarterly earnings treadmill—where pension sponsors rank managers quarterly, managers demand near-term corporate earnings, and corporations inflate results to protect their stock—creates systemic pressure on hypergrowth companies to smooth or accelerate reported earnings. The newsletter introduces the fictional composite firm Fast Track Incorporated (FTI) to personify the dynamics of hypergrowth under investor scrutiny.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1983** — Quarterly treadmill — structural description: Pension sponsors rank managers quarterly; a few bottom-quartile quarters means dismissal. Managers demand near-term earnings. Corporations inflate prospects to stay in portfolios.
- **1983** — Hypergrowth P/E amplification risk: As P/E multiples soar, investor expectations rise proportionally; any miss causes a disproportionate stock crash that lingers in 'penalty box' for multiple quarters.
- **1982** — Tandem Computers — billing/booking irregularity: Fiscal 1982 results being restated downward due to booking and billing practices.
- **1982** — Datapoint Corporation — booking irregularity: Earlier billing and booking irregularities; compensation plan heavily skewed to bookings rather than shipments or customer acceptance.
- **1983** — FTI revenue range — hypergrowth context: $250M to $500M annual revenue (quarter-billion to half-billion dollar sales level)
- **1983** — Second treadmill — analyst escalation spiral: Successful earnings smoothing by CFO leads analysts to take smooth earnings for granted; estimates rise; CFO warns estimates are not achievable; rubber band eventually snaps.
- **1983** — Options for hypergrowth firms to escape treadmill: Two escape paths: (1) run even faster — grow through the pressure; (2) settle back into moderate-speed lane matching industry growth.
- **1983** — Analyst incentive structure — quarterly performance ranking: Sell-side analysts who discover a stock early face competitive pressure from later-arriving analysts who escalate estimates to differentiate their recommendations.
- **1983** — Prediction: Tandem-type earnings glitches are inevitable for hypergrowth firms: Earnings glitches for hypergrowth companies are inevitable; investors compete to short-sell at the top.

- **1983** — IBM competitive threat framing: Analysts perpetually worry whether IBM or some other colossus will 'eat FTI's lunch'; this worry drives intensive quarterly questioning of management.
