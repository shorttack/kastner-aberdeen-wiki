# Uses and Abuses of the Corporate Hockey Stick — Or Is This Any Way to Run an Industry?

> Executive Viewpoint #3 introduces the 'hockey stick' concept — the skewed end-of-period concentration of bookings, billings, and shipments common to fast-growing technology companies. Casale argues that the quarterly hockey stick is structurally superior to the annual cycle for managing high-tech operations and examines two management approaches (sales force management via 'stick' and factory management via 'carrot') for moderating the pattern. The newsletter analyzes how Datapoint's booking-oriented compensation plan and Tandem's booking-to-revenue disconnect exemplify the pathological form of the hockey stick.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1983** — Hockey stick definition: Bookings, billings, and shipping curves skewed toward end of operating period; shape resembles a hockey stick. Pattern exists across industries including commuters, fast food, agriculture, and investment management.
- **1983** — Shift from annual to quarterly operating cycle — historical context: 1950s: corporate America on annual cycles; quarterly earnings estimates and portfolio measurements were 'barely dreamed of'. Shift to quarterly competitively imposed on the computer industry.
- **1983** — Four small hockey sticks vs. one large — quarterly cycle advantages: Quarterly cycle superior to annual: (1) salesperson given a quarter closes faster than given a year; (2) factory flow smoother with 4 peak cycles vs. 1; (3) management gets more accurate quarterly business outlook; (4) improves cash flow and reduces…
- **1983** — Conditions required for flat (pancake) operations: Flat booking/shipping curve works only if: (1) monopoly product; (2) demand greatly exceeds supply; (3) product life measured in years; (4) control of raw material supply; (5) labor surplus at low wages. These conditions are exceptions in high-tech.
- **1983** — Two methods to flatten the hockey stick: Method 1 (Stick): Manage salesforce to optimize factory flow; goal is low-cost production. Method 2 (Carrot): Manage factory to maximize near-term sales; goal is market share leadership. Both are variants on the same theme.
- **1983** — Pitfalls of downsizing for backlog building (stick approach): Risks: (1) loss of market share as competitors deliver what you forego; (2) customer satisfaction slips if deliveries stretch; (3) newly announced competitive product can melt backlog and devalue WIP/finished goods inventories.
- **1982** — Datapoint — booking-skewed compensation plan failure: Datapoint's compensation plan until recently was heavily skewed towards bookings; resulted in large booking numbers that did not convert to shipments or revenues.
- **1975** — Data General — payment-skewed compensation plan: In early years: compensation skewed to customer payment; improved receivables but 'turned salesmen into dunning agents'; first months of quarter spent collecting rather than selling.
- **1982** — Tandem — booked shipments not resulting in revenues: Even booked shipments did not always result in immediate revenues; fiscal 1982 results restated downward.
- **1983** — Optimal compensation plan — continuous tweaking: Perfect compensation plan does not exist; changing market conditions for products and salespeople prevent it. Plans must be continuously tweaked with special incentives for early collection, monthly performance, and specific products.
- **1982** — Atari/Warner — annual hockey stick investment magnitude: Annual hockey stick: last few weeks of Q4 determine the entire year. Warner Communications Atari Division recent debacle cited as investment-magnitude example.
- **1983** — Customer buying cycle drives compensation plan length: Monthly compensation plans work only if customer buying cycle is roughly same length; 'to a very large extent, the customer drives the plan.'
- **1983** — Investment relevance — daily vs. weekly vs. quarterly cycles: Daily cycles give investors updated information every day; weekly (e.g., money supply statistics) 52 data points per year; quarterly cycle imposes peculiar accounting implications specific to tech sector.
- **1983** — Prediction: booking/billing practices at Tandem will continue to generate controversy: Fiscal 1982 results at Tandem being restated downwards; further analysis of operating rationale behind events at Tandem and Datapoint to follow in EVP-4.
- **1983** — Prediction: booking/billing practices at Tandem will continue to generate controversy: [UNVERIFIED]
- **1983** — Stock prices at time of publication (Jan 5 1983): Data General: $40; Datapoint: $18; Tandem: $24.125
- **1983** — Hockey stick universality across industries: Pattern appears in: commuter traffic (2 per day), McDonald's lunch/dinner rushes, quarter-end portfolio window dressing, agricultural harvest cycles. Hockey stick is a universal human organizational phenomenon.
- **1983** — Accounting implications of quarterly cycle — teaser for EVP-4: The quarterly cycle in the computer industry has peculiar aspects related to accounting practices; to be analyzed in next two Executive Viewpoints.
