# Hypergrowth Balance Sheets - Neglected Stepchildren?

> EVP #5 in the Executive Viewpoint series analyzes balance sheet dynamics of a composite hypergrowth tech company (FTI), focusing on accounts receivable DSO expansion, inventory valuation asymmetry, and the distorting effect of end-of-quarter shipment concentration. Casale argues that apparent receivables deterioration is a symptom of loose operational cost control rather than credit quality issues, and that balance sheets are systematically underanalyzed by institutional investors focused on P/E ratios.

**Author:** Charles T. Casale · **Date:** 1983-01-07 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *First systematic quantitative treatment of hypergrowth balance sheet distortions in tech-sector investment research; part of a pioneering five-issue series on hypergrowth dynamics that preceded mainstream sell-side coverage of these phenomena.*
**Prescience:** high — *Casale's core prediction—that apparent receivables deterioration masks underlying cost-control problems and will not resolve without operational fixes—correctly describes the pattern observed in numerous subsequent tech-sector earnings restatements and write-downs.*
