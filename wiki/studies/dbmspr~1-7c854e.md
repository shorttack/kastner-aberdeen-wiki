# Data Management: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary on the Database Management Systems (DBMS) market, covering relational, object-relational, object, embedded, and mobile DBMS categories. The report surveys major vendors including Oracle, IBM, Informix, Sybase, Microsoft, and Computer Associates, assesses turbulent 1997 market dynamics including revenue stagnation among major vendors, and forecasts the impact of Windows NT, packaged applications, commoditization, and Internet technologies on DBMS market segmentation. Aberdeen predicts continued Oracle dominance on Unix, Microsoft SQL Server growth on NT, and emerging opportunities in business intelligence, mobile user, and Web content management segments.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1998-05-01 · **Type:** other-research
**Importance:** medium — *Comprehensive 1998 snapshot of the DBMS market at a pivotal inflection point when Oracle, Informix, and Sybase faced revenue pressure and Microsoft SQL Server began its ascent; catalogs 25+ vendors across RDBMS, ORDBMS, ODBMS, embedded, and mobile categories.*
**Prescience:** high — *Aberdeen's Oracle/Unix dominance prediction proved accurate; SQL Server 7.0 enterprise push prediction proved accurate; commoditization forecast proved correct; ORDBMS dominance over pure ODBMS proved correct; mobile/embedded DBMS growth as niche proved accurate.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1997** — DBMS market condition: Turbulent; Oracle/Informix/Sybase revenues stalled or slumped
- **1998** — DBMS market growth outlook: Moderate pace overall; Unix enterprise flat; newer segments exploding
- **1997** — Object DBMS (pure) revenues: <$200 million
- **1998** — ORDBMS share of new DBMS revenue: >50% of new DBMS revenue (first year ORDBMS > pure RDBMS)
- **1998** — Oracle8 projected license revenues: ~$2 billion 1998 license revenues
- **1998** — Oracle Unix enterprise market outlook: Will continue to dominate Unix enterprise but face commoditization pressure
- **1997** — SQL Server revenue growth rate: >50% annual revenue growth
- **1998** — SQL Server 7.0 significance: May be the biggest DBMS story of 1998
- **1997** — Informix market situation: Revenue restatement and lowering in 1997; refocused strategy
- **1998** — Informix competitive outlook: Can capture high-end OLTP/BI/ERP but will struggle in commoditized NT segments
- **1998** — Sybase competitive situation: Suffering from marketplace dynamics despite good product; mobile and tools products driving fortunes
- **1998** — IBM DB2 market outlook: Will expand overall share particularly on IBM servers; will emerge as BI and e-commerce leader
- **1997** — CA Jasmine market position: First enterprise ODBMS from major vendor; off to good start but ahead of mass market
- **1998** — Windows NT DBMS market growth: Explosive growth continuing; branch offices/departments/SMB scaling NT
- **1998** — Windows NT enterprise scalability forecast: NT should scale sufficiently for many enterprise production apps within 2-3 years
- **1998** — DBMS commoditization drivers: Windows NT growth, packaged applications model, mass-deployment sales
- **1998** — DBMS services/consulting revenue trend: Consulting/services business outpacing software licensing revenues
- **1998** — Packaged application influence on DBMS buying: Packaged applications (SAP/Baan/PeopleSoft/Oracle) increasingly drive DBMS purchasing
- **1998** — Object-Relational vs. pure Object DBMS split: IBM/Oracle/Informix advocate ORDBMS; Microsoft/CA/Sybase advocate separate relational+object databases
- **1998** — Java DBMS support status: Java support has become a required DBMS capability
- **1998** — Business intelligence / data warehousing DBMS segment: Very strong unit sales and revenues; one of hottest DBMS sub-segments
- **1996** — RDBMS + ORDBMS combined new revenue share: >80% of all new DBMS revenues
- **1998** — DBMS market segmentation trend: Market subdividing into: enterprise production / enterprise DSS / branch office / SMB / single PC / thin client / mobile / PCD
- **1998** — Future DBMS market consolidation: Aberdeen does NOT expect near-term consolidation among major vendors; expects major vendors to buy small start-ups
- **1998** — Mobile/PCD DBMS market characterization: Significant new niche market; very strong unit sales growth
