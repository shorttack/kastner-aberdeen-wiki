# Digital Consumer Electronics and Information Technology — Staff Observations

> Internal Aberdeen memo on the Digital Consumer Electronics and Information Technology (DCEIT) sector at a Q2-2002 inflection: Intel's $400M revenue shortfall, aggressive CPU price cuts, and AMD's 22% share gains driving PC OEM repositioning. Documents retail observations from Caroline Kastner's tracking research and catalogs vendor-specific line moves at Gateway and Dell ahead of the 2002 back-to-school season.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — Q2 2002 revenue shortfall announcement: $400M shortfall; Europe new soft spot
- **2002** — Market reaction: Semiconductor-sector sell-off; semis viewed as leading tech out of two-year recession
- **2002** — Pricing action: Aggressive desktop and laptop chip price cuts (two weeks prior to memo)
- **2002** — Price-cut driver — manufacturing: 300mm wafers at 0.13 micron yield more chips at much lower cost per chip
- **2002** — AMD market share: 22% of Intel-compatible microprocessor market
- **2002** — Competitive positioning: At higher clock speeds (2.566 GHz) P4 outperforms AMD; tech advantage vs AMD price advantage
- **2002** — Seasonal price-cut rationale: Prep for Q3 back-to-school and Q4 Christmas buying seasons
- **2002** — Retail research attribution: Aberdeen's retail PC tracking research (Caroline Kastner) documented month-over-month changes
- **2002** — More hardware per dollar: Faster microprocessors due to Intel price reductions
- **2002** — Disk inventory dynamics: Lower HDD costs due to IBM inventory dumping
- **2002** — CPU/memory tradeoff: As CPU speeds increase, memory and HDD GB offered decreases at same price point
- **2002** — Feature substitution: Upgraded optical drives and better monitors compensate for lower memory
- **2002** — Retailer offer stability: Offers unchanged in past three weeks; many already rock bottom
- **2002** — Q2 PC unit decline: PC sales down as much as 22% this quarter
- **2002** — Free shipping bundles: $60-$110 free shipping on popular models at HP, Sony, Dell, Gateway, Compaq, eMachines
- **2002** — Recent promo shift: Gateway 80GB HDD upgrade early May -> DVD-ROM/CD-RW upgrades or $100 mail-in rebate later
- **2002** — Bundle pivot: HP and Compaq formerly emphasized CPUs alone; now pushing PC + monitor + sometimes printer bundles
- **2002** — Gateway 300S/300X refresh: 1.3 GHz Celeron -> 1.7 GHz Celeron at same price
- **2002** — Gateway P4 boost: 2.0A GHz -> 2.26 GHz for same cost
- **2002** — Dell 4400 -> 4500s refresh: Faster P4; less HDD GB
- **2002** — Dell 4500s mid/high soup-up: Launch 1.7 GHz Celeron -> P4 1.8/2.0 GHz same price
- **2002** — Market restart post price cuts: Price cuts + seasonal setup will move PCs through H2 2002
- **2003** — P4 mainstreaming follow-through: P4 mainstreaming confirmed: Pentium 4 with HT became the dominant Intel desktop SKU through 2003-2005, capturing >70% of Intel desktop shipments until Core 2 Duo replaced the NetBurst architecture in July 2006.
