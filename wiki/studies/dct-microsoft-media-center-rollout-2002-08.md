# Microsoft Rolls Out Media Center

> News analysis of Microsoft's announcement of Windows XP Media Center Edition — a living-room PC variant combining TV tuner, hardware video encoder, and infrared remote, first demoed at CES 2002 under the 'Freestyle' codename. HP, NEC, and Samsung are the launch OEMs; Kastner frames the offering as the strongest PC-in-the-living-room push to date while noting Windows task-scheduling vulnerabilities and competition from SonicBlue and TiVo.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — Product announcement: Windows XP Media Center Edition — sold only via selected OEMs
- **2002** — Debut venue: First shown by Bill Gates at CES January 2002 under codename 'Freestyle'
- **2002** — Required hardware: Infrared remote, TV tuner, hardware video encoder
- **2002** — Tuner flexibility: Terrestrial broadcast, cable, or satellite TV — or combinations
- **2002** — Storage advantage: Few megabits-per-second compression enables hundreds of hours of TV storage
- **2002** — UX heritage: UI concepts adapted from UltimateTV — simplified desktop, list of TV/music/photos
- **2002** — Launch OEMs: HP, NEC, Samsung
- **2002** — Market-entry timing: Media Center PCs to reach market in time for Christmas season 2002
- **2002** — Strategic framing: Not first PC-in-living-room attempt, but 'strongest push to date'
- **2002** — Platform critique: Windows vulnerable to task-scheduling conflicts — critical interrupts unserviced for seconds
- **2002** — Adequacy caveat: May be adequate if users avoid aftermarket apps and peripherals
