# Personal Information Technology — Aberdeen Practice Launch

> Program-launch description of Aberdeen Group's Personal Information Technology practice, established by Peter Kastner in 2001. Articulates the nine market paradoxes driving the practice (PC commoditization, digital-device convergence, home-network emergence, 3G wireless, digital TV transition, in-home services needs), catalogs service offerings, technology segments, strategic market questions, key market trends, and the supplier coverage list (Microsoft, Intel, AMD, Compaq, Dell, HP, IBM, Sony, Toshiba, Apple, Gateway, Micron, AOL, Epson, Lexmark, Canon, Nvidia, Cirrus Logic).

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2001** — Practice founding: Aberdeen's newest practice area: Personal Information Technology
- **2001** — Market paradox 1: PC commoditization + lowered margins driving share pressure
- **2001** — Market paradox 2: PVRs and MP3 players replacing VCRs and cassette players
- **2001** — Market paradox 3: Office-life extending into personal time via email etc.
- **2001** — Market paradox 4: Digital technology enabling new leisure activities (photo, music, video production)
- **2001** — 'Computer in every home' obsolescence: Market strategic goal obsolete; add-on PCs per family member is new driver
- **2001** — Market paradox 5: Multiple-PC households drive broadband connectivity and home-network demand
- **2001** — Home-server emergence: New PCs such as the home server distinct from enterprise servers
- **2001** — Digital TV and 3G wireless as buying drivers: Drive new waves of consumer buying; existing brands don't have assured share
- **2001** — Technology segment list: PCs, Productivity, SOHO, Microprocessors, Consumer OS, Consumer IT (digital appliances/storage/printing/TV-stereo), Digital leisure, Information services
- **2001** — Service offerings: Custom consulting, market research, education, analysis; Aberdeen Lab product testing
- **2001** — Strategic Q: replacement-market dynamics: What will change today's replacement-only home computer market?
- **2001** — Strategic Q: microprocessor overshoot: Are microprocessor speeds far outdistancing consumer/SOHO needs?
- **2001** — Strategic Q: last-mile: When and how will the last-mile broadband problem be solved in advanced countries?
- **2001** — Windows XP impact: Will Windows XP drive a new burst of high-end PC growth?
- **2001** — WiFi trend: 802.11b wireless rapidly growing to compete with wired home-networking
- **2001** — Xbox challenge: Japan's gaming computers lack traditional OS/app support; can they move up-scale vs Wintel PCs?
- **2001** — Flat-panel pricing milestone: LCD monitors at consumer price point; excellent for desk-space-conscious SOHO
- **2001** — Digital imaging impact: Digital imaging growing fast enough to materially impact traditional photography-based markets
- **2001** — Cross-brand challenge: Enterprise brand does not automatically translate to consumer sales
- **2001** — Supplier coverage list: 18 suppliers tracked: Microsoft, Intel, AMD, Compaq, Dell, HP, IBM, Sony, Toshiba, Apple, Gateway, Micron, AOL, Epson, Lexmark, Canon, Nvidia, Cirrus Logic
- **2001** — Practice intersections: Home Networking (Cohen); Messaging (Gardner); Intel Servers (Manter); Digital Content (Hoch); Internet Appliances (Ro)
- **2001** — Kastner biography: In 2001, Kastner initiated the Personal IT practice — focus on PCs, SOHO, and convergence
