# MGMT MEMO Volume 7, Number 5 — Digital's State of the Company Meeting

> mgmtmemo7_5.html, Summaries of nine executive speeches delivered at Digital Equipment Corporation's May 5, 1988 State of the Company Meeting in Merrimack, NH, attended by over 600 senior managers. The theme was 'One Company, One Strategy, One Message — Leading the Way to Enterprise-Wide Computing,' with major sections on desktop/workstation strategy, transaction processing, DECwindows, database systems, and VAXcluster high availability. This document captures DEC at its revenue peak (~$11B, 120K+ employees) articulating a bold enterprise strategy that proved partially prescient and partially fatal.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1988** — employee-count: 120000+
- **1988** — revenue-growth-rate-FY87: 24%
- **1988** — operating-profit-margin-FY87: 17%
- **1988** — cash-balance-after-buyback: 2.1 billion USD
- **1988** — stock-price-peak: approximately $170/share
- **1988** — revenue-growth-rate-FY88-Q1-Q3: 21%
- **1988** — capital-expenditure-run-rate: 1.5 billion USD/year
- **1988** — pretax-margin-Q1-FY88: 13.5%
- **1988** — corporate-strategy-theme: One Company One Strategy One Message — Leading the Way to Enterprise-Wide Computing
- **1988** — PC-market-dismissal: DEC deliberately chose not to compete in personal computers for the home; positioned this as a strategic strength not weakness
- **1988** — on-market-surveys: Market surveys are a serious danger; customers only want what they have seen; that's not where new ideas come from
- **1988** — vms-vs-unix-positioning: VMS is more modern than Unix — 12 years old vs Unix 19 years old; VMS designed for the whole job to last almost forever
- **1988** — dual-OS-strategy: Sell VMS and Unix (ULTRIX) with equal enthusiasm; they are not equal but serve different purposes
- **1988** — strategic-motivation: Strengthen position relative to IBM and assume leadership role in enterprise-wide information systems
- **1988** — OSI-vs-SNA-networking-outcome: OSI networking standards will replace IBM SNA as enterprise networking standard; SNA described as closed, complex, hierarchical — not meeting real needs
- **1988** — NAS-integration-targets: MS-DOS, OS/2, IBM SNA, Cray supercomputing, and Apple Macintosh named as non-DEC architecture targets for NAS
- **1988** — desktop-computing-market-1991-forecast: 60 billion USD (40% of worldwide computer revenue)
- **1988** — terminal-market-survival: Analysts predicted death of terminals replaced by PCs — that didn't happen; terminals remain preferred for many applications; shipping 500000+ terminals this year
- **1988** — workstation-market-position: DEC became fastest-growing workstation vendor in industry FY1988; surpassed Sun Microsystems growth rate; leaped over Apollo to number two position
- **1988** — commercial-workstation-market-capture: Seven times more commercial computing users than technical users; DEC must win commercial desktop business while keeping technical users
- **1988** — OS2-upgrade-cost-barrier: Converting an IBM PC-AT to run OS/2 may cost as much as $8000 (per Computerworld March 7 issue)
- **1988** — VMS-services-for-MSDOS-market-position: VMS Services for MS-DOS is the leading integration product in the market; won numerous awards
- **1988** — Apple-DEC-joint-development: DEC announced joint development with Apple; bringing Apple desktop systems into DEC enterprise-wide computing environment; 'just one part of our approach'
- **1988** — DECwindows-strategy-four-pillars: Common user interface + common programming interface + X Windows industry standard + quick delivery — four-part workstation software strategy
- **1988** — DECwindows-as-de-facto-standard: As DECwindows becomes a de facto standard... the environment will become that much more productive
