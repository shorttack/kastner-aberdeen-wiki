# Dell: Applying Its Business Value Model to Storage

> This Aberdeen Group vendor profile examines how Dell applies its signature supply chain management and price/performance business model to enterprise storage in 2004. Co-authored by David Hill and Peter Kastner, the report covers the Dell|EMC CX product family (third-generation FC arrays), the new AX100 entry-level SATA SAN, Dell PowerVault NAS solutions, and Dell's software bundling and service integration strategies. Aberdeen concludes that Dell deserves a place at the business storage discussion table across all enterprise sizes.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — Storage budget constraint survey finding: 60% of Aberdeen survey respondents cite managing storage within current budget and headcount constraints as critical success factor
- **2004** — Dell core storage strategy pillars: Three fundamental Dell storage strategies: (1) deliver industry-leading value, (2) drive standards, (3) move high-end functionality downstream
- **2004** — Dell SCM advantage over storage competitors: Superior SCM techniques and ability to quickly pass cost reductions to customer are Dell's key ongoing advantages
- **2004** — Standards bodies Dell participates in: FCLC (Fibre Channel), Serial ATA II, Serial Attached SCSI (SAS), and SNIA — Dell actively driving storage standards
- **2004** — SAS/SATA mixed backplane standardization: Dell driving Serial ATA II and SAS standards to enable mixed-backplane enterprise servers
- **2010** — SAS/SATA mixed backplanes — actual outcome: SAS/SATA mixed backplanes became universal in enterprise servers confirming Dell's standards-push prediction.
- **2004** — Dell|EMC partnership extension: In 2003, Dell and EMC extended original five-year agreement to co-brand storage products for another two years through December 2008
- **2004** — Dell|EMC partnership as strategic foundation: Partnership demonstrates long-term value both companies see from the relationship; expected to continue post-2008
- **2016** — Dell|EMC partnership outcome — actual outcome: Dell acquired EMC Corporation for $67 billion in 2016 — the largest technology acquisition in history. The partnership profiled in this document became full corporate integration.
- **2004** — Dell|EMC CX third generation — performance improvements: CX300: 70% faster than CX200; CX500: 100% faster than CX400; CX700: 25% faster than CX600 — at same price
- **2004** — CX300 upgrade investment protection: When upgrading CX200 to CX300, customer replaces only disk controller not full array; no data migration downtime required
- **2004** — Midline storage as emerging buyer trend: Aberdeen research shows obtaining satisfactory performance at less cost is emerging as a major buyer trend — satisfied by new midline products
- **2004** — Dell|EMC midline ATA integration: Dell|EMC arrays with ATA drives require several FC drives for FLARE OS; rest of drives can be ATA/FC mix
- **2004** — Midline pool use cases in Dell|EMC arrays: Fixed content storage (fast access; lower performance than online) and nearline disk-to-disk backup and continuous data protection
- **2004** — AX100 product concept and origin: Dell conceived AX100 to meet customers' needs for easier-to-use Fibre Channel system at lower price than CX line
- **2004** — AX100 capacity specification: AX100 scales up to 3 TB on SATA drives; wizard-based setup; dual controllers; mirrored cache
- **2004** — AX100 as midline SAN democratization: Large companies with many remote sites and small organizations deterred by SAN complexity and cost; AX100 addresses this
- **2008** — Entry-level SATA SAN democratization — actual outcome: SATA-based entry-level SANs became standard at workgroup/branch level by 2008. AX100 concept validated and expanded by EMC's later CX4 and VNXe product lines.
- **2004** — Dell as storage integrator vs OEM: Viewing Dell as storage integrator (not OEM) explains its efforts to simplify disk array, tape, and software selection
- **2004** — Dell storage market scope claim: Dell's storage team deserves a place at the business discussion table of large, medium-sized, and small companies and government agencies — and workgroups of any size
- **2004** — Dell standards approach as competitive advantage: For customers, Dell's standards orientation makes it easier and less costly to integrate Dell storage into heterogeneous environments
- **2004** — SnapView availability democratization: CX300 and AX100 now have SnapView point-in-time copy capability whereas CX200 did not — functionality moving downstream
- **2004** — PowerVault NAS family overview: PowerVault 775N and 770N (midrange file servers); PowerVault 745N (entry-level alternative to microfiche/optical); Windows Storage Server 2003
- **2004** — Standards OS for NAS vs proprietary: Dell chose Windows Storage Server 2003 over proprietary NAS OS to eliminate IT administrator learning curve
- **2004** — Dell PowerVault tape strategy: Dell standardizes tape automation on DLT derivatives and LTO derivatives; PowerVault 160T uses LTO-2 exclusively; future emphasis on LTO
