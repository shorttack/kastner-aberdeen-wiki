# Whatever Happened to Interactive TV?

> E-Commerce Times reporter Lou Hirsh examines why interactive TV (ITV) failed to materialize as a mass-market category circa 2002. Aberdeen Group chief research officer Peter Kastner argues consumer time and dollars have shifted to broadband Internet and that interactive gaming from Microsoft and Sony will not require a TV set. Kastner predicts ITV capabilities must be built into future TV sets because consumers won't pay extra for set-top hardware on existing TVs.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — Consumer time/dollars shifting to broadband: Consumers' time and dollars have become invested in technologies like high-speed broadband Internet
- **2002** — Gaming displaces ITV need: Companies like Microsoft and Sony are rolling out interactive gaming technologies that will not require a TV set
- **2002** — ITV must be built into TV sets: ITV capabilities will have to be included with future television sets, since many consumers will be unwilling to spend extra dollars on new hardware for existing sets
- **2002** — ITV STB consumer rejection: Right now, people are not going to alter their TVs to fit them for interactive TV functions
