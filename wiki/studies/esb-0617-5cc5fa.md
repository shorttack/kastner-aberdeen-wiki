# Enterprise Service Bus: an SOA Middleware Foundation

> 38-page Aberdeen Group benchmark report sponsored by Fiorano and IBM. Part of 'Next Steps in SOA Series'. ESB as messaging middleware for SOA. Survey findings on ESB adoption patterns across company sizes. Three SOA camps identified: SOA Lite, Enterprise SOA, SOA ERP. 90% of respondents will exit 2006 with SOA experience. ESB adoption concentrated in enterprises >$500M revenue. BEA Systems top primary ESB vendor (57% BIC), IBM WebSphere second (37%), webMethods third. Creates context for EAI-to-ESB transition. Covers hardware server forecasts driven by ESB deployment. Aberdeen PACE framework applied.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2006** — global_it_spending_2006: $1.3 trillion in global enterprise IT spending in 2006
- **2006** — soa_business_value_opportunity: $143 billion/year business value from application maintenance gap between BIC and average organizations (11% of IT budget)
- **2006** — soa_adoption_2006: 90% of survey respondents will exit 2006 with experience in SOA planning, design, and/or programming
- **2006** — large_enterprise_soa_experience: 1/5 of enterprise-size organizations (>$1B) have more than 1 year SOA programming experience vs 1/16 of mid-size
- **2006** — small_company_soa_plans: Almost 80% of small companies have no immediate plans to start SOA programming
- **2006** — esb_deployed_rate: 64% of survey has ESB software deployed
- **2006** — xml_accelerator_deployment: 55% of survey has XML accelerators deployed
- **2006** — registry_deployment_rate: 46% of survey has SOA metadata repository/registry deployed
- **2006** — soa_security_governance_deployment: 36% have SOA security and SOA management and governance deployed
- **2006** — bea_primary_esb_vendor: BEA Systems (AquaLogic): 57% primary ESB vendor for BIC; 14% primary SOA vendor overall
- **2006** — ibm_esb_market_share: IBM WebSphere: 37% primary ESB vendor for BIC; 22% primary SOA vendor overall
- **2006** — webmethods_esb_share: webMethods: 29% primary SOA vendor overall; EAI functional equivalency protecting incumbent position
- **2006** — microsoft_soa_share: Microsoft: 22% primary SOA vendor; 6% primary ESB vendor; dominant only for .Net-only sites
- **2006** — oracle_soa_share: Oracle (Fusion): 18% primary SOA vendor; 8% primary ESB vendor; combined Oracle/SAP 19% indicating SOA ERP as valid category
- **2006** — sap_soa_share: SAP (NetWeaver): 16% primary SOA vendor; 4% primary ESB vendor; 1/6 view SAP as primary SOA vendor
- **2006** — registry_stumbling_block_bic: Creating metadata repository/registry stymied 50% overall and 73% of BIC
- **2006** — scaling_stumbling_block: Scaling to production volumes/reliability/availability: 50% overall, 36% BIC
- **2006** — security_governance_stumbling_block: Establishing ESB security, governance, management: 46% BIC, 36% overall
- **2006** — testing_deployment_stumbling: Testing and deployment stages problematic: 43% overall, 27% BIC
- **2006** — esb_purchase_factor_integration: Ease of integration flexibility with current/planned applications: 43% Very Important (top factor)
- **2006** — esb_purchase_factor_lifecycle: ESB business process control, change, management, governance, lifecycle features: 29% Very Important, 35% Somewhat Important
- **2006** — composite_app_creation: 3/5 (61% BIC, 36% all) creating composite applications supporting web services
- **2006** — four_plus_projects_2006: 46% will deliver more than 3 major application integration projects using SOA in 2006 (BIC) vs 33% overall
- **2006** — esb_server_forecast: ESB deployments to drive 3700-5500 edge application servers and 2700-4500 datacenter servers; approximately 0.15-0.25% of projected 4 million servers in 2006
- **2006** — large_enterprise_esb_adoption: 5/6 of large enterprises (>$1B) have already installed ESB technology; 10% will install in 2006
