# The Great Rebate Runaround

> BusinessWeek cover story (Dec 5 2005, Brian Grow with Rishi Chhatwal) that became the canonical exposé of the U.S. mail-in rebate industry. Peter S. Kastner, then director of consulting firm Vericours Inc., provides the pivotal 40% statistic: 'fully 40% of all rebates never get redeemed because consumers fail to apply for them or their applications are rejected,' translating into more than $2B of extra revenue per year for retailers and suppliers. Aberdeen's Paula Rosenblum adds: 'anything less than 100% redemption is free money.' The article details industry lingo (breakage, slippage), TCA's 10-35% expected redemption rates, TiVo's $5M rebate-expense reduction from 50,000 of 104,000 subscribers failing to redeem, Massachusetts suing Young America for $43M in uncashed checks, NY AG Spitzer settling with Samsung for $200K over apartment-address rules, California SB (Figueroa) vetoed by Gov. Schwarzenegger, consumer complaints to BBB tripled 2001-2004 (964 to 3,641), and Best Buy / Staples transitions away from mail-in rebates. Kastner's Vericours affiliation here (not Aberdeen) is notable — this is his post-Aberdeen consulting/advisory period.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2005** — Kastner 40% non-redemption estimate: Peter S. Kastner, director of consulting firm Vericours Inc., estimates fully 40% of all rebates never get redeemed because consumers fail to apply for them or their applications are rejected — translating into more than $2 billion extra revenue for…
- **2005** — Paula Rosenblum free-money framing: 'The game is obviously that anything less than 100 percent redemption is free money' — Paula Rosenblum, Aberdeen Group director of retail research
- **2005** — Rebate market scale estimates: ~1/3 of computer gear and >20% of digital cameras/camcorders/LCD TVs sold with rebates; ~400M rebates/year; $6B total face value; Staples alone $3.5M/week
- **2005** — TiVo rebate-expense reduction: 50,000 of 104,000 new subscribers (48%) failed to redeem mail-in offers, reducing expected rebate expense by $5M and cutting Q1 loss to $857K from $9.1M prior-year
- **2005** — TCA published redemption-rate expectations: TCA Rebate Redemption Guide cited 10% redemption for $10-on-$100 rebates and 35% for $50-on-$200, implying clients could gain 20% higher unredeemed rates than competitors
- **2005** — BBB rebate complaints tripled 2001-2004: BBB complaints grew from 964 (2001) to 3,641 (2004) — a 3.78x increase
- **2005** — NY AG settles Samsung apartment-address case: October 2005: NY AG Eliot Spitzer settled with Samsung Electronics America for $200,000 payment to 4,100 consumers denied rebates due to apartment-address form design
- **2005** — MA suit vs Young America for audit: Nov 7 2005: Massachusetts officials sued Young America Corp demanding audit of $43M in uncashed rebate checks (1995-mid-2002)
- **2005** — Best Buy 2-year mail-in-rebate phase-out: Best Buy announced in this article it plans to phase out mail-in rebates within 2 years
- **2004** — Staples EasyRebates launch: Staples (Framingham MA) switched to online EasyRebates system 1 year before article, cutting wait 10wks to 4wks and dropping rebate complaints 25%
- **2008** — Best Buy eliminates mail-in rebates: Best Buy eliminated mail-in rebates effective early 2008, fulfilling the 2-year phase-out pledge made in this 2005 article
- **2007** — FTC rebate-advertising guidance: FTC increased enforcement on rebate advertising; staff guidance on 'after rebate' pricing disclosure followed CT AG-style investigations nationally 2006-2010
- **2005** — Mail-in rebates in decline: Article frames mail-in rebates as under regulator pressure and facing retailer retreat; implicit prediction of decline
- **2010** — Mail-in rebates largely eliminated from CE retail: By 2010 most major US CE retailers had eliminated traditional mail-in rebates in favor of instant-rebate pricing or online-claim systems; TCA/Parago/Young America rebate-processor sector contracted substantially 2008-2014
