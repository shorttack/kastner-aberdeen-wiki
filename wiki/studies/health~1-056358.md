# Healthcare: 1998 Industry Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 survey of the Healthcare Information Systems (HIS) market, covering integrated HIS vendors, independent software vendors, professional services firms, and systems suppliers. The report maps the three application tiers—business, clinical, and management information—and assesses the impact of managed care consolidation, client-server adoption, Internet/intranet deployment, and Year 2000 compliance on healthcare IT buyers. Brief vendor profiles are provided for fifteen named companies.

**Author:** Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 1998-05-01 · **Type:** other-research

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — HIS market primary driver: Mandate to reduce costs while delivering quality care; managed care consolidation accelerating competition
- **1998** — Client-server adoption in HIS: Advances in client-server technology allowing cost-effective HIS implementation; merging with legacy data access
- **1998** — HIS custom development trend: Majority of HIS are custom-developed; trend shifting toward packaged applications tailored to provider needs
- **1998** — IT budget as percentage of operating budget: Typical healthcare provider IT budget ~4% of annual operating budget; majority of IT departments projecting staffing increases
- **1998** — EMR adoption level: Most EMR solutions operate on departmental level vs. enterprise-wide; some enterprise-wide versions emerging; 'who owns the data' unresolved
- **1998** — Physician Internet usage: Aberdeen estimates over 70% of all physicians use the Internet for research
- **1998** — Online health plan claims processing timeline: Within 12-18 months, forward-looking health plans will provide online functionality for claims processing and payment
- **1998** — Enterprise EMR future state: Single electronic patient record shared across the enterprise will someday emerge as an operational and technical reality
- **1998** — Speech recognition maturity status: Significant progress made by suppliers with discipline-specific continuous speech dictation; IBM MedSpeak for Radiology cited
- **1998** — Speech recognition adoption outlook: Speech recognition technologies will soon become more commonplace in healthcare
- **1998** — Wireless technology importance: Advances in wireless technologies will become increasingly critical communication medium across the industry
- **1998** — HIS supplier consolidation: Like the industry it serves, HIS supplier groups are consolidating; start-up vendors using new technologies are emerging
- **1998** — Cerner market position: Proven leader in HIS; $245M revenue; strong growing services organization; identified integration as significant revenue opportunity
- **1998** — HBOC market position: 5500+ employees; 1997 revenues >$1.2B; comprehensive suite for hospitals, physician offices, home health, managed care; partnerships and acquisitions strategy
- **1998** — IDX market position: Acquired Phamis July 1997; combined revenues >$251M; 1400 customers; historically strong in ambulatory care; merging inpatient/outpatient strategy
- **1998** — MEDITECH market position: 1200+ customers; 1997 software license revenues $193M; founded 1969; transitioned from proprietary minicomputer/mainframe to distributed environments
- **1998** — SMS market position: 3000+ customers worldwide; 1997 revenues >$779M; 25+ year presence; Physician Enterprise Manager released Feb 1998; equity stake in Visteon
- **1998** — Microsoft HIS role and trajectory: Important HIS player; NT Server powerful and rapidly growing; Microsoft healthcare technology partner ecosystem expected to multiply
- **1998** — Oacis market position: 1997 revenues $25M (22% YoY growth); strong clinical data extraction; Oacis Passport web-based CIS launched early 1997; challenge: professional services partnerships
- **1998** — Healthcare provider IT adoption lag: Healthcare providers historically lagged other industries in IT adoption; conservative buying patterns; now accelerating investment
- **1998** — Data warehouse role in HIS: Data warehouses provide foundation for decision support by integrating legacy and client-server data; typically terabyte-scale on high-end Unix
- **1998** — HIS security and privacy concern: Security and privacy are major concerns in healthcare IT
- **1998** — Year 2000 healthcare risk: Healthcare Y2K risk elevated due to life-threatening potential; dependent on business partners and payers having Y2K-compliant systems for electronic billing
- **1998** — Supplier success criteria for HIS market: Applications must integrate with existing systems; intuitive interfaces requiring minimum training; phased implementation produces best results
- **1998** — Integrated Delivery Network (IDN) formation: Healthcare consolidating vertically and horizontally into IDNs; combining disparate systems including many older legacy systems is major challenge
