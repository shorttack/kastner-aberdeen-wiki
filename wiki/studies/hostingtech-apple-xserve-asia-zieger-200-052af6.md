# Apple looks to Asian markets to break out of niche role with Xserve (HostingTech)

> HostingTech magazine (November 2002) by Anne Zieger profiles Apple's campaign to use the Xserve 1U rack-mount server (running Mac OS X Unix-based OS) to break into Asian datacenter markets — its niche markets being media/entertainment, broadband/Internet services, education/research, and biotechnology/life sciences. Asian server market (excluding Japan) Q1 2002: $1.3B, down from $1.4B prior year (-7%) per IDC. Apple Q&A: CEO Steve Jobs; Cupertino CA; founded 1977; ~9,612 employees; 2001 revenues $5.36B; URL apple.com. Quotes from multiple analysts. Tim Bajarin (Creative Strategies): 'Apple is quite committed to going after this server marketplace, and over time could be a worthy competitor.... It is our opinion that the Asian market could be Apple's best place to gain initial ground.' Tim Deal (analyst): 'It's easy to use, and physically easy to maintain. It should appeal to both the veteran I.T. manager and to new I.T. managers.' Daniel Duerr (Grey Zone — Xserve channel partner): 'The cost for disk storage is unprecedented. This is one of the big things Apple did right.' Quote from Peter Kastner, chief research officer with Aberdeen Group: 'For Apple's computer-intensive imaging customers, the Xserve offers a less expensive and [more scalable product] not available from Apple until now, with the ability to port many Unix/Linux applications easily.' ## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — Apple corporate snapshot: CEO Steve Jobs; Cupertino CA; founded 1977; ~9,612 employees; 2001 revenue $5.36B
- **2002** — Asian server market Q1 2002: $1.3B (excluding Japan), down from $1.4B year prior — 7% decline in revenue
- **2002** — Xserve target verticals: Media/entertainment, broadband/Internet services, education/research, biotechnology/life sciences
- **2002** — Xserve storage cost approach: Chose ATA over SCSI to enable lower storage cost; nearly half-terabyte (480 GB) fully-loaded model at prices much lower than buying independent NAS
- **2002** — Sony 480 GB NAS pricing: Sony 480 GB NAS server retail price: $4,100-$4,400 per channel retailer
- **2002** — Maxtor 480 GB pricing: Maxtor 480 GB models ~$3,600 per channel retailer
- **2002** — Apple commitment and Asia opportunity: 'Apple is quite committed to going after this server marketplace, and over time could be a worthy competitor. It is our opinion that the Asian market could be Apple's best place to gain initial ground.'
- **2002** — Xserve usability appeal: 'It's easy to use, and physically easy to maintain. It should appeal to both the veteran I.T. manager and to new I.T. managers.'
- **2002** — Storage-cost achievement: 'The cost for disk storage is unprecedented. This is one of the big things Apple did right.'
- **2002** — Imaging customer strategic fit: 'For Apple's computer-intensive imaging customers, the Xserve offers a less expensive and [more scalable product] not available from Apple until now, with the ability to port many Unix/Linux applications easily.'
- **2002** — Channels-and-acceptance caution: 'No one should expect miracles overnight. Apple has a good product but needs to build channels and acceptance, while worldwide market conditions are poor at best.'
- **2011** — Xserve discontinuation: Apple discontinued Xserve in January 2011 — confirming Kastner's 'channels and acceptance' caution; Apple reverted to non-rack-mount Mac mini/Mac Pro server-class offerings
