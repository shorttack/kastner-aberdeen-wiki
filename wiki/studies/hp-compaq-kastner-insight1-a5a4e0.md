# The HP-Compaq Merger: If There's the Will, There's a Way

> Aberdeen InSight by Peter Kastner analyzing the proposed HP-Compaq merger announced September 2001. Identifies significant product overlap across servers (Intel/Windows, Unix, Itanium, Linux) and services, and warns that customers are confused and concerned about a merger focused on 'more' rather than 'better.' Argues that sustained outward customer focus — via strong marketing and sales retraining — is the key to merger success, and that without it Dell, IBM, and Sun would be the beneficiaries. ## Key observations (top 25)

- **2001** — Customer sentiment on HP-Compaq merger: Customers worried; believe combined HP will look inward on integration challenges rather than maintain customer focus
- **2001** — Product overlap: Intel/Windows servers: Both HP and Compaq offer Intel/Windows-based servers; significant overlap
- **2001** — Product overlap: Unix enterprise servers: Both HP and Compaq offer Unix server lines; overlap area
- **2001** — Common 64-bit architecture: Itanium: Both HP and Compaq working with Intel Itanium as common 64-bit architecture for future Windows and Unix environments
- **2001** — Linux support overlap: Both HP and Compaq support Linux as additional application platform
- **2001** — Compaq storage differentiation: HP's printer and Compaq's storage products are possible exceptions to significant product integration decisions required
- **2001** — Combined services organization comparison: Will be much more comparable to IBM's Global Services; better able to provide higher-level services to more global clients
- **2001** — Customer loyalty assessment: Customer base ranges from fanatical (HP 3000 and VAX) to somewhat satisfied; no rush by customers to other suppliers
- **2001** — Competitive beneficiaries if merger fails: Winners will surely be Dell, IBM, and Sun if HP-Compaq focuses inwardly for next two years
- **2001** — Key risk: inward focus: If HP and Compaq concentrate on complex internal integration, they will lose customers to Dell, IBM, and Sun
- **2001** — Aberdeen recommendation: customer communication: Immediate steps needed even prior to merger completion; timely customer communications with articulated differentiation
- **2001** — Aberdeen recommendation: better not more: Clear and early merger-benefits 'better not more' promise to customers and partners required
- **2001** — Aberdeen recommendation: sales force: Significant sales force retraining needed; delicate balance between enterprise sales, channels, dealers, consumer retail must be carefully aligned
- **2001** — HP-Compaq merger success prediction: Can succeed if strong outbound marketing program with 'better not more' message and outward customer focus sustained for 2+ years
- **unknown [REVIEW]** — HP-Compaq merger actual outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2001** — HP-Compaq partnership strengths: Industry-leading partnerships in high availability with top companies: Cisco, Oracle, Microsoft, SAP
- **2001** — Marketing execution deficit: Marketing execution excellence has been largely absent for the past two years prior to merger announcement
- **2001** — Timeline for sustained customer focus required: For the next two years, and likely longer, HP and Compaq must keep high-touch outward customer focus
- **2001** — Merger complexity assessment: Significant multidimensional product-integration decisions required in nearly every HP and Compaq product and service line of business
- **2001** — Middle-tier customer expansion opportunity: Opportunity exists to expand middle-tier customers with direct sales contact leading to expanding base
