# Project eLiza Positioning and Messaging Workshop Report

> Aberdeen Group consulting deliverable to IBM following the May 16, 2001 Somers positioning workshop for Project eLiza — IBM's self-managing, self-healing server architecture initiative. Provides a high-level computing vision and three key messages (Best Practices Automation, Simplification, Lower Cost) mapped to three stakeholder classes (LOB Executive, IT Management & Staff, Internal/External Users). Predicts 'computers managing computers' in five to ten years. Flag: IBM-internal confidentiality.

**Author:** Joyce Becknell, Gordon Haff, Peter Kastner (Aberdeen Group) · **Date:** 2001-06-01 · **Type:** market-study
**Importance:** high — *Foundational positioning document for IBM's eLiza/autonomic-computing launch; primary source for analyst-driven messaging architecture that shaped IBM's subsequent autonomic computing manifesto.*
**Prescience:** high — *2001 prediction that 'in five to ten years... computers managing computers and running business processes automatically' was realized 2010s-2020s via autonomic computing, cloud auto-scaling, AIOps, and self-healing Kubernetes infrastructure.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2001** — ibm-systems-mgmt-leader: IBM is a leader in systems management today; eLiza positioning reflects starting from a position of strength rather than a standing start.
- **2001** — eliza-not-eservers: eLiza is NOT equivalent to or a synonym for e-servers; eLiza is a server management strategy, not a server strategy.
- **2001** — eliza-heterogeneous: eLiza is not IBM server-specific; it will manage customer environments including solutions from IBM and others (IBM best/first supported, but not only).
- **2001** — eliza-api-transparent: eLiza self-management is transparent to developers — third-party software will not require modification using a whole new API set.
- **2001** — eliza-products-not-concept: eLiza is products, not just a concept or an internal IBM engineering rallying point.
- **2001** — computers-running-business: Computers are running the business in a fundamental way — without working computers, a business will largely cease to operate.
- **2001** — real-time-enterprise-arrived: The real-time enterprise has arrived — enabled by compute tools and decision support; just-in-time manufacturing requires end-to-end automated supply chains.
- **2001** — manual-backup-failure: Manual backup procedures are increasingly unable to deal with computer failures, even in non-e-Business contexts such as grocery chains; the computer system IS the business system.
- **2001** — it-architecture-defines-process: The computer system is the total instantiation of the business process; IT architecture implicitly defines time, place, and manner of employee/customer/supplier interaction.
- **2001** — computers-managing-computers-5-10-years: In five to ten years, one of the big paybacks will be computers managing computers and running business processes automatically; people taken out of the loop, focused on exceptions.
- **2001** — computers-managing-computers-outcome: Autonomic computing, AIOps, Kubernetes operators, cloud auto-scaling, and self-healing infrastructure realized this prediction in the 2010s-2020s, with further acceleration from ML-driven operations and AI agents in the 2020s.
- **2001** — exception-handling-steps: Knowledge-worker exception handling follows: discover exception, query cause, make decision, transact to change, report/collaborate; eLiza self-management should perform all steps subject to preset policies.
- **2001** — policy-driven-pager-modes: Preset-policy pager modes: (1) problem fixed, (2) approve default procedure, (3) choose among actions, or (4) no notification needed — contrasted with low-bar 'wireless page that something is wrong'.
- **2001** — msg-best-practices-automation: Message 1: Best Practices Automation — leveraging IT and domain expertise of IBM and others to address skills, expertise, flexibility, technology, and heterogeneity for more effective business processes.
- **2001** — msg-simplification: Message 2: Simplification — rooting out complexity wherever found by limiting and directing human interaction to high-level tasks requiring human decision-making.
- **2001** — msg-lower-cost: Message 3: Lower Cost — reducing overall costs, improving expense predictability, control, and accounting.
- **2001** — stakeholder-lob-executive: Stakeholder class 1: Line-of-Business (LOB) Executive — responsive to benefits of others' experience, improved total customer experience, domain expertise, predictable variable cost, improved ROI on IT.
- **2001** — stakeholder-it-management: Stakeholder class 2: IT Management & Staff (CIO, architects, developers, operations) — responsive to adaptable architectures, easier capacity planning, specialist-silo cut-through, lower lifecycle cost, reduced routine code.
- **2001** — stakeholder-users: Stakeholder class 3: Internal & External Users/Customers — responsive to services that 'just work', proactive handling, minimal training, self-help, passed-through cost savings.
- **2001** — stretch-goals-quantifiable: Set clear parameters around eLiza: specific quantifiable 'stretch' goals (e.g., 90% reduction in management costs), milestones, and proof points; lay out in IBM statement-of-direction style.
- **2001** — brand-eliza: Compelling need to brand eLiza — clear identity at non-technical level, logo (e.g., 'eLiza-enabled'); cited Budweiser iguanas as precedent.
- **2001** — define-when-eliza-is: Key part of process is defining what and when eLiza is; eLiza is ultimately things as well as a concept; does not exist today; first phase must come into being.
- **2001** — not-business-as-usual: Sub-text to eLiza theme: 'this is not business as usual' — CEO/CIO-level pitch matched by dramatic proof points (e.g., reducing management keystrokes so one operator can manage 10x more servers).
- **2001** — avoid-great-technology-positioning: Avoid positioning eLiza as 'great technology' (even though it is) — focus on business outcomes.
- **2001** — developer-message-mask-complexity: eLiza message to developers should emphasize masking of complexity — eLiza is not about mastering a thousand more APIs; it acts as a 'super operating system' providing a reliable foundation.
