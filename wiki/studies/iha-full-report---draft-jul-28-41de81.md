# Consumer Adoption of Memory Cards: A Qualitative Study

> Aberdeen Group qualitative study commissioned by the Internet Home Alliance (IHA) to understand consumer adoption barriers and purchase intent for SD Memory Cards in 2003. Research found digital electronics market maturing rapidly with digital cameras as primary entry point. Key findings: consumers broadly aware of memory cards but unable to identify specific formats/brands; primary adoption barrier is lack of compatible equipment; SD card seen as versatile with high 'cool factor' after demonstration; market transition to SD expected to take a decade; preferred capacity 256MB-1GB; willingness to pay premium of 10-15% for SD capability. Covers retail channel expectations, communication strategies, and data transfer behaviors.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — Digital electronics market maturity assessment: More mature than pre-study conjecture; receptivity and future buying patterns maturing
- **2003** — Digital camera ownership among focus group participants: Virtually all participants (after desktop computers and printers)
- **2003** — MP3 player ownership among focus group participants: Only a few respondents have MP3 players
- **2003** — Home network ownership and wireless data transfer: Only a few respondents have LANs; wireless transfer limited to PDAs only
- **2003** — Memory card format awareness: Majority unable to identify type/brand of memory card they own
- **2003** — Memory card usage pattern in digital cameras: Most users never remove memory card from camera; transfer via USB cable not card
- **2003** — Key advantages of memory cards as identified by users: Small size/portability; capacity choice; reusability; improving prices
- **2003** — Key disadvantages of memory cards as identified by users: Small size (loss risk); incompatible formats; price at high capacities
- **2003** — Post-demonstration reaction to SD Memory Card: Largely positive; high 'cool factor'; versatility as key differentiator
- **2003** — Primary barrier to SD Memory Card adoption: Lack of compatible equipment; need to acquire SD-enabled devices
- **2003** — Expected digital electronics transition timeline: A decade (approximately)
- **2003** — Preferred memory card capacity (post-demonstration): 256 MB to 1 GB (where price not a consideration)
- **2003** — Consumer purchase intent - wait and see attitude: Majority will wait to see if SD becomes industry standard
- **2003** — Willingness to pay premium for SD capability in new products: Substantial proportion willing to pay 10-15% premium; notable proportion would not pay any premium
- **2003** — SD as purchase decision factor: Minor consideration initially; 'tie-breaker' between otherwise identical products
- **2003** — Recommended retail distribution channels for SD Memory Cards: Consumer electronics stores (Best Buy), computer stores, office superstores, warehouse clubs, mass merchants (Target, Wal-Mart), online; eventually pharmacies, grocery stores, toy stores
- **2003** — Recommended communication strategies for SD Memory Card: Product demonstrations (in-store, continuous loop video, infomercials); mass media (TV, radio, print); online advertising; direct mail
- **2003** — Consumer desire for wireless data transfer: Identified as ultimate goal by multiple respondents; frustration with cable proliferation
- **2003** — Email as behavioral driver for digital camera adoption: E-mail is a key behavioral driver; photos emailed to friends/family and printed locally
- **2003** — SD card format war risk: SD is one contender but not the assured winner; card type not well understood by consumers
- **2003** — Music downloading behavior among younger consumers: Kazaa and P2P widely used by respondents under 35; downloaded to PC then burned to CD
- **2003** — Consumer digital technology household engagement pattern: In family households, typically one tech-savvy adult or teen handles digital data transfer; non-family households all members participate
- **2003** — SD card size concern: Key perceived drawback: notably smaller than most currently used cards; risk of loss or being swallowed by children/pets
- **2003** — Digital photography social and commercial use patterns: Photos primarily for family/social sharing; minority also use for commercial purposes (eBay listings, rental property docs, freelance news)
- **2003** — Primary digital storage media in consumer households: Photos on PC hard drive (some on CDs); music on CDs; video on VHS tapes; no one uses memory cards for permanent storage
