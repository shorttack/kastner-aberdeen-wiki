# Videoconferencing Boost — Intel TeamStation

> InformationWeek reports Intel's TeamStation room videoconferencing system — based on standard PC technology and priced at $9,999 ($11,999 with monitor) versus the $20,000 typical for proprietary room systems. Caterpillar Financial deployed in 12+ cities. Aberdeen Group analyst Peter Kastner explains how PC-based filtering produces 'less jerky video' over low-speed lines and predicts the technology will be 'fundamental to communications in the 21st century.'

**Author:** Mary E. Thyfault · **Date:** 1998-05-04 · **Type:** news-article

**Prescience:** high — *Kastner's 1998 prediction that PC-based videoconferencing 'will be fundamental to communications in the 21st century' was triumphantly correct — Zoom (2011), Microsoft Teams (2017), and Google Meet are direct architectural descendants.*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — PC-economics videoconferencing thesis: PC technology and filtering software compress video, send over low-speed line, decompress at other end — creating less jerky video presentation
- **1998** — 21st-century communications prediction: As the costs come down, this technology will be fundamental to communications in the 21st century
- **1998** — TeamStation pricing: $9,999 base; $11,999 including monitor; vs $20,000 for proprietary room systems
- **1998** — Caterpillar TeamStation deployment: Six North American cities, five European cities, and Singapore
- **1999** — Caterpillar 1,000-user 1999 target: Caterpillar projects 1,000 videoconferencing users by mid-1999
- **1998** — Pre-TeamStation conference-room critique: People have had this wonderful suite of applications at their desktop, then you walk into a conference room and you're faced with 1950s technology
- **1998** — Standard-PC-vs-proprietary architectural shift: By relying on standard PC technology, Intel is able to price TeamStation below proprietary room systems
