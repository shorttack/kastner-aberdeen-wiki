# Report of Peter S. Kastner: Intelligent Electronics Inc. v. Andersen Consulting LLP

> Expert witness report by Peter S. Kastner of Aberdeen Group in the litigation between Intelligent Electronics Inc. and Andersen Consulting LLP. Analyzes IE's IT strategy, Andersen's systems integration performance, AS/400 platform decisions, and the PC distribution industry dynamics of the mid-1990s. Concludes that Andersen performed its engagement responsibilities competently and that IE's business failures stemmed from strategic market positioning errors.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner · **Date:** 1999-06-21 · **Type:** expert-report

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1993** — market-position: $3 billion revenue; third-largest US PC distributor behind Ingram Micro and Tech Data
- **1994** — speed-imperative: Speed of IT implementation was critical — IE needed to transform IT within 12-18 months to survive competitive pressure
- **1994** — strategic-positioning: IE positioned itself as leading-edge technology company — a reseller not just a distributor
- **1994** — it-competency-gap: IE lacked internal IT competency to execute a major systems transformation without external help
- **1994** — business-model-error: IE's fundamental strategic error was pursuing a reseller/value-added model rather than low-cost distributor model as industry consolidated
- **1999** — performance-assessment: Andersen Consulting performed competently on the IE/2000 engagement given the constraints imposed by IE
- **1999** — project-ownership: IE owned the IE/2000 project — IE management made all key decisions including scope, timing, and technology choices
- **1999** — it-strategy-quality: Andersen's IT Strategy Report was an excellent piece of work — thorough, well-researched, and appropriate for IE's situation
- **1994** — option-2-recommendation: Option 2 (build new client-server system while maintaining legacy) was the best strategic choice among the three options presented
- **1994** — company-survival: IE's long-term viability was questionable regardless of IT outcome — the PC distribution industry was consolidating to 2-3 major players
- **1996** — bankruptcy-filing: IE filed for bankruptcy in 1996 and was liquidated by 1997
- **2000** — bankruptcy-filing: MicroAge filed for bankruptcy in 2000 — another mid-tier PC distributor eliminated by consolidation
- **2001** — bankruptcy-filing: Inacom filed for bankruptcy in 2001 — third mid-tier PC distributor eliminated
- **1994** — platform-viability: AS/400 was a viable platform for distribution in 1994 with strong RPG developer ecosystem and proven reliability
- **1994** — platform-longevity: AS/400 platform would remain viable for distribution applications well beyond IE's planning horizon
- **2025** — platform-status: IBM AS/400 (now IBM i) remains active and supported in 2025 — over 30 years after IE case; still widely used in distribution
- **1994** — platform-status: HP 3000 was already in decline by 1994 — losing market share and developer support
- **2003** — platform-discontinuation: HP announced end-of-life for HP 3000 in 2003; platform fully discontinued 2008
- **1994** — system-assessment: MSD was IE's core distribution system — RPG-based on AS/400; functional but aging and difficult to extend
- **1994** — upgrade-decision: MSD upgrade (Issue 1) was a reasonable interim step — keeping legacy operational while building new system was standard practice
- **1999** — resignation-assessment: Andersen's resignation from the IE/2000 project was justified given IE's failure to meet commitments and changing project conditions
- **1994** — scope-management: IE repeatedly changed project scope and requirements — a key factor in IE/2000 difficulties
- **1999** — quality-assessment: Andersen's work product quality was comparable to industry standards for major SI firms based on Aberdeen's research across 7 major SI firms
- **1999** — staffing-adequacy: Andersen's staffing levels and expertise were appropriate for the IE/2000 engagement scope
- **1994** — methodology-assessment: Andersen's Application Software Factory (ASF) methodology was a reasonable development approach — standard for large SI engagements of the era
