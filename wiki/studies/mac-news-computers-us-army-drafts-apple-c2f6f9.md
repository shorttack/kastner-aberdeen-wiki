# US Army Drafts Apple Xserve for Supercomputer

> MacNewsWorld coverage of the U.S. Army's MACH 5 supercomputer built on Apple Xserve G5 (via COLSA), projected at ~25 teraflops and ranked second after DOE systems at the time. Peter Kastner, executive vice president and chief research officer at Aberdeen Group, calls the deal a 'wakeup call' and declares Apple 'not a one-shot wonder, but a force to be reckoned with' in supercomputing after Virginia Tech's earlier System X deployment.

**Author:** Blane Warrene (reporter, MacNewsWorld); Peter Kastner (quoted source) · **Date:** 2004-06-22 · **Type:** press-article
**Importance:** medium — *Kastner's analyst endorsement of Apple as a legitimate HPC vendor at the Xserve G5's peak moment, positioning the Army MACH 5 deal as validation of Apple's first supercomputing success (Virginia Tech 2003).*
**Prescience:** low — *Kastner's 'force to be reckoned with' framing did not pan out: Apple discontinued Xserve in 2011 without further major HPC wins, and Apple effectively exited supercomputing by the late 2000s.*
