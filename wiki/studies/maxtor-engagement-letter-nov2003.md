# Aberdeen Group Engagement Letter — Maxtor Pools of Storage Executive Evangelism Program

> A formal engagement letter from Aberdeen Group to Maxtor Corporation's VP of Worldwide Corporate Marketing, Stephen DiFranco, outlining a $114K four-phase program to create and evangelize the 'Pools of Storage' / midline storage market category. The program encompassed a strategy workshop, interactive toolkit development, 100–250-respondent message-testing surveys, and a US/Europe press evangelization tour. Crucially, it articulated the SAS/SATA backplane as the enabling hardware architecture for a new enterprise storage tier.

**Author:** Peter S. Kastner; Sandra Tubbs / Aberdeen Group · **Date:** 2003-11-13 · **Type:** consulting-report
**Importance:** high — *First documented go-to-market blueprint for creating the midline/tiered-storage market category; articulated SAS/SATA mixed-backplane architecture as a standard years before it became universal, and established Aberdeen as the intellectual anchor for an entire new storage tier.*
**Prescience:** high — *The SAS/SATA backplane vision proved exactly correct: mixed SAS/SATA backplanes became the universal enterprise server standard from 2004 onward. The lexicon ('Online, Midline, Nearline, Offline') entered industry vocabulary. The market category Maxtor and Aberdeen set out to create — tiered/midline…*

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — Project title: Pools of Storage Executive Evangelism Program
- **2003** — SAS/SATA mixed-backplane as enterprise standard: SAS/SATA backplane enabling plug-play-populate of any mix of SAS and SATA drives proposed as the efficient system for Pools of Storage in detached storage and arrays
- **2006** — SAS/SATA mixed-backplane adoption outcome: SAS/SATA mixed backplanes became the universal enterprise server standard from 2004; every enterprise server ships with mixed backplanes today
- **2003** — Market strategy approach: Lexicon creation — establishing four named Pools of Storage (Online Midline Nearline Offline) as industry standard vocabulary
- **2003** — Four-phase engagement structure: Phase 1: Strategy Workshop; Phase 2: Toolkit Development; Phase 3: Message Testing (100-250 surveys); Phase 4: Evangelization (US + Europe press tour)
- **2003** — Total project cost: $114000
- **2003** — Estimated travel expenses: $6000 (phases 1-3) + $3500 (phase 4)
- **2003** — Project start date: November 17 2003
- **2003** — Strategy Workshop date and location: December 9 2003; Shrewsbury MA
- **2003** — Phase 2 tool delivery deadline: January 9 2004
- **2003** — Market success criteria — industry lexicon adoption: All players in Serial Storage ecosystem must adopt Maxtor's Pools lexicon
- **2003** — Market success criteria — press endorsements: Endorsement required from industry publications in US and Europe and from at least four Fortune 500 CIOs
- **2003** — Market positioning risk to avoid: Must prevent misperception that Pools of Storage means hierarchical storage management (HSM)
- **2003** — Customer pain point targeted: TCO pain of storing fixed content on expensive high-speed drives designed for speed instead of capacity
- **2003** — Pools of Storage lexicon — four tiers: Online (Transactional Processing); Midline (Fixed Content); Nearline (Disk Backup and Staging); Offline (Tape Archive)
- **2003** — Midline storage as new enterprise storage category: Midline solutions fill a vacuum for fixed content with an economic and scalable set of products within a year
- **2006** — Midline/tiered storage adoption outcome: By 2006 Warner Bros running multi-tiered FC/SATA/tape environment; Wikibon 2011 estimated SATA holds 85% of enterprise data and 40% of spend by 2015
- **2003** — Evangelization channels — US and Europe press tour: East-coast US; West-coast US; two cities in England; continental Europe
- **2003** — Target evangelization audiences: Press; industry trade associations; trade shows; SNIA user storage groups; online seminars; early adopter face-to-face interviews; Web Center of Excellence
- **2003** — Message testing research scope: 100-250 Internet surveys plus 5-8 face-to-face or telephone interviews; merge with April 2003 Aberdeen survey results
- **2003** — ILM as strategic framing for Pools of Storage: If ILM is 'getting control over storage' then Pools of Storage is the economic and flexibility 'best approach'
- **2006** — ILM as enterprise data management concept outcome: ILM became a core concept in enterprise data management; adopted by EMC NetApp Dell and others as standard product feature framework
- **2003** — Decision Tool architecture: Web-based ILM Pools of Storage Decision Tool; Microsoft IIS + SQL Server 2000; 3-month Aberdeen hosting; Call to Action PDF capture
- **2003** — Decision Tool TCO modeling capability: Quantifies TCO impact with 3 implementation paths: conservative; industry norm; aggressive
- **2003** — Competitive Value Prospect Tool architecture: Microsoft Excel 2000+; up to 20 business metric areas; advanced heuristics; simulation capability; detailed financial statements; web access to financial data (One Source / D&B)
