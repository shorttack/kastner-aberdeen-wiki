# Maxtor Survey Report (RAMP Results Presentation)

> Final 38-slide RAMP results presentation by Peter Kastner and David Hill for Maxtor Corporation, May 2003. Synthesizes findings from 70 telephone survey respondents and 5 face-to-face interviews (Timex, Applied Materials, State Street, Inovant/Visa, Kaiser Permanente) on enterprise willingness to adopt ATA (low-cost/midline) disk as a complementary storage tier. Key findings: 65% of enterprises use 60%+ of available storage; storage growth below vendor projections; 70% have read-only data but cannot easily identify it; 75% likely to purchase low-cost disk; backup/restore remains a major pain point. Key conclusions: MaXLine should be positioned as 'complementary' not 'substitute' storage; 'mid-line' is the correct market term; positioning should emphasize 'better than ATA' rather than 'less than SCSI'; disk-based backup/copy presents a major opportunity.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — Survey methodology: 70 telephone respondents + 5 face-to-face interviews
- **2003** — Respondent industry distribution — top verticals: 2/3 from Banking/Financial/Insurance, Healthcare, and Manufacturing (Industrial)
- **2003** — Respondent revenue distribution — large companies: Half of 70 companies have revenues >$1B
- **2003** — IT budget distribution — very large ($50M+): 25.7% (18/70)
- **2003** — IT budget distribution — large ($10-$49M): 22.8% (16/70)
- **2003** — Respondent scope — entire company: 48.6% (34/70)
- **2003** — CIO reporting to President/CEO vs. CFO: 58.6% report to President/CEO; 31.4% to CFO
- **2003** — Implication of CEO IT reporting: Focus is not primarily on cost; purchase decisions considered in light of business purposes
- **2003** — OS platform presence — mainframe: Nearly 2/3 (65%) have mainframe
- **2003** — OS platform presence — Windows: All but 1 (99%) have Windows; 68.6% responsible for Windows storage
- **2003** — OS platform presence — Unix/Linux: ~75% have Unix/Linux; 51.4% responsible for Unix/Linux storage
- **2003** — Respondents with ≥6TB storage: 53% of the 51 who answered (27/51)
- **2003** — Storage utilization ≥60% (disk in use vs. available): 65.1% of respondents
- **2003** — Conclusion: ATA disk demand cannot be met by repurposing existing disks: New ATA storage must come from new growth not from existing unused capacity
- **2003** — Mainframe storage share — respondents with >33%: Over 50% of mainframe users
- **2003** — Windows storage share — respondents with >33%: 82%; 35% have >70% Windows storage
- **2003** — Storage growth projection — next 12 months: Only about half plan to increase storage; growth less than vendor predictions
- **2003** — Storage vendor revenue outlook: Overall revenue shortfall for hardware portion of storage industry foreseen; prices fall faster than growth
- **2006** — Actual outcome: storage vendor revenue pressure: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2003** — Storage vendor response to revenue pressure: Vendors will seek new uses for disk (e.g. ATA) to maintain unit shipments
- **2003** — Desired maximum utilization — majority want ≥60% full: Solid majority across all OS platforms want 60%+ utilization
- **2003** — Progress in networked storage (SAN/NAS) vs. DAS: Progress continues but much storage remains DAS; IT moves slower than vendors want
- **2003** — Online disk copies — majority have 1 copy; ~1/3 have 2+: One copy: 48.5%; two or more: 32.4%; no extra copy: 19.1%
- **2003** — Tape copies — majority have 2-7 copies; 30% have 10+: 2-7 copies: 46.8%; 10 or more: 30.6%; one copy: 22.6%
- **2003** — Respondents with read-only or seldom-written data: 70% (49 of 70) said yes
