# Microsoft Vows To Appeal EU Ruling

> TechNewsWorld / E-Commerce Times article on Microsoft's vow to appeal the European Commission's March 2004 antitrust ruling and $612M fine. Peter Kastner of Aberdeen Group argues Microsoft has 'silent friends' who may become vocal, and that the EU setting a standard different from US law threatens not only Microsoft but every global player.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — EU extraterritorial antitrust risk: If EU sets a totally different standard for business conduct than the US, it threatens not only Microsoft but every other global player — because US has already determined Microsoft's practices legal.
- **2004** — Silent friends framing: As Microsoft's appeal goes forward it will be interesting to note which companies emerge as allies; Microsoft has many 'silent friends' who might become vocal.
- **2004** — Why Microsoft appealed instead of paying: Microsoft could have paid the fine; an appeal was not the only option. But paying would have meant they couldn't address the issues of law and commerce they're seeking to clarify.
- **2004** — EU fine: European Commission announced a $612 million fine alongside Windows Media Player unbundling and interoperability remedies.
- **2004** — Microsoft appeal announcement: Microsoft publicly committed on 2004-06-08 to appeal the EU decision.
- **2004** — EU standard-setting trajectory: Kastner warning implies EU would emerge as an independent global standard-setter distinct from US regulators.
- **2024** — EU became global tech-antitrust standard-setter: EU General Court upheld bulk of 2004 ruling in 2007; Digital Markets Act (2022) and ongoing cases through 2024 made EU the de-facto global tech-regulation standard-setter.
