# Mid-Line Disk Storage Emerging as Significant Cost-Saving Opportunity

> The seminal Aberdeen Group white paper that defined and named the 'midline' storage tier — ATA-based online storage positioned between high-performance FC/SCSI disk arrays and magnetic tape. Based on 75 face-to-face and telephone interviews with storage managers at billion-dollar enterprises, it presented a four-level storage pyramid, documented that 75% of enterprise buyers were moderately or highly likely to purchase midline storage, and predicted that over 50% of enterprise data would eventually migrate to the midline tier. The paper established ILM principles (Ageing, Freezing, Accumulation, Redundancy) as the rationale for a new storage tier.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — Research sample — primary interviews: 75 face-to-face and telephone interviews with storage managers at companies with $1B+ revenue; includes several Fortune 50-size businesses
- **2003** — Operating system coverage of research: Storage accessed by servers using: mainframe; Windows; Unix/Linux
- **2003** — Enterprise storage landscape — FC/SCSI dominance: More than half of enterprises are completely run online using Fibre Channel (FC) and SCSI disks
- **2003** — ATA vs FC/SCSI capacity advantage: ATA disks have 2-8x storage capacity of FC/SCSI; up to 300 GB per ATA disk compared to 142/72/36 GB per FC/SCSI disk
- **2003** — ATA vs FC/SCSI cost per GB advantage: ATA disks are about half the cost per gigabyte versus FC/SCSI
- **2003** — SCSI vs ATA cost multiple: SCSI disks can reach more than 5x the cost of ATA disks
- **2003** — Midline system cost savings vs high-performance disk: Midline solutions can be as much as half the price of high-performance disk-based systems
- **2003** — 2TB backup appliance cost comparison: ATA-based: $20K-$30K vs SCSI-based: $35K-$50K
- **2003** — Large FC environment savings from midline migration: In 30TB+ FC environments: savings greater than $100K off deals formerly $300K+
- **2003** — Midline purchase intent — high willingness: Over 25% of enterprise storage buyers showed high willingness to purchase higher-capacity/lower-cost disks
- **2003** — Midline purchase intent — moderate willingness: Nearly 50% showed moderate willingness to purchase higher-capacity/lower-cost disks
- **2003** — Midline purchase intent — combined likely buyers: 75% of storage buyers are moderately or highly likely to buy mid-line storage in the coming year
- **2003** — Highly likely to purchase low-cost disk storage: ~40% of respondents said highly likely to purchase low-cost disk storage
- **2003** — Current supplier discussion of low-cost options: 60% of respondents said their current storage supplier had already discussed low-cost storage options with them
- **2003** — Large static-data applications prevalence: 60% of respondents have single applications with very large non-changing/static data (video images data warehouse detailed data old e-mails)
- **2003** — Data restore speed — unacceptable performance: Nearly 2/3 (67%) of respondents said restoring critical data takes longer than desired
- **2003** — Data restore frequency: Over 60% of survey respondents report data restorations more than once a year; 20% report a data restore monthly or more frequently
- **2003** — Backup management burden: ~50% of respondents said backup management is a burden
- **2003** — Critical data restoration guarantee failure rate: ~50% of respondents would not guarantee all critical data can be restored on a given day
- **2003** — Backup window problem prevalence: >80% of survey respondents said backup windows could be a problem with off-line backups
- **2003** — ILM four principles: Ageing (value and use of content change as content ages); Freezing (content changes from dynamic to fixed); Accumulation (data grows; access per piece drops); Redundancy (more copies for faster access and data protection)
- **2003** — Storage pyramid — four key levels: Level 1 (top): Solid-state disks (ultra-performance); Level 2: High-performance FC/SCSI (mission-critical OLTP); Level 3: Mid-line ATA (fixed content aged data); Level 4 (bottom): Tape (backup/restore off-site)
- **2003** — Midline storage definition: High-capacity online ATA disks in the middle tier between high-use FC/SCSI disk arrays and magnetic tape off-line storage; aimed at data that needs to be online with moderate access and rate-of-change requirements
- **2003** — Data migration to midline tier — minimum estimate: At least 20% of enterprise data fits the definition of inactive/seldom-used online storage appropriate for high-capacity/low-cost ATA
- **2003** — Data migration to midline tier — long-term projection: Over half of enterprise data will eventually fall into the mid-line
