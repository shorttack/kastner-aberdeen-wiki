# Networking: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering the enterprise networking market — including LAN/WAN technologies, hubs, routers, switches, and high-speed networking. The report profiles major networking suppliers including Cisco, 3Com, Bay Networks, and Cabletron, and identifies key market trends such as the transition from hubs to switches, the rise of Gigabit Ethernet, and the impact of Internet traffic on network design. It also addresses Year 2000 compliance concerns for network infrastructure.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — Networking market size: In excess of $24 billion; fastest growing IT segment
- **1998** — Router displacement by routing switches: Routing switches replacing routers in enterprise LAN core; ~10x capacity at ~1/10 cost
- **2000** — Router displacement by routing switches: Verify actual adoption rates by 2000-2002.
- **1998** — Fast Ethernet adoption as upgrade path: Fast Ethernet adopted as primary upgrade from 10 Mbps; 10/100 NIC prices at 10 Mbps levels
- **1998** — Gigabit Ethernet vs. ATM preference: Gigabit Ethernet is attractive alternative to ATM for Ethernet backbone users; ATM not accepted end-to-end
- **1998** — ATM deployment reality: ATM posted healthy growth in 1997 as WAN and LAN backbone but failed as end-to-end desktop technology
- **1998** — Wireless LAN market status: Wireless occupies niche; 802.11 ratification improves interoperability; bandwidth approaching 10 Mbps
- **1998** — VPN as WAN alternative: VPNs using tunneling/security protocols turn public Internet into private WAN at fraction of private line costs
- **1998** — Network market growth rate: Overall revenue growth not to exceed 15% due to price declines and Y2K resource diversion
- **1998** — Intel market entry in networking: Intel and Compaq making successful networking debuts; driven by high-volume/low-cost manufacturing
- **1998** — Cisco competitive position: Cisco profiled as dominant enterprise router and switch vendor; major internetworking supplier
- **1998** — 3Com market position: 3Com holds 40% NIC market share; leads Ethernet managed hubs and Fast Ethernet workgroup hubs
- **1998** — Hub market trajectory: Workgroup switch prices dropped; switches replacing hubs; hubs survive as low-end SOHO/SME product
- **1998** — Y2K impact on networking: Y2K compliance overlooked for network components; critical for hub/router/switch firmware; worst in SME
- **1998** — Internet traffic impact on networks: Internet adoption inverted 80/20 traffic rule — now 80% of traffic crosses backbone; bursty and unpredictable
- **1998** — Voice/data convergence timeline: Converged networks (voice/video/data) will not become widespread for several more years due to quality/uncertainty
- **1998** — IT outsourcing for network services: As network complexity increases more IT orgs consider outsourcing; particularly remote access
- **1998** — xDSL technology status: xDSL technologies emerged; 128 Kbps IDSL to 7 Mbps ADSL; not yet widely deployed or geographically available
- **1998** — SOHO networking opportunity: SOHO is fastest growing segment; 90% of businesses worldwide; ~40% use Internet as part of business
- **1998** — Consumer home networking status: ~45 million US households with home computers; ~25% own more than one PC; nascent home networking market
- **1998** — Bay Networks competitive position: Bay Networks profiled as major internetworking supplier; acquired by Nortel Networks
- **1998** — Alteon Networks competitive position: Alteon profiled as high-speed networking newcomer focused on Gigabit Ethernet switches
- **1998** — Optical networking (DWDM) potential: DWDM enables up to 1 terabit on single fiber; will remove WAN bottleneck; stimulate enterprise bandwidth demand
