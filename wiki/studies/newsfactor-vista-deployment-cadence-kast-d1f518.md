# Vista: How Soon Until Deployment?

> NewsFactor reports analyst views on enterprise Vista adoption pace. Peter Kastner, Aberdeen vice-president and research director for enterprise technology, frames a three-year hardware-lifecycle 'cadence' that places full Vista deployment in 2010 as new Vista-laden machines replace XP. Kastner predicts Microsoft will stop shipping XP in small business retail systems in 2007, forcing Vista uptake. He disagrees with Gartner's recommendation that businesses wait until 2008.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2006** — Hardware-cadence Vista deployment framework: Companies on a three-year hardware lifecycle buy a fraction of total hardware needs per month — Vista will be fully deployed sometime in 2010
- **2006** — No-light-switch-conversion thesis: There's no light-switch conversion where everyone turns on Vista one morning
- **2007** — XP small-business retail cutoff: Sometime in 2007, Microsoft will likely stop availability of Windows XP in small business systems sold at retail, forcing a Vista switch
- **2006** — Disagreement with Gartner 2008-wait recommendation: We disagree with Gartner that business needs to wait until 2008 to start deploying Vista
- **2006** — Patch-cadence rebuttal: Fixes come out every month, not just in service packs
- **2006** — Vista enterprise launch date: Vista to be available for companies as early as November 30 along with new versions of Office and Exchange
- **2010** — Vista full deployment 2010: Vista will be fully deployed sometime in 2010 as new Vista-laden machines replace old XP machines
- **2006** — Aero hardware horsepower concern: Features like Vista's much-hyped Aero interface need more horsepower than most business machines currently have
