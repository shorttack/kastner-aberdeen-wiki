# Niagara: Sun's Palaver Starter? (IT Blogwatch)

> Computerworld IT Blogwatch aggregation (2005-11-15) curating reactions to Sun Microsystems' UltraSPARC T1 'Niagara' launch — an 8-core, 8-thread-per-core chip promising 70-watt power envelopes for servers. Peter S. Kastner (quoted from a reader comment) recalls being briefed on the Niagara roadmap circa 2000, argues Sun's poor delivery record in SPARC has hurt badly, and would have been market-turning in mid-2002 — though Niagara is a big help to Sun's SPARC base, he doubts it will shake the market's penchant for x64 AMD Opteron servers.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2000** — Early Niagara roadmap briefing: I recall being briefed on the Niagara roadmap circa 2000.
- **2005** — Sun's SPARC delivery record: Sun has had a poor delivery record in SPARC over the past several years. That has hurt the company; some say it has hurt badly.
- **2005** — Right recipe but wrong timing: With today's big WSJ story on computer power consumption, Sun's eight cores in a 70-watt envelope is the right recipe. But wouldn't this have been market-turning, say, mid-2002?
- **2005** — Niagara impact on x64 momentum: Niagara is a big help to Sun's SPARC base, but I doubt it will shake the market's penchant for x64-based servers, in Sun's case, AMD Opterons.
- **2005** — Niagara chip specs: UltraSPARC T1 (Niagara) servers will be available with 4, 6, or 8 cores; each core capable of 8 threads; maximum server power consumption 70 watts.
- **2005** — Multithreading + power focus: Sun pitched Niagara as recapture strategy for users who switched to low-cost Windows/Linux x86 via multithreading and power consumption advantages.
- **2005** — IBM FUD response: IBM circulated a poison-pen FUD gram criticizing Niagara launch timing as an attempt to buy momentum with an early product release.
- **2010** — Sun acquired by Oracle; Niagara line absorbed: Oracle acquired Sun in January 2010; SPARC T-series continued under Oracle through T8 (2017) but never reclaimed x64 market share; by 2017 Oracle laid off the Solaris/SPARC core team.
