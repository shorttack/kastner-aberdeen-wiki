# 40th Anniversary of the Independent Software Industry

> Kastner blog post on OnComputersTips marking the 40th anniversary of the independent software industry. On June 23, 1969, IBM unbundled software and services under US antitrust pressure, catalyzing the modern software business. Kastner recalls being a few months into his first programming job — combining COBOL labor-accounting programs for his employer, a subsidiary of Wang Laboratories — on an IBM 360-65 mainframe. Recounts the rise of Cullinet/John Cullinane, Computer Associates/Charles Wang, Gene Amdahl, and the 1970s-1980s minicomputer era.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1969** — First programming job at Wang subsidiary: In June 1969 I was a few months into my first programming job at a subsidiary of Wang Laboratories, combining a handful of labor accounting programs written in COBOL.
- **1969** — IBM unbundling of software and services: On June 23, 1969, IBM — then the most powerful force in computing and under antitrust fire — unbundled software and services from its mostly-mainframe business model, catalyzing the modern independent software industry.
- **1980** — Wang trajectory: Wang would later become a big player in the 1980s; desktop computers were still being invented by some colleagues a few offices away in 1969.
- **1969** — Computing environment: My computer was an IBM 360-65, top of the line mainframe ancestor of today's IBM Z-series. Had tapes, disks, telecom, printers. About 1/400th of the computing power of an iPhone 3G — cost over $10 million.
- **1969** — Mainframe bundling economics: When you bought or rented an IBM 360, it came bundled with IBM systems engineers to care for IBM software and typically the customer's software too.
- **1969** — Software development environment 1969: In 1969 there were few choices in software development: few small custom-programming shops, most software developed in-house very slowly and at great cost. - **1970** — Culprit and IDMS: John Cullinane built Culprit, a report generator, then IDMS network database — among the earliest independent software products.
- **1976** — Computer Associates genesis: Charles Wang founded Computer Associates around a mainframe tape library utility — showing anyone could build an independent software business.
- **1975** — Amdahl plug-compatible mainframes: Gene Amdahl, IBM 360 architect, founded Amdahl Corp. and made plug-compatible mainframes at lower cost than IBM.
- **1975** — Minicomputer industry rise: The 1970s spawned the independent minicomputer industry: DEC, Data General, Wang, Prime Computer, and eventually Sun Microsystems — all independent, none beholden to IBM.
- **1980** — IBM PC era: In the 1980s IBM picked Microsoft and Intel for its new PC business — further validating independent software/hardware ecosystem model.
