# Point Solutions Versus Integrated Oracle Applications: The Road to IT Investment ROI

> Oracle-sponsored Aberdeen Executive White Paper (June 2001) — SAME core research as study 6 ('Leaving Well Enough Alone', July 2001) but Oracle-branded. Conclusion substitutes 'Oracle provides such an application suite' for the vendor-neutral closing. Chronologically predates study 6 — this is likely the commissioned original; study 6 is the vendor-neutral generalization Aberdeen then released on its own behalf. Cites Beneficial Life and Indian Motorcycle (Oracle customers) only — drops Credit Suisse First Boston/Carreckers (PeopleSoft) and State of Michigan (Lawson) references that appear in study 6.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2001** — integration-budget-share: 40% of enterprise IT budget on average is committed to application integration; may rise to 70% in certain situations.
- **2001** — nike-400m-failure: Nike's four-year, $400M SAP+Siebel+i2 integration effort caused missed quarterly earnings due to integration failures (not application failures).
- **2001** — porter-durable-advantage: Porter: 'very rarely will individual companies be able to gain durable advantages from deployment of best-of-breed applications.'
- **2001** — integration-lag: 2-3 year lag between stand-alone point vendors and integrated suite releases of same functionality; ~3 years to functional parity; but suite version has higher BPI.
- **2001** — implementation-timelines: 18-24 months for integrated suite; 3-6 months for one point solution; 16-20 months to integrate four point applications.
- **2001** — ariba-i2-ibm-dissolved: Ariba/i2/IBM partnership launched with $1M ad campaign; dissolved less than 6 months later.
- **2001** — beneficial-oracle: Beneficial Life (Oracle Applications customer) reports more than 50% of IT staff on customization/integration.
- **2001** — indian-oracle-roi: Indian Motorcycle implemented Oracle suite in 1999 to run entire business; ROI realized within 1 year of production cutover.
- **2001** — integration-cost-ratio: Point solution requires 3-4x license fees in initial integration investment; ERP total can reach 10x license fee; suite module drops to factor of 1.
- **2001** — beneficial-integration-cost: When third-party software is architecturally close to existing: integration cost ≈ software cost; when distinctly different: 2-3x software cost.
- **2001** — xml-standards-promise: XML standardization via RosettaNet/OAGI (adopted by Microsoft, Oracle, SAP) will prove invaluable to future integration ease.
- **2001** — xml-actual-outcome: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2001** — integration-issues-list: Primary integration issues: data model variation, workflow/messaging incompatibility, discrete dev cycles, cross-product BPI, lack of standard open persistent interfaces.
- **2001** — oracle-positioning: Paper concludes: 'Oracle provides such an application suite, enabled with comprehensive intra-enterprise and business-to-business best practice business processes designed to generate a sustainable benefit stream.'
- **2001** — integrated-suite-winners: ERP vendors of 1990s are likely the winners in the long run with packaged integrated cross-enterprise solutions.
- **2001** — integrated-actual: [UNVERIFIED]
- **2001** — ar-sponsored-research-pattern: Pattern of sponsored-then-vendor-neutral paper release evidenced by this study (June) preceding study 6 (July) — same core research, different framing.
