# Prime Computer — Industry Product Requirements Plan (Five-Year Plan, July 1981, COMPANY CONFIDENTIAL)

> Internal Prime Computer Inc. five-year Industry Product Requirements Plan dated July 15, 1981 (COMPANY CONFIDENTIAL), authored by Peter Kastner of Prime's Market Planning and Management Department of Marketing Development with input from Prime market managers (the 'and Others' coauthors). The plan synthesizes vertical-market plans for Construction Engineering, Power Utilities, Manufacturing Engineering, Manufacturing Production, Banking, Government, and Energy (Petroleum) into a single statement of common product requirements for Prime's first half-of-the-1980s strategy. Forecasts product revenue from planned markets growing from $117.7M in 1980 (44% of corporate goal) to $1,193.1M in 1985 (81% of corporate goal). Covers seven System Use Categories (Computational, Timesharing/Program Development, CAD/CAM, Office Automation, Transaction Processing, Query, Prime Information / Small Business) crossed against five system price bands ($30-49K Rabbit through $300K+ Prime 850). Identifies five priority near-term product gaps: programmable parallel-channel interface (HPC-4 / MPC-4 Special), floating-point precision, COBOL at full ANSI intermediate level, FORTRAN 77 compile/runtime performance, and Hyperchannel. Recommends a $10K-$30K personal computer with integral Primenet networking before mid-1980s. ## Key observations (top 25)

- **1981** — Plan thesis: 'The set of product requirements common to all markets is where most of Prime's product development attention and investment should be placed.'
- **1981** — Total planned-market product revenue 1980 (worldwide, $M): 117.7
- **1981** — Total planned-market product revenue 1981 (worldwide, $M): 217.0
- **1981** — Total planned-market product revenue 1985 (worldwide, $M, forecast): 1193.1
- **1981** — Planned-market revenue share of corporate goal 1985: 81% of $1,458M corporate goal (forecast)
- **1981** — Top-5 priority near-term product gaps (1981-82): (1) Programmable parallel-channel interface (HPC-4 / MPC-4 Special); (2) Floating-point precision and consistency; (3) COBOL at full ANSI intermediate level; (4) FORTRAN 77 compile/runtime performance; (5) Hyperchannel interface
- **1981** — Seven System Use Categories: Computational, Timesharing/Program Development, CAD/CAM, Office Automation, Transaction Processing, Query (DB & Batch), Prime Information (small business)
- **1981** — Five system price bands (1981 product line): $0-50K (Rabbit ~$40K), $50-130K (250-II $65K), $130-220K (550-II $89K), $220-300K (750 $155K), >$300K (850 $300K)
- **1981** — Need for $10K-$30K personal computer with networking: 'Market Planning strongly believes that a very low end system, in a $10K-$30K price band, is needed before the mid-1980's' — networked, professional/managerial/engineering workstation
- **1981** — F1000 TP revenue timing: 'We do not foresee significant corporate revenue streams from TP at Fortune 1000-type firms before 1984' — slow but progressive growth from pilots
- **1981** — Competitive benchmark thesis: 'DEC will remain the competitive benchmark indefinitely, and we see the competition increasing in intensity as DEC broadens its product line and matures its software.'
- **1981** — Four 'attack' markets: Local-area networks of terminals and other devices; Distributed processing including personal/departmental systems; Office automation; CAD/CAM
- **1981** — Prime OA candor: 'The OA product has been plagued with several problems: stability, low word processing functionality, lack of networking and ease of use.'
- **1981** — Computational competition: 'We usually only see one competitor when computational timesharing is called for, albeit that competitor is DEC's VAX'
- **1981** — TP small/medium-business channel strategy: 'Stand-alone TP system sold to small and medium companies will require applications packages and support such that we recommend a channel of distribution strategy using system builders and dealers.'
- **1981** — Banking SNA gap: 'Lack of SNA limits Prime's applicability within many major banks.'
- **1981** — Government floating-point revenue loss YTD 1981: $1.5M lost YTD 1981 due to floating-point precision deficiencies
- **1981** — Government FIPS-COBOL revenue risk: $5M loss potential in Government if Prime COBOL does not pass FIPS standards
- **1981** — TEMPEST market sizing: $50M market in next two years for OA-compatible TEMPEST secure terminals
- **1981** — Manufacturing Production: Use mix at $0-50K (1982 forecast %): 0% Comp / 10% TS-PD / 5% CAD / 10% OA / 40% TP / 35% Query
- **1981** — Construction Engineering: Use mix at $130-220K (1982 forecast %): 60% Comp / 30% TS-PD / 10% CAD
- **1981** — Energy: 1981 product revenue ($M): 38.2 (forecast 1981; up from 2.5 in 1980; 359.4 in 1985 forecast)
- **1981** — Government: 1981 product revenue ($M): 35.0 (1981 forecast; 245.0 in 1985 forecast)
- **1981** — Manufacturing: 1981 product revenue ($M): 97.9 (1981 forecast; 456.9 in 1985 forecast — largest single market)
- **1981** — PRIMOS ease-of-use weakness: 'The user interface to PRIMOS bothers a number of customers in different vertical markets. We have lost business because of ease-of-use issues. We are often second rated to DEC VAX.'
