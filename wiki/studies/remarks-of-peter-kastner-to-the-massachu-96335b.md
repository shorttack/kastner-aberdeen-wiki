# Remarks of Peter Kastner to the Massachusetts Chamber of Commerce

> Peter Kastner's January 29, 2003 remarks to the Massachusetts Chamber of Commerce on the state of the Massachusetts high-tech economy following the 2001-2002 dot-com bust. Kastner documents 15,000 MA high-tech jobs lost in 2002 (5.7% of the base), argues no 90-day turnaround is possible given recession breadth and severity, and catalogs job losses: Lucent contracting, HP reengineering, IBM's pending Rational Software (Westford) acquisition, Genuity/Woburn acquired by Level 3 with 800 job cuts, Precise Software absorbed into Veritas. Forecasts four sources of 2003 recovery: (1) Aberdeen Group's global IT spending forecast of 3-4%, back-end loaded due to Q1 war concerns; (2) mid-career unemployed executives spawning 2004 Massachusetts garage-startup wave (heated garages preferred); (3) continued industry consolidation returning cash to MA shareholders (IBM-Rational $2.1B cash payout); (4) strategic capital investments with MA value-add, notably Intel's multi-billion-dollar xScale ARM business in the former Digital Equipment Hudson plant. Style: brief, wry, tonally characteristic Kastner — 'all in all, 2002 was like a root canal — best forgotten as soon as possible.'

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — MA high-tech 2002 job losses: 15,000 jobs lost in Massachusetts high-tech in 2002 — 5.7% of the high-tech job base — with many at the high-income white collar, middle manager, and executive level.
- **2003** — No 90-day turnaround: Based on the duration, breadth and severity of this recession, there will be no 90 day turnaround that gets us back to what we had been two or three years ago.
- **2003** — 2003 IT spending forecast: Aberdeen Group is predicting a global expansion in information technology spending in the 3%-4% range — back-end loaded in 2003 due to Q1 concerns about the economy and war.
- **2004** — 2004 MA startup wave: I expect 2004 to be a banner year for new and innovative products from companies now building ideas and prototypes in Massachusetts garages. Preferably, heated garages.
- **2003** — VC closed to new ventures: The funding for these new companies will come from angels, friends, and family, since the traditional VC sources of funds is largely closed out for new ventures at this time.
- **2003** — IBM-Rational cash payout: IBM acquisition of Rational Software resulting in cash payouts of $2.1 billion.
- **2003** — Level 3-Genuity deal: Genuity of Woburn was acquired by Level 3 Communications with the loss of 800 jobs.
- **2003** — Veritas-Precise acquisition: Precise Software was purchased by Veritas.
- **2003** — Intel xScale in Hudson: Intel is building a whole new multi-billion dollar xScale business around the ARM-architecture microprocessors manufactured in the old Digital Equipment plant in Hudson.
- **2003** — Root canal metaphor: All in all, 2002 was like a root canal — best forgotten as soon as possible. Yet in spite of these difficult changes to the Massachusetts high-tech situation, all is not doom and gloom.
- **2003** — IT spending outcome 2003: Global IT spending grew roughly 3-4% in 2003 (Gartner reported ~4%; IDC ~3%), matching Aberdeen/Kastner forecast.
- **2004** — MA startup wave 2004: 2004 did see elevated MA startup formation — especially in biotech (life sciences cluster), Web 2.0 (early social / consumer internet), and infrastructure software — validating the forecast.
- **2006** — Intel xScale divested: Intel sold the XScale ARM processor business to Marvell Technology in June 2006 for $600M, abandoning the ARM strategy Kastner had highlighted. Intel remained committed to x86; Hudson fab continued x86 production.
- **2003** — IBM-Rational closed: IBM-Rational acquisition closed February 21, 2003 for $2.1B in cash — consistent with Kastner remarks.
