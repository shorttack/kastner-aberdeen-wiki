# Information Security: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 overview of the enterprise information security market, sizing it at $4.285 billion in 1997 with a projected 24% CAGR to $10 billion by 2000. The report maps the security landscape into access-control and risk-management categories, identifies fragmentation and non-interoperability as the dominant pain points, and forecasts consolidation, the rise of VPNs, and the commoditization of standalone firewalls. Supplier profiles cover 14 vendors including Check Point, Axent, Network Associates, IBM, and VeriSign.

- **1997** — Second-largest security spending category 1997: Anti-virus and applet controls — $890 million

- **1997** — Fourth-largest security spending category 1997: Information access controls — $343 million
- **1998** — Standalone firewall market trajectory: commoditizing; fragmenting to high-end and low-end; embedding into routers
- **2001** — Standalone firewall commodity outcome: Prediction proved accurate; Check Point maintained high-end while low-end commoditized.
- **1998** — VPN adoption driver: replacing expensive leased lines; ROI in 1-3 months
- **1998** — IDS market trajectory: will go mainstream by 1999 with automated rules-engines
- **1998** — Anti-virus market growth rate: declining rate; moving to gateway; unit volumes shrinking
- **1998** — Market structure — two dominant security categories: access-control solutions vs. risk-management solutions
- **1998** — Expectation that Microsoft would emerge as security consolidator: IS decision makers do NOT expect Microsoft
- **1998** — Security buyer demand pattern: want consolidation; no single supplier dense enough yet
- **1998** — Security decision-making governance: key business executives commonly involved alongside IS executive
- **1998** — Web computing security requirement: Web computing requires security unlike client-server
- **1998** — Security supplier consolidation trend: larger suppliers with capital acquiring innovative smaller suppliers
- **1998** — Security professional services demand trajectory: IS will spend more on professional services for security
- **1998** — Plug-compatible security systems emergence: few will emerge before 2000 enabling integrated I&A and IAC
- **1998** — Vertical market security sophistication: financial services and telecom bypassing Big 5 consulting for in-house and specialist contractors

- **1998** — PKI/digital certificate adoption driver: web-based I&A will drive spending through 2000 as enterprise integrates customers and partners
