# Sequent's NUMA-Q 2000 Solutions: Reliability Availability Scalability Plus Technology Investment Protection

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Sequent Computer Systems' NUMA-Q 2000 server architecture, a fusion of SMP/MPP technologies using Intel Pentium Pro quads and the IQ-Link interconnect scalable to 252 processors. The study positions NUMA-Q 2000 as the technology leader for mission-critical OLTP and data warehousing requiring data-center-class scalability, reporting order-of-magnitude performance improvements over prior Symmetry systems and capacity for 10,000 concurrent user sessions. Aberdeen recommends Sequent for enterprise IS buyers seeking technology investment protection through proven scalability, predicts all hardware suppliers will adopt fusion technology within 2-5 years, and notes Sequent must continue delivering TPC benchmark proof points against competitors including Digital, HP, IBM, NCR, Tandem, Sun, and Data General.

- **1997** — NUMA-Q 2000 maximum scalability: 252 processors (63 quads) demonstrated in-lab; 32 GB/s bus bandwidth; 16 GB/s I/O bandwidth; 64 GB RAM; 100 terabytes storage
- **1997** — NUMA-Q 2000 performance vs prior Symmetry systems: Up to order-of-magnitude (10x) performance increase over previous high-end Symmetry systems
- **1997** — NUMA-Q 2000 Internet session capacity: Simulation of up to 10
- **1997** — NUMA-Q 2000 backup throughput: Up to 500 GB per hour backup performance
- **1997** — Mervyn's production data warehouse size: >1 terabyte in production; approaching 10 terabytes next year
- **1997** — Fortune-1000 database growth rate: Hundreds of databases 100GB+ implemented; growing 6-7x every 18 months
- **1997** — Mission-critical OLTP availability requirement: 99.99% availability required for mission-critical systems (less than 1 hour downtime/year)
- **1997** — Data warehouse growth rate: Large-scale DSS applications growing at rates exceeding 3x per year; expanding into terabyte range
- **1997** — Sequent competitive strategy: Fusion technology: SMP + MPP + commodity servers; experience-driven solutions; best-of-breed RDBMS packaging
- **1997** — Digital Equipment competitive approach: TruClusters: new clustering + MPP technology on AlphaServer; not quad-based
- **1997** — HP competitive approach: EPS31 connects up to 16 T600 servers via fiber channel switch; not quad-based
- **1997** — IBM competitive approach: RS/6000 SP emphasizes MPP; recently ported major RDBMSs; then later acquired Sequent itself
- **1997** — Sun competitive approach: Ultra Enterprise x000: big-bus SMP scaling; scale-up via larger shared bus
- **1997** — Data General competitive approach: cc-NUMA approach with Intel quads; focus on workstation-to-midrange buyers
- **1997** — Fusion technology industry adoption prediction: All hardware suppliers will move towards fusion technology within the next 2 to 5 years
- **1999** — Fusion/NUMA technology industry adoption outcome: IBM/HP/Sun adopted NUMA-like architectures within 2-5 years as predicted; IBM x440 (2002) used ccNUMA derived from Sequent acquisition
- **1997** — Sequent independent leadership prediction: Sequent will continue to differentiate and deliver large-scale user value over next few years
- **1999** — Sequent independence outcome: Sequent acquired by IBM for $810M in July 1999; product roadmap collapsed before acquisition; NUMA-Q discontinued
- **1997** — Aberdeen overall NUMA-Q 2000 verdict: Sequent solutions worth a close look for enterprise IS buyers; data-center-ready; technology investment protection validated
- **1997** — Technology investment protection criterion: proven record: Supplier must have proven record in high-performance open-systems technology
- **1997** — Technology investment protection criterion: clear roadmap: Supplier must have clear roadmap to leverage technology advantages in future
- **1997** — Technology investment protection criterion: least-cost migration: Server architecture must offer clear least-cost migration path for scaling
