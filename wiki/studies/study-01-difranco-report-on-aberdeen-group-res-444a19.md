# Report on Aberdeen Group Research into New Category for MaXLine

> DiFranco's board-level memo to Maxtor executives summarizing Aberdeen Group's commissioned research into defining 'fixed content' storage as a new category. The report argues that Aberdeen surveyed 70 Fortune 1000 companies and found sufficient market readiness to position MaXLine drives as the leading brand in an emerging midline storage category. DiFranco explicitly frames the strategy as replicating Aberdeen's CRM category creation for Siebel — with Maxtor as the anticipated category leader.


_Published 2003, author **Stephen DiFranco**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- DiFranco commissioned Aberdeen to research whether 'fixed content' storage could be defined as a new category and whether MaXLine could be positioned as the principal brand
- Aberdeen created the CRM category for Siebel generating a $2B revenue stream before any competitor could field a competing application
- $2B revenue stream attributed to Aberdeen-led CRM category creation
- 70 companies interviewed including Fortune 1000 companies and government agencies
- Approximately 50% of sample had revenues over $1B; mix included smaller companies and divisions
- 90% of respondents reported to their company's CEO or CFO — strategic thinkers responsible for data center operations and economics
- Most surveyed storage systems are 50-60% filled; expansion triggered at 80% fill
- At least 40% of respondents in all three operational categories expected to increase storage requirements
- Survey showed bi-polar response to whether data had variable usage frequency — key indicator that a segment of the market will advance concepts to others creating category opportunity
- Majority of sample indicated they have non-changing or 'fixed content' data
- HIPAA regulations under consideration will likely require US enterprises to create fixed-content data warehouses with real-time access
- [UNVERIFIED]
- SEC regulations under consideration will require real-time access to fixed-content data — driving enterprise demand for midline storage
- [UNVERIFIED]
- The mid-line category of HDDs can resolve both near-line backup and restore window issues with better mid-line software
- [UNVERIFIED]
- Surveyed companies (banking finance insurance) have greatest adoption of SAN networks and largest data centers
- Backup is limited by restore times being too long and backup windows too short — midline can resolve both
- With Aberdeen research + vocal customer advocates + leading research firm + system vendor support Maxtor can lead the midline category and position MaXLine as the brand of choice
- [UNVERIFIED]
- DiFranco explicitly states he has no interest in shrinking the SCSI TAM; midline should create new opportunities not replace on-line storage
- MaXLine and Atlas positioned as 'co-operative enterprise brands' — on-line and mid-line enterprise storage complement each other
- Aberdeen identified as expert in identifying and launching hardware and software industry categories — key selection criterion
- Plan to include publicity + speaking opportunities + customer leverage + analyst promotion to position MaXLine as principal midline brand
- Multiple government regulations (HIPAA + SEC) will require enterprises to create fixed-content data warehouses creating a compliance-driven market
