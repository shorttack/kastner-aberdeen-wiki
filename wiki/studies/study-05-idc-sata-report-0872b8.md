# Tired Storage and Capacity-Oriented Disk Drives (SATA) — Another Small Step Toward Data and Information Consolidation


## Short-horizon prescience (3-year / 5-year)

- **3-year verdict:** high — 3y Rule A: mean=3.63 over 35 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].
- **5-year verdict:** high — 5y Rule A: mean=3.97 over 35 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].

> IDC report based on 50 in-depth executive interviews across 10 North American vertical markets examining enterprise acceptability of SATA/capacity-oriented disk drives in storage arrays. The study provides quantitative forecasts for SATA storage system revenues and HDD shipments 2003-2007 forecasting SATA will reach a quarter or more of storage array terabytes over time. Key findings include that compliance requirements are the primary driver for fixed-content adoption and that software management tools are the primary inhibitor for tiered-storage deployment.


_Published 2003, author **Robert C. Gray, Dave Reinsel (IDC)**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Capacity-oriented technology will be embedded within arrays as a class of capacity managed through current tools `[ps=5]`
- Over time capacity-oriented storage shipments will reach a quarter or more of the storage array terabytes shipped `[ps=5]`
- 12% of total installed base TBs estimated as appropriate for fixed-content management applications — IDC believes this is low and will grow with compliance `[ps=5]`
- Most receptive applications are (1) mirrors and snapshots for data protection and (2) repositories for fixed content `[ps=4]`
- Lack of effective information management software and concurrent lack of information policies will be the major inhibitor for aggressive adoption of tiered storage `[ps=4]`
- Spending on IT in general and storage in specific expected to increase in 2004 — majority expect storage growth `[ps=4]`
- Raw survey results suggest 14% of all storage dollars in 2004 would be on SATA/high-capacity low-cost storage `[ps=4]`
- Number one SATA concern mentioned 30% of time: less reliable than SCSI or FC; performance second; 'too new' tied for second `[ps=4]`
- Industries with compliance requirements (Financial/Health/Pharmaceutical) have highest ratio of near-line tape to disk — highest long-term conversion opportunity for SATA `[ps=4]`
- Data protection (mirroring/snapshots/rapid restore) is most popular use for SATA; 29% of total installed base TBs targeted; many already engaged `[ps=4]`
- 60% of all reported installed terabytes (4563 TB total sample) are considered over-served — appropriate for capacity-oriented storage `[ps=4]`
- Total internal storage revenue: $6.8B in 2003 flat through 2007 (0.4% CAGR); TB growth: 364K TB (2003) to 1.29M TB (2007) at 37.2% CAGR; $/GB declining from $19 to $5 `[ps=4]`
- Market is moving now and can grow toward 50% of new capacity (per executive summary heading) `[ps=4]`
- Organizations with growing storage under cost pressure especially those with compliance requirements requiring permanent retention and instant retrieval: Financial/Transportation/Pharmaceutical `[ps=4]`
- Biggest market is for classes of capacity within storage arrays currently being sold; only minority want separate array products `[ps=4]`
- Data integrity requires RAID-5 for SATA systems; no compromise in uptime acceptable; IDC recommends products withstand 2 drive failures without application interruption `[ps=4]`
- Centera reported at application level to be one-third the performance of conventional RAID-based architecture by enterprise interviewees; indexing features reported missing `[ps=4]`
- Most organizations lack active information management policies; everyone considers their data most important; no incentives in place; some considering charge-back models for storage `[ps=4]`
- Functional SANs now 3+ years old; users actively looking for better value; many transforming one-size-fits-all storage strategy; tier-2 applications being migrated to appropriate arrays `[ps=4]`
- There is no place in the datacenter for lower availability at the application level simply because the storage uses capacity-oriented technology `[ps=3]`
- Strong tendency in corporate IT to lean toward status quo rather than adopt fundamentally different technology; ATA in enterprise is fundamental departure from SCSI/FC status quo `[ps=3]`
- Compliance requirements are setting the stage; organizations with clear tiering and compliance efforts already implementing document management for compliance; this will be first source level of metadata for true data tiering and ILM `[ps=3]`
- There is not currently enough space for multiple classes/tiers of storage products based on SATA — market should be analyzed as single tier `[ps=2]`
- Capacity-oriented disk storage poses little near or medium term threat to tape; in fact larger disk capacity means more data to transfer to tape for archiving `[ps=2]`
- Avoid: insurance industry; public sector without oversight or competition; highly profitable niche markets without cost pressures — hostile to capacity-oriented story `[ps=2]`
