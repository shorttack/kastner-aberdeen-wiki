# The HSP Market: Are You Ready for the Shakeup?

> This Aberdeen Group InSight analyzes the hosting service provider market during the 2001 downturn and evaluates which providers were best positioned to survive. It argues that profitability pressure, capital scarcity, operational execution, market execution, and customer stability would reorder HSP leadership over the following 12 to 18 months.


_Published 2001, author **Dana Tardelli**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Providers with rich coffers, loyal customers, or scalable operating processes would remain standing. `[ps=5]`
- WorldCom and Cable & Wireless strengthened hosting presence through acquisitions of Digex and Digital Island. `[ps=5]`
- Entry-level hosting providers faced a longer growth curve to critical mass than originally suspected. `[ps=4]`
- The hosting services market was at a crossroads after five years of evolution. `[ps=3]`
- The investing community had turned on the hosting sector as a whole. `[ps=3]`
- The next 12 to 18 months would prove difficult for hosting providers. `[ps=3]`
- Big telecoms and IT outsourcers would likely be the largest beneficiaries of the downturn as customers sought stability. `[ps=3]`
- Well-funded second-generation pure plays would emerge from the downturn with a business model and reputation that could disrupt the landscape. `[ps=3]`
- IBM Global Services, Digex, and Qwest were the few competitors positioned comfortably atop the market. `[ps=2]`
- IBM Global Services, Qwest, and WorldCom/Digex received positives across financial strength, internal execution, market execution, and client base. `[ps=2]`
- Aberdeen maintained optimism about the market upside despite near-term stress. `[ps=0]`
- Inflow and Relera each had more than $300 million in funding. `[ps=0]`
- Financial strength
- Ability to execute internally
- Ability to execute in the market
- Stability of existing client base
