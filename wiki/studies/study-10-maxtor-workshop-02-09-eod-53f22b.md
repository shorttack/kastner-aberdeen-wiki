> Full slide deck from the February 13 2004 Maxtor midline category creation workshop at Shrewsbury MA. Covers Aberdeen's ILM and Pools of Storage framework, competitive analysis of Seagate/WD/Hitachi, the 10-step category creation methodology, a detailed market awareness-building roadmap with quarterly milestones, and competitive positioning analysis of MaXLine vs Atlas disk drives.


_Published 2004, author **Aberdeen Group (Peter Kastner, David Hill, John Boyne)**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- ILM is a next-decade initiative; no single vendor can deliver on ILM vision anytime soon `[ps=5]`
- Unlike ILM software, Pools of Storage can be implemented now; has value proposition beyond TCO/ROI `[ps=4]`
- SCSI drives increasing capacity to near midline; PC ATA drives in RAID offering comparable availability at lower cost `[ps=4]`
- Midline storage includes enterprise-class high-capacity ATA disks that bridge the gap between high-use high-performance FC/SCSI disk arrays and magnetic tape storage `[ps=4]`
- 1.Define 2.Differentiate 3.Debunk 4.Define argument/position/messages 5.Deliver to influencers 6.Create awareness 7.Evangelize 8.Measure 9.Let influencers take over 10.Nurture `[ps=4]`
- A category is a distinct market segment which is watched and measured independently of other segments by market influencers `[ps=4]`
- Atlas: high I/Os/high cost/mission-critical; MaXLine: moderate I/Os/high capacity/moderate cost/not mission-critical `[ps=4]`
- 1.ILM 2.Pools of Storage 3.Disk Drives; strategy is to drive buying discussion to Pools of Storage in 2004 while nodding to ILM `[ps=4]`
- If it's measured and reported as a separate category it will likely become a separate category `[ps=4]`
- WD should be positioned as midline competitor; already refers to Raptor as Enterprise Serial ATA; press doesn't need to understand subtleties `[ps=3]`
- ILM will come to be the big storage-related mindshare and market battle of the decade `[ps=2]`
- Complex ILM-based storage circa 2008; storage-related labor hours on production systems reduced by 80% `[ps=2]`
- Maxtor: FC/midline/nearline; Hitachi: FC/not interested in midline/nearline; Seagate: FC/not needed/nearline; WD: enterprise SATA/could migrate/nearline `[ps=2]`
- Seagate will deny existence of midline because midline would threaten its hegemony in FC disk `[ps=1]`
- Hitachi has IBM heritage bringing technical respect but too many diverse interests to focus on midline `[ps=1]`
- 50% of IT practitioners recognize midline category by Q4 2004 `[ps=1]`
