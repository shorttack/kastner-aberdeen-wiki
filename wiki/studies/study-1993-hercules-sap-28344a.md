# Aberdeen Group Recommendation: HP 9000 Series 800 for SAP R3 Implementation at Hercules, Inc.

> Aberdeen Group evaluates three RISC/Unix platform suppliers — Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC), IBM, and Hewlett-Packard — for Hercules Inc.'s planned SAP R3 implementation and recommends HP 9000 Series 800 as the sole supplier based on five criteria: breadth of product line, supplier experience, Unix stability, upgradeable RISC processor, and ISV support. The report advises against Hercules' planned two-tier architecture and recommends a three-tier-plus topology for distributed enterprise computing. Aberdeen also warns that the platform decision will be 'winner-take-all' for 3-5 years, making the selection consequential.


_Published 1993, author **Peter S. Kastner / Aberdeen Group (signed by John R. Logan, VP)**, type **consulting-report**._


## Top observations

- Hercules should implement SAP R3 on HP 9000 Series 800 as sole supplier
- Implement three-tier-plus topology instead of planned two-tier; two-tier has 'fatal flaws' for distributed enterprises
- Decision will be winner-take-all; Hercules committed to one hardware supplier for next 3-5 years if selection made June 1993
- 45%+ market share in commercial RISC/Unix systems
- 15% market share in commercial RISC/Unix systems
- HP 9000 Series 800 was original targeted hardware platform for SAP R3 redevelopment
- Original target: all R3 modules by mid-1994; rewriting proprietary mainframe database to Unix required Oracle adoption
- Applications grow at 30-40% per year for first three years after initial SAP R3 installation
- Still beta — not ready for production; ISVs and early adopters confirmed; no TPC-A results available
- Production yields mostly 133-200 MHz; unable to produce 200 MHz in quantities; first-generation chip with no proven next-gen
- 550 applications stated by ISVs as planned ports as of March 1993; multiuser support not a priority without demand
- Strongly recommends Hercules NOT select DEC as supplier of choice for SAP R3
- Not highly stable according to user reports; major reason few large enterprises deployed large RS/6000 counts; new Toronto AIX development center expected to produce more stable AIX/6000 before end 1993
- End of RIOS processor visible; entire IBM product line (PC + RS/6000 + AS/400 + mainframes) migrating to PowerPC 600
- RS/6000 is viable but relatively poor choice vs HP 9000 Series 800; multiple deficiencies outweigh advantages
- 7 generations of PA-RISC implementations; Hitachi developing high-end next-gen; Samsung developing low-end
- HP 890-400 has more throughput capability than Hercules' current IBM 3090-200 mainframe
- HP is RISC/Unix reference port for both SAP and Oracle; Oracle's most important multiuser platform in revenue
- Only supplier with both high-end and midrange SMP; broadest product line; granular midrange by processor speed
- Longest history of Unix commitment; HP PSO claimed world's largest Unix services organization
- HP-UX in top tier for Unix stability; OpenView extensions shared with industry accelerated stable Unix acceptance
- 7 generations of PA-RISC; proven track record; Hitachi and Samsung developing next-gen implementations
- SAP and Oracle reference port; majority of ISVs for multiuser Unix marketplace
- Hercules goal to remove mainframe by Q1 1996; achievable with HP 9000 Series 800 and SAP R3
- HP dominated commercial RISC/Unix through late 1990s; acquired Compaq (including DEC/Tandem) in 2002 for $25B
