# PyramidOLTP Overview

> This Aberdeen Group presentation frames mid-1990s enterprise computing with its Three Tier Plus model and then profiles the leading commercial midrange RISC/UNIX suppliers. It combines market-share and revenue sizing with vendor-by-vendor strengths, challenges, and buying guidance for IBM, Hewlett-Packard, Digital Equipment, Sun Microsystems, and AT&T GIS/NCR. The deck is also notable for explicit forward-looking claims about RS/6000 momentum, UltraSPARC, Digital large in-memory databases, and NCR's Intel server role.


_Published 1995, author **Wayne T. Kernochan; Robert J. Sakakeeney**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Restructuring leaves customers uncertain about sales coverage, support continuity, and product commitments, while limited Alpha UNIX critical mass and weak near-term revenues raise viability concerns. `[ps=5]`
- 1996 will be an important product year for Sun because of the UltraSPARC 64-bit rollout. `[ps=5]`
- Global support, broad product range, customer intimacy, deep services, and broad third-party support make IBM a scale player in enterprise midrange computing. `[ps=4]`
- IBM must improve processor leadership, re-energize the field, win leading-edge buyers, work better with ISVs, and compete more effectively against software solution suppliers. `[ps=4]`
- Customers value IBM support and relationships, VARs use RS/6000 where AS/400 is too costly, and IBM's surrounding software stack matters as much as raw price/performance. `[ps=4]`
- Expect RS/6000 growth to continue in 1996 as datacenter managers experiment with Tier-2 surround strategies. `[ps=4]`
- HP risks missing opportunities through lean execution, must evolve beyond box selling, lacks a very high-end RISC/UNIX option, and faces Intel/NT pressure at the low end. `[ps=4]`
- HP is viewed as the mainframe-alternative leader and a highly respected company, but customers should not treat it as invincible and should expect IBM and Sun in most competitive deals. `[ps=4]`
- Development of large in-memory databases could revolutionize enterprise OLTP and complex DSS. `[ps=4]`
- AT&T GIS/NCR is positioned around open systems spanning departmental WorldMark servers to Teradata decision support, using Unix and Intel rather than RISC. `[ps=4]`
- NCR is losing ground to RISC vendors and Compaq, lacks high-end OLTP performance leadership, may see erosion from financial uncertainty and market pullback, and has mishandled some Teradata accounts. `[ps=4]`
- Mainframe and massively parallel systems anchor production and decision-support tiers while PC, workstation, LAN, and branch tiers distribute departmental and point-of-sale workloads. `[ps=3]`
- Sun is cast as the open-systems client/server originator with strong innovation, broad application availability, favorable reseller economics, and unusually strong ISV relationships. `[ps=3]`
- Sun combines a large application base, strong alternate channels, and attractive entry price/performance, while gaining commercial client/server traction through networking and application development. `[ps=3]`
- NCR offers broad compatible server coverage, strong TPC price/performance, a stable UNIX implementation, and strong vertical-market presence in retail and banking. `[ps=3]`
- Alpha performance and aggressive pricing give Digital leading price/performance, while OSF/1 standards breadth and NT-on-Alpha plans reinforce an enterprise open-platform story. `[ps=2]`
- Digital is regaining respect with the industry and Wall Street, remains an excellent engineering company, and is deepening strategic commitments with Oracle. `[ps=2]`
- Sun suffers from limited direct support, few production OLTP references, historically painful OS transitions, questionable real-world scalability, and declining influence in standards groups. `[ps=2]`
- Intel is depending on NCR to be the premier supplier of P6-based servers for enterprise computing. `[ps=1]`
