# If Workgroup Collaboration is a Problem, How Are We Going to Collaborate With the World?

> This Aberdeen Group presentation frames mid-1990s groupware as a rapidly growing market that is about to expand beyond departmental messaging into enterprise, intranet, and internet-enabled collaboration. It mixes market sizing and adoption data with Peter Kastner's guidance on strategy, requirements, best practices, and ROI. The deck also serves as a vendor-facing assessment of Oracle InterOffice as an extensible, server-centric collaboration platform positioned for that transition.


_Published 1996, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Extends face-to-face communication across organizational and geographic boundaries while eliminating the requirement for face-to-face interaction. `[ps=5]`
- Geographically dispersed enterprises create persistent demand for better collaboration. `[ps=5]`
- Future groupware systems must look outward to customers, suppliers, and even competitors rather than only inward to a workgroup. `[ps=5]`
- The Internet will expand collaboration outside the enterprise and become the backbone for worldwide collaboration. `[ps=5]`
- Groupware is a starting point for applications rather than the finish line, so infrastructure is a better long-term investment than a workgroup-only point solution. `[ps=5]`
- A simple ROI spreadsheet is the wrong tool for collaboration because benefits are broad, diffuse, and often appear negative in narrow models. `[ps=5]`
- Electronic mail already ties worldwide companies together. `[ps=4]`
- Workflow can automate routine processes, remove unnecessary steps, and offset reduced clerical support. `[ps=4]`
- Cycle-time pressure pushes firms toward virtual organizations and round-the-clock project teams. `[ps=4]`
- Leveraging organizational knowledge is becoming a competitive weapon. `[ps=4]`
- Electronic mail is the logical first step in collaboration rollout, and top executives should use it first; IBM is cited as an example. `[ps=4]`
- Key requirements are scalability, platform and network breadth, open standards, internet awareness, and application extensibility. `[ps=4]`
- Used collaboration to extend management and control across widely separated sites. `[ps=4]`

- Ideal groupware should be portable, high-performance, scalable, easy to use, efficient in protocol design, and strong in distributed management, distributed security, and legacy interoperability. `[ps=4]`
- InterOffice takes a server-centric approach built around fewer, larger servers that are easier to administer than a server-per-workgroup model. `[ps=4]`
- Many suppliers, including Novell, lack a clear vision of future collaboration requirements. `[ps=3]`
- There will be two types of organizations in the future: the quick and the dead. `[ps=3]`
- An enterprise groupware strategy cannot come from the IT department alone and requires senior-management direction. `[ps=3]`
- InterOffice can add collaboration functions such as email, workflow, and scheduling to specific business applications. `[ps=3]`
- Oracle7 is the robust, proven, scalable data repository underneath InterOffice. `[ps=3]`
- InterOffice offers broad standards coverage across platforms and is ready for both internet and intranet deployment. `[ps=2]`
- InterOffice gains value from integration with Oracle RDBMS, ConText, video services, and OLAP. `[ps=2]`
- Integration leads the market by 6-12 months. `[ps=2]`
- Standards such as MAPI let InterOffice work with other vendors' best-of-breed and legacy products. `[ps=2]`
