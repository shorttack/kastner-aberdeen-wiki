# Emerging Technologies: Assessing Strategic Benefits

> Aberdeen Group presentation for the Vision 2000 conference (May 1996) analyzing enterprise superserver architectures. The study compares symmetric multiprocessing (SMP), clustering, and Sequent's emerging NUMA-Q technology, arguing that NUMA will extend Intel-standard computing beyond traditional mainframe capabilities while preserving software investments. Covers the business case for enterprise IT investment, multi-tier application architectures, and technology planning through 2001.


_Published 1996, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **presentation**._


## Top observations

- Technology equals productivity; superior use of technology leads to superior corporate returns
- Direct IS to lower SG&A by 20% for 70% PBT improvement; lower COGS by 5% for 27% PBT improvement
- Multi-tier (3-tier) client-server architectures demand enterprise superservers
- Good to 4 processors; fair to 8; few do more than 10 well; Sequent does very well
- Well understood technology with new chip-set accelerators and inexpensive engineering
- Today's system software and customer applications are not cluster-enabled; require re-engineering
- Message-passing burns CPU and memory cycles; messaging between nodes is very complex technology requiring time to mature
- Aimed at world's toughest commercial computing problems: OLTP, complex DSS, and messaging
- Engineering elegance delivering scalability with minimal overhead and no application software changes
- Will be a benchmark for high-end commercial computing leadership into the 21st century
- NUMA-Q will extend Intel-standard to well beyond even traditional mainframes while preserving software investments
- 3x-5x increases in demand for information resources over next 5 years (by 2001)
- 10x-15x more client and server CPU horsepower by 2001
- Cheap, infinite network bandwidth with ATM by 2001
- Like 1996, the information highway will still be under construction in 2001
- Four levels: Level 1 Client, Level 2 Workgroup, Level 3 OLTP, Level 4 IS Enterprise Operations
- Four-step process: Decide, Analyze, Transact, Report -- mapped to DSS, Brain, OLTP/Workflow, Messaging
- There is no time for application downtime -- the computer system is the business
- 3% of total revenue (part of SG&A)
- 35% of total revenue
- Good scaling to 4 processors; fair to 8; diminishing returns beyond 10
- 20% SG&A reduction yields 70% PBT improvement
- Traditional SMP and cluster implementations are ill-suited to efficient scalability
- Enterprises harshly driven to cut SG&A while raising productivity; new multi-tier architectures demand new breed of enterprise superservers
- Huge advantages to IS Buy vs. Make strategy
