# CA's Unicenter TNG Framework: Entry-Level for the Industry's Best Enterprise Management Software Solution

> Aberdeen Group analyst Peter S. Kastner profiles Computer Associates International's Unicenter TNG Framework, a free/bundled subset of Unicenter TNG announced July 14, 1997 at the CA World conference. The Framework provides auto-discovery, an object repository, 2D/3D mapping, event management, scheduling, and virus scanning—enabling 10-minute enterprise network discovery. Kastner analyzes CA's strategic shift to a 'razor/razor-blade' distribution model, bundling the Framework with server hardware from nine partners including HP and Digital Equipment, to establish it as a de facto enterprise management standard against IBM Tivoli, HP OpenView, and Sun Solstice.


_Published 1997, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **analyst-profile**._


## Top observations

- After difficult start-up phase, OpenVision sold earlier in 1997; acquired by Veritas Software `[ps=5]`
- CA revenues grew to $6.1B by 2000; Unicenter reached one-quarter of overall revenue; however CA faced major accounting fraud scandal 2004-2006; HP OpenView not permanently displaced; Tivoli remained competitive; CA acquired by Broadcom 2018 for $18.9B `[ps=5]`
- Library of all collected device information including version, release category, planning/upgrade/expansion properties `[ps=4]`
- Calendar-based job scheduling for cross-platform automated operations `[ps=4]`
- Active server-based scanning at email/attachment entry point; monitors shared folders `[ps=4]`
- Sun capably supports Solstice customers but 'more interested in partnering for future technology than building own new products' `[ps=4]`
- 2D geographic topology maps + 3D virtual-reality floor plan maps showing physical device locations within buildings `[ps=3]`
- Stores policies/operational thresholds; flags unusual conditions; updates repository as configurations change; periodic virus scanning `[ps=3]`
- Market consolidated but around IBM Tivoli, HP Operations Manager (OpenView successor), and CA; Microsoft also entered with SCOM; open-source Nagios/Prometheus disrupted by 2010 `[ps=3]`
- Razor/razor-blade model: give away Framework (razor) with server hardware; sell Unicenter TNG upgrades (razor blades) to mass market `[ps=2]`
- Serious competitor with large installed base but has 'clearly lost momentum'; HP has not decided whether to reinvigorate OpenView product development `[ps=2]`
- Framework will become de facto enterprise management standard; Unicenter TNG will earn industry leadership position and competitors' respect `[ps=2]`
- Framework's aggressive merchandising will result in competitive consolidation; fewer but stronger choices will make IT and ISV decision-making easier `[ps=2]`
- CA actively lining up SAP AG to use Framework as application management base `[ps=2]`
- 10 minutes to install on NT server and discover all enterprise devices (Aberdeen's own installation) `[ps=1]`
- CA's most obvious competitor; TME requires remote-network configuration estimates and 'days spent in network and asset evaluation' vs. CA's 10-minute Framework setup `[ps=1]`
- Those deploying distributed systems at 1000 bank branches will 'cry for joy' at Framework's easy installation and self-discovery `[ps=1]`
- CA Framework enables typical IS administrators to configure enterprise management in minutes from CD; vs. Tivoli's days for network and asset evaluation `[ps=1]`
- $1 billion annual run rate (industry-leading) `[ps=0]`
- 7,000+ customers `[ps=0]`
- Identifies all network devices including IP devices for virtual network connections `[ps=0]`
- More than 1.5 million copies of Framework to ship with systems in next year per CA estimates `[ps=0]`
- Third-largest independent software company; $4B+ annual revenue; revenues grew from ~$1.2B to $4B in seven years `[ps=0]`
- CA acquired Digital Equipment's Polycenter management products and tightly integrated them with CA management console `[ps=0]`
- $4+ billion annual revenue (Unicenter TNG at $1B run rate = ~25% of total) `[ps=0]`
