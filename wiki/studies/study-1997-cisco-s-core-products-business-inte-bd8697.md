> Aberdeen Group Company Profile (January 1997) of Cisco Systems, Inc., examining how the undisputed leader in enterprise internetworking has evolved from a router-only vendor to a comprehensive end-to-end networking solutions provider. The report documents Cisco's acquisition strategy including the 1996 StrataCom acquisition, its IOS software as the de facto internetworking standard, and two key new technologies: NetFlow Switching (enhancing router performance with per-flow statistics and QoS) and Tag Switching (combining Layer 2/3 switching submitted to IETF). Aberdeen analyzes Cisco's positioning for ISPs and the emerging Gigabit Switch Router (GSR/12000 Series). The report correctly identifies Cisco's strengths (IOS investment protection, comprehensive product line, channel execution) and key challenge (can Cisco maintain leadership if switching replaces routing?). Aberdeen's answer: yes, through technology assimilation and Tag Switching.

## Top observations

- Establishes flow between source/destination on first packet; subsequent packets switched not routed; exports per-conversation statistics; enables per-flow QoS and security `[ps=5]`
- End-to-end internetworking solutions provider; assimilating switching into historically router-centric portfolio; acquiring promising technologies ($40B market cap as acquisition currency) `[ps=5]`
- Tag Switching evolved into MPLS (RFC 3031, January 2001); MPLS became fundamental to carrier backbone networks and VPNs; still essential to carrier infrastructure as of 2026; Tag Switching itself obsolete but its successor dominant `[ps=5]`
- Cisco sold 1,000 GSR 12000 units within first year (by November 1998); became major commercial success; backbone routers for major ISPs and carriers; still referenced in carrier networks `[ps=5]`
- NetFlow v5 became widely deployed standard for network flow monitoring; NetFlow v9 (Flexible NetFlow) became basis for IETF IPFIX standard (RFC 7011, 2013); still fundamental to network monitoring and security analytics as of 2026 `[ps=5]`
- Implemented in 7000/7500 series routers; per-port enabling; flow cache expires inactive flows; 10% overhead on router processing `[ps=4]`
- Combines Layer 2 switching performance with Layer 3 routing scalability; eliminates connection set-up congestion; pre-establishes accelerated paths; enables ATM switch participation in Layer 3 routing `[ps=4]`
- Multigigabit crossbar switching fabric sustaining 80 Gbps; high port density; interfaces OC-3, OC-12, OC-48; Gigabit Ethernet, Packet over SONET, ATM; fully distributed forwarding; topology-driven route-table switching `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen predicts Cisco will maintain dominant position by assimilating switching into its portfolio; IOS investment protection retains installed base; management committed to full-service provider status `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen views GSR addition as both appropriate and necessary for ISPs and large enterprise; will complement the 7500 router addressing different needs and markets `[ps=4]`
- Cisco maintained dominant enterprise networking market position through 2000s and beyond; peaked at $555B market cap (March 2000); retained IOS as de facto standard; still dominant in enterprise networking as of 2026 `[ps=4]`
- Cisco IOS remained the de facto internetworking standard through the 2000s; evolved into IOS XE and IOS XR for modern routing platforms; still the basis of Cisco's networking OS as of 2026 `[ps=4]`
- Once a mission-critical distributed client-server application is deployed, high-speed network performance is required to sustain it; CEOs recognize network investments create strategic competitive and shareholder value `[ps=4]`
- Increased throughput, improved traffic engineering, bandwidth management, QoS support, differentiated Level 3 services, platform and transport flexibility, cost advantage `[ps=4]`
- More than 80% of Internet backbone traffic carried by Cisco routers `[ps=3]`
- Cisco IOS described as de facto standard in internetworking; unifying thread across disparate networks; embedded in hardware, server software, client OS `[ps=3]`
- Tag Switching promises significant features for internet and enterprise scaling; submitted to IETF; uncertainty about whether it will achieve IOS-level industry-standard enthusiasm `[ps=3]`
- Cisco acquired StrataCom for $4.67B (June 1996); first true integration was BPX+Stratm Technology+Tag Switching; StrataCom technology became foundation for Cisco's ISP/carrier product line `[ps=3]`
- High-performance ASICs for ATM switching; complete ABR support; virtual path trunking; weighted fair queuing; multicast; higher density lower cost ATM switching `[ps=2]`
- 80% of LAN traffic formerly local; now 80% crosses backbone with only 20% remaining in subnet; imbalance demanding correction `[ps=2]`
- $4 billion in sales during last four quarters `[ps=0]`
- $40 billion market capitalization `[ps=0]`
- Over 40% of the LAN switching market `[ps=0]`
- Not yet available for field trials in early 1997; submitted to IETF; endorsed by numerous third parties `[ps=0]`
- Four-fold increase in system capacity from new high-performance system processors and high-speed interfaces `[ps=0]`
