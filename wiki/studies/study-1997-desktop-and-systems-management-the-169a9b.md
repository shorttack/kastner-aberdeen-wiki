# Desktop and Systems Management The HP Way: Using Open Standards To Lower Costs and Complexity

> Aberdeen Group evaluation of HP's DMI-based desktop management strategy. Argues HP's open-standards Vectra Corporate PC and TopTOOLS suite will help enterprises reduce PC total cost of ownership by 50% or more and give HP a competitive edge over Compaq's proprietary Intelligent Manageability approach. DMI certification predicted to become a de facto enterprise standard in 1997.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **Vendor Strategy Profile**._


## Top observations

- DMI reached end-of-life on March 31 2005; superseded by CIM and Windows Management Instrumentation (WMI); DMTF ended active development December 31 2003 `[ps=5]`
- HP merged with Compaq in 2002 for $25 billion; Vectra line discontinued; replaced by HP Evo (based on Compaq platform); DMI competition moot `[ps=5]`
- IS managers require three things: better data integration between management environments; better automation of manual tasks; better PC manageability especially remote configuration `[ps=5]`
- Microsoft shipped Windows Management Instrumentation (WMI) with Windows 2000; became the dominant desktop management API; effectively superseding DMI in enterprise environments `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen projects HP DMI-based management combined with best practices could reduce total cost of PC ownership by 50% or more `[ps=3]`
- IS managers have PCs on 3-5 year tax depreciation schedules; need to extend life by 2 years beyond 3-year major technology cycles `[ps=3]`
- DMI certification will become a de facto industry standard for desktop management; Aberdeen expects DMI to be a major competitive differentiator in 1997 `[ps=2]`
- HP Vectra BIOS flash capability allows IS managers to extend PC life by 2 years beyond typical 3-year major technology cycles `[ps=2]`
- IS buyers have made DMI adherence a checklist item for PC purchases; failure to implement means no sale `[ps=2]`
- HP was the first major supplier to introduce DMI-compliant PCs starting in 1992 `[ps=1]`
- HP TopTOOLS is the only product capable of upgrading BIOS of multiple PCs simultaneously from a central console `[ps=1]`
- HP expects to reduce desktop management costs by at least 20-25% (approximately $3,000 per PC) in the near term `[ps=0]`
- HP Vectra Corporate PC features more than 350 total DMI attributes `[ps=0]`
- DMTF has more than 122 PC hardware and software supplier members beyond steering committee `[ps=0]`
- HP OpenView Professional Suite manages small-to-medium companies with 10-2,000 nodes; OpenView Network Node Manager supports LANs and WANs with more than 250 nodes `[ps=0]`
- TopTOOLS includes AssetTOOLS (inventory), ConfigTOOLS (configuration/troubleshoot), FlashTOOL (remote BIOS upgrade), SafeTOOLS (health monitoring), ProtectTOOLS (security) `[ps=0]`
- Vectra PC platform prices declined significantly in 1996 due to CPU and memory price reductions `[ps=0]`
- Average cost to manage a single networked PC is 2x to 3x its original purchase price
- HP added approximately 300 DMI manageable attributes plus 65 unique HP DMI attribute extensions to Vectra PC line
- HP will position its DMI-based desktop management environment as a major differentiator to surpass Compaq in enterprise PC sales
- Compaq Intelligent Manageability is described as a proprietary solution requiring Compaq hardware and an invitation program for third-party ISVs
- NightDIRECTOR uses Magic Packet technology (co-developed with AMD) to remotely power on PCs for maintenance without disturbing end users
- Magic Packet technology evolved into Wake-on-LAN (WOL) standard; still widely supported in enterprise PCs and servers as of 2024
- DMTF sponsors annual DMI compliance bake-off to certify supplier compliance; certification is a competitive differentiator
- HP OpenView rebranded as HP Software Division products in 2007 after acquisitions of Mercury Interactive and Peregrine Systems; eventually sold to MicroFocus; now part of OpenText
