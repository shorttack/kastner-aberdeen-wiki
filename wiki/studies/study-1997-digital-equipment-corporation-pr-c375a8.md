# Digital Equipment Corporation — Telecommunications Market Profile

> Aberdeen Group profiled Digital Equipment Corporation's telecom industry strategy in May 1997, examining DIGITAL's four solution areas (Intelligent Network, TMN, Customer Care & Billing, Broadband Services) and five key ISV partners: CGI, CMG, Dialogic, Kenan Systems, and Vienna Systems. Aberdeen concluded DIGITAL was well-positioned for telecom market expansion via Alpha platform strengths and partner ecosystem.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **Vendor Profile**._


## Top observations

- 2,000,000+
- 7x24 worldwide; 2-hour response time
- 50% faster on Alpha
- Billing rate processing not possible with 32-bit Intel
- Proactive fault detection before customer visibility
- European market leader for messaging
- Software supports multiple gateways; open APIs; TAPI
- Unified multi-service billing from single mechanism
- Partner-led ecosystem; systems integration focus
- Intelligent Network, TMN, CC&B, Broadband Services
- Start-up; relied on DIGITAL brand for credibility
- Supporting US customers; expanding via partnerships; Ericsson network certification
- Computing, communications, new media convergence
- Slow due to legacy analog incompatibility
- Increasing use in wireless/cellular for roaming and phone personality
- Well-positioned with best-in-class partners
- Will expand presence in key telco segments
- Vienna products to ship to DIGITAL customers Spring 1997
- CMG service platform soon to be certified by Ericsson
- Alpha will differentiate DIGITAL from 32-bit competitors
- Compaq acquired DEC for $9.6 billion in June 1998, confirmed across multiple study records.
- Lucent acquired Kenan Systems for approximately $1.48 billion in stock in 1999. Kenan was later transferred to Amdocs. The acquisition validated Aberdeen's view of Kenan as premium telecom billing software.
- Nokia acquired Vienna Systems for approximately $90 million in December 1998, entering the IP telephony gateway market as predicted.
- CMG and Logica merged in 2002 to form LogicaCMG, creating a ~$3 billion revenue IT services firm. LogicaCMG was later acquired by CGI Group in 2012.
- Compaq announced Alpha processor phase-out in 2001 and sold Alpha IP to Intel. HP continued limited Alpha sales until the last system sold in April 2007.
