# IBM's Solution-Centric Global Healthcare Industry Practice - Just What the Doctor Ordered

> Aberdeen Group's Volume 10 Number 6 Viewpoint analyzes IBM's Global Healthcare Industry practice and its strategy for becoming the trusted lead supplier for healthcare IT. The study examines three pivotal IBM strategic shifts under CEO Lou Gerstner: creation of 11 global industry groups, adoption of a services-and-solutions strategy (1996 services revenue of $15.9B, +24.8% YoY), and networked computing vision. Aberdeen evaluates IBM's Health Network Solutions portfolio across five areas (Foundation, Applications, Advanced Applications, MedSpeak, ClinWare), its ISV partnership ecosystem, and Global Services capabilities. Customer cases include Arkansas Blue Cross/Blue Shield, Kaiser Permanente, and Allina Health Systems. Aberdeen concludes IBM is well-positioned as lead supplier partnering with ISVs to reduce implementation risk for risk-averse healthcare CIOs.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **viewpoint**._


## Top observations

- $15.9 billion
- 24.8% YoY increase
- 200+ employees
- 3 factors: understaffed IS, ISV credibility gap, care-provider organizational transition
- ISVs will derive 20-40% of revenue from services to succeed
- IBM predicted to sustain healthcare leadership role via services + partner ecosystem
- Positive: reduces radiology report turnaround from days to minutes
- Addresses critical pharma need; streamlines data collection, decreases time-to-market
- ~450 physicians linked in health network covering ~half of state covered lives
- Notes-based system providing immediate secure access to high-risk patient data
- Provider directory, member services, health info, nurse line e-mail for plan members
- IBM sold Watson Health data/analytics products for ~$1B after $5B investment; 7000 staff at peak
- IBM spun out managed infrastructure services as Kyndryl in 2021
- Dragon Medical (Nuance) became market leader; IBM MedSpeak discontinued
- HITECH Act 2009 mandated EHR meaningful use; Epic and Cerner dominated market
- Platform + services integrator positioning; compete via ISV partnerships not solo product
- Country-management structure replaced with 11 vertical industry groups including Global Healthcare
- Majority of healthcare IS on proprietary mainframe/minicomputer; client-server not yet on all cylinders
- CIOs reluctant to work with ISVs; do not want to double a startup's revenues
- Combination of EIS, benchmark analytics, clinical performance tracking with APM consulting
