# Kodak Completes Wang Software Acquisition

> Aberdeen Group analyzes Kodak's $260M acquisition of Wang Software to form Eastman Software Inc. The study examines synergies in workflow imaging COLD document management and HSM technology and forecasts dominant market position for the combined entity in document-intensive business solutions.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **Announcement Profile**._


## Top observations

- Eastman Software was sold before achieving dominant position; workflow market moved to SharePoint Lotus Notes and later BPM platforms; Eastman Software technology did not achieve predicted dominance `[ps=5]`
- Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2012; digital photography disrupted core film business; emerged in 2013 as much smaller commercial printing company `[ps=5]`
- HSM evolved into Information Lifecycle Management (ILM) and later cloud storage tiering; Eastman Software HSM did not achieve predicted role; Veritas StorageExec and IBM Tivoli became market leaders `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen forecasts HSM technology will play key role as organizations need access to digital images in multi-terabyte decision support systems `[ps=3]`
- Aberdeen believes Eastman Software is extremely well-positioned as independent Kodak subsidiary to expand leadership in document-intensive businesses `[ps=2]`
- Wang imaging technology embedded in Windows 95 and NT 4.0; HSM joint development continued with Eastman Software `[ps=2]`
- IDAR provides backbone of complete enterprise document life cycle solution combining imaging workflow COLD DM and HSM `[ps=2]`
- Eastman Software's workflow capabilities will position it as dominant force in desktop and departmental server market `[ps=1]`
- $16 billion multi-national corporation `[ps=0]`
- Kodak paid $260 million in cash for Wang's software business `[ps=0]`
- $1.4 billion annual revenue in fiscal year 1997 `[ps=0]`
- More than 100% revenue growth for last two years in Wang Software business unit `[ps=0]`
- Kodak sold troubled Eastman Software unit in August 2000 to private investment group; financial terms undisclosed but widely reported as significant loss on $260M investment `[ps=0]`
- Wang acquired Sigma Imaging Avail Systems and Vantage Technologies to enhance market position and accelerate NT platform transition `[ps=0]`
- Aberdeen field interviews indicate considerable enthusiasm from Wang Software customers resellers and partners about Kodak's document-imaging focus and additional R&D funding `[ps=0]`
- 700 employees worldwide post-acquisition
- 20000 customers in Kodak BIS installed base
- Windows XP/2000 did not further develop Wang/Eastman imaging integration; Microsoft shifted focus to SharePoint for document management rather than continuing Wang-era imaging integration
- COLD dramatically improves distribution options for service bureaus and supplements customers' existing COM capabilities
- COLD technology became obsolete by mid-2000s as internet-based document delivery and digital archiving replaced optical disk output; market largely gone by 2005
- Kodak has nine customer-focused business units; customer-centric rather than product-oriented view of market
- Document imaging and work management entering new stage of explosive growth; production-level capabilities now deliverable at lower price points to distributed departmental environments
