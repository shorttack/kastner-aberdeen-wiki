# Managing the People Dimension of Major Technology Transitions

> In September 1996, Hewlett-Packard engaged Aberdeen Group to conduct one-on-one interviews with senior IT executives and project leaders undergoing major technology transitions. The study identifies education and training as the critical enabler of successful technology adoption, documents barriers including time pressure and ROI measurement challenges, and finds that on-demand, customized training is preferred over classroom instruction. Author Susan Irving concludes that people and process challenges consistently exceed technical challenges in large-scale technology transitions.


_Published 1997, author **Susan Irving**, type **impact-brief**._


## Top observations

- HP commissioned Aberdeen interviews with IT executives undergoing major technology transitions, Sep 1996
- Education (communication, change management, process training, skills training) consistently named as critical enabler for technology adoption
- Without training: best case = underutilization; worst case = outright rejection of new system
- Education consistently short-shifted due to cost containment, competitive pressure, and day-to-day operating demands
- Traditional classroom training demand supplanted by universal appeal for on-demand, on-line, real-time, continuous access learning
- Classroom training seen as: too long, not specific enough, overwhelming detail unlikely to be retained
- Respondents demand training tailored to unique project requirements: 'I don't want a least-common-denominator one-size-fits-all approach'
- Decision-makers require trainers with both educational credentials AND implementation/operational expertise
- Prefer local training provider but want consistent global/national/regional delivery capability
- Project managers strongly believe in education value but rarely use ROI measures to demonstrate this to senior management
- Educational investments cut because: difficult to communicate effect of smaller education budget on project outcome
- Most executives found people and process issues far more challenging than technology issues during transitions
- Most successful projects implemented disciplined project plans that fully embraced and incorporated education tracks
- Leaders who deferred or eliminated education are 'playing catch up' on the actual technology roll-out
- HP engaged Aberdeen to produce research validating HP's training and education services offerings for enterprise technology transitions
- Aberdeen implicitly predicts on-demand, customized, online training will supplant classroom instruction for enterprise technology transitions
- E-learning market grew to $35B+ by 2011; corporate LMS market (Cornerstone, SuccessFactors, Saba) became mainstream; LinkedIn Learning, Coursera launched; on-demand fully replaced classroom as primary modality
- Implicit prediction: change management (people+process dimension) will become a recognized discipline alongside technical project management
- Prosci ADKAR model (1999), Kotter 8-step model (1996 book), ACMP founded 2011; change management became recognized profession with dedicated certifications and consulting practices worth $10B+
- One-on-one interviews with key project leaders and decision-makers, September 1996; qualitative primary research
- 'Humans are built to avoid anxiety so our goal was to get people through the transition with grace and poise' — executive respondent
