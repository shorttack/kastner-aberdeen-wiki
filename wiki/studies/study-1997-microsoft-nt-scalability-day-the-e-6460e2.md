# Microsoft NT Scalability Day: The Emperor Has No Clothes

- **5-year verdict:** high — 5y Rule A: mean=3.59 over 22 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].

> Peter S. Kastner of Aberdeen Group delivers a pointed critique of Microsoft's Scalability Day event in New York, where Bill Gates declared NT and BackOffice ready for any enterprise. Kastner dissects five benchmark demonstrations as unrealistic or misleading, argues NT remains best suited to departmental (not enterprise) applications, and warns that Microsoft's all-or-nothing platform claims risk opening a 'trust gap' with enterprise customers. He concludes Unix, the AS/400, and even the mainframe legitimately retain the enterprise high-end.


_Published 1997, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **impact-brief**._


## Top observations

- Aberdeen: NT jumped from workgroup to departmental (~250 users) by Nov 1995; NT Server 4.0 'a giant step forward' July 1996 `[ps=4]`
- Future release of SQL Server on future release of NT will support Very Large Memories `[ps=4]`
- NT and BackOffice require capable partner help for enterprise-class: high transaction rates, high availability, fault tolerance, mainframe connectivity, systems management `[ps=4]`
- Windows 2000/2003 substantially improved enterprise scalability; NT Server licenses grew 50% faster than competitors in 1998 per IDC; Windows eventually captured server market share from Unix but Unix/Linux maintained enterprise high-end into 2000s; Linux won server market by 2010s `[ps=4]`
- Unix maintained enterprise dominance through 2005; Windows servers achieved revenue parity with Unix by 2005 per IDC; Linux ultimately displaced both Unix and Windows NT in data centers by 2010s; Kastner's Unix defense proved correct for 2000-2005 window `[ps=4]`
- Microsoft pursuing 'all-Microsoft solutions' strategy from desktop to enterprise despite partner dependencies; schizophrenic about partnering `[ps=4]`
- 'Any business of any size can now run its enterprise applications on Microsoft software and industry-standard hardware' — Bill Gates at Scalability Day, New York `[ps=3]`
- Microsoft to sell satellite images over Internet starting October using future SQL Server version `[ps=3]`
- Unstated reason for Scalability Day: address continuing NT scalability debacles where line-of-business projects push NT beyond real-world capabilities `[ps=3]`
- 'Real enterprise high-end legitimately remains the province of Unix, the AS/400, and even the mainframe' `[ps=3]`
- Scalability Day demonstrations will 'distance the company even further from scalability reality' and 'open up a hard-to-close trust gap' `[ps=3]`
- 'Scant help for executives designing for today's IT projects' — much of Scalability Day demonstrations depended on next year's software `[ps=3]`
- 'Demonstrates performance of computer system large enough to manage daily banking activities of a quarter of the world's population' `[ps=2]`
- Single two-processor Pentium Pro with NT and IIS processed 100M hits in 24 hours — one-third greater than Microsoft's own site traffic `[ps=0]`
- Intel microprocessor performance improvements given 'scant credit' for NT scalability at Scalability Day `[ps=0]`
- 'Let's not be distracted by marketing hype from reality — or caught by the Big Lie' `[ps=0]`
- Exchange Server benchmark; all Internet POP3 and SMTP; workload spread over 24 hours; product not shipping until Q1-1998
- Oracle and Informix supported VLM on Digital Equipment's Unix on Alpha for over a year before Microsoft's announcement
- NT will be 'better in 1998 than it is now' but remains best suited to departmental applications; unable to scale to Fortune 500 enterprise loads in mid-1997
- Aberdeen had 'considerable difficulty finding sites with more than a few hundred users' on NT
- NT scalability will improve; many world's applications are now NT-capable but enterprise high-end requires Unix/AS400/mainframe for now
- Exchange 2000/2003 substantially improved scalability beyond hundreds-of-user limitation; became dominant enterprise email platform with 400M+ seats; Kastner's scalability concern proved temporary
