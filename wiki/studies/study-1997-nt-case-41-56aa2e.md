# NT Case 41: Migration Migraines

> Aberdeen Group OnSite case study of a Fortune 100 company's troubled migration from IBM OS/2 to Windows NT across call center operations (~12,000 clients). A 1995 business executive mandate drove total Microsoft adoption despite IS concerns. Migration began Spring 1996, missed mid-1997 deadline at 70% completion. Results: 33% performance decrease, 50% management cost increase, daily workstation reboots, SQL Server instability, IS staff exodus, and suppressed help desk reporting. Aberdeen frames this as a governance failure: business executives dictated both the what and the how of IT implementation.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **Case Study**._


## Top observations

- -33% (one-third decrease in performance)
- +50% (fifty-percent increase in management costs)
- 2-3 reboots per day minimum (most stable workstations)
- 70% complete; remaining 30% considered extremely difficult
- 35,000
- 12,000
- 5,000
- Frequent database crashes requiring hours or days to rebuild
- More frequent than desired or expected; time-consuming recovery
- Poor; known bugs unfixed across three micro-revisions
- Cache management, referential integrity, replication, messaging, log management, SQL debugging tools all viewed as inadequate
- Unable to service more than modest numbers of end-users; increasing domain engine count required
- Random packet drops, router timeouts, garbage packet congestion; cause unidentifiable
- Monthly customer service level reports suspended due to embarrassment
- Exodus of skilled engineers; replaced by junior staff
- Business executives dictated both WHAT and HOW of IT implementation
- Business executives should define requirements; IS executives should determine implementation
- 1995 business decision likely to have material impact on operations and bottom line in 1998
- Windows NT/2000 eventually dominated enterprise; Microsoft addressed stability issues via service packs and Windows 2000
- OS/2 is being abandoned despite operational stability
- IBM ended OS/2 support in 2006
- Concern over Year 2000 (Y2K) compliance of SQL Server
- Microsoft selling NT as a loss-leader to gain enterprise market share
- Windows Server dominated enterprise by early 2000s; NT 4.0 succeeded by Windows 2000/2003
