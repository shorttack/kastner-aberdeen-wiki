# Viasoft y2K: c.era — Reinventing the Year 2000 Solution

> Aberdeen Group's May 1997 Announcement Profile examines Viasoft's c.era cooperative strategic initiative, which aggregates Y2K remediation tools and services from multiple vendors through a single-point-of-contact model anchored by Viasoft's Rochade repository. Aberdeen endorses c.era as a comprehensive flexible approach to Y2K compliance especially for large-scale heterogeneous environments, and urges IS buyers to evaluate Viasoft immediately. The study also identifies that Y2K investment can generate reusable IT assets including data warehousing and modernization benefits beyond 2000.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Cooperative ecosystem platform; unique approach to Y2K requiring multi-vendor cooperation; single-point-of-contact model
- Core technology tying process steps and technologies together; IT asset warehouse for programs/applications/enterprise information
- Primary Y2K-affected language; c.era explicitly supports COBOL remediation across MVS and AS/400 platforms
- Organizations use c.era as single point of contact for all Y2K vendor selection and management
- Includes process model plus Rochade repository plus enabling technology infrastructure across all major platforms
- Gathers best tools and practices through pre-qualification; fosters cooperation among multiple vendor projects
- Raises Y2K awareness and improves risk management for enterprise
- Captures valuable information assets from existing systems; by-product creates reusable components for post-Y2K use
- Framework enables: Y2K efforts; data warehousing; Eurocurrency introduction; modernization; packaged-application implementation
- Y2K scope typically greater than anticipated; heterogeneous environments require multi-vendor approach; no single vendor can fix all issues
- Organizations that leverage Y2K efforts for future strategic initiatives will gain competitive advantage; Viasoft positioned to deliver this
- After Y2K deadline passed revenues collapsed; Viasoft acquired by Allen Systems Group for $152M in April 2000 ($8.40/share) — stock had traded at much higher levels in 1997-1998
- Project scope typically greater than anticipated; impact on each department and system is very different
- Y2K remediation was an ~$600 billion global effort (est.); heterogeneous multi-vendor approaches were indeed required; no catastrophic failures occurred
- Founded 1983 as Software Renovation Technology; 14 years helping Global 5000 organizations manage software assets before Y2K
- Designed for large-scale complex Y2K efforts; particularly for unique or industry-specific problems; banking/finance/securities/telecom/aerospace/government/insurance named
