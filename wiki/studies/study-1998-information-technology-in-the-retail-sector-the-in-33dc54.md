# Information Technology in the Retail Sector: The Integration Imperative

> Aberdeen Group's 120-page report examines how U.S. retail organizations can transform IT investment from a cost center into a strategic competitive asset, estimating the retail sector spent $11.1 billion on IT in 1997. The study maps IT spending across the five divisions of retail operations — supply chain management, merchandise management, in-store systems, decision support, and corporate administration — and benchmarks application adoption by retailer category. It introduces the 'integration imperative' thesis that siloed retail IT systems must be unified to enable data-driven merchandising and efficient supply chains.


_Published 1998, author **Susan Rinehart and Donald Bellomy (Aberdeen Group)**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- $11.1 billion
- Over 90% of U.S. retail companies employ fewer than 20 individuals
- IT spending heavily skewed toward larger enterprises
- SCM is first of five retail IT operational divisions identified by Aberdeen
- Merchandise management is second of five retail IT divisions
- In-store systems (POS) is third of five retail IT divisions
- Decision support analytics is fourth of five retail IT divisions
- Corporate administration IT is fifth of five retail IT divisions
- IT managers must leverage technology investment from cost center into strategic tool for new revenue opportunities
- IT suppliers must recognize emerging retail business/technology needs to plan solutions for current and future requirements
- All retailers share severe margin pressure regardless of category
- Integration of siloed IT systems is the defining strategic imperative for retail competitiveness
- IT requirements of apparel stores differ markedly from supermarkets; unique characteristics by retail category
- Emerging; supply chain integration was major unmet need in retail in 1998
- Mature category; in-store transaction systems well-established but not integrated with back-office
- Emerging; data warehousing and retail analytics nascent in 1998
- Retailers that integrate IT across five divisions will gain sustainable competitive advantage; those that don't will decline
- Walmart's integrated supply chain and IT systems enabled sustained dominance; became world's largest retailer through IT-driven efficiency
- Kmart failed to integrate IT systems; filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2002; unable to compete with Walmart
- Sears Holdings filed Chapter 11 in October 2018; failure to execute digital integration was major factor
- Amazon built the most integrated retail IT stack in history; achieved $638B net sales by 2024
- Internet will become a significant retail sales channel requiring integration with physical operations
- U.S. e-commerce retail sales reached $960B in 2021; omnichannel integration became the dominant retail model
