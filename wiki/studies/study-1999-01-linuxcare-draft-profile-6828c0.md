# Linuxcare, Inc.: Enterprise Service and Support for All Things Linux (Draft Profile)

> Aberdeen Group profiles Linuxcare, Inc., a San Francisco-based firm providing enterprise Linux services, support, training, and certification. Founded in 1998, Linuxcare attracted KPCB as its first Linux-related investor and signed OEM contracts with Dell, IBM, and Sun for back-end Linux support across server and desktop product lines. Aberdeen concludes that Linux adoption is accelerating and that Linuxcare has proven its viability as the emerging Linux specialist services firm; several sections are marked as incomplete pending customer interviews. This is a DRAFT profile circulated before finalization.


_Published 1999, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **company-profile**._


## Top observations

- Aberdeen finds Linux adoption among enterprise firms starting to accelerate overall; Linux not a flash in the pan `[ps=5]`
- Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum support packages varying in response time and coverage; also backline support `[ps=4]`
- Within the next year (by 2000), IT managers will have a Linux-based alternative in every major application area `[ps=4]`
- Freeware business model means buyers get support they paid for: little to none; enterprise OS with no support is a non-starter `[ps=4]`
- Kleiner Perkins Caulfield and Byers chose Linuxcare as its first Linux-related investment `[ps=0]`
- Dell contracted Linuxcare for back-end Linux support for OptiPlex, Dimension desktop lines, Precision workstations, and PowerEdge Servers; www.dell.linuxcare.com mirror site `[ps=0]`
- IBM gave Linuxcare contract to support Linux on the RS/6000 line `[ps=0]`
- Sun signed Linuxcare to provide support for Star Office suite (acquired from German Star Division) `[ps=0]`
- TurboLinux tapped Linuxcare to provide services and support to its North American customers `[ps=0]`
- Compaq Computer certified DeskPro product line through Linuxcare Labs `[ps=0]`
- This is a DRAFT profile; Aberdeen note: finalization pending mid-October 1999 after customer/OEM interviews; several sections marked incomplete `[ps=0]`
- Founded 1998 as Linux consulting company; grew to full services firm via OEM demand
- Dell, IBM, and Sun have publicly sought Linuxcare's technical service and support capabilities for their first Linux offerings
- Linuxcare supports all Linux hardware platforms: Alpha, Intel, MIPS, PowerPC, Sparc, StrongArm
- 24x7 call center, Linuxcare University (training), Linuxcare Labs (certification/benchmarking), Linuxcare Labs (porting/migration)
- Linux enterprise application coverage expanded significantly by 2001. By 2001, Oracle, SAP, IBM DB2, and major enterprise applications all had Linux ports. Linux server revenues grew 63% from 2001 to 2002 to $2 billion, reflecting strong enterprise adoption. However, Linuxcare itself was acquired by Turbolinux and eventually rebranded as Levanta.
- Andrew Tridgell, original author and current team leader for Samba, is a Linuxcare guru
- Paul Mackerras, leader of Linux port to Power Macintosh and Fujitsu multicomputer platforms, is a Linuxcare guru
- Densa Techno Tokyo K.K. (Hitachi subsidiary) partnering with Linuxcare for Japanese Linux support market
- Aberdeen views Linuxcare as having proven its viability as a source of Linux-and-beyond technical expertise
