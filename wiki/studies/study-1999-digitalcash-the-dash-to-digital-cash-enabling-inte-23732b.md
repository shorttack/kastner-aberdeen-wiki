# The Dash to Digital Cash: Enabling Internet Commerce Microtransactions

> This Aberdeen Group market review and competitive analysis examines the emerging digital cash and micropayment market of October 1999, identifying 21 vendors offering diverse payment approaches — including credit-card aggregation, ISP/telco billing, smart cards, hardware-embedded chips, and electronic wallets. The report provides a SWOT analysis and competitive positioning map, profiles vendors such as CyberGold, eCash Technologies, Qpass, MasterCard/Mondex, Microsoft, and Visa, and predicts that well-funded players with large global installed bases (particularly credit card companies and ISPs/telcos) will most likely succeed while underfunded pure-play digital cash vendors will struggle.


_Published 1999, author **Aberdeen Group (revised from Worldscape Strategies)**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Credit cards used for 90% of online purchases
- Not well suited for numerous small purchases ($0.05-$5); high processing costs disincentivize merchants
- Failed miserably in U.S.; no viable business case for issuers
- $5 to $16 depending on microchip memory size
- Adopted in France/Germany/UK/South Africa/South America due to high credit card fraud and expensive telecom
- EMV chip cards adopted in U.S. from 2015 (liability shift); stored-value smart cards remain niche
- ISPs and telcos expected to be particularly proactive; will deploy digital cash into billing infrastructure within next two years
- Mobile carrier billing for digital content (app stores/ring tones) succeeded; ISP billing did not materialize broadly
- Banks showed typically conservative approach; lack of viable business models prevented bank buy-in
- U.S. banks did not spearhead digital cash; PayPal/Stripe/Square succeeded outside traditional banking
- 1 million users claimed by CyberGold
- Closed August 31 2001; part of wave of digital currency closures (Flooz/Beenz/CyberGold)
- Will challenge traditional means of exchanging value but challenge will take time to unfold; 2-3 year momentum build
- Greater chance of success for credit card companies with large global installed bases
- Visa (NYSE: V) remains dominant; Visa Direct enables real-time payments globally; market cap ~$500B in 2024
- MasterCard (NYSE: MA) remains dominant global payments network; market cap ~$400B in 2024
- Holder of DigiCash patents after DigiCash bankruptcy 1998; digital cash software and service provider
- Went bankrupt; acquired by InfoSpace (now Blucora) in 2002
- Digital content and commerce enablement for content providers
- Acquired by Amdocs for $275M in 2006; over 6x annual revenues
- One of few vendors providing comprehensive digital rights management solutions; ~9 years in business
- Acquired by Sony/Philips joint venture for $453M in 2002
- Hardware-based chip creates market for trusted PCs; ready-made network for security vendors
- Pay-per-use and rent-to-own models for digital content enabled by trusted hardware
- Heavy-wallet approaches not working; lighter versions emerging to maximize user friendliness
