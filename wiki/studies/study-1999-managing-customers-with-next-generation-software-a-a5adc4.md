# Managing Customers with Next-Generation Software Applications: 1999 Edition

> This fifth-annual Aberdeen Group study surveys the CRM software market in 1999, providing market size and growth forecasts through 2002, detailed segment analysis (sales force automation, marketing automation, customer service, field service, help desk), and profiles of over 60 vendors. The report identifies the convergence of CRM with e-business as the dominant industry force, forecasts continued consolidation at the enterprise tier, and highlights the ASP delivery model and mid-market growth as key opportunities. Aberdeen positioned Siebel Systems as the dominant enterprise CRM vendor while warning that web-architected startups would challenge client-server incumbents.


_Published 1999, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Dominant single supplier at enterprise tier
- ~$3.7 billion (ISV revenues)
- Approximately $2.6 billion
- Projected to exceed $10 billion
- CRM and e-business will converge into unified multi-channel customer management
- ASP delivery will gain significant traction especially in mid-market
- Mid-enterprise ($50M-$1B revenue; 50-500 users) represents largest untapped opportunity
- Siebel will face serious challenge from web-native startups but retains near-term dominance
- Acquired by Oracle Corporation for $5.8 billion (Sept 2005)
- Acquired by PeopleSoft for $433M (October 1999)
- Acquired by Nortel Networks for $2.1 billion (October 1999)
- Salesforce.com pioneered SaaS CRM; became dominant by 2010s
- Core CRM segment enabling field sales activity management
- Emerging segment for campaign management and lead generation
- Contact center and case management CRM
- Scheduling and dispatch for field technicians
- Internal IT and customer-facing technical support
- Managing indirect sales channels and partners
- Mature but under threat from web-native architectures
- Emerging; web startups have architecture advantage but lack enterprise process knowledge
- Major database/ERP vendor using resources to attack CRM market
- SAP leveraging ERP installed base to cross-sell CRM functionality
- ERP market in doldrums due to Y2K completion and post-ERP investment fatigue
- Growing role for PDAs and mobile in CRM field operations and data access
- CRM moving beyond early-adopter industries (high-tech; financial) to mainstream enterprises
