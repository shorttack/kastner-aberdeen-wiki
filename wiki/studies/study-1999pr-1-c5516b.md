# Aberdeen's IT Professional Services Practice — Coverage Areas

> This document describes Aberdeen Group's IT Professional Services practice structure and coverage areas as of January 1999, organized in a matrix by market initiative and supplier type. It identifies four key market segments—Windows 2000 infrastructure, ERP/Supply Chain, Customer Information Systems, and Education—and provides a detailed vendor landscape for each. The document concludes with a sample IBM Global Services profile illustrating Aberdeen's analyst methodology.


_Published 1999, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- Aberdeen structures coverage in matrix: by market initiative x supplier type (IPSP/ISV/OEM/VAR)
- Windows 2000 infrastructure, ERP/Supply Chain, CIS/Data Knowledge, Education ranked as top four IS buyer demand areas per Aberdeen surveys
- ERP solution technology introduction has accelerated over past several years; few corporations can stay current
- EC services include Systems Design/Integration, Project Management, Lifecycle Management, Training
- ISVs (SAP/Baan/Oracle/PeopleSoft) increasingly building professional services forces to deploy their own software
- OEMs eroding reseller margins via internet-based direct sales; VARs/resellers turning to professional services as growth strategy
- CIS and Data Knowledge are among fastest-growing segments of Enterprise Business Applications (EBA) market
- Research in 1999 focused on transition from classroom training to distance learning and technology-based training solutions
- Distance learning provides greater flexibility at lower costs (financial and opportunity); internet/intranet delivery options
- IBM's E-Business initiative unifies all marketing, product, and services strategies under single theme
- IBM Global Services has more than 110000 skilled professionals deployed in 164 countries
- $19 billion of $78 billion total IBM annual revenue
- Aberdeen estimates IBM derives ~$11.5 billion annually in product support revenues
- $15.2 billion total revenue; approximately one-third from single client (GM)
- Hewlett-Packard has approximately 25000 professional services staff; number two OEM services provider
- Compaq + Digital + Tandem workforce estimated at ~34000 professionals; strengthens enterprise-level and NT-based services
- HP strong in Unix-oriented professional services but comparatively slow to build NT deployment services at scale
- ~500000 AS/400-class systems installed; ~100000 Unix systems; cited as IBM enterprise computing heritage
- Cost per transaction minute dropped from ~$300 three years prior to <$50 on Unix/NT platforms as of 1999
- Commoditization created worldwide shortage of qualified IS professionals for deployment and lifecycle management
- IBM claims primary competitors are Andersen Consulting and EDS, not HP/Compaq
- IBM likely world's largest provider of IT-related professional services and business strategic planning
- IBM customers cite reliability as primary reason for purchasing IBM professional services; pricing secondary consideration
- Some IBM customers cite inconsistent service delivery on global basis; IBM established centers-of-competency to address
- 4 practice areas covered in 1999: Windows 2000 Infrastructure, ERP/Supply Chain, CIS/Data Knowledge, Educational Services
