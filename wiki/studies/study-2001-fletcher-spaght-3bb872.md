> Fletcher Spaght Inc. (FSI) strategy consulting presentation (40 slides) prepared for Aberdeen Group. The primary case study covers Ascendant (Omnigy commerce platform) — a B2C/B2B e-commerce ASP — with FSI conducting 45 customer interviews across e-tailers, 3rd-party fulfillers, and component providers. FSI analyzed Omnigy's market positioning, segment priorities (computer H/W & S/W, apparel/sporting, food/beverage as top segments), and recommended focus on 'Supplier Enablement' over 'B2C' given >$10M/sale economics. Also documents the FSI-Aberdeen collaboration model and FSI's firm profile (est. 1983, Boston). Reveals Aberdeen's business model, partnership strategies, and growth plans circa 2001.


_Published 2001, author **Fletcher Spaght Inc.**, type **consulting-report**._


## Top observations

- Multi-functional C-level sale: CTO/IT (CRM); Sales & Marketing; Operations; Finance; Customer Service — multiple decision makers with different value propositions, seldom concurrent purchases `[ps=5]`
- Doug Witt FedEx: 'I very much like that Omnigy is offered on an ASP basis… I like ASP because of the low up front cost and the fact that it is a scalable system' `[ps=5]`
- Market wants rapid time-to-market, low investment 'commerce platform'; ASP model preferred for low capital expenditure and scalability `[ps=4]`
- Customers want ASPs with established hosting track record; performance risks drive preference for proven vendors; Digital River had 700+ stores as proof `[ps=4]`
- Existing ERP, Siebel, and OMS installations create go/no-go hurdle; financially and emotionally difficult to scrap existing systems `[ps=4]`
- eCRM importance and complexity highest for Clicks&Bricks/e-Tailer; lowest for e-Marketplace; varies by supply chain position `[ps=4]`
- Phase 1: Project Planning/Analysis Internal Data; Phase 2: Market Research; Phase 3: Competitive Analysis; Phase 4: Develop/Assess Strategic Alternatives; Final Review and Action Plan `[ps=4]`
- Market wants broad functionality especially in back-end; not necessarily tied to full-service providers; front-end (CRM, shopping cart) complex piece `[ps=4]`
- Focus on 'Supplier Enablement' opportunity; B2C low priority; Supplier Enablement = >$10M/sale vs B2C $1M+/-; 100s of customers per sale vs 1; viral marketing vs one-offs `[ps=3]`
- Bill only time spent productively; commit to not-to-exceed total; frequently put portion at risk; progress payment schedule; reimbursement for incurred expenses `[ps=3]`
- Leverage 3rd party fulfillment as primary channel; investigate web consulting channel; focus direct sales on very few target markets (B2B e-marketplaces, B2C e-tail if resources permit) `[ps=3]`
- Chris Fletcher, Aberdeen Group: 'If Omnigy is saying that a single customer record is added value, that is exaggerated. Many other companies have this [capability].' `[ps=3]`
- Supplier Enablement: >$10M/sale; 100s of customers/sale; exchange dynamics/viral marketing. B2C: ~$1M/sale; 1 customer/sale; each sale unique. FSI recommends Supplier Enablement `[ps=3]`
- Digital River; Escalate; OrderFusion; CommerceOne; multi-point solution providers with order management and fulfillment management `[ps=2]`
