# Caveat Emptor: HP's Consumer IT Leadership Needs New Focus on Consumers


## Short-horizon prescience (3-year / 5-year)

- **3-year verdict:** high — 3y Rule A: mean=3.80 over 20 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].
- **5-year verdict:** high — 5y Rule A: mean=3.75 over 20 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].

> First-person account by Aberdeen analyst Peter Kastner of his experience with HP's C315 digital camera, focusing on problems encountered when upgrading from Windows ME to Windows XP. The study documents HP software incompatibility with XP, inadequate documentation, high support costs, and argues HP must improve consumer product support to achieve its stated consumer IT leadership goal.


_Published 2001, author **Peter Kastner**, type **lab-journal**._


## Top observations

- consumer digital cameras largely replaced by smartphone cameras by approximately 2015 `[ps=5]`
- consumers need simpler instructions than IT professionals and have higher support expectations and lower pain tolerance `[ps=5]`
- cannot afford many support calls on mass-market products and remain profitable `[ps=4]`
- HP has a lot of work ahead to achieve world-class leadership in consumer IT `[ps=4]`
- programs and devices that worked with prior Windows may not work with XP `[ps=4]`
- HP became major consumer IT player then split into HP Inc. and Hewlett Packard Enterprise in November 2015 `[ps=4]`
- lead consumer IT market including access PCs imaging products and new consumer electronics `[ps=3]`
- dissatisfied consumers will go elsewhere guaranteeing HP loses race for consumer mind and market share `[ps=3]`
- worked well for 6 months `[ps=0]`
- missing .dll file prevented use as primary image-handling program `[ps=0]`
- major incompatibility after in-place upgrade to Windows XP `[ps=0]`
- website claim of native XP support technically correct but misleading `[ps=0]`
- HP photo transfer software incompatible with Windows XP `[ps=0]`
- single tech support call cost HP more than retail price of camera `[ps=0]`
- 45 minutes `[ps=0]`
- HP documentation failed entirely to address XP compatibility for in-place upgrade users `[ps=0]`
- Windows XP became most successful Windows version; reached end-of-life April 2014 after 12+ years `[ps=0]`
- consistently high quality photographs `[ps=0]`
- ROM upgrade failed due to XP disk formatting process corrupting camera digital media `[ps=0]`
- many unhappy HP camera owners who upgrade to Windows XP plus bundled PC+camera purchasers at risk `[ps=0]`
