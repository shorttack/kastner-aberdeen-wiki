# HP's Acquisition of Compaq: Beyond the Naysayers, the Promise and the Opportunity

> This Aberdeen Group collection of seven InSight pieces examines the HP-Compaq acquisition from multiple perspectives: corporate strategy, PC business integration, Windows/Wintel strategy, Unix platform consolidation, Linux opportunities, storage strategy, and IT services mix. Peter Kastner, Aberdeen's Chief Research Officer, identifies significant upside potential for synergy while warning that execution excellence and message clarity must surpass pre-merger levels. The collection argues the combination could create an industry powerhouse if HP prioritizes customer outreach and sales force retraining.


_Published 2001, author **Peter Kastner; Robert Dorin; Bill Claybrook; Aberdeen Group Research Team**, type **collection**._


## Top observations

- Significant upside potential for synergy in many areas; primary focus must be clarity of message and execution excellence
- Combination could create industry powerhouse IF execution excellence surpasses pre-merger efforts
- Partial success: merger cost savings exceeded targets by 30% in H2 2002 ($651M vs plan), but Fiorina ousted Feb 2005; HP split into HP Inc. and HPE in 2015
- Success must stem from the PC business — Compaq's Wintel strategy should be adopted by new HP
- PC sales dropped 19% to $4.8B in first combined quarter (Q3 FY2002); Personal Systems cut operating loss by 50%+ sequentially in Q4 FY2002
- New HP must create order in high-end Unix platform strategy — HP-UX and Tru64 require rationalization
- HP discontinued Tru64 advanced features Dec 2004; migrated to HP-UX on Itanium; many Tru64 developers laid off
- HP acquisition of Compaq enhances Linux business for the new company
- HP reported 18th consecutive quarter of Linux market-share leadership in Q4 FY2002
- HP/Compaq combination presents prescription for storage success — combined storage portfolio is complementary
- 17% sequential increase in on-line storage revenues; strong StorageWorks wins including $200M with Ericsson
- HP/Compaq combined IT services must offer more than size; service mix quality critical
- Technology services revenue fell 7% to $3B in first combined quarter — services underperformed
- HP/Compaq must focus on customer outreach, clarity of messages, and marketing execution excellence; programs should start immediately
- We've kept our eye on the ball. We did well.
- $16.5 billion (vs. $18.6B combined year-earlier)
- $18.0 billion (vs $16.5B prior quarter — 9% sequential increase)
- $651 million in H2 2002 — 30% above plan; 12,500 net workforce reductions, 25% above plan
- 58% sequential revenue increase in Q4 FY2002; HP ranked #1 on TOP500 Supercomputer Sites enterprise category
- 30% worldwide server market share per Gartner Dataquest (leading all vendors)
- Printing systems revenue increased 10% to $4.7 billion — most profitable division
- Big computer systems revenue fell 22% to $3.8 billion; $422M operating loss called 'unacceptable' by Capellas
- 30 largest new contracts averaged $120M+ each; totaling $3.7B in new long-term revenue
- Numerous naysayers exist but Aberdeen sees significant upside potential that should not be ignored
- Capellas predicted PC division would return to profitability in H1 FY2003
