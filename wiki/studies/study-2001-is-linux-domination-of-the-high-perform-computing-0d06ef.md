# Is Linux Domination of the High-Performance-Computing (HPC) Marketplace Imminent?


## Short-horizon prescience (3-year / 5-year)

- **3-year verdict:** high — 3y Rule A: mean=4.20 over 10 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].
- **5-year verdict:** high — 5y Rule A: mean=4.60 over 10 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].

> This Aberdeen InSight examines the price/performance advantages of Linux clusters over competing HPC computer systems and argues that Linux is positioned to dominate high-performance computing within three years. The study outlines multiple reasons why Linux will successfully replace proprietary systems in HPC environments, citing Linux's rapid development pace and cost advantages.


_Published 2001, author **Bill Claybrook**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Linux positioned to dominate HPC within three years (by 2004) `[ps=5]`
- By November 2004 Top500: Linux-based clusters became dominant; majority of Top500 systems ran Linux by mid-2000s `[ps=5]`
- Linux clusters offer superior price/performance vs. proprietary HPC systems `[ps=5]`
- SGI filed bankruptcy 2006 (emerged 2007); Cray survived as niche vendor; Sun Solaris HPC largely displaced `[ps=5]`
- By November 2017, Linux ran on 100% of Top500 supercomputer systems `[ps=5]`
- Multiple Linux-based clusters in Top20: Columbia SGI Altix (Linux), MCR Linux Cluster, Lightning Linux Cluster visible in Top500 Nov 2004 `[ps=4]`
- Linux server revenue grew 63% to $960M Q4 2003; topped $1B quarterly Q3 2004 (42.6% YoY growth) `[ps=0]`
- Linux will be successful alternative to proprietary systems in HPC
- Linux development moving faster than any other commercial OS; multiple reasons cited for HPC suitability
- Linux cluster commodity approach enables HPC cost reduction vs. proprietary parallel systems
