# WebEx: Taking Conferencing to the Business Communications Summit

> This Profile examines WebEx Communications, Inc.'s conferencing platform and its private-network infrastructure approach to delivering data-rich online business meetings. The study covers WebEx Meeting Center, WebEx OnCall, WebEx OnStage, and the forthcoming WebEx Training Center, arguing that WebEx's utility-grade infrastructure positions it as the leader in business communications.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Market leader in data-rich online business meetings
- Uniquely used by WebEx; ideally suited for live data-rich business communications
- Core platform for live data-rich online business meetings
- Live remote hands-on customer technical support service
- Large online events and web seminars service
- Rolling out April 2002 to address considerable internal training market
- WebEx Training Center launched April 2002 as predicted
- Private-network infrastructure approach uniquely positions WebEx as long-term leader
- $3.2 billion acquisition at $57/share in May 2007
- Utility-grade conferencing infrastructure as competitive moat
- Covers data-rich online meetings, remote support, large events, and training
- Market for online business meetings growing; WebEx best positioned
- Considerable market for internal training addressable by WebEx Training Center
- Web-based business communications as complement/successor to videoconferencing
- Cisco completed tender offer for WebEx May 22 2007 at $57/share
- PlaceWare acquired by Microsoft in 2003 and became Office Live Meeting
- WebEx positioned as business communications summit — top-tier utility infrastructure
