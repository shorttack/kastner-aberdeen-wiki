# A View of Sun's 2003 Worldwide Analyst Conference

> This Aberdeen Group Perspective summarizes Sun Microsystems' 2003 Worldwide Analyst Conference (WWAC) in San Francisco. Sun communicated that customer demand centers on reducing data center costs and complexity, driving investment in Project Orion, N1 grid architecture, and new storage products. Notably, Sun affirmed that Solaris on SPARC would remain its flagship operating system for the foreseeable future despite an evolving Linux strategy.


_Published 2003, author **Bill Claybrook; Dan Tanner**, type **perspective**._


## Top observations

- Solaris on SPARC designated flagship OS for foreseeable future
- Active development to reduce data center costs and complexity
- In development as part of Sun's cost/complexity reduction strategy
- Altering Linux strategy to be more attractive to users
- Drive down data center costs and complexity
- Solaris on SPARC will remain flagship for foreseeable future
- Sun acquired by Oracle 2010; SPARC/Solaris reduced to legacy status; no major new releases after Solaris 11.4 (2018)
- Discontinued after Oracle acquisition; concepts absorbed into Oracle cloud strategy
- Linux/x86 displaced Solaris/SPARC as enterprise standard; Oracle continued strong Linux support
- 2003 Worldwide Analyst Conference, San Francisco; attendees included industry analysts
