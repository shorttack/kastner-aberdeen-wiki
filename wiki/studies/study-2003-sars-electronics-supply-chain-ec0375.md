# Asian Virus Casts Shadow Over Supply Chain: SARS Impact on Asia/Pacific Semiconductor and Electronics Manufacturing

> Aberdeen Group analyst report (Russ Craig and Peter Kastner) excerpted by PlanetAnalog/EBN on April 16, 2003, examining the potential impact of the SARS epidemic on the PRC- and Taiwan-centered global electronics supply chain. The authors quantify Asia/Pacific's 37% share of 2002 semiconductor consumption ($52B), document early SARS clusters in Guangdong, Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Taiwan, and assess the vulnerability of dependable component supply — particularly for no-second-source items like PRC-made AC/DC power supplies — should quarantines extend. The report ends with a prescient warning that investor confidence in the PRC will take years to rebuild given government opacity about the outbreak.


_Published 2003, author **Russ Craig; Peter S. Kastner**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- 37% / $52 billion
- 38% / $65 billion; PRC alone 11%
- 85%+
- All moving to PRC
- 8 plants with commitments to NEC, Philips, Oki, Elpida, Infineon
- 792 reported cases, 34 deaths
- Temporary closure; 305 workers quarantined
- Barred employee travel to PRC and HK until epidemic controlled
- Postponed due to SARS (K.Y. Ho)
- 1,000+ people; schools closed
- ~100 new cases per day
- Single-source dependence: laptop assembly cannot ship without PRC power supply
- Literally months of 24-7 work; 'nuclear winter' for semi/electronics
- Disruption of airplane flights carrying high-value/rush electronics
- Ships not significantly delayed once at sea; <10-day incubation
- Years to dispel new level of suspicion by investors of government's ability to protect investments
- Japanese/Korean firms will slow their rush to PRC; Western firms face disrupted chains now
- Dependable supply is an 'oft-overlooked rule' of the electronics industry; SARS may break it
- PPE, semi, consumer electronics shortages 2020-2022 confirmed Kastner-Craig no-second-source thesis
- The deceit of the PRC government in hiding what has become a serious global health threat will not be quickly forgotten.
