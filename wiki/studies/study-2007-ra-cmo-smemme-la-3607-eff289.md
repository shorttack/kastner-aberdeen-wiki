# The CMO's Strategic Agenda: Creating a Customer-Centric Marketing Organization (SME & MME Benchmark Report)

> This November 2006 benchmark report, part of Aberdeen's CMO Strategic Agenda series, benchmarks roughly 500 small/medium and midmarket enterprises (SME & MME) on building customer-centric marketing organizations. It presents findings on customer retention, return on marketing investment (ROMI), and revenue growth performance.


_Published 2007, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **benchmark**._

- Do organizations that adopt best-in-class, customer-centric marketing practices derive greater profitability, market share, ROMI, or increased revenues as a result? `[ps=5]`
- This research revealed that best-in-class marketers _consistently_ demonstrate higher performance across KEY business and marketing metrics, they also measure specific metrics that directly link marketing efforts and overall business goals. `[ps=4]`
- Technologies that will yield best-in-class results _must_ enable integration of data and enhance sophistication of marketing processes in order to add value. `[ps=4]`
- This Aberdeen research, which reflects a strong cross section of various industries from small enterprises (SME) with less than $50M in revenues as well as the mid-market (MME) with revenues below $2B, clearly demonstrated that customer-centric companies adopt a closed-loop, lifecycle marketing process that successfully brings together the right metrics, differentiating capabilities, and integrate `[ps=4]`
- Those companies that track customer behavior, use customer profitability modeling, maintain centralized knowledge and data management systems, and possess real-time decision support, including business intelligence tools integrated with marketing and customer data, ranked as best-in-class with higher frequency than all other participants. `[ps=4]`
- By utilizing customer lifetime value as a variable in their planning and assessment phase, best-in-class organizations are better able to break down organizational silos to optimize their marketing investments and produce results superior to those of their counterparts. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-class organizations often utilize “customer lifetime value” metrics in modeling and predicting which mix of customers, portfolio products, sales, marketing, or media channels will support them in attaining their revenue targets and goals, and do so at rates which are significantly higher than their peers. `[ps=4]`
- Figure 4 demonstrates that companies that implement prediction capabilities realize higher ROMI. `[ps=4]`
- However, if an organization is unable to track or measure the effectiveness of each interaction per customer, it will have a significant impact on its ability to understand which message, offer, touch-point, or number of interactions influences their customers’ purchasing decisions and, ultimately, customer acquisition and profitability rates (Figure 5). `[ps=4]`
- Best in class companies are the most likely able to track customers and integrate touch-points; permitting a closed-loop approach which raises campaign sophistication. `[ps=4]`
- Not surprisingly, companies that leveraged technology and business processes to plan and create marketing initiatives were rewarded with higher ROMI and gross annual sales (Figure 3).Conversely, the consequences of not employing technology are clear. `[ps=4]`

- It’s unfortunate that, while respondents reported having enabling technology tools such as CRM, marketing automation, BI/Market Analytics, or call centers at their disposal, only 13% of organizations have developed the processes needed to integrate and automate their planning across channels, products, and/or brands. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-class technology enablers implemented real-time integration and BI to facilitate multi-channel optimization and rules-based campaign execution. `[ps=4]`
- As Figure 11 shows, companies have more formal interaction across all channels have a greater ability to dynamically respond and adjust to marketing pressures. `[ps=4]`
