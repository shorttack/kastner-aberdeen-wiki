# The Future of ATM (1991, missing source)

> **This is a placeholder page for a missing source document.** Aberdeen Group published a ~100-page report in 1991 authored by Charles Robbins (working under Tom Willmott) arguing that ATM (Asynchronous Transfer Mode) would become the universal networking backbone and desktop standard. The report set the pin for Aberdeen's networking thesis for several years and is referenced indirectly throughout the 1995-1998 networking studies in this archive. The original PDF has not been recovered and is tracked in [`_missing_sources.csv`](https://github.com/shorttack/aberdeen-group-archive/blob/main/_missing_sources.csv) in the source archive.

## What we know

- **Author**: Charles "Charlie" T. Robbins (the report's primary author), working under Tom Willmott (Aberdeen's networking practice lead at the time). Willmott often gets cited externally; per Peter Kastner, Robbins is the correct author of record.
- **Length**: approximately 100 pages
- **Year**: 1991 (exact month unknown)
- **Publisher**: Aberdeen Group
- **Thesis**: ATM would displace Ethernet, FDDI, and Token Ring as the universal backbone and would extend to the desktop, becoming the single converged transport for voice, video, and data.
- **Influence**: This report shaped Aberdeen's ATM coverage from 1991 through approximately 1997, when [[study-1997-bay-networks--switch-node-routing-s-4d9885]] and [[study-networ-1-96462b]] began documenting ATM's failure at the desktop and the rise of Gigabit Ethernet as the campus backbone.

## Why the gap matters

The 1991 Robbins report is the **founding artifact** of Aberdeen's networking thesis. Without it, the archive's networking timeline starts in 1995 mid-thesis-evolution, missing the four years where the original pin was set. Any longitudinal analysis of Aberdeen's ATM-versus-Ethernet position should treat 1991-1994 as a known provenance gap, anchored by this report.

The recovered 1995-2000 studies (see related links below) document the **shift** away from this thesis, not its origin.
