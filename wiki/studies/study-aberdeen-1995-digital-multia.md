# Digital's Multia Enterprise Client: Finally Someone Listened

> This Aberdeen Group product profile evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's Multia 'enterprise client' desktop, introduced in late 1995 with both Alpha 64-bit RISC and Intel Pentium 100 models. The study employs product profiling, technology assessment, and competitive analysis to position Multia as a turn-key universal client that addresses the longstanding IS requirement for a single desktop capable of simultaneously accessing mainframes, Unix servers, PC servers, and the World Wide Web via pre-configured emulators and multiple communications protocols. Key findings include that Multia is priced $500–$800 above a comparable PC but justifies the premium through integrated interoperability, centralized IS desktop management, pre-configured terminal emulators (3270, VT 340/420, Netscape Navigator, Rumba, Kea!), a photo-album-size footprint, and chip-upgradeable dual Alpha/Intel architecture. Aberdeen concludes that Digital is the only major computer vendor that listened to enterprise IS requirements and predicts that Multia's centralized management capabilities will meaningfully reduce IS staffing and support costs at remote sites, and that the new 'market-oriented Digital' has finally arrived as a credible enterprise client vendor.

## Top observations

- turn-key universal enterprise client desktop; NOT positioned as a personal computer
- $500–$800 above comparable PC desktop (depending on options)
- approximately the size of a family photo album
- supports both Alpha 64-bit RISC and Intel Pentium 100 models
- ZIF socket design allows processor upgrades as new architectures become available
- 256 KB standard, upgradeable to 512 KB
- 16 MB standard, expandable to 128 MB
- 810 MB or 1 GB hard drive
- Microsoft Windows NT Workstation 3.51; enables multitasking and stability
- TCP/IP, IPX/SPX, DECnet, LAT, NetBEUI, X Serial Line Protocol; NFS in X Server option
- IBM 3270 terminal emulation pre-configured via Rumba (Wall Data) and Kea! (Attachmate)
- embedded VT 340/420 terminal emulation pre-configured for DEC/OpenVMS host access
- Netscape Navigator embedded pre-configured for World Wide Web access
- Digital-supplied X11.R6 X Server with predefined protocols, system connections, and workgroup customization
- IS admins can remotely configure desktops, control application availability, enable/disable hardware (e.g., floppy), schedule software updates, distribute software
- IS management and support costs will decrease; fewer remote site administrators required
- End-user efficiency increases because systems are made more available through central IS management
- Digital is the only computer supplier that has listened to and acted on IS enterprise client requirements
- New market-oriented Digital Equipment Corporation has 'finally arrived'
- Organizations requiring turn-key access to multiple server types: multiple transports, diverse apps (Windows, Unix X, VT, 3270), and centralized IS configuration management
- Single desktop device provides simultaneous access to mainframes, Unix servers, PC servers, and the World Wide Web via icon clicks
- DIY assembly of equivalent third-party emulators and protocols would be less stable and less robust than integrated Multia
- IS administrators can remotely enable or disable floppy drive; important for government and financial security
- Administrators can remotely manage downed desktops, obtain system configuration info, schedule software updates, and perform software distribution
- Multia enables IS and end-user organizations to stop working at cross purposes
