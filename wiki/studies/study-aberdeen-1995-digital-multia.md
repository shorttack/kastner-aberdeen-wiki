# Digital's Multia Enterprise Client: Finally Someone Listened

> This Aberdeen Group product profile evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's Multia 'enterprise client' desktop, introduced in late 1995 with both Alpha 64-bit RISC and Intel Pentium 100 models. The study employs product profiling, technology assessment, and competitive analysis to position Multia as a turn-key universal client that addresses the longstanding IS requirement for a single desktop capable of simultaneously accessing mainframes, Unix servers, PC servers, and the World Wide Web via pre-configured emulators and multiple communications protocols. Key findings include that Multia is priced $500–$800 above a comparable PC but justifies the premium through integrated interoperability, centralized IS desktop management, pre-configured terminal emulators (3270, VT 340/420, Netscape Navigator, Rumba, Kea!), a photo-album-size footprint, and chip-upgradeable dual Alpha/Intel architecture. Aberdeen concludes that Digital is the only major computer vendor that listened to enterprise IS requirements and predicts that Multia's centralized management capabilities will meaningfully reduce IS staffing and support costs at remote sites, and that the new 'market-oriented Digital' has finally arrived as a credible enterprise client vendor.

## Top observations

- Administrators can remotely manage downed desktops, obtain system configuration info, schedule software updates, and perform software distribution `[ps=5]`
- IS management challenge: compromised corporate data security from lax distributed computing data management `[ps=5]`
- Multia product line discontinued approximately 1997; Alpha processor line discontinued 2004 following HP acquisition of DEC via Compaq `[ps=5]`
- Digital Equipment Corporation acquired by Compaq in 1998 for approximately $9.6 billion; Multia and Alpha product lines eventually discontinued `[ps=5]`
- Failed: Digital was acquired by Compaq for $9.6B in June 1998; Multia product line discontinued ~1997 before acquisition; DEC never achieved enterprise client market leadership `[ps=5]`
- Failed: Alpha processor discontinued by Compaq/HP by 2004 (final Alpha chip: EV7z); Intel/AMD x86-64 dominated. DEC's Alpha never achieved desktop market penetration. `[ps=5]`
- Microsoft Windows NT Workstation 3.51; enables multitasking and stability `[ps=4]`
- IS management and support costs will decrease; fewer remote site administrators required `[ps=4]`
- IS administrators can remotely enable or disable floppy drive; important for government and financial security `[ps=4]`
- IS management challenge: hardware and software inventory redundancy `[ps=4]`
- IS management challenge: Tower-of-Babel inter-company communications from lack of standardization `[ps=4]`
- Concept validated but implementation shifted: thin clients (Sun Ray, Wyse, Citrix/RDP) and eventually VDI/virtual desktops became the enterprise answer to universal access, not hardware-integrated desktops `[ps=4]`
- Organizations requiring turn-key access to multiple server types: multiple transports, diverse apps (Windows, Unix X, VT, 3270), and centralized IS configuration management `[ps=3]`
- Unverifiable at product level: Multia discontinued ~1997; DEC acquired by Compaq 1998. Thin-client concept (which Multia anticipated) was validated by Oracle NC and Windows Terminal Server `[ps=3]`
- turn-key universal enterprise client desktop; NOT positioned as a personal computer `[ps=2]`
- Digital is the only computer supplier that has listened to and acted on IS enterprise client requirements `[ps=1]`
- New market-oriented Digital Equipment Corporation has 'finally arrived' `[ps=1]`
- Multia enables IS and end-user organizations to stop working at cross purposes `[ps=1]`
- approximately the size of a family photo album `[ps=0]`
- supports both Alpha 64-bit RISC and Intel Pentium 100 models `[ps=0]`
- 256 KB standard, upgradeable to 512 KB `[ps=0]`
- 16 MB standard, expandable to 128 MB `[ps=0]`
- 810 MB or 1 GB hard drive `[ps=0]`
- TCP/IP, IPX/SPX, DECnet, LAT, NetBEUI, X Serial Line Protocol; NFS in X Server option `[ps=0]`
- IBM 3270 terminal emulation pre-configured via Rumba (Wall Data) and Kea! (Attachmate) `[ps=0]`
