# Sun Microsystems Computer Company: Sun Emerges as a Top-Tier Commercial Systems Supplier

> Aberdeen profiles Sun Microsystems Computer Company (SMCC) as it transitions from technical workstation leadership to commercial server market contender. The study examines Sun's SPARC-based server product line from workgroup to enterprise tiers, evaluates its response to commercial buyer requirements (performance, scalability, availability, cost of ownership), and assesses Sun's competitive position against IBM and HP in commercial Unix server sales.


_Published 1995, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **product-profile**._


## Top observations

- on short list of preferred Unix suppliers in major corporations
- 1982
- Unix V.4 with SMP extensions and high security levels
- workgroup (SPARCserver 4/5/20), departmental (1000E), enterprise (SPARCcenter 2000E)
- SPARCcluster 1000/2000 PDB and SPARCcluster HA
- recent TPC benchmark results demonstrate competitive commercial DB performance
- performance, scalability, availability, cost of ownership (3-5 year)
- leveraging technical installed base + new ISV/marketing programs for commercial apps and mainframe downsizing
- contributed to 12+ worldwide standards organizations; positioned as open systems leader
- Sun will extend commercial market presence beyond technical compute base
- "network is the computer" becomes de-facto enterprise standard
- SPARC server line will remain competitive in commercial market
- 68.5% of US UNIX/RISC units shipped (2 of every 3)
- lost $2.4 billion in FY2003 after $1.85B net income in FY2000
- acquired by Oracle Corporation for $7.4B (net $5.6B); completed January 27, 2010
- largely migrated to Linux on x86; SPARC/Solaris confined to legacy and specialized workloads
- Solaris SMP extensions position Sun for mission-critical 7x24 Unix deployments
- considerable resources devoted to RDBMS performance optimization on SPARC/Solaris
- Netra s/i for Systems Management and Internet access; SPARCcluster for HA and PDB
- 1991 (first commercial server line)
- SAP and Oracle Applications referenced as key commercial application targets on Sun servers
- performance + scalability + availability + TCO: 4-factor universal framework
- Mountain View, CA 94043; 415-960-1300
- Sun most credible open systems vendor; 12+ standards org contributions vs IBM and HP proprietary histories
- SMP support will be key to growing mission-critical deployments
