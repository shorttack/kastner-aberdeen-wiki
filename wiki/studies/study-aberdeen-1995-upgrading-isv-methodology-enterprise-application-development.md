# Upgrading To ISV Methodology For Enterprise Application Development

> Aberdeen examines ADM's SCRUM methodology — a codification of ISV best practices for enterprise application development — and argues that in-house IS organizations should adopt ISV-style rapid iterative development over traditional waterfall planning-heavy methods. The study details SCRUM's three phases (Planning, Sprints, Closure) and ADM's Product Management Facility implementation, concluding that IS teams must adopt sprint-based methods with small teams, short cycles, and flexible toolsets to compete with the speed of the best ISVs.


_Published 1995, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **practice-viewpoint**._


## Top observations

- 5 principles: quick upfront planning, sprint teams of ≤7, progress reviews at sprint end, done when functional+quality objectives met or budget/time runs out, subsequent versions not just patches
- 3 phases: Planning and System Architecture, Sprints (multiple), Closure
- 1-7 members; full staffing includes developer, QA person, documentation member
- 1 to 6 weeks per Sprint
- 400,000 beta users before completion
- fixing mistake after release costs 10-100x the cost of identifying during planning
- Enterprise IS prioritizes cost, compatibility, functionality, quality; ISVs prioritize time-to-launch, functionality, personnel, quality (cost not primary)
- 4 modules: Process Engineer, Planner, Developer, Product Management Facility (new for SCRUM)
- IS organizations must adopt ISV-style rapid development or face growing frustration with management demands
- SCRUM will help IS unlock OOP potential; most IS orgs still not planning to use OOP for mission-critical apps
- SCRUM is not a passing fad or one-shot fix; ISVs using it for sustained competitive advantage
- Agile Manifesto (February 2001) codified SCRUM-aligned principles; SCRUM became one of most-adopted Agile frameworks globally
- Waterfall largely displaced by Agile/SCRUM for software development; remains in regulated industries only
- SCRUM/Agile adopted by majority of software development teams globally; Scrum.org holds ADM trademarks
- SCRUM named for rugby action (forwards quickly move ball via team skill/strength); not an acronym
- Backlog: approved modifications from prior Sprint assigned to teams at start of next Sprint period
- adopting SCRUM would be radical departure from traditional methods for most in-house enterprise development groups
- Sprint review includes teams, project manager, customers/prospects, enterprise senior executives
- OO tools required for SCRUM to be more than theoretical vision; tools that didn't exist even one year ago
- Mainframe app suppliers failed transition to Unix/GUI; DOS app suppliers failed Windows 3 transition—inability to change fast enough
- Booch, Rumbaugh, Jacobson created Unify Metamodel underpinning SCRUM intellectual basis
- Mid-Sprint platform change (OS/2 to Windows NT Client) assigned to teams at next Sprint start—SCRUM flexibility demonstrated
- Burlington, Massachusetts
- Volume 8/Number 17, December 7, 1995
- New technology not proprietary—competitors use same tools; SCRUM/speed-to-deliver is the competitive differentiator
