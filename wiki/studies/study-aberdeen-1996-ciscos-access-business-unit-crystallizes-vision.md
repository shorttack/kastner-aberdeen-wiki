# Cisco's Access Business Unit Crystallizes Its Vision

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Cisco Systems' Access Business Unit (ABU) January 1996 product rollout, assessing the company's strategy to dominate all segments of the remote access market: ROBO, dial-up/access server, SOHO, and embedded/server-based systems. The study traces Cisco's 15-month journey through acquisitions (Combinet, Internet Junction, Network Translation) and product launches (AS5200, 750 series, CiscoRemote, CiscoPro), arguing that Cisco's IOS software is becoming the de facto standard for remote access. Aberdeen concludes that Cisco's end-to-end access strategy across carrier, enterprise, SOHO, and home segments has all the ingredients of a successful win strategy.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- 'Firmly believing that the home of today is the office of tomorrow' — Cisco's vision of networking from enterprise core to home `[ps=5]`
- Scalability services enabling migration path for growth and change across enterprise and SMB `[ps=4]`
- Exclusively two-tiered distribution for SMB; premier channel partners (Tech Data, Ingram Micro) responsible for VAR/integrator sales `[ps=4]`
- Cisco's goal is to make IOS ubiquitous throughout the industry, becoming a force to be contended with on all fronts of the remote access market `[ps=4]`
- Cisco grew to $18.9B revenue by FY2000 (from $2B in 1996); IOS became definitively the de facto internetworking OS; Cisco dominated all four ABU segments `[ps=4]`
- Cisco's strategy to add software value-add for ISPs looking to offer more value will prove attractive; IOS software sell will prove compelling long-term `[ps=4]`
- Ascend Communications acquired by Lucent Technologies in 1999 for $20B; Cisco defeated Ascend in ISP/access server market through IOS differentiation `[ps=4]`
- ISPs shifting from price-only to demanding intelligent software, managed modems, security, and single-vendor solutions `[ps=4]`
- 1994 assessment: Cisco blueprint carefully crafted but lacked dial-up depth and channel relationships. 1996: channel and dial-up gaps largely closed `[ps=4]`
- Multi-pronged targeting of four segments: ROBO (regional/branch office), dial-up/access server, SOHO/professional office, embedded and server-based systems `[ps=3]`
- Cisco acquired Combinet in August 1995; spawned 750 ISDN series for SOHO and home `[ps=2]`
- Shiva Corporation filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 1999; Cisco won the SOHO remote access market `[ps=2]`
- ABU has marched toward 50% market share in key remote access segments `[ps=2]`
- 50% of Cisco's $2 billion overall revenues derived from the Access Business Unit `[ps=0]`
- Reliable, adaptive routing services `[ps=0]`
- Integrates CSU, comms server, channel banks, modems, switches, routers in single chassis; 48 (T1) or 60 (E1) integrated modems; digital + analog dial-in `[ps=0]`
- Integrates Network TeleSystems TCP/IP, Farallon Timbuktu Pro, AirSoft Powerburst, Netscape Navigator 1.2, Qualcomm Eudora 1.5.2, Cisco IOS extensions `[ps=0]`
- TACACS+ announced March 1995; supports independent user authentication, authorization, accounting, and encryption `[ps=0]`
- Cisco IOS has become the de facto standard in internetworking; provides cohesion to disparate networks; functions as a unifying thread
- WAN optimization services including compression, bandwidth-on-demand, dial-on-demand routing
- Management and security services including TACACS+, CiscoSecure AAA
- Triple-A security: authorization, authentication, accounting + NAT via TACACS+; per-user access control at server level
- Phase 1: market engagement; Phase 2: end-to-end solutions rollout; Phase 3 (current): enhanced services and enterprise-wide scalability
