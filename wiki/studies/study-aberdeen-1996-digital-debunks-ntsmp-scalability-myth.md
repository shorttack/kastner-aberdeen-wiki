# Digital Debunks the NT/SMP Scalability Myth

> This Aberdeen Group Market Viewpoint (Volume 9, Number 22, November 22, 1996) argues that IT decision-makers should reconsider the widespread belief that Windows NT Server lacks SMP scalability beyond four processors. Using Transaction Processing Council TPC-C benchmark data, Aberdeen demonstrates that Digital Equipment Corporation's 4-way Pentium Pro server achieves 6,712 tpmC at $65/tpmC, outperforming established midrange Unix and proprietary servers at a fraction of the cost. Aberdeen concludes that Microsoft's real strategy is performance clustering, not 8-way SMP scaling, and that Digital is optimally positioned to lead the NT midrange server market.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Three-pronged: (1) maximize 4-way performance via CPU tuning; (2) continue limited SMP beyond 4-way; (3) pursue high-end via performance clustering (Wolfpack) `[ps=4]`
- Build highly-tuned 4-way NT servers and performance clusters to capture large midrange market segments; Microsoft-aligned `[ps=4]`
- Digital's $65/tpmC pricing driven by manufacturing economies of scale plus Internet-technology transaction handling that eliminates expensive Tuxedo front-end `[ps=4]`
- 4-way NT servers will at least double and perhaps triple in speed by year-end 1998, reaching 11,000-16,000 tpmC range `[ps=4]`
- By 1998, 4-way Pentium II/Xeon NT servers exceeded 15,000 tpmC; prediction substantially correct; however DEC was acquired by Compaq January 1998 before full realization `[ps=4]`
- 8-way and beyond NT systems: high-margin, low-volume; primarily for decision support, data warehousing, and consolidation servers `[ps=4]`
- More than 100,000 NT high-availability/performance clusters deployed by year-end 1999; NT performance clusters exceed 20,000 tpmC by early 1999 `[ps=3]`
- Microsoft Cluster Service (MSCS) shipped in Windows NT 4.0 Enterprise Edition 1997; Windows 2000 Advanced Server extended to 4-node clusters; HA clustering widely adopted by 1999 though performance clustering adoption was slower than 100,000 unit projection `[ps=3]`
- NT 8-way+ systems will remain low-volume as long as NT is 32-bit; 64-bit NT needed for full high-end capability `[ps=3]`
- Broadest NT product line; largest MS training org in world; 2,000+ Microsoft-certified specialists; tight Microsoft alliance `[ps=2]`
- NT scalability limits beyond 4-way are not engineering-based but are a deliberate market economics decision by Microsoft `[ps=2]`
- 6,712 tpmC at $65/tpmC (NT, 4-way Pentium Pro, Microsoft SQL Server) `[ps=0]`
- 6,671 tpmC at $90/tpmC (NT, 4-way, Microsoft SQL Server) `[ps=0]`
- 8,311 tpmC at $95/tpmC (UnixWare, 4-way, Sybase SQL Server with Tuxedo) `[ps=0]`
- 5,774 tpmC at $198/tpmC (AIX, 8-way, Sybase SQL Server) `[ps=0]`
- 4,939 tpmC at $232/tpmC (HP/UX, 4-way, Oracle 7) `[ps=0]`
- Exceeds 30,000 tpmC (Unix-based AlphaServer clusters) `[ps=0]`
- 2,400 tpmC at $242/tpmC (Compaq ProLiant Intel-based) `[ps=0]`
- 2,000 to 15,000 tpmC (midrange); below 2,500 tpmC (workgroup); above 15,000 tpmC (high-end/enterprise) `[ps=0]`
- NCR scaled NT on sixteen processors `[ps=0]`
- Sequent scaled NT to 28 processors; sold multiple 14-processor NT/SMP systems `[ps=0]`
- 1,496 tpmC at $320/tpmC (OS/400, 1-CPU, DB2/400)
- Over 90% of all server systems sold are 4-way-or-less
- Digital has clustering core expertise since VAX clusters (1980s); early NT cluster leader; high-availability clusters already at market debut
- Making a Digital NT server decision should prove irresistible to many IT decision makers; Aberdeen expects Digital to lead NT midrange charge
