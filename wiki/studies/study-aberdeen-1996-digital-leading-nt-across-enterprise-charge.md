# Digital: Leading the NT-Across-the-Enterprise Charge

> This Aberdeen Group profile (July 1996) evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's comprehensive NT-across-the-enterprise strategy, encompassing three operating environments (OpenVMS, Digital Unix, Windows NT), a full product suite from commercial desktops to enterprise servers, and a deep strategic partnership with Microsoft. Aberdeen concludes that Digital is uniquely positioned to become the leading enterprise NT supplier because none of its major competitors — IBM, HP, or Sun — are strategically committed to NT, leaving Digital unchallenged at the high end. The study examines channels strategy, ISV application portfolio, systems management capabilities, and the critical Digital-Microsoft relationship.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Stated NT leadership commitment but protecting HP/UX Unix base; no plans for NT on PA-RISC; will await HP/Intel combined chip for enterprise NT `[ps=5]`
- Digital acquired by Compaq January 1998 for $9.6B; Digital's NT product line and expertise absorbed into Compaq; independent NT market leadership not achieved `[ps=5]`
- Meets product breadth/depth requirements but lacks enterprise-level systems integration and deployment experience necessary for mission-critical NT `[ps=4]`
- Dual-channel: direct sales to create demand, indirect (distributors/VARs/SIs) for order fulfillment; internal sales force compensated for VAR referrals eliminating channel conflict `[ps=4]`
- Windows NT and Windows 2000 Server did become dominant enterprise server platform by 2000-2002; Unix market share declined significantly; NT adoption prediction was accurate `[ps=4]`
- OS/2 was discontinued in 2001; IBM never achieved strategic NT server commitment; IBM shifted focus to Linux (2000) instead; prediction of IBM weakness in enterprise NT was accurate `[ps=4]`
- IT managers require vendors with enterprise-experienced professional services organizations for NT deployment in mission-critical environments `[ps=3]`
- Deploys NT clients; no strategic NT server commitment; Ultra positioned to directly compete with NT servers `[ps=3]`
- Three-tier: ClientWORKS (desktop) -> ServerWORKS Manager (workgroup SNMP) -> POLYCENTER/CA Unicenter (enterprise) `[ps=3]`
- With Digital's enterprise services and Microsoft's NT, NT will become the dominant enterprise server platform; days of Digital's rapid growth will return `[ps=3]`
- Continuous computing server for back-end database/application services supporting NT clients; VMS migration path via Affinity program `[ps=2]`
- Premier client/server environment: Intel for commercial desktop/low-end servers; Alpha for high-performance technical and commercial desktops and application servers `[ps=2]`
- Binary translation/emulation enables 32-bit Windows/NT x86 apps on Alpha with significant performance improvements over x86 architectures `[ps=2]`
- Microsoft to work with Digital on 64-bit NT addressing for next major post-4.0 NT release `[ps=2]`
- Digital is clearly in an NT leadership position today — this race is Digital's to win (or lose); Aberdeen sees positive indications that Digital can execute strategy successfully `[ps=1]`
- IBM's conflicted OS/2 vs NT strategy and lack of public NT commitment leaves door wide open for Digital in enterprise NT `[ps=1]`
- 1,400+ client/server applications on Alpha platform; direct support for 300 PC and NOS applications `[ps=0]`
- 5,000+ Microsoft support personnel including 1,000 Microsoft Certified Professionals (ramping to 1,700 near-term) `[ps=0]`
- 1,400+ NT applications qualified on Alpha platforms including database, business, banking/financial, groupware, Internet, software tools `[ps=0]`
- NT clustering capabilities released for all Intel Prioris servers and AlphaServers 1000/2000/4000; NT RAID storage options added for high-availability `[ps=0]`
- 2,000+ NT applications on Intel Celebris XL Personal Workstation; 1,200+ on Alpha-based Alpha XL `[ps=0]`
- IT managers require vendors to have demonstrated, public strategic commitment to NT as primary enterprise platform
- IT managers require vendors to offer full product breadth from desktop to server plus platform extensions (scalability, availability, management)
- IT managers require financial stability from NT platform vendors for long-term support commitments
- High-end 64-bit Unix for decision-support, data warehouses; 32-bit NT not expected to compete in 64-bit market for several years
