# Digital: Leading the NT-Across-the-Enterprise Charge

> This Aberdeen Group profile (July 1996) evaluates Digital Equipment Corporation's comprehensive NT-across-the-enterprise strategy, encompassing three operating environments (OpenVMS, Digital Unix, Windows NT), a full product suite from commercial desktops to enterprise servers, and a deep strategic partnership with Microsoft. Aberdeen concludes that Digital is uniquely positioned to become the leading enterprise NT supplier because none of its major competitors — IBM, HP, or Sun — are strategically committed to NT, leaving Digital unchallenged at the high end. The study examines channels strategy, ISV application portfolio, systems management capabilities, and the critical Digital-Microsoft relationship.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- IT managers require vendors to have demonstrated, public strategic commitment to NT as primary enterprise platform
- IT managers require vendors to offer full product breadth from desktop to server plus platform extensions (scalability, availability, management)
- IT managers require vendors with enterprise-experienced professional services organizations for NT deployment in mission-critical environments
- IT managers require financial stability from NT platform vendors for long-term support commitments
- 1,400+ client/server applications on Alpha platform; direct support for 300 PC and NOS applications
- 5,000+ Microsoft support personnel including 1,000 Microsoft Certified Professionals (ramping to 1,700 near-term)
- 1,400+ NT applications qualified on Alpha platforms including database, business, banking/financial, groupware, Internet, software tools
- Continuous computing server for back-end database/application services supporting NT clients; VMS migration path via Affinity program
- High-end 64-bit Unix for decision-support, data warehouses; 32-bit NT not expected to compete in 64-bit market for several years
- Premier client/server environment: Intel for commercial desktop/low-end servers; Alpha for high-performance technical and commercial desktops and application servers
- No public strategic commitment to NT; OS/2 division actively competes with NT; ISSC division markets NT — conflicted strategy
- Stated NT leadership commitment but protecting HP/UX Unix base; no plans for NT on PA-RISC; will await HP/Intel combined chip for enterprise NT
- Deploys NT clients; no strategic NT server commitment; Ultra positioned to directly compete with NT servers
- Meets product breadth/depth requirements but lacks enterprise-level systems integration and deployment experience necessary for mission-critical NT
- Binary translation/emulation enables 32-bit Windows/NT x86 apps on Alpha with significant performance improvements over x86 architectures
- Three-tier: ClientWORKS (desktop) -> ServerWORKS Manager (workgroup SNMP) -> POLYCENTER/CA Unicenter (enterprise)
- Aug 1995 alliance: Digital technology swap for Microsoft underwriting NT/Office/BackOffice training for thousands of Digital consultants; Digital has 1,000 MCPs — largest MS professional services org in world
- Microsoft to work with Digital on 64-bit NT addressing for next major post-4.0 NT release
- Dual-channel: direct sales to create demand, indirect (distributors/VARs/SIs) for order fulfillment; internal sales force compensated for VAR referrals eliminating channel conflict
- Digital is clearly in an NT leadership position today — this race is Digital's to win (or lose); Aberdeen sees positive indications that Digital can execute strategy successfully
- Digital acquired by Compaq January 1998 for $9.6B; Digital's NT product line and expertise absorbed into Compaq; independent NT market leadership not achieved
- With Digital's enterprise services and Microsoft's NT, NT will become the dominant enterprise server platform; days of Digital's rapid growth will return
- Windows NT and Windows 2000 Server did become dominant enterprise server platform by 2000-2002; Unix market share declined significantly; NT adoption prediction was accurate
- IBM's conflicted OS/2 vs NT strategy and lack of public NT commitment leaves door wide open for Digital in enterprise NT
- OS/2 was discontinued in 2001; IBM never achieved strategic NT server commitment; IBM shifted focus to Linux (2000) instead; prediction of IBM weakness in enterprise NT was accurate
