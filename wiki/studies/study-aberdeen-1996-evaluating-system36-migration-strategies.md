# Evaluating System/36 Migration Strategies

> This Aberdeen Group White Paper (September 1996) evaluates four migration strategies for IBM System/36 owners: stay put, migrate to Advanced 36, migrate to AS/400, or migrate to Windows NT PC-LAN. Based on interviews with System/36 owners and a five-criteria decision framework (capital cost, operating cost, MIS transition cost, business disruption, supplier support), Aberdeen recommends migrating to the Advanced 36 as clearly the best choice for the vast majority of owners. The study documents the Year 2000 compliance imperative and provides detailed cost tables for 10-user and 20-user configurations across all options.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- AS/400 division is IBM's most profitable hardware division; IBM will be supporting AS/400 for a significant time to come; resellers and software developers still working on new products `[ps=5]`
- Installed base declining at a rate in excess of 20% per year; even third-parties finding support less profitable `[ps=4]`
- Entry level AS/400 around $8,000; initially comparable to Advanced 36 but may need larger configuration due to SSP Environment performance degradation `[ps=4]`
- OS/400 Version 3 Release 6 and Version 3 Release 7 are Year 2000 compliant; growing number of AS/400 applications also Year 2000 compliant `[ps=4]`
- PC-LAN migration may be appropriate for those automating for the first time; it is not practical for System/36 owners who cannot afford to build new IT infrastructure from scratch `[ps=4]`
- Majority of System/36 owners prefer NOT to migrate due to high satisfaction with stable platform; yet universally say 'it is time to move' due to external support ecosystem collapse `[ps=4]`
- AS/400 can function as application server, PC server (with data auto-inserted into PC spreadsheets), printer server, fax server, Internet gateway, and data warehouse `[ps=3]`
- Y2K compliance deadline proved real; System/36 installed base dropped to near zero by 2000; IBM's 90%+ migration rate to AS/400 solutions validated both the urgency and Aberdeen's migration recommendation `[ps=3]`
- Priority #1 for System/36 owners; Advanced 36 rated Best, AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable `[ps=2]`
- Priority #3; Advanced 36 rated Best (1-day migration via TDL), AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable (cannot reuse any S/36 skills or data) `[ps=2]`
- Priority #4; Advanced 36 rated Best (same apps, same users, max 3-day cutover), AS/400 Acceptable, PC-LAN Unacceptable (all new processes) `[ps=2]`
- Priority #5; AS/400 rated Best (multi-billion dollar IBM division), Advanced 36 Acceptable (IBM direct support), PC-LAN Unacceptable (local dealers, hourly billing, no accountability) `[ps=2]`
- Advanced 36 is clearly the best choice for the vast majority of current System/36 owners; all former System/36 owners interviewed who chose Advanced 36 were extremely happy `[ps=2]`
- Most System/36 applications will become dysfunctional well before January 1, 2000 due to two-digit year fields and forecasting date ranges `[ps=2]`
- Declined from approximately 87,000 sites (1992) to projected 42,000 sites (1996) — decline of more than 50% in four years `[ps=0]`
- Over 90% of System/36 sites that migrated in 1995 moved to AS/400 solutions — mainly the Advanced 36 `[ps=0]`
- Hardware: $24,125; Systems software: $9,690; Basic accounting package: ~$12,500 (additional) `[ps=0]`
- Hardware: $40,875; Systems software: $15,040; Basic accounting package: $22,500 `[ps=0]`
- $9,800 to $21,000 depending on model and additional peripherals; all existing System/36 applications run without modification `[ps=0]`
- Maintenance reduced from ~$330/month for larger System/36s to ~$95/month for even larger Advanced 36s (including internal storage) `[ps=0]`
- Advanced 36 runs batch processing jobs 8-10 times faster than System/36 — described as System/36 on steroids `[ps=0]`
- Advanced 36 detects potential problems before they occur and automatically reports to system manager requesting IBM service call scheduling `[ps=0]`
- $10,000 per year to manage, maintain, and upgrade a Windows NT Server + $1,000 per year for each attached PC
- Average of one day to migrate from System/36 to Advanced 36 using IBM Transition Data Link (TDL); TDL transfers data at 45MB per hour
- Most System/36 applications will become dysfunctional well before January 1, 2000 — forecasting and planning use future dates starting years ahead; SSP Year 2000 compliant apps very hard to find
