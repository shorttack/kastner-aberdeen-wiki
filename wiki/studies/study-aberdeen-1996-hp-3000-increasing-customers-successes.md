# HP 3000: Continuously Increasing Customers' Successes

> Aberdeen Group analyzes Hewlett-Packard's 'Customer First' strategy for the HP 3000 midrange platform, arguing that HP's decision to focus on retaining its large and loyal installed base rather than pursuing new customers is pragmatic and financially sound. The study documents deep customer loyalty, a $1.2 billion annual revenue base, and a strategic commitment to continued platform enhancement into the HP 3000's third decade.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- $1.2 billion
- $500-600 million annually
- 5% planning full replacement
- 65% of enterprise customer base
- 30% of enterprise customer base
- Customer First (installed-base retention; not new customer acquisition)
- New customer acquisition platform; Unix/RDBMS focused
- Continuous customer feedback driving product development
- Customer feedback guiding ISV application partnering
- Direct HP corporate customer dialogue bypassing traditional sales channels
- Open interfaces and interoperability with Unix, NT, MVS ahead of demand
- Improving HP 3000 OS to match best-in-class Unix and NT alternatives
- Over 30 models; 918LX at $26,000 to 996/800 SMP at $720,000
- ~2x performance improvement at 10% price premium by end of 1996
- 30-35% performance improvement; no price change
- Integrated MPE/iX + IMAGE/SQL architecture; best-in-class reliability and availability
- Not cost-effective for component-based systems with portable ISV RDBMS
- HP will support HP 3000 customers for decades; Customer First ensures continued viability
- By year 2000 HP will design Merced processors into HP 3000 and rewrite MPE/iX
- HP can count on stable customer demand for a long time into the future
- HP announced end-of-life November 2001; support extended to December 31 2010
- MPE/iX was never ported to Itanium/Merced; HP 3000 EOL announced instead
- Substantial portion of installed base continued using HP 3000 through 2010 EOL; third-party support ongoing post-2010
- Realistic and pragmatic; correctly positions HP 3000 among its own product family
- User-based pricing cuts up to 40% on low-end systems
