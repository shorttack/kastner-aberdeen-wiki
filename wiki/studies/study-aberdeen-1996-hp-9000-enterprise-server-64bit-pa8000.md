# HP 9000 Enterprise Server Performance Leadership Again: The 64-bit PA-8000 Has Arrived

> Aberdeen Group evaluates HP's September 1996 announcement of PA-8000-based HP 9000 enterprise servers, documenting benchmark performance results (K460 at 12,321 tpmC — a 2.5x improvement over its PA-7200 predecessor) and positioning HP's PRISM framework as the standard for Unix enterprise server selection. The study asserts HP's competitive superiority over IBM, Sun, and Digital in the Unix server market, and outlines HP's roadmap through PA-8200, PA-8500, and eventual Merced processor transitions.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- 12,321 tpmC at $186/tpmC (4-way, 180MHz PA-8000)
- 4,939 tpmC (4-way, 120MHz PA-7200)
- 5,300 tpmC at ~30% better price/performance vs IBM RS/6000 R40
- 5,775 tpmC at $248/tpmC (8-CPU PowerPC 604, 112MHz)
- 11,466 tpmC (12-CPU, Sun's highest published result)
- 1,600-1,700 tpm
- 800-900 tpm
- 500-600 tpm
- 1,900-2,000 tpm
- 11.8 SPECint95 / 20.2 SPECfp95 / 3,200-3,300 tpm
- 200-250% improvement from K420 to K460 within one year; industry average 35% per year
- 10.2 (PA-8000 support) → 11.0 (VLM, mid-1997) → 11.X (Merced)
- Performance, Resilience, Integration, Security, Management
- PA-8000 processor; new disk array and tape library storage; leading TPC-C results
- MC/ServiceGuard clustering; high-availability features in each HP-UX release
- Praesidium technology: transaction application gateways, smartcards, antivirus, non-repudiation
- Performance leadership through continuous processor roadmap; investment protection via in-box upgradeability
- Positioned as MVS Parallel Sysplex alternative for mainframe Y2K migrations
- 50% performance increase over PA-8000; systems in 3Q97
- 100% improvement over PA-8000 (2x); end of first-half 1998
- Anticipated mid-1998 delivery in servers
- PA-8200 (240MHz) shipped 1997 as projected; PA-8500 delayed to mid-1999
- Itanium (Merced) shipped in servers in 2001; 3 years later than predicted
- HP 9000 server line discontinued in 2003; replaced by Itanium-based Integrity Servers
- >15,000 tpmC; shipping 1Q97
