# HP's Networking Vision: Designed with Workgroups in Mind

> Aberdeen Group examines HP's Workgroup Networks Division strategy for small and mid-sized businesses (SMBs), finding that HP's premise — workgroups grow up into enterprise networks — accurately maps to the bottom-up, haphazard LAN growth patterns observed in target verticals (education, healthcare, government, manufacturing). The study validates HP's workgroup-centric networking vision and endorses its standards-based approach.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- Leader in 10Base-T stackable hubs, high-speed hubs, NICs, and network printers
- Small to mid-sized organizations; networks 50 to 1,000+ nodes
- 10Base-T (10 Mbps) too slow for multimedia/videoconferencing; driving demand for 100VG and 100Base-T
- Home-Grown: networks designed in-house due to limited resources; wiring done by maintenance staff
- Lack of Pre-Planning: networks built department by department with no coordination or uniformity
- Mixed Environments: technology hodgepodge requiring interoperability; varying speed and performance needs
- Group Decision-Making: augmented by consensus rather than single IT expert directive
- Guerrilla Networking: constrained budgets; resourceful network managers wearing many hats
- Workgroup-first: workgroups as building blocks for enterprise networks; standards-based interoperability
- Full solution set: connectivity products, PCs, printers, servers, scanners, network management as complete workgroup solution
- Scalable 10/100 Mbps; shared and switched configurations; supported by HP
- Market success in both Windows and Unix versions; backed by exemplary service and support
- HP leader in 10Base-T hub market; contributed to 10Base-T wiring standards
- Timing is right for HP's approach; workgroup-ready networking solutions will define next purchase wave
- 100VG will remain relevant for SMB workgroup upgrades alongside 100Base-T
- Networking spending in SMB market will grow as workgroup applications drive bandwidth needs
- 100VG lost to 100Base-T as dominant fast Ethernet standard; 100Base-T became universal by late 1990s
- HP Workgroup Networks Division reorganized; ProCurve brand emerged for HP networking; HPE later acquired Aruba (2015) and Juniper (2025)
- SMB networking market grew strongly through late 1990s-2000s; switched Ethernet dominated; HP ProCurve was successful
- HP's networking vision firmly taps into market realities; workgroup premise confirmed by customer interviews
- Low maintenance, standardized equipment, easy installation, reliability, good security; 5-year technology life expectancy
- Corporations gravitating to one-stop solutions; HP full solution set positions well for this trend
