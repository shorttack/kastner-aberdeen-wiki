# The Vectra XW Personal Workstation: Hewlett-Packard's NT-based Alternative for Technical Computing

> Aberdeen Group profiles Hewlett-Packard's Vectra XW personal workstation, the company's first major Windows NT/Intel platform aimed at technical computing markets traditionally served by Unix/RISC workstations. The study analyzes HP's mixed NT-Unix strategy, benchmarks the Vectra XW against Silicon Graphics Indigo 2 and NeTpower Calisto, evaluates software solution supplier relationships in MDA/EDA/GIS markets, and concludes HP has built a compelling price/performance alternative that threatens low-end Unix workstations.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- HP commits to both NT-based personal workstations AND Unix/RISC workstations; 'freedom-to-choose' positioning
- Silicon Graphics maintains Unix-only position in technical computing; no NT commitment
- Sun Microsystems maintains Unix-only position in technical computing; no NT commitment
- HP is the only major workstation supplier committed to deploying and managing mixed NT-Unix environments
- SGI Indigo2 (256MB RAM) scored 133 Price*Bench95; HP Vectra XW (192MB RAM) scored 39 Price*Bench95 — XW at ~1/3 relative cost
- HP Vectra XW slightly faster than NeTpower Calisto but ~30% less expensive
- 50% of HP Unix workstation sales through direct sales force; 50% through indirect distributors/VARs
- $9,100 (single CPU) to $11,800 (dual CPU) including 17-inch monitor
- 100% of HP PC sales go through indirect channels; no direct sales for PC line
- HP VISUALIZE Unix workstations outperform Vectra XW in compute-intensive tasks: CFD, FEA, crash/stress analysis
- Vectra XW well-suited for component design, small sub-assembly design, 2D/3D technical apps + productivity tools
- Single or dual 200 MHz Pentium Pro; 64-512 MB ECC RAM; 2-4 GB SCSI; SCSI-20 I/O; 10/100BT LAN; OpenGL TX 2500 graphics
- TX 2500 based on 3Dlabs GLINT; 8MB frame buffer + 8MB texture DRAM; supports OpenGL and Heidi APIs
- HP has pre-existing ISV relationships in MDA/EDA/GIS from Unix workstation business — time-to-market advantage
- Primary challenge for HP is educating resellers to sell technical solutions alongside commercial PC products
- HP and Microsoft compete (HP/UX vs NT) but HP is important channel for Microsoft; HP is platform+integrator, Microsoft is software company
- Aberdeen: Vectra XW poses a legitimate price/performance threat to low-end Unix workstations; SGI/Sun Unix-only stance is a risk
- SGI exited traditional Unix workstation market by early 2000s; company filed bankruptcy in 2006 and 2009; acquired by Rackable Systems 2009, then HPE 2016
- Aberdeen: 'All of these factors bode well for the future success of HP's Vectra XW personal workstation'
- HP Vectra line discontinued in 2002 following HP-Compaq merger; replaced by Evo line. NT/Intel workstations did succeed broadly but HP's specific Vectra XW brand was retired
- MDA: PTC, SDRC, EDS/Unigraphics, Computervision, Dassault, MacNeal-Schwendler, Ansys, AutoDesk; EDA: Mentor Graphics, Synopsys, Cadence, Viewlogic; GIS: ESRI
- HP Professional Services Organization provides NT deployment expertise; critical given IS organizations' unfamiliarity with NT
- HP enjoys some of the highest customer satisfaction ratings in the workstation industry
- XM: $1,739+; XA: $3,000+; XU: ~$5,000; XW: $9,100-$11,800; VISUALIZE: above XW
