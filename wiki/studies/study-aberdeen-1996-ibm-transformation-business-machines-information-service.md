# IBM: The Transformation From Business Machines To Information-Service Solutions

> Aberdeen Group profiles IBM's strategic transformation under Lou Gerstner from a hardware-centric company to a service-focused information solutions provider, centered on IBM Global Services' $20.1 billion (1995) and 100,000 employees. The study documents IBM's Network Computing vision, 12 industry solution groups, Year 2000 opportunity, strategic acquisitions (Lotus/Notes, Tivoli), and identifies structural vulnerabilities including OS/2 weakness and partner ecosystem tensions.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- By early 2000s IBM became leader in IT services and consulting; $7B+ cost avoidance 1996-1998; services dominant revenue driver `[ps=5]`
- Revenue up 4.4% but hardware declined 0.6%; services (ex-maintenance) up 26.4% `[ps=4]`
- IBM commits to fulfilling any customer request for information-service solutions including using competitor products (HP Unix, Microsoft BackOffice) `[ps=4]`
- Installed in almost every Global 500 datacenter; knows customers' infrastructure, staff, and business processes `[ps=4]`
- IBM sold more Notes licenses in first 6 months of ownership than Lotus had in previous 6 years; Domino Notes (internet version) extends value `[ps=4]`
- Replication of implementations combined with Network Computing migration will increase revenues by >$8B/year in foreseeable future `[ps=4]`
- Be first source for installed base's next-generation information solutions; Network Computing supplier of choice globally `[ps=3]`
- Excellent for Global Services desktop deployments but lacks ISV support for Microsoft Office 95; perception of proprietary strategy haunts IBM `[ps=3]`
- Independent professional service providers will downplay IBM offerings as Global Services competes against them; ISVs will view IBM as competitor `[ps=3]`
- IBM is the largest business phoenix — rising and soaring from near disaster — we may ever have the pleasure to observe `[ps=3]`
- Most complete IT product line of any company; 25% of R&D staff required annually to work with customers via Global Services `[ps=2]`
- Follower in RDBMS functionality and performance vs Oracle, Sybase, Informix; IS decision makers value best-in-class `[ps=2]`
- Y2K may be bigger and more profitable than Network Computing opportunity; IBM ideally positioned for Y2K Global Services engagement `[ps=2]`
- $4.2 billion (38.3% earnings increase over 1994) `[ps=0]`
- $20.1B revenue; $29B backlog; 100,000 employees (of 225,000 total IBM) `[ps=0]`
- 100,000 at study; on-track for 110,000 by end 1996; 45% of IBM total employees `[ps=0]`
- 140 functional areas replicable; planned increase to 170+ `[ps=0]`
- IBM spun off infrastructure services as Kyndryl (2021); consulting retained as IBM Consulting; Aberdeen had warned against spin-off `[ps=0]`
- $71.9 billion (12.1% increase over 1994)
- 30% annual growth for past 4 years in customer-intimate professional services
- Unified IBM presentation replacing former competing hardware divisions (S/390, AS/400, RS/6000, PC server) fighting in front of customers
- Closest to truly global organization: unified industry solution groups treating global customer operations as single IT project; local presence in smaller metropolitan areas
- Employees hardened by 1990s layoffs; motivated to prove value; no entitlement culture remaining
- OS/2's lack of ISV support and proprietary perception will continue to limit IBM's desktop influence
- OS/2 discontinued; IBM ended development support 2006; Aberdeen's prediction of strategic liability proved correct
