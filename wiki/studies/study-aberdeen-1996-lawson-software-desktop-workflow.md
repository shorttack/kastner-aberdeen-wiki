# Lawson Software Delivers the Desktop and Workflow Combination One-Two-Punch

> Aberdeen Group evaluates Lawson Software's Open Enterprise Desktop (OED) and forthcoming integrated Lawson Workflow as a compelling 'one-two-punch' in the client-server solutions (CSS) marketplace, asserting that Lawson's multi-tier workflow architecture and process-oriented GUI give it a significant competitive advantage over Oracle, PeopleSoft, and SAP. The study analyzes workflow forms, integration levels, and scalability criteria while concluding that Lawson is uniquely positioned across multiple market segments.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Over 20 years of core-business applications experience as of 1996; strong AS/400 midrange heritage
- 4 Process Suites incorporating 20 predefined best-practice process models (AP Invoice Approval, Requisitions, Credit Check, Employee Review)
- Multi-tier product/market strategy combining best-of-breed workflow with integrated process-oriented GUI desktop
- Internal development of Oracle Workflow via Cooperative Application Initiative (CAI); initial availability Q1 1996
- Proprietary workflow in version 5.0: email-based, database-based (stored procedures/triggers), and planned third-party APIs
- Moved from third-party Cascade (spring 1995) to internally-developed R/3 integrated workflow management system
- Process-oriented GUI with Lawson Toolbar and Intelligent Folder Tabs; C/C++ Win32; supports Windows 95 and NT
- Three levels: Personal (OED agents), Departmental/Workgroup (OED + Departmental Workflow Engine), Enterprise (OED + Process Suites)
- MAPI-compliant email with embedded active/passive application objects; future VIM support in v7.0
- R&D working on OED release with Java-based Internet deployment; Aberdeen expects Lawson in CSS Internet vanguard
- Cross-domain workflow spanning Lawson and non-Lawson via plug-and-play APIs; supports Wang OpenWorkflow and FileNet Visual Workflow
- Three deployment levels (personal/departmental/enterprise) matching diverse computing environments
- Data capture of key process efficiency metrics; Rapid Process Development (RPD) methodology for continuous improvement
- Unstructured, user-defined process sequences; lowest complexity; suitable for knowledge workers
- Semi-structured process support for workgroups; intermediate complexity
- Highly structured, repeatable OLTP-grade processes; highest complexity and integration depth
- OED's business-process perspective 'stands unique among CSS' and represents 'distinct market advantage'
- Lawson should be on any prospective customer's evaluation list; uniquely qualified to compete across multiple market segments
- Lawson will escalate the client-server fray and could redefine the CSS value-added technology model; potential for significant competitive market advantage
- When OED with Java is delivered, Lawson could find itself in the vanguard of CSS suppliers able to fully exploit the Internet's business potential
- Lawson Software acquired by Infor/Golden Gate Capital for approximately $2 billion in 2011 after IPO in 2001; grew to 4,500 customers and $736M revenue
- Lawson did implement web-enabled delivery (Self-Evident Applications 1997, browser-based Insight II 1999) but did not become a CSS Internet vanguard leader
- PeopleSoft acquired by Oracle for $10.3 billion in January 2005 after hostile takeover battle
- 20 predefined models across 4 suites vs. competitors offering single enterprise workflow strategy
- OED and Lawson Workflow available on AS/400 (OS/400 V3R6) in addition to Windows 95/NT
