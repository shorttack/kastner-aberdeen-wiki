# Mitel's NeVaDa (Networked Voice and Data) – Speeding Toward Convergence

> Aberdeen Group profiles Mitel Corporation's NeVaDa (Networked Voice and Data) platform, announced April 2, 1996, as a strategic move to transform Mitel's SX-2000 PBX into an integrated voice-data-video server on a broadband ATM/SONET enterprise backbone. The study documents the IT industry's growing demand for voice-data convergence, evaluates NeVaDa's modular collapsed-backbone topology with Madge Networks LAN switching, and assesses Mitel's competitive position and strategic risks in the emerging CTI market. Aberdeen concludes NeVaDa is a logical step toward convergence but notes Mitel must move quickly, secure ATM technology, and build credibility in the data networking community.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Data and telecom systems still separate in most organizations
- Enterprise users investigating migration to ATM and SONET for high-speed on-demand multimedia LAN/WAN
- Up to 192 voice ports per peripheral node
- OC-3 fiber optic (155 Mbps) backbone integrating voice and LAN packet data; ATM cells for voice+data multiplexing
- T1/E1 DSU links and PRI gateways via SX-2000 LIGHT DSU nodes
- Ethernet, Token Ring, FDDI, and 100 Base-T in switched or shared media modes
- Managed from UNIX workstation running HP OpenView; unified management interface for end-to-end control
- Evolution: proprietary PBX → modular distributed nodes → open-standards call server on broadband backbone
- Open standards + Token Ring + Ethernet support + strategic IT partnerships (DEC, Intel, Microsoft, Madge)
- Eliminates wiring redundancies by combining voice and data on single OC-3 backbone
- Adding ports does not affect existing ones (Madge Networks LAN switching characteristic)
- Enables CTI multimedia applications at desktop cost-effectively via single IT-centric infrastructure
- Initial distrust of telecom vendors in IT community; perception of lacking integration/software skills
- Must secure ATM technology to meet proposed ATM-enabled upgrade in timely fashion
- CTI arena becoming increasingly competitive; must implement vision quickly and effectively
- IT view of the world is and will continue to be the de facto communications model; voice must integrate into IT
- Logical and required step; as migration-path strategy makes good common sense; first major PBX manufacturer for full enterprise CTI
- True convergence of voice, data, and video in single IT-centric infrastructure will occur
- Mitel can achieve CTI market leadership if it implements vision quickly and secures ATM technology
- VERIFIED — VoIP (SIP/H.323) fully replaced PBX by ~2010; UCaaS (Teams, Zoom, Webex) fulfills unified multimedia vision
- Survived via multiple restructurings; became significant UC vendor; acquired Aastra (2013), ShoreTel (2017); filed Ch.11 Mar 2025, exited Jun 2025
- Filed bankruptcy April 2003; operational business acquired by Network Technology/Ringdale 2006
