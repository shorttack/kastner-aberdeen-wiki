# Network Appliance: Data Appliances for Commercial Network Computing

> Aberdeen Group profiles Network Appliance (NetApp) as a pioneer in network-attached storage (NAS) for commercial computing environments. The study examines NetApp's filer hardware (F220, F330, F540), Data ONTAP software with WAFL file system, and multiprotocol support (NFS, CIFS, HTTP). Aberdeen concludes NetApp is well-positioned to capitalize on the shift from application-server-based storage to dedicated network-attached data servers, citing revenue growth from $2M (1994) to $47M (FY1996) approaching $100M.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- FY1997 run-rate indicates fast approach to $100 million revenue mark `[ps=5]`
- NDMP became established industry standard for NAS backup; widely supported by backup vendors including Veritas, Legato, IBM `[ps=5]`
- NetApp became leading NAS/hybrid cloud storage vendor; NASDAQ: NTAP; $6.27B FY2024 revenue; industry leader for 30 years `[ps=5]`
- NetApp plans HTML/Java-based administrative front-end across multiple workstations for lower operational costs `[ps=5]`
- NetApp delivered web-based management interfaces for ONTAP by late 1990s; became standard feature of NAS management `[ps=5]`
- NetApp leading NDMP standardization effort will result in greater centralized control and interoperable NAS backup ecosystem `[ps=4]`
- Auspex Systems ceased operations ~2000-2001; failed to compete with NetApp's multiprotocol approach `[ps=4]`
- Application response time degrades as more users share networked data; IS forced to upgrade application servers repeatedly `[ps=4]`
- Data scattered across workstations and servers; expensive to maintain availability and backup `[ps=4]`
- IS decision makers responsible for networked computing 'ought to look at and closely evaluate NetApp — for an already proven solution' `[ps=4]`
- Data availability in excess of 99.99% via integrated RAID 4 and hot-spare drive reconstruction `[ps=3]`
- Only vendor providing seamless access to NFS, CIFS, and HTTP simultaneously on one filer `[ps=3]`
- Single-function NAS competitor lacking CIFS/HTTP multiprotocol support `[ps=3]`
- $2 million `[ps=0]`
- $14.5 million `[ps=0]`
- NetApp actual FY1997 revenue approximately $166 million; exceeded $100M target `[ps=0]`
- Dedicated single-purpose NAS appliance: no user applications on data server; multiprotocol NFS/CIFS/HTTP
- $47 million
- WAFL provides faster reads and writes over network than local storage attached to general-purpose servers
- Up to 20 simultaneous on-line read-only snapshots; enables online backup while applications are active
- Revenue approximately evenly split between commercial and technical (CAD/CASE) application environments by 1996
- NetApp's focus on advanced OS software features (vs. hardware) will keep it in the lead against competitors
