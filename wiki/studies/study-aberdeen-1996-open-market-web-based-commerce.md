# Open Market: Delivering the Infrastructure For Truly Open Web-based Commerce

> Aberdeen Group profiles Open Market, Inc. (Cambridge, MA), a pioneer in enterprise-grade web commerce infrastructure. The study examines Open Market's product suite—OM-Transact, OM-Axcess, and OM-SecureLink—designed to support many-to-many web-based transactions across multiple content servers and back-office systems. Aberdeen strongly recommends Open Market's platform as the most comprehensive and production-ready solution for enterprises building next-generation electronic commerce infrastructure, and contrasts it favorably against IBM and point-to-point proprietary competitors.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Many-to-many web commerce infrastructure for Commerce Service Providers (CSPs)
- Modular plug-in, database-centric, geographically dispersed; Oracle or Sybase backend
- MD5 hash + SSL + ticketing; HTTP-standard compliant
- Over $80 million raised in IPO
- $50 million
- $27 million
- Cyber-mall approach: all transaction applications hosted on one central site
- IBM products lack advanced functionality; focus on IBM CSP services, not enterprise B2B
- Commodity HTTP server focus; not a comprehensive e-commerce infrastructure
- Commodity HTTP server (IIS); not addressing enterprise back-office commerce
- Revenue growth rate may increase significantly given CSP customer endorsements
- Should be leading infrastructure for enterprise web commerce if mindshare achieved
- AT&T named as industry-leading CSP using Open Market products
- Corporations using Open Market to provide Web capabilities for multiple divisions
- Banks, telcos, internet access providers extending offerings via Open Market
- Authentication, payment processing, order management, shipping/tax calculation, digital goods fulfillment
- 13 ISV partners for front-office web development (including Informix, Powersoft, SoftQuad)
- Sales force automation integration with Open Market back-office
- Enterprise IS executives would be highly remiss not to fully evaluate Open Market
- Acquired by Divine Inc. for approximately $59 million in 2001
- Divine filed for bankruptcy early 2003; Open Market assets split between Soverain and FatWire
- Did not sustain leadership; acquired below IPO valuation and acquirer went bankrupt
- Central access management across HTTP layer and individual database layer
- Literally hundreds of suppliers claiming to offer web-based transaction tools
- In-house custom programming is Open Market's chief competitor
