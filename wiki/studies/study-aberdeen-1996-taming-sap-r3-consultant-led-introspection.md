# Taming SAP R/3 With Consultant-Led Enterprise Introspection

> Aberdeen Group's Viewpoint argues that successful SAP R/3 implementations require enterprise introspection before deployment: organizations must honestly evaluate their need for change, ability to adopt R/3 best practices, and internal resource availability. The study introduces a three-question framework for determining implementation path, critiques both 'Big Bang' and 'blue-sky' reengineering approaches, and recommends consultant-led strategic analysis to determine the correct path and budget before R/3 deployment.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- Company's ability to adopt best practice processes embedded in R/3 must be assessed before implementation `[ps=5]`
- Prolonged blue-sky reengineering planning projects proven wasteful; customers frustrated; multi-million technology investment underemployed `[ps=5]`
- Strategic R/3 change requires: explicit business objective consensus; evaluation of all critical processes; coordination of reengineering with R/3 functionality; aggressive change management `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen separately published 'Decision-Makers Guide to Professional Services Support for SAP America Implementations' indicating large consultant-led market `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen implies R/3 will be implemented broadly; professional services for R/3 will be a major industry `[ps=5]`
- End-user project control is critical; questions raised by R/3 implementation must be answered at senior levels quickly and decisively `[ps=5]`
- R/3 pioneers report massive reengineering required with strong change management expertise `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen recommends outside firm with R/3 knowledge AND business grounding to facilitate enterprise introspection before any R/3 project `[ps=5]`
- Implementing SAP R/3 throughout an enterprise is a once-in-twenty-year project transforming operational infrastructure `[ps=4]`
- SAP customers tell Aberdeen R/3 has more functionality than any other client-server integrated business process application `[ps=4]`
- Organization must honestly evaluate corporate need and desire for fundamental enterprise change before implementing R/3 `[ps=4]`
- Availability of internal skills and resources to accomplish both anticipated business and technology changes must be assessed `[ps=4]`
- Enterprise must assess both strategic need for change AND desire for change; executives and managers may differ on both dimensions `[ps=4]`
- Mismatch of R/3 implementation strategy with sought-after business improvements avoidable via early enterprise introspection `[ps=4]`
- SAP ASAP methodology (1996), project management frameworks, and pre-implementation readiness assessments became industry standard; Aberdeen's recommendation institutionalized `[ps=3]`
- Vast majority of senior executives evaluating or implementing R/3 recognize that use of outside professional services firms is mandatory
- Departmentally-focused Big Bang implementation: appeal to customers anxious about costs; misses opportunity for enterprise-wide process reengineering
- Consultants can play critical role in raising and framing strategic questions but should not answer them; project team must answer proactively
- SAP R/3/ECC became dominant global ERP platform; SAP consulting became $50B+ annual industry; ASAP methodology institutionalized the introspection process Aberdeen described
- Organization must identify: executive sponsor; core team knowledge workers; change agents in business units
- Strategic change = enterprise-level wholesale process reengineering; Tactical change = business unit operating efficiency improvement
