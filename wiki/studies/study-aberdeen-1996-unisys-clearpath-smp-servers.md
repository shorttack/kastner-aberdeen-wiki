# Unisys Corporation - ClearPath SMP Servers

> Aberdeen Group profiles Unisys Corporation's ClearPath SMP Server product line following Unisys's 1995 reorganization into three business groups. The study examines Unisys's strategy of providing 'full-service' computing to enterprises overwhelmed by open systems complexity, covering entry-level (SMP5200/5400), mid-range (SMP61000), and high-end server offerings, clustering via Veritas Vx Reliant, and the balance between Unix openness and proprietary ClearPath reliability/availability/security (RAS) requirements.


_Published 1996, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Move to open systems shifted responsibility for component integration from suppliers to users; MIS overwhelmed by hundreds of components requiring integration expertise `[ps=5]`
- Reliability, Availability, Security (RAS): old data center standard now critical as applications shift from 'nice to have' to 'must have' `[ps=5]`
- Since 1994 Aberdeen profile, Unisys mission evolved from systems-centric to solution-centric enterprise computing `[ps=4]`
- Desktop, mid-tier servers, and back-end systems hardware requiring enterprise-wide integration `[ps=4]`
- Desktop, mid-tier, and back-end operating systems requiring consistent administration `[ps=4]`
- Unisys positions as 'one-stop-shop': hardware + OS + middleware + integration services to relieve MIS complexity burden `[ps=3]`
- Unisys reorganized 1995 into 3 groups: Global Customer Service Group, Information Service Group, Computer Systems Group `[ps=3]`
- OPUS: 8-128 Pentium processors; SVR4 Unix with Chorus microkernel; developed with Intel; suited for highest-performance requirements `[ps=3]`
- Protocols, devices, network operating systems — internal and external links requiring unified management `[ps=3]`
- Aberdeen: Unisys mission to continue moving from systems-centric to solution-centric; ClearPath SMP line to mature as enterprise platform `[ps=3]`
- Unisys transitioning from proprietary SVR4/MP Unix to SCO-consortium UnixWare 2.1 across server line in 1996 `[ps=2]`
- Unisys Information Services Group (ISG) serves as in-house systems integrator; provides tight supplier ecosystem control unavailable to pure hardware vendors `[ps=2]`
- Unisys is the largest reseller of Oracle; strong RDBMS relationships including Oracle, Informix, Red Brick `[ps=2]`
- SMP5200 (workgroup): April 1996, 1-2 processors, 100/133 MHz; SMP5400 (departmental): April 1996, up to 4 processors; SMP6400: H2 1996, 166/200 MHz Pentium Pro `[ps=0]`
- SMP5200: 256 MB memory, 24 GB disk capacity; suitable for resource-hungry workgroup applications `[ps=0]`
- SMP61000: up to 10 processors at 150 MHz (200 MHz Pentium Pro in Q3); up to 4 GB RAM initially, 8 GB by year-end; 7 PCI + 5 EISA slots `[ps=0]`
- SCM (Synchronous Coherent Memory) passive backplane: 64-bit, 533 MBps; up to 24 processors expected by year-end upgrade `[ps=0]`
- SMP5400/6400: dual PCI bus, large memory, internal RAID (0,1,5); SMP61000: 6 hot-swappable disks, redundant power supplies `[ps=0]`
- Veritas Vx Reliant: cluster 2-4 SMP nodes; 99.9% to 99.999% uptime; optional distributed lock manager for Oracle Parallel Server `[ps=0]`
- Aberdeen expects Unisys to enhance SCM bus by year-end to support 24 processors in SMP61000 `[ps=0]`
- SMP61000: 6 hot-swappable internal disks (RAID 0/1/5); additional modular cabinets for 7+ more hot-swappable drives; enterprise-grade I/O `[ps=0]`
- Unisys ClearPath Forward platform survived and continues with roadmap to 2050; however Unisys exited commodity Intel server market and refocused on services and ClearPath legacy platforms `[ps=-1]`
