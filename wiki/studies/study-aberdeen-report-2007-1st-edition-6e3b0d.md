# The Aberdeen Report — 2007

> This study analyzes The Aberdeen Report — 2007 using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2007, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- With 79% of departments reporting no plans to use business process outsourcing (BPO), 73% deciding not to outsource their data centers, and 66% passing on IT operations outsourcing, IT will continue to play a pivotal role in the corporation’s investments and directions. `[ps=4]`
- Emerging technologies like SOA, Web 2.0, global positioning, and others will have to overcome the euphoria of the early adopters and demonstrate how they can support what 52% of the respondents said is their top two advantages, namely customer satisfaction and reputation. `[ps=4]`
- Current adoption rates for hugely hyped technologies like SOA and Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) are disappointingly low, with 57% of IT departments having no plans to use SOA and 70% giving RFID the cold shoulder. `[ps=4]`
- Despite perceived immaturity, Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) is gaining ground with 72% of IT departments using, or planning to use, VoiP. `[ps=4]`
- While only 15% of companies currently use an On Demand offering, the promise of rapid implementations and reduced up front expenditure is so tempting that 47% of companies believe they will utilize On Demand solutions in 2007. `[ps=4]`
- Manufacturing is still dominated by Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP), with 73% of the respondents currently indicating its presence. `[ps=4]`
- Wireless mobility applications ranked as the most important technology for 20% of respondents, beating out well-established technologies like Business Intelligence and Human Capital Management (HCM). `[ps=4]`
- While manufacturers worldwide remain strong adopters (Figure 11) of Enterprise Planning Products (ERP), its importance has just been usurped by the need for “good data”. `[ps=4]`
- That said, 30% of manufacturers believe that ERP maintenance fees—a lucrative source of revenue for ERP vendors— will be a top expense for 2007. `[ps=4]`
- Moreover, only 7% of respondents believe that the purpose of supply chain is basic sustenance and 23%, taking their cue from supply chain success stories (e.g., Dell), believe that supply chain is already a competitive advantage for their organization. `[ps=4]`
- Interestingly, 100% of companies with revenues over $5 billion are using workflow/Business Process Management (BPM) as part of their product innovation and development initiatives. `[ps=4]`
- While customer satisfaction and retention are intuitive drivers of any service and support strategy, the introduction of revenue creation as a key business driver (Figure 23) heralds a shift in thinking. `[ps=4]`
- of service and support organizations that are part of companies making more than $1 billion a year plan to invest primarily in technology, with only 29% indicating that staffing is a top two priority. `[ps=4]`
- Given that the mandate for marketing is to generate the maximum number of qualified leads for the least cost, it is little wonder that online methods of communication have become the prominent form for reaching customers (Figure 28). `[ps=4]`
- Respondents are taking advantage of new communications protocols and technologies (Figure 29) to forge closer relationships with customers. `[ps=4]`
- More specifically, organizations cited the need to adjust recognition principles and practices to support higher performance (cited as the #1 area of HCM development by 39% of survey respondents). `[ps=4]`
- Conversely the buzz of Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) has been slow to deliver on the hype that accompanied its introduction (Figure 35). `[ps=4]`
- **Industry:** Only 1.5% of Computer Equipment and Peripherals companies plan to adopt Vista in 2007. `[ps=4]`
- **Job Function:** 89% of Finance/Accounting Managers in decision-making positions rejected a Vista upgrade in 2007, while only 11% planned to push it through. `[ps=4]`
- Finding the right person at the right time is nearly impossible according to 65% of companies. `[ps=4]`
- For example, IBM dominates the large IT managed services industry but at the small and medium-sized business (SMB) end, independent contractors own nearly 40% of the business. `[ps=3]`
- Manufacturing comes under tremendous pressure in 2007 as price compression, shortened lead times, shrinking budgets, and booming costs all conspire to create a myriad of challenges (Figure 9). `[ps=3]`
- Figure 15 identifies a grass roots approach to leveraging technology in the supply chain through better visibility, collaboration, planning and reporting. `[ps=3]`
- Almost one-third of respondents identified establishing an E-sourcing portal as the most cited initiative planned and while On Demand is not seen as a transformational strategy it is currently used by 25% of the organizations polled and on the planning horizon for an additional 28%. `[ps=3]`
- The investment in technology is also underscored by the fact that 96% of survey respondents have no plans to outsource sales. `[ps=3]`
