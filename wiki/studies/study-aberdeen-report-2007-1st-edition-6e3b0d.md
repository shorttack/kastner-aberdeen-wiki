# The Aberdeen Report — 2007

> This study analyzes The Aberdeen Report — 2007 using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2007, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- While it may come as no surprise that business fundamentals of buy low, sell high, collect early, and pay late in principal still apply, there are critical changes underfoot in the relative importance of strategic priorities organizations make to empower their success (Figure 2). `[ps=5]`
- Emerging technologies like SOA, Web 2.0, global positioning, and others will have to overcome the euphoria of the early adopters and demonstrate how they can support what 52% of the respondents said is their top two advantages, namely customer satisfaction and reputation. `[ps=5]`
- The pressure to outsource is a leading concern for only 4% of IT departments. `[ps=5]`
- Despite perceived immaturity, Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) is gaining ground with 72% of IT departments using, or planning to use, VoiP. `[ps=5]`
- Manufacturing is still dominated by Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP), with 73% of the respondents currently indicating its presence. `[ps=5]`
- While manufacturers worldwide remain strong adopters (Figure 11) of Enterprise Planning Products (ERP), its importance has just been usurped by the need for “good data”. `[ps=5]`
- Respondents cited not only technology but also process-oriented approaches as ways to achieve manufacturing goals, as shown in Figure 10. `[ps=5]`
- In fact, 48% of respondents in this area identified cross functional teams as being the most important transformational strategy for their group in 2007 (Figure 13). `[ps=5]`
- By contrast, only 18% of respondents named cost cutting as the primary goal. `[ps=5]`
- Moreover, only 7% of respondents believe that the purpose of supply chain is basic sustenance and 23%, taking their cue from supply chain success stories (e.g., Dell), believe that supply chain is already a competitive advantage for their organization. `[ps=5]`
- Figure 15 identifies a grass roots approach to leveraging technology in the supply chain through better visibility, collaboration, planning and reporting. `[ps=5]`
- When it comes to technology initiatives within procurement there does not appear to be any shortage (Figure 18) of planned, pending, and active projects. `[ps=5]`
- While customer satisfaction and retention are intuitive drivers of any service and support strategy, the introduction of revenue creation as a key business driver (Figure 23) heralds a shift in thinking. `[ps=5]`
- _“Right now, [services and support] revenues are small, less than 5% of our overall revenues. `[ps=5]`
- Given that the mandate for marketing is to generate the maximum number of qualified leads for the least cost, it is little wonder that online methods of communication have become the prominent form for reaching customers (Figure 28). `[ps=5]`
- Respondents are taking advantage of new communications protocols and technologies (Figure 29) to forge closer relationships with customers. `[ps=5]`
- It focues on attracting, developing, leveraging, and retaining talent while aligning the purpose and performance of that human capital to specific and measurable outcomes that link to business goals (Figure 30). `[ps=5]`
- The emphasis on compensation barely surpassed the importance of a company’s ability to systematically source, track, hire and efficiently onboard employees, with 39% of respondents citing this as one of their top two initiatives. `[ps=5]`
- While investments in wide area networks (WAN), local area networks (LAN), wireless LAN, and mobile convergence were identified by respondents as generally equally weighted with one-third voting these as top two initiatives, unified communications weighed in as the top technology to influence communications in 2007 with 53% ranking it as most important (Figure 33). `[ps=5]`
- **Industry:** Only 1.5% of Computer Equipment and Peripherals companies plan to adopt Vista in 2007. `[ps=5]`
- **Job Function:** 89% of Finance/Accounting Managers in decision-making positions rejected a Vista upgrade in 2007, while only 11% planned to push it through. `[ps=5]`
- Revenue growth and profitability were cumulatively cited by 73% of all survey respondents when it came to the number one goal of corporations in 2007. `[ps=5]`
- Having addressed Y2K fears, survived the dot com demise, and accepted regulatory compliance as a cost of doing business (47% of respondents also says it slows progress), it’s interesting to note how organizations are spending their money in 2007. `[ps=4]`
- Figure 3 clearly identifies the top 3 investment areas for the year as sales and marketing, technology, and staffing. `[ps=4]`
- Current adoption rates for hugely hyped technologies like SOA and Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) are disappointingly low, with 57% of IT departments having no plans to use SOA and 70% giving RFID the cold shoulder. `[ps=4]`
