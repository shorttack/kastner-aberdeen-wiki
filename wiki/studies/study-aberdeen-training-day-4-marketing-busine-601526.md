# Marketing and Business Development Basics (Twelve Vital Hours, Session 4)

> Aberdeen Group's Day 4 / Session 4 training for new analyst hires, delivered August 2000 by partners Logan, Willmott, and Kastner. Covers the professional-services trust-building framework, supplier product life-cycle stage analysis, how to close supplier-initiated meetings with Profile / RAMP / Personal Retainer Service proposals, and the anatomy of Aberdeen's written proposals. Includes pricing guidelines ($15,000 Profile fee; $60,000-$100,000 RAMP; $15,000/year retainer), the 'numbers game' philosophy for building a client base, and Aberdeen's go-to-market history from 1988 to 1994. Together with Sessions 1, 2, and 3, completes the Twelve Vital Hours orientation curriculum.


_Published 2000, author **Peter S. Kastner / John Logan / Thomas Willmott / Aberdeen Group**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- Accountants, architects, doctors, insurance brokers, lawyers, plumbers, undertakers, and all Aberdeen employees are in the Professional Services Business
- Clients hire on trust; they never get to see results until after a project is complete
- Trust means always being honest and objective; telling client 'You are the best' is easy but if market share declines, trust is broken
- Trust means respecting client confidence; clients will not trust you in future if you break another firm's NDA, even in your enthusiasm
- Trust means doing what you say you will do and quickly disclosing what Aberdeen cannot do
- A successful 'first date' project — such as preparing a Profile or giving a speech — can build the trust necessary to move to the next level
- What functionality, distribution channels, and other attributes (pricing) must our product have to be successful? What market segments should we target?
- How can Aberdeen better educate the market about the value and advantages our products and services provide users? With whom should we partner?
- We have heard the initial market response to our value proposition. Could Aberdeen tell us what it really means and where we should invest our resources for success?
- What factors are stopping prospective buyers from acquiring products from us? How can we cross the chasm?
- How can we differentiate our products and services so that the majority of IS decision makers will see us as the safe choice for the future?
- How does our firm become or remain #1 in our marketplace?
- What additional products, services, distribution channels, target markets should we add to further fuel growth rates and outflank competition? Who should we consider acquiring?
- How do we maintain the loyalty of our installed base and hold off competition from alternative technologies?
- How do we motivate our installed base to continue to invest in our products to maximize the return on their past investments?
- What should we do to rebuild momentum? And if we cannot, how can we best extend product's life while using key assets to enter new, high growth markets?
- Suppliers find it difficult to know how to do business with Aberdeen
- If not optimistic, odds are very slim that the firm will engage Aberdeen at end of this meeting; do not write a Profile on a product that is inappropriate for the market
- Aberdeen can help leverage your marketing messages through an independent perspective in the form of a Profile; fee approximately $15,000; if findings contrary to expectations, no requirement to publish
- Aberdeen does not churn out paper; $15,000/year: 2 consulting days on-site, quarterly telephone updates, telephone inquiries, all practice area publications
- I honestly believe you are misunderstanding how real IS decision makers view this issue; RAMP: $60,000-$100,000; initial findings 30 days; complete 60 days
- Aberdeen will not work on projects where there is a potential conflict of interest between two different clients
- The more suppliers you meet with, the more projects you will obtain; the more Aberdeen publications you author, the more projects you will obtain
- Year 1: Profile with web rights $25K; Year 2: Analysts Plus Service + Profile + Professional services $65K; Year 3: Personal Retainer + two Profiles + RAMP $150K+
- Return press calls Wednesday afternoon and Thursday mornings; prepare three Viewpoints on distributed computing; mail to 650 most influential executives on six-week cycle
