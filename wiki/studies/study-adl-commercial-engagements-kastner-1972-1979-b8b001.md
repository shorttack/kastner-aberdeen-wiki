# Kastner's ADL Commercial Engagements: Chase Manhattan TOOS, UFRJ Brazil Hospital, Houghton Mifflin, Ocean Spray, and Other Industry Studies (1972-1979)

> Kastner's commercial-systems engagements at Arthur D. Little Systems, 1972-1979. Covers (a) Chase Manhattan Bank Trust Operating Operating System (TOOS) using COSMOS and IBM 360/65 to replace failing UNIVAC card-based trust system; (b) UFRJ Brazil teaching hospital administration on Burroughs B6700 (forms-based bicycle-courier batch design); (c) Houghton Mifflin Honeywell-2000 sales analysis and crisis-mode order-entry remediation; (d) Ocean Spray Cranberries Burroughs B1700 financial/order-entry system; (e) Citibank international funds-transfer minicomputer network proposal; (f) Mass Mutual COBOL incentive bonus on IBM 370/135; (g) Nortel North American order processing on IBM 370/158 CICS+TOTAL; (h) Parade Magazine business-systems study; (i) DEC payroll-personnel on DEC-10; (j) AS&E CAT-scanning audit; (k) US Navy CASDOS ship design.


_Published 2025, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- Chase Manhattan trust operations relied on Univac 96-column card processing; Univac exit from unit-record business in 1972 forced replacement
- Chase chose IBM 360/65 mainframe for new trust operations system
- Chase janitorial staff inadvertently dumped world's supply of Univac unit-record spare parts at sea over a long weekend
- Chase IT project produced 400 written COBOL programs with no system flow chart
- ADL substituted COSMOS-based approach for floundering Chase IT project, conceived by Murray Sherry
- Kastner was lead designer and chief programmer of the securities subsystem under TOOS
- Mary Ellen 'Mel' Holland — Marine Midland alum and first woman with Columbia MBA — co-developed Chase TOOS customer application
- Once converted, IBM 360/65 processed 174,000 unique securities held by 25,000+ trust clients with combined value over $10 billion (1975 dollars)
- Rockefeller's quarterly Exxon dividends required field width >$99,999,999.99; Kastner asked 'how big' and was told 'one more 9'
- Kastner and Holland produced 25M lines of debugged COBOL ready in under a year using COSMOS
- Kastner's COSMOS code at Chase reportedly never caused an application rerun once in production
- UFRJ teaching hospital administration system designed for Burroughs B6700 university mainframe
- Forms carried by bicycle-courier from hospital to data center continually, replacing originally proposed online terminals due to USD foreign-exchange constraints
- Design documents and COBOL code written in English; forms documents translated by professional translators
- Burroughs B6700 indexed sequential access method for COBOL did not work; Kastner wrote a small program to demonstrate the bug, brought results back to Boston for Burroughs fix
- Kastner directed conversion of hospital inventory control system from Burroughs B3500 to B6700 in university environment
- Hans Nijkamp was Dutchman hired as UFRJ project leader; Kastner viewed appointment as unnecessary
- Peter Kastner's wife Kate (COBOL programmer) joined the UFRJ project; ADL paid two reduced stipends
- Barbara Kruesi volunteered from ADLS as third COBOL programmer on UFRJ
- Houghton Mifflin was a Honeywell shop running 2000-series mainframe
- HM's new order-entry/inventory system was so flawed that bookstores refused to pay invoices and physical inventory failed audits; HM could not close books and risked NYSE suspension
- After remediation, more than 100 days of HM production had to be re-run for NYSE reporting
- HM teenager driving cards/tapes/disks to Honeywell regional data center on Route 128 flipped the van
- Bomb threats during HM crisis cleared the building for hours; by the fifteenth, no big deal
- Burroughs B1700 with Forte II language and NDL was selected; TC500 intelligent terminals at four locations across country
