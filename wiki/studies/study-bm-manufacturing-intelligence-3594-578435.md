## Top observations

- Best in Class manufacturers are leveraging intelligent solutions that can also provide real-time visibility, event management and analytical tools that enhance proactive and predictive control to drive execution, instead of only operating as another data collection system. `[ps=5]`
- While manufacturers in the study were all at different levels of maturity with either MES or MI, Best in Class performers while more mature in their adoption of MES, were additionally 10% more likely than laggard and average companies to have also invested in manufacturing intelligence. `[ps=5]`
- Through this enhanced layer of intelligence, Best in Class are placing a priority on quality measures, providing portal capabilities from operators to decision-makers, and enabling root cause analysis. `[ps=5]`
- Best in Class have successfully improved performance since implementation of their MI solutions, with significant gains in yield in particular (Table 1). `[ps=5]`
- These gains exceed competitors, with yield in particular 20% higher than laggard and average companies, which is due largely to 68% of Best in Class in turn placing a priority on using manufacturing intelligence to _reduce variability_ . `[ps=5]`
- Best in Class companies reap significant benefits by inserting a layer of Manufacturing Intelligence between the two in order to both aggregate data from disparate sources and turn it into actionable intelligence. `[ps=5]`
- Figure 1 highlights the continuing pressures manufacturers face today driving performance improvement initiatives, including the introduction of Manufacturing Intelligence solutions. `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen found Best in Class companies reaping significant benefits by inserting this layer between the two, thereby _**aggregating data from disparate sources, enabling actionable intelligence**_ . `[ps=5]`
- By creating a unified view, manufacturers can leverage existing data sources from plant or shop floor data collection and execution systems, as well as enterprise level data such as orders, schedules and inventory from enterprise applications (Figure 2). `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen found Best in Class companies more effectively closing this loop and delivering ROI (Return on Investment) on enterprise applications by leveraging existing data and proactively managing the flow and process of manufacturing. `[ps=5]`
- While Best in Class are reaping these rewards, Aberdeen found the visibility gap between ERP and the factory floor still persists in the majority of companies. `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen has observed the growth of MES adoption, with its _Global Manufacturing: MES and Beyond Benchmark Report_ (May 2006) _,_ which revealed that 71% of respondents had implemented or were planning to implement MES. `[ps=5]`
- Although the deployment of MES is in some stage of deployment by most of the survey participants, the maturity of these solutions and the ability to provide detailed information to decision-makers and operators in real-time serve to differentiate Best in Class from the rest. `[ps=5]`
- Incorporating MI into integration strategies correlates with Best in Class performance `[ps=5]`
- Observe that Best in Class companies are _**more**_ likely than average and laggard companies to insert MI into their integration strategies. `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen also found Best in Class twice as likely as laggard manufacturers to have _MES integrated for real-time KPI monitoring_ (60% versus 31%,) and 41% of Best in Class manufacturers with _integrated MI for real-time KPI monitoring_ versus 16% of laggards. `[ps=5]`
- The path to Best in Class status involves not only the implementation of portal capabilities, but also in coupling them with predictive, real-time analytic capabilities and event management. `[ps=5]`
- Through enabling a portal view into processes as they occur and monitoring events and drilling down into areas of concern, Best in Class manufacturers are taking a proactive view of manufacturing and providing the tools that look beyond symptoms to provide the detail to enable root cause analysis. `[ps=5]`
- In terms of processes, Best in Class manufacturers enabled with real-time analytics are more likely to have the ability to determine the root cause of problems and to have enhanced predictive capabilities to prevent future quality and scheduling problems. `[ps=5]`
- Additionally, 56% of Best in Class manufacturers (versus 48% of laggard companies, not shown) have set priorities for improving product quality and reducing variability. `[ps=5]`
- Through the correlation of process information and product measurement and testing, Best in Class performers are able to improve visibility and enhance predictive control while improving the quality of products. `[ps=5]`
- To enable truly proactive decision-making through manufacturing intelligence, Best in Class manufacturers can monitor events in real-time and take corrective action when plant production falls out of specification. `[ps=5]`
- In fact, Best in Class manufacturers are leveraging manufacturing intelligence to make proactive and predictive decisions based on throughput, efficiency, effectiveness, and process bottleneck constraint analysis. `[ps=5]`
