# Digital's top finance chief quits (DEC CFO William Steul resigns)

> Boston Globe (May 1994) reports DEC CFO William M. Steul, 51, resigned a month after DEC's stock plummeted on a $183M unexpected Q3 loss. Second top-rank departure since the April 15 announcement (Edward E. Lucente, worldwide sales chief and #2 exec, left days after). Vincent J. Mullarkey, 46, corporate controller (Bentley College 1971, joined DEC same year), promoted to CFO. Quote from Franc Romano of Aberdeen Group (former DEC employee) criticizes Mullarkey: 'The only financial experience Vin has ever had was with Digital. I don't believe he brings the broad perspective that is necessary there or the cash management skills the company is going to need.' Counter-quote from Richard Buchanan of Forrester Research: DEC's accounting system 'is so arcane and quite specific to the history that Digital has passed through. To bring in an outsider to decipher that would be tough to do' but 'is broken in many ways and needs to be fixed.' Steul was 22-year DEC veteran, two years as CFO; predecessor was James Osterhoff (Ford Motor recruit, highly regarded for financial discipline). Mullarkey was Robert Palmer's controller in DEC semiconductor group.


_Published 1994, author **Aaron Zitner (The Boston Globe)**, type **trade-press-article**._


## Top observations

- $183M loss announced April 15, 1994; stock plummeted; analysts criticized DEC's inability to predict its own results
- DEC CFO William M. Steul, 51, resigned May 1994; 22-year DEC veteran; CFO since June 1992
- DEC worldwide sales chief Edward E. Lucente (also #2 exec) left days after April 15 loss
- Vincent J. Mullarkey, 46, corporate controller (joined DEC 1971), promoted to VP/CFO
- 'The only financial experience Vin has ever had was with Digital. I don't believe he brings the broad perspective that is necessary there or the cash management skills the company is going to need.'
- DEC's financial accounting system 'is so arcane and quite specific to the history that Digital has passed through. To bring in an outsider to decipher that would be tough to do.' Also: 'is broken in many ways and needs to be fixed.'
- Romano was a former DEC employee — gives him insider perspective on the financial-organization politics
- Steul's predecessor at DEC was James Osterhoff (Ford Motor recruit) — 'highly regarded for imposing financial discipline'
- Steul named CFO by Olsen in June 1992, weeks before board asked Olsen to resign as president — 'rise had been controversial because he had not worked in the finance operation for more than a decade'
- DEC sold to Compaq Computer in 1998 for $9.6B — the consolidation Kastner predicted in 1989 WSJ quote (Honeywell Bull article) extended to DEC
