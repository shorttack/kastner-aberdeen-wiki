# What Every CFO Should Know About the Year 2000 IT Tsunami (CA CFO Executive Exchange December 1997)

> 'What Every CFO Should Know About the Year 2000 IT Tsunami' — Peter S. Kastner presentation to Computer Associates' CFO Executive Exchange, December 3-6, 1997. Argues that CFOs must treat Y2K as an existential business-continuity issue, with specific deadlines: Y2K-safe applications in place by September 30, 1998 for five quarters of testing/cutover; twelve-month renovation program started by September 30, 1997. Recommends customer/supplier audits, EDI-link testing, insurance review, and management-options (renovate/replace/outsource/retire) analysis.


_Published 1997, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- No catastrophic Y2K failures on 1/1/2000 attributable to major enterprises that followed the recommended remediation timeline; enterprises that started late (post-1998) reported higher cost overruns and near-misses. `[ps=5]`
- Must audit Y2K progress of customers and suppliers and test EDI links through 1999. `[ps=4]`
- Renovate / Replace / Outsource / Retire — four options for each Y2K-affected application. `[ps=4]`
- Majority of US enterprises are acting as though they have 2-3 years to fix Y2K, but true deadline is 15 months earlier; 'if you are not working on the problem today, you may have already failed'. `[ps=4]`
- Customers, suppliers, and competitors may be in trouble; existing value chains have every probability of experiencing broken links; opportunity to move into competitors' territory if they fail. `[ps=2]`
- To be Y2K-safe by 1/1/2000: Y2K-safe applications in place by 9/30/98 for 5 quarters of testing/cutover; 12-month renovation/replacement program started by 9/30/97.
- EDI links were a frequent source of Y2K remediation scope creep in 1999; Kastner's early-audit recommendation was widely adopted through 1998-1999.
- Kastner regularly spoke at CA executive events through the late 1990s (Acapulco CIO Conference May 1997; CFO Executive Exchange December 1997) — evidence of deep analyst-client relationship with CA.
