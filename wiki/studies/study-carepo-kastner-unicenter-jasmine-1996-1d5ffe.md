# Aberdeen Group Assessment of Unicenter and Jasmine for Computer Associates

> Letter-form report from Peter S. Kastner (Group Vice President, Aberdeen Group) to David M. Hood (Director of Analyst Relations, Computer Associates) following a two-day analyst roundtable evaluation of CA's Unicenter and Jasmine products. Kastner delivers pointed positioning recommendations to maximize 1997 revenue, including brand-led advertising, ISV instrumentation, a VAR program, non-traditional environments, a national fault-response center, and competitive collateral against Tivoli. The letter also proposes a broad Aberdeen-CA consulting engagement.


_Published 1996, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- dominant market share, largest installed base, impressive/leading technology
- 'Ask for the #1 product by name' branding stage
- business-executive ads in Business Week, Forbes
- Measure, Secure, Adapt, Manage
- instrument name-brand first-tier business apps for Unicenter management
- aggressive worldwide VAR program for lower/middle-tier markets
- establish a partner-run national fault-response center for small companies
- invest modestly to produce references and success stories
- target management of office buildings, shop-floor robots, PBX, routers
- CA has no competitive white paper to hand to analysts
- 'We are unconvinced that Tivoli's product as now constituted will be IBM's product of three years from now'
- Tivoli TME was repeatedly rearchitected post-1996 IBM acquisition; rebranded through multiple generations (TME 10, Tivoli Enterprise, etc.)
- 'not to buy from a company whose initials are Computer Associates'
- commission a TCO study of Unicenter vs selected competitors
- Aberdeen consulting proposals for high-level market messages, white papers, CEO/CIO speeches, partner training, go-to-market consulting, case studies
- can tie computing resources together — past, present, and future
- CIOs hold service contracts with user orgs; Unicenter can raise service levels, scale, cut costs
- new support model for unserved small companies via partners
