> Executive Viewpoint #2 examines the structural pressures that led to the billing and booking accounting irregularities at Tandem Computers and Datapoint Corporation. Casale argues that Wall Street's quarterly earnings treadmill—where pension sponsors rank managers quarterly, managers demand near-term corporate earnings, and corporations inflate results to protect their stock—creates systemic pressure on hypergrowth companies to smooth or accelerate reported earnings. The newsletter introduces the fictional composite firm Fast Track Incorporated (FTI) to personify the dynamics of hypergrowth under investor scrutiny.


_Published 1983, author **Charles T. Casale, C.F.A.**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Pension sponsors rank managers quarterly; a few bottom-quartile quarters means dismissal. Managers demand near-term earnings. Corporations inflate prospects to stay in portfolios. `[ps=5]`
- As P/E multiples soar, investor expectations rise proportionally; any miss causes a disproportionate stock crash that lingers in 'penalty box' for multiple quarters. `[ps=5]`
- Earlier billing and booking irregularities; compensation plan heavily skewed to bookings rather than shipments or customer acceptance. `[ps=5]`
- Successful earnings smoothing by CFO leads analysts to take smooth earnings for granted; estimates rise; CFO warns estimates are not achievable; rubber band eventually snaps. `[ps=5]`
- CFO builds 'cushion into operating methods' to immunize earnings from real business volatility; methods become invisible to analysts once normalized. `[ps=5]`
- In the 1950s, corporate America worked on annual cycles; the shift to quarterly earnings estimates and portfolio measurements was gradual but transformed the incentive landscape. `[ps=5]`
- Sell-side analysts who discover a stock early face competitive pressure from later-arriving analysts who escalate estimates to differentiate their recommendations. `[ps=4]`
- Earnings glitches for hypergrowth companies are inevitable; investors compete to short-sell at the top. `[ps=4]`
- Analysts perpetually worry whether IBM or some other colossus will 'eat FTI's lunch'; this worry drives intensive quarterly questioning of management. `[ps=4]`
- EVP-3 and EVP-4 to analyze: (1) operating cycle of hypergrowth firms — backlog, bookings, billings; (2) financial statement distortions caused by operating cycle. `[ps=4]`
- Two escape paths: (1) run even faster — grow through the pressure; (2) settle back into moderate-speed lane matching industry growth. `[ps=3]`
- Fiscal 1982 results being restated downward due to booking and billing practices. `[ps=0]`
- $250M to $500M annual revenue (quarter-billion to half-billion dollar sales level) `[ps=0]`
- [UNVERIFIED] `[ps=0]`
- FTI heroically made quarter despite losing a week of shipments from external disaster; analysts shaved estimate but earnings came through intact, raising multiple. `[ps=0]`
