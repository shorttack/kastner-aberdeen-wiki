> CNBC Technology Edge (hosted by Bruce Francis, Aug 6 1994) examines IBM, DEC, and HP transitioning from proprietary systems to open systems and client-server. Peter S. Kastner (Aberdeen Group VP), Stephen Roach (Morgan Stanley), Pat Sochi (TCG), and George Tabak (Ingersoll Rand) score HP highest (visionary, ~10 years ahead on open systems), IBM as recovering, and DEC as severely troubled — VAX/VMS declining 20-40% annually, Alpha introduced too soon without applications, confused Ultrix→OSF migration messaging. Kastner predicts DEC may be acquired below book value (e.g., by an RBOC), notes mainframes will be around 'a long, long time' due to installed software, and warns the Intel/HP alliance reshapes post-2000 chip economics.


_Published 1994, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **transcript**._


## Top observations

- HP has the highest score on visionary open-systems transition; started ~10 years ago
- IBM has solid low-end AS/400 foundation; RISC chip planned for next year to accelerate
- DEC introduced Alpha much too soon; no apps, third-party support absent, 64-bit unused
- Shift from Ultrix to OSF causes the same migration cost as moving to Sun, HP, or IBM — opens the door for competitors
- DEC will focus on a thousand companies and sell through channels — others must dial 1-800-DIGITAL
- DEC's VAX/VMS business declining at 20-40% annually
- DEC now ~40% of sales from PCs — company 'tearing itself apart internally'
- DEC installed base will be there a long time, but company doesn't understand how important it is to re-satisfy VAX base
- DEC could be sold way below book value to a cash-rich company, perhaps an RBOC
- [DEFERRED]
- Mainframes will be around for a long, long time because of installed software
- [DEFERRED]
- HP-Intel alliance to build post-2000 chips says a lot to HP and Intel customers about direction
- HP-Intel partnership will set the dominant chip architecture after 2000
- [DEFERRED]
- Apple, IBM, Motorola OLE and object-orientation will play significant role via embedded tech and faster apps
- Open-ended technology acquisition is over; service sector now demands measurable productivity paybacks due to deregulation and globalization
- Hardware vendors must show how technology helps the business complete its mission faster, better, cheaper than competitors — a new computer is just a commodity
- Vendors must partner with third parties to deliver integrated solutions rather than commodities
- IBM, DEC, HP are 'dinosaurs' that must metamorphose or face extinction
- Product introduction was measured in years; now every six months — no one is willing to wait
- NCR was purchased by AT&T — example of consolidation Kastner expects to continue
- Consolidation is a trend that will be with us for years, if not decades
