# This Is What 'Computer Dating' Looked Like In The 1960s

> Business Insider historical piece recounting the mid-1960s origins of computer dating: the Harvard-originated Operation Match (1965) and its New York follow-on Project TACT (Technical Automated Compatibility Testing) by Lewis Altfest and Robert Ross, running on an IBM 1400 Series computer for $5 per client. TACT spread across NYC before becoming the subject of a 1966 Brooklyn DA grand jury over student questionnaires. Cited as the direct ancestor of today's Match.com.


_Published 2011, author **Aimee Groth**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- $5 per client
- More than 100 questions per client
- Five blue cards (for women) or five pink cards (for men) listing matches
- Brooklyn Board noticed students filling out 'questionable' dating surveys; Brooklyn DA Aaron Koota convened grand jury on invitation to 'Pick em cuter by computer'
- Business Insider frames 1965 TACT and Operation Match as precursors to the ~20M-member Match.com of 2011; 46-year lineage from batch IBM 1400 to web-scale dating
- ~20 million members
