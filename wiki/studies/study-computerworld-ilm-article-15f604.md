# Waiting for ILM?

> Published in Computerworld on 29 July 2004, this advisory article argues that integrated cross-application ILM software is 4-5 years away from maturity but that enterprises should begin evolving toward ILM now using a six-step recipe: (1) centralize storage into SANs/NAS, (2) classify data across three axes (type/access/pool), (3) create lifecycle policies, (4) populate new applications on appropriate pools, (5) drive economies of scale via virtualization, (6) implement intelligent ILM circa 2008-2010. The article introduces the four-pool storage model (online FC/SCSI, midline, nearline, offline tape) and projects 45% annual storage growth driving a swing toward midline disk for reference data.


_Published 2004, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- Integrated cross-application ILM software is 4-5 years from practical enterprise-wide maturity (circa 2008-2009)
- [UNVERIFIED]
- SANs supplemented with NAS now affordable for even small enterprises
- Midline disk at approximately 25% of FC/SCSI cost for moderate random access workloads
- More than 50% of enterprise data is semi-structured or unstructured and seldom changing
- 45% per year (industry projection)
- Midline disk-to-disk backup compresses backup times by up to 50% vs tape
- Offline tapes represent an enormous liability in litigation; data retention policy adherence required
- Every application platform changes within 5 years; use refresh points to migrate data to proper pool
- Rising terabytes-per-admin ratio as virtualization becomes embedded and automation reduces intervention
- Storage-related hours on production systems reduced by 80% when ILM matures circa 2008-2010
- [UNVERIFIED]
- FC/SCSI growth projected to be modest as midline pool absorbs workloads previously on expensive disks
- Six steps must be taken in order; each step implies existence of the prior one
- Increasing number of IT professionals standardizing on four-pool storage model
- Systematically analyzing which pool data belongs in is messy but enormously beneficial
