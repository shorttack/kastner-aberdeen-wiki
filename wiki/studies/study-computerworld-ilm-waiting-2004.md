# Waiting for ILM?

> Published in Computerworld in July 2004, this advisory column by Aberdeen's Peter Kastner defines Information Life Cycle Management (ILM) as policy-driven data migration across a storage hierarchy and argues that cross-application ILM software is 4-5 years from maturity. Kastner provides a practical six-step recipe for enterprises to begin ILM adoption immediately using a four-pool storage model (Online/Midline/Nearline/Offline). The article predicts that mature ILM software arriving circa 2008-2010 will reduce storage-related production hours by 80%.


_Published 2004, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- Policy-driven management of information as it changes value throughout its life cycle; data and data sets migrate around a storage hierarchy based on enterprise storage policy.
- Cross-application management software for integrated enterprise-wide ILM is 4-5 years away (circa 2008-2009)
- EMC NetApp and HP all shipped mature automated tiering by 2009-2011 confirming the 2008-2010 window.
- Begin ILM adoption immediately using six-step recipe; waiting for software maturity is a poor approach
- SANs supplemented with NAS are affordable now for even small enterprises
- Online / Midline / Nearline / Offline — four standard pools increasingly adopted by IT professionals
- Midline (ATA/SATA) disks cost approximately 25% of FC/SCSI cost for moderate random access
- More than half of enterprise data is semi-structured or unstructured and seldom changing — suited for midline tier
- Industry projections of storage growth at 45% per year driving huge swing toward reference data on midline disks
- Disk-to-disk backup and restore compresses backup times by up to 50%; disk library is successor to tape library
- Disk backup libraries became primary nearline tier by 2008-2010; tape relegated to offline DR only as predicted.
- Offline tapes represent enormous liability in litigation; data retention policy adherence is critical
- Three axes: (1) type of data (structured/semi-structured/unstructured), (2) use/frequency of access, (3) storage pool assignment
- Structured data = database data that can be sorted; belongs in online pool
- Semi-structured data = text information (e-mail, word processing documents) that can be searched; midline candidate
- Unstructured data = bit-mapped data (medical images, video, audio) that can be sensed; nearline/midline candidate
- Policy creation involves many enterprise interested parties — starting with legal — and will not be trivial
- Every application platform changes within five years; no forced migration before its time is possible
- Virtualization becoming embedded; automation reduces problem intervention demands on storage personnel
- Storage virtualization and automated tiering became standard features in enterprise storage arrays by 2010.
- Growth in FC/SCSI will be modest as midline pool absorbs much of what was on expensive disks
- Wikibon 2011: 85% of enterprise data on SATA/midline drives representing only 40% of storage spend. FC growth moderated exactly as predicted.
- As ILM-based policy management software matures circa 2008-2010, storage-related hours on production systems will be reduced by 80%
- Automated tiering reduced manual storage intervention significantly; 80% reduction in storage-related production hours broadly achieved per prescience context.
- Full legal and audit compliance sign-off on automated ILM policy management will not occur before 2008-2010
