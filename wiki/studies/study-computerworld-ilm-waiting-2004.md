# Waiting for ILM?

> Published in Computerworld in July 2004, this advisory column by Aberdeen's Peter Kastner defines Information Life Cycle Management (ILM) as policy-driven data migration across a storage hierarchy and argues that cross-application ILM software is 4-5 years from maturity. Kastner provides a practical six-step recipe for enterprises to begin ILM adoption immediately using a four-pool storage model (Online/Midline/Nearline/Offline). The article predicts that mature ILM software arriving circa 2008-2010 will reduce storage-related production hours by 80%.


_Published 2004, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- Offline tapes represent enormous liability in litigation; data retention policy adherence is critical `[ps=5]`
- Policy creation involves many enterprise interested parties — starting with legal — and will not be trivial `[ps=5]`
- Virtualization becoming embedded; automation reduces problem intervention demands on storage personnel `[ps=5]`
- More storage will exist in 2010 than in 2004 even with 80% efficiency gain — growth overrides efficiency savings `[ps=5]`
- Midline: capacity-oriented disks for active fixed content and compliance data; ~25% cost of FC/SCSI; moderate random access `[ps=5]`
- Policy-driven management of information as it changes value throughout its life cycle; data and data sets migrate around a storage hierarchy based on enterprise storage policy. `[ps=4]`
- More than half of enterprise data is semi-structured or unstructured and seldom changing — suited for midline tier `[ps=4]`
- Industry projections of storage growth at 45% per year driving huge swing toward reference data on midline disks `[ps=4]`
- Disk backup libraries became primary nearline tier by 2008-2010; tape relegated to offline DR only as predicted. `[ps=4]`
- Three axes: (1) type of data (structured/semi-structured/unstructured), (2) use/frequency of access, (3) storage pool assignment `[ps=4]`
- Growth in FC/SCSI will be modest as midline pool absorbs much of what was on expensive disks `[ps=4]`
- Wikibon 2011: 85% of enterprise data on SATA/midline drives representing only 40% of storage spend. FC growth moderated exactly as predicted. `[ps=4]`
- Full legal and audit compliance sign-off on automated ILM policy management will not occur before 2008-2010 `[ps=4]`
- Online dynamic data: OLTP and high-activity DSS on FC/SCSI disks including structured database data; highest performance tier `[ps=4]`
- Nearline buffered data: adds midline disk-to-disk backup and restore to existing tape pool; compresses backup times by up to 50% `[ps=4]`
- Offline data: offsite sequential tape for disaster recovery; no change from current practice; data retention liability risk `[ps=4]`
- Compliance data cited as a primary use case for the midline disk storage tier alongside fixed content `[ps=4]`
- Cross-application management software for integrated enterprise-wide ILM is 4-5 years away (circa 2008-2009) `[ps=3]`
- Begin ILM adoption immediately using six-step recipe; waiting for software maturity is a poor approach `[ps=3]`
- SANs supplemented with NAS are affordable now for even small enterprises `[ps=3]`
- Midline (ATA/SATA) disks cost approximately 25% of FC/SCSI cost for moderate random access `[ps=3]`
- Disk-to-disk backup and restore compresses backup times by up to 50%; disk library is successor to tape library `[ps=3]`
- Storage virtualization and automated tiering became standard features in enterprise storage arrays by 2010. `[ps=3]`
- Automated tiering reduced manual storage intervention significantly; 80% reduction in storage-related production hours broadly achieved per prescience context. `[ps=3]`
- Online / Midline / Nearline / Offline — four standard pools increasingly adopted by IT professionals `[ps=2]`
