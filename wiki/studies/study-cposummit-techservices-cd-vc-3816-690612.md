# The 2006 CPO’s Summit: Technology and Services Benchmark Report

> This study analyzes The 2006 CPO’s Summit: Technology and Services Benchmark Report using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


## Top observations

- 42% of attendees voiced “integration” as their top concern for implementing an OnDemand solution. `[ps=5]`
- CPO interest in On-Demand technologies looks even stronger through 2007 and beyond as more than half say they will adopt new solutions or expand existing ones (Figure 5). `[ps=5]`
- It is no surprise that the majority of CPOs chose an integration scenario that links contracts to the upstream sourcing process as well as the down stream e-procurement process (Figure 8). `[ps=5]`
- Nearly one-third of CPOs indicated that the top area of investment for their sourcing initiative was T spend analysis (Figure 10). `[ps=5]`
- After all, the 40% of CPOs using OnDemand today are doing so for a reason. `[ps=4]`
- Almost half of CPOs have their spend data split across 2 to 5 different systems (Figure 5). `[ps=4]`
- Thirty-eight percent of CPO’s Summit attendees reported that the reporting and analytics feature of a spend analysis was the most attractive feature in selecting such a solution (Figure 6). `[ps=4]`
- However, fully integrated solutions with robust features are not readily A available and 41% of CPOs are choosing best-of-breed point solutions. `[ps=4]`
- Most organizations have a contract management process defined, but it is often informal and more often unsupported by adequate systems. 75% of CPOs have less than half of their spend going through a formal process (Figure 9). `[ps=4]`
- Our detailed conversations with CPOs indicated an average 3-year contract lifecycle. `[ps=4]`
- He said, “Strategic sourcing running wild will have horrible unintended consequences.” Best in Class organizations strive for consistent singledigit savings rather than intermittent, double-digit improvements. `[ps=4]`
- The majority of CPOs (60%) reported that they are not currently using an On-Demand solution for their procurement and supply management needs (Figure 2). `[ps=1]`
- Attendees of the second annual two-day event represented more than $1.46 trillion in spending power and included supply management luminaries from such leading companies as Purchasing Magazine, IBM, Dell, Sun Microsystems, 3M, MetLife, State Street, BAE Systems, Alcoa, Unicredito and many more. `[ps=0]`
- The question behind the data in Figure 9 is the length and frequency of the core contract process. `[ps=0]`
- Together with the data gathered about contract management process length and frequency, the data in Figure 11 sheds a somewhat positive light on sourcing. `[ps=0]`
- Financial services represented 18% of the sample, followed closely by general manufacturing, which accounted for 15% of respondents and professional services at 13%. `[ps=0]`
- Life sciences represented 8% of the sample. `[ps=0]`
- _**Company size** :_ About 57% of respondents were from large enterprises (annual revenues above US$1 billion); 8% were from midsize enterprises (annual revenues between $50 million and $1 billion); and 36% of respondents were from small businesses (annual revenues of $50 million or less). `[ps=0]`
