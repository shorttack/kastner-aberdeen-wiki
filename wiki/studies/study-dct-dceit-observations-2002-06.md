# Digital Consumer Electronics and Information Technology — Staff Observations

> Internal Aberdeen memo on the Digital Consumer Electronics and Information Technology (DCEIT) sector at a Q2-2002 inflection: Intel's $400M revenue shortfall, aggressive CPU price cuts, and AMD's 22% share gains driving PC OEM repositioning. Documents retail observations from Caroline Kastner's tracking research and catalogs vendor-specific line moves at Gateway and Dell ahead of the 2002 back-to-school season.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- $400M shortfall; Europe new soft spot
- Semiconductor-sector sell-off; semis viewed as leading tech out of two-year recession
- Aggressive desktop and laptop chip price cuts (two weeks prior to memo)
- 300mm wafers at 0.13 micron yield more chips at much lower cost per chip
- 22% of Intel-compatible microprocessor market
- At higher clock speeds (2.566 GHz) P4 outperforms AMD; tech advantage vs AMD price advantage
- Prep for Q3 back-to-school and Q4 Christmas buying seasons
- Aberdeen's retail PC tracking research (Caroline Kastner) documented month-over-month changes
- Faster microprocessors due to Intel price reductions
- Lower HDD costs due to IBM inventory dumping
- As CPU speeds increase, memory and HDD GB offered decreases at same price point
- Upgraded optical drives and better monitors compensate for lower memory
- Offers unchanged in past three weeks; many already rock bottom
- PC sales down as much as 22% this quarter
- $60-$110 free shipping on popular models at HP, Sony, Dell, Gateway, Compaq, eMachines
- Gateway 80GB HDD upgrade early May -> DVD-ROM/CD-RW upgrades or $100 mail-in rebate later
- HP and Compaq formerly emphasized CPUs alone; now pushing PC + monitor + sometimes printer bundles
- 1.3 GHz Celeron -> 1.7 GHz Celeron at same price
- 2.0A GHz -> 2.26 GHz for same cost
- Faster P4; less HDD GB
- Launch 1.7 GHz Celeron -> P4 1.8/2.0 GHz same price
- Price cuts + seasonal setup will move PCs through H2 2002
- P4 mainstreaming confirmed: Pentium 4 with HT became the dominant Intel desktop SKU through 2003-2005, capturing >70% of Intel desktop shipments until Core 2 Duo replaced the NetBurst architecture in July 2006.
