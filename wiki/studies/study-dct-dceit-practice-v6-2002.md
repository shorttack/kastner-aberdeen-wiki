# Digital Consumer Electronic and Information Technology (DCEIT) Practice — v6 Overview Deck

> Sixth-revision Aberdeen DCEIT practice overview deck covering PC-CE convergence, six competitor categories (broadband, CE point-product, game, PC/peripheral, display, software, new entrants), Aberdeen's research approach, fundamental principles governing the convergence, named vendor products (Motorola DCP501/BMC 8000/BMC 9000, Scientific Atlanta Explorer 8000, Pace Micro Home Media Center, Thomson ADSL Gateway, Netopia, 2Wire, Intel reference designs, Broadcom, ESS, Wind River Tornado), and implications for CE companies across channels, supply chain, and customer support. Final v6 iteration of a deck evolved through DCT-Board-Presentation (Jul 2002), DCT-practice-jul-26 (Jul 26 2002), and DCEIT-Practice (Aug-Sep 2002). Craig is Research Director for Semiconductors and DCEIT; Kastner is EVP/CRO overseeing the DCT practice.


_Published 2002, author **Russ Craig, Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Two converging worlds (CE and PC) meet in a broad spectrum of possibilities; six player categories: broadband, point-product CE, game, PC/peripheral, display, software, new entrants; semiconductors as arms merchants
- Target is mass consumer market (not geek early adopters); no singular solution; information interface need not be PC; single system management/content catalog accessible remotely; multiple content/computing centers in home; consumer choice over redundancy; industry standards with acceptable proprietary integration; plug-and-play imperative; upgradeability imperative
- Identify end-user requirements; work with established and emergent vendors; leverage other Aberdeen practices for enabling-tech elements; hypothesize products and approaches; test and refine
- DVD/CD/MP3 player + cable receiver + audio/video receiver/amp + surround sound + digital theater sound
- BMC 8000: network+firewall, PVR, MP3 jukebox. BMC 9000 adds multiple-TV support, dual-tuner PVR, optional HD, optional DVD
- WebStar: Firewall+Router+Networking+802.11b+HPNA 2.0+USB 1.0+Ethernet. Explorer 8000: pause live TV, dual-record, picture-in-picture, PVR, 50 hours storage
- Hard drive; two 802.11b video streams; one wireline video stream; thin-client MPEG-2 decoder at each TV
- 10/100 Ethernet + USB + HomePNA 2.0 + unlimited video channels with surround + VOD + EPG + Internet/email + interactive services
- $50 install + $9.95/mo (cable modem + gateway + firewall + HomePNA for 3 devices); 802.11b option $30 additional + $14.95/mo
- HomePNA $249.95; 802.11b $449.95; DSL fee separate; customer self-install
- DSL 4000 enables short-haul DSL MPEG-1+2 video; IP500 enables MPEG-4 over longer DSL estimated to cover 70% of rural areas
- MSOs are financially constrained: capital equipment (STB) must be very cost-effective; profitability is marginal; strong interest in new fee services and content consumption expansion; tremendous rental consumer access; limited field skills, no centralized support, marginal consumer credibility
- 3-6 month design cycles leveraging feature-rich semiconductor reference designs; Chinese contract manufacturing; low overhead; fluid organization — versus historic CE: 2-year cycles, backward integration, heavy overhead, ponderous structures
- Broad DCEIT industry forces will make share and profitability loss inevitable for incumbents who fail to adapt
- Closed online gaming network; $49/year fee including VOIP headset; gaming fees set by game software companies
- Game console platforms have possible media-server base, high-speed graphics rendering, Internet access, DVD play, large hard drive, saturating teenage market, strong channel presence, razor-and-blades pricing suggesting potential hardware subsidies to improve position
- Success of narrowband interactive gaming suggests pent-up demand for broadband format; key requirement beyond connectivity is display/processor (P-4) in home; EA platform-agnostic and potential key strategic partner
- Increasing yields of large flat-panel displays will make technology economic in consumer products; ability to integrate digital electronics on display substrates will enable display-centric computers and video products; display makers may become end-product manufacturers as value-add grows
- Four-inch color display; 30 GB storage for 100 hours of video; DRM; 3+ hours battery life
- Broadband gateway + DVD playback + PVR + TV reception + MP3 + interactive gaming + content storage + VOD + home networking; Linux/embedded XP/CE OS support
- STB chipset with PVR capability; single-chip ADSL router/bridge with USB and Ethernet
- Integrated software platform and development environment for broadband access gateways: RTOS + embedded web server + Ethernet + USB + xDSL + DOCSIS + voice/multimedia + home networking options (802.11a/b/g, HomePNA, Bluetooth, HomePlug)
- 24-27 GB discs versus 4.7 GB current; likely available 2003 in read/record products
- Potential inexpensive 100 Mbit short-range wireless; may be technology of choice for streaming video
- Open systems/interoperability required for unbundled offerings; box-level products commoditized rapidly; DRM and effort to program media servers with legacy content may create first-mover edge; expensive recurring-fee business models for undifferentiated content will become bundled give-aways
