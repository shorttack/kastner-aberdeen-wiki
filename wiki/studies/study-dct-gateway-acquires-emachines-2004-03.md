# Gateway's Acquisition of eMachines Bodes Well

> Aberdeen analysis of Gateway's $235 million acquisition of privately-held eMachines. Argues the deal is a shrewd distribution-channel play: eMachines's shelf space at Best Buy, Circuit City, and CompUSA lets Gateway deemphasize its costly 170-store chain, combine Gateway's high-end all-in-ones and plasma TVs with eMachines's budget retail strength to out-compete HP, and expand digital consumer electronics distribution.


_Published 2004, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- 235000000
- privately-held
- #2 brand in retail stores in United States
- went private 3 years before 2004 acquisition
- 170 retail showrooms
- results have not justified management's confidence in retail strategy
- ongoing decline in core PC sales has made life difficult
- Dell is 'gorilla' in North American PC marketplace
- stays in PC value segment (low price)
- eMachines success reason HP became price-aggressive last year
- major shelf space at Best Buy, Circuit City, CompUSA
- Gateway will over time deemphasize and likely exit 170-store chain
- Gateway will barge in with second brand next to eMachines over time
- Gateway high-end all-in-one media center + eMachines low-end = full-line HP competitor
- Gateway leader in plasma TVs could gain electronics-altar placement in big-box stores
- eMachines relationship with retailers enables more Gateway DCE sales with less apparent captive-brand conflict
- astute and timely move to expand distribution channel
- eMachines built quiet brand loyalty through big-box electronics and computer stores
- Gateway one of the innovative leaders in delivering digital consumer technology
