# HP's Consumer IT Leadership Needs More Focus On Consumers (Sep 5 2001 draft)

> September 5, 2001 Aberdeen Lab InSight draft on HP's consumer IT execution, based on hands-on experience with HP's C315 digital camera. Earlier version of the 'Caveat Emptor' InSight later published December 5, 2001. Documents the Windows XP upgrade-in-place compatibility failure caused by HP's bundled photo-transfer software interfering with Windows XP native camera support — and the 45-minute support call that cost HP more than the camera's retail price per Aberdeen help-desk research.


_Published 2001, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Lead the market in consumer IT — access PCs, imaging, new consumer electronics
- Simplified, intuitive; quick-start; searchable web; published FAQs
- HP gave analysts C315 at May conference (amateur-photographer context)
- Photographs high quality; transfer straightforward; HP photo web site publishes free
- Difficult to recommend without strong caveats about installation and ease-of-use
- Minor hitch: ACD See missing .dll; installed alternative; off in <30 minutes
- Upgrade-in-place + bundled photo-transfer software = native-XP camera support broken
- HP photo transfer interferes with Windows XP native camera support
- Windows XP sees camera as removable disk; formatting fails with severe error
- Camera ROM upgrade failed because XP formatting had altered camera media
- HP support technician solved immediate problem in ~45 minutes — high marks to tech
- Single call cost HP more than the retail price of the camera
- Do NOT install HP photo transfer software — use native XP support
- Many unhappy HP camera owners will emerge on XP upgrade; new-PC + camera bundles also affected
- Can't afford many customer support phone calls and still make money on mass-market products
- Qualitatively test consumer experience with IT products; evaluate real products as consumers would
- Many sophisticated tech consumers are more afraid of and less patient with IT than suppliers expect
- HP exited the consumer digital camera market in 2008, selling the Photosmart camera line and licensing the HP brand; HP's consumer imaging strategy ultimately centered on printers rather than capture devices as the prediction's direction anticipated.
