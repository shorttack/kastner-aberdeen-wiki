> News analysis of Microsoft's announcement of Windows XP Media Center Edition — a living-room PC variant combining TV tuner, hardware video encoder, and infrared remote, first demoed at CES 2002 under the 'Freestyle' codename. HP, NEC, and Samsung are the launch OEMs; Kastner frames the offering as the strongest PC-in-the-living-room push to date while noting Windows task-scheduling vulnerabilities and competition from SonicBlue and TiVo.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Windows XP Media Center Edition — sold only via selected OEMs `[ps=5]`
- First shown by Bill Gates at CES January 2002 under codename 'Freestyle' `[ps=5]`
- Media Center PCs to reach market in time for Christmas season 2002 `[ps=5]`
- Windows XP Media Center Edition OEM rollout confirmed: HP, Dell, Gateway, Sony, Toshiba all shipped MCE SKUs through 2003-2005; MCE was folded into Vista Home Premium/Ultimate (2007) and Windows 7 (2009) as mainstream features. Last standalone MCE was 2005 Edition; 'Windows Media Center' app removed from Windows 10 (2015) as streaming displaced DVR-centric use case. `[ps=5]`
- Few megabits-per-second compression enables hundreds of hours of TV storage `[ps=4]`
- If Media Center fails to match SonicBlue/TiVo reliability, PC banished from living room for years `[ps=4]`
- May be adequate if users avoid aftermarket apps and peripherals `[ps=3]`
- Windows vulnerable to task-scheduling conflicts — critical interrupts unserviced for seconds `[ps=2]`
- Infrared remote, TV tuner, hardware video encoder `[ps=0]`
- Terrestrial broadcast, cable, or satellite TV — or combinations `[ps=0]`
- UI concepts adapted from UltimateTV — simplified desktop, list of TV/music/photos `[ps=0]`
- HP, NEC, Samsung `[ps=0]`
- Not first PC-in-living-room attempt, but 'strongest push to date' `[ps=0]`
- Filed bankruptcy March 2003 `[ps=0]`
