# PC Replacements: Lawyers, Auditors, and Common Sense Rules

> Aberdeen InSight arguing that the roughly 50 million Windows 98/NT 4 corporate PCs still in service by mid-2003 should be replaced. Windows NT 4.x end-of-support (June 30 2003) and Windows 98/SE (January 16 2004) expose enterprises to unpatched security risk, auditor scrutiny, and potential negligence claims. Mid-2003 is framed as an auspicious PC replacement window anchored by Windows XP Pro SP1, Server 2003, Office 2003, Intel Springdale, P4 Hyper-Threading, and Centrino notebooks.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Windows XP Pro Service Pack 1 is out in the field and working well; no stability problems; rated as great client OS on modern desktops and laptops `[ps=4]`
- Intel Pentium 4 with Hyper-Threading now mainstream; better at multi-tasking and thread-aware applications; Springdale chipset inaugurates 18-month platform/image stability period with 800 MHz FSB, better graphics, more efficient Ethernet `[ps=4]`
- About one-third of corporate PC purchases in 2003 will be notebooks; Intel Centrino wireless laptops feature long battery life and work-wireless productivity `[ps=4]`
- CFOs/CEOs resist PC replacement because old machines still run; but security risk from unpatched OS (Win 98/NT), auditor scrutiny, and potential negligence claims create fiduciary case for replacement `[ps=4]`
- Intel Springdale chipset inaugurates 18-month period of platform and software image stability (800 MHz FSB); makes mid-2003 an auspicious time to begin a corporate PC replacement cycle `[ps=4]`
- More than 50 million aging PCs (pre-2000, Windows 98 and NT era) sitting on corporate desks worldwide as of mid-2003 `[ps=3]`
- W95.Tenrobot virus (discovered April 3 2003) only infects Windows 95/98/Me systems; gives hacker remote access via IRC; demonstrates old software remains attractive to attackers; Win 98 and NT have no hack-detection facilities `[ps=3]`
- Aberdeen Group itself replaced its old PC ecosystem in 2002-2003 despite down economy; concluded the risk to knowledge-worker productivity of not doing so outweighed the cost `[ps=3]`
- Windows NT 4.xx enters non-support stage June 30 2003; Windows 98/SE on January 16 2004; no more security hot fixes or service packs after these dates `[ps=2]`
- Aberdeen believes Big Four auditors will scrutinize clients running mission-critical processes on unsupported Windows 98/NT PCs, similar to Year 2000 scrutiny `[ps=2]`
- Business PC prices on Aberdeen reference desktop (includes Windows XP Pro and Office XP Pro licenses) run $1,250-$1,400 from top suppliers in April 2003; equates to roughly $2/day over 3-4 years in capital asset costs `[ps=0]`
