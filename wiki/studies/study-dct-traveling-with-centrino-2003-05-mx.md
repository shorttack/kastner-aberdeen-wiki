# Traveling With Centrino

> Hands-on review after two months of travel with a Gateway 450 Intel Centrino notebook. Covers the Pentium M + chipset + WiFi Centrino platform architecture, 15in 1400x1050 screen trade-offs, 6.2lb weight versus preferred thin-and-light 4lb class, 1.5 GHz performance versus Pentium III/P4 desktops, battery life including DVD playback plus full business day, WiFi range including a Cometa McDonald's hotspot picked up from a 15th-floor hotel room, and corporate 3-per-7-desktops adoption forecast.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Centrino is Intel's trademark for a notebook architecture consisting of Pentium M processor + chipset + wireless radio; designed to maximize performance per watt `[ps=4]`
- Gateway 450 Centrino 1.5 GHz is subjectively as fast as a 2 GHz Pentium 4 desktop for notebook workloads; fast bootups and crisp response; processor power unlikely to be a helpdesk complaint `[ps=4]`
- Wireless infrastructure springing up rapidly around the country especially in hotels and meeting places; 802.11b at 11 Mbps current standard with 802.11a expected that summer and 802.11g the following year `[ps=4]`
- Corporate PC planners set to buy three notebooks for every seven desktops (30% notebook share) in the U.S. in 2003 `[ps=4]`
- Kastner expects new Centrino model introductions in next few weeks covering thin-and-light, tablet, and executive-performance form factors; Centrino technology covers all notebook form factors `[ps=4]`
- Based on Gateway 450 experience, Intel Centrino platform is an excellent base for a long-term wireless notebook program; with Intel's stable software image commitment, Centrino notebooks will deliver better ROI at lower TCO than replaced models `[ps=4]`
- At 6.2 lbs the Gateway 450 is heavier than Kastner's preferred 4lb thin-and-light class but acceptable for corridor cruisers; true road warriors should wait for thin-and-light Centrino models; desktop-replacement form factor viable for many use cases `[ps=4]`
- Intel's Centrino launch backed by $300 million advertising campaign touting the unwired experience as of May 2003 `[ps=0]`
- Gateway 450 Centrino notebook: Pentium M 1.5 GHz, 512MB DDR, 15in LCD 1400x1050, ATI Mobility graphics, standard 14.8V 4200mAh battery, 6.2 lbs, multibay `[ps=0]`
- Gateway 450 standard battery plays a full DVD movie plus one hour of email pruning; with optional multibay battery, Kastner routinely ends business day with over 40% capacity remaining; never below 40% in normal day `[ps=0]`
- Centrino 802.11b WiFi range superior to older PC cards and Compaq TC 1000 built-in; logged into Cometa hotspot in a McDonald's from a 15th-floor hotel room across the street, approximately a football field distance `[ps=0]`
