# Traveling With Centrino

> Hands-on review after two months of travel with a Gateway 450 Intel Centrino notebook. Covers the Pentium M + chipset + WiFi Centrino platform architecture, 15in 1400x1050 screen trade-offs, 6.2lb weight versus preferred thin-and-light 4lb class, 1.5 GHz performance versus Pentium III/P4 desktops, battery life including DVD playback plus work day, WiFi range improvements including a Cometa McDonald's hotspot picked up from a 15th-floor hotel room across the street, and corporate 3-notebooks-per-7-desktops adoption forecast.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- $300 million Centrino launch campaign
- Pentium M + chipset + wireless radio
- 11 Mbps 802.11b today, 802.11a summer 2003, 802.11g next year
- 6.2
- 1400 x 1050 pixels, 15in LCD
- 512MB DDR, 1.5 GHz Pentium M
- 14.8V 4200 mAh standard + optional multibay battery
- 4 pounds preferred; 'confirmed thin-and-light guy'
- desktop users moving to notebook feel 2 GHz P4 equivalent
- standard battery plays DVD movie plus 1hr+ email
- typical day ends with 40%+ battery remaining
- logged into Cometa hotspot in McDonald's from 15th-floor hotel room across street ~football field away
- prefer distance at 1 Mbps over 11 Mbps at 30 feet
- 3 notebooks per 7 desktops in US corporate 2003
- Centrino becoming mainstream for business laptops
- thin-and-light, tablet, executive-performance Centrino models coming
- Centrino notebooks deliver better ROI at lower TCO than replaced models
- three coast-to-coast trips during the review period
- wireless infrastructure springing up rapidly in hotels and meeting places
