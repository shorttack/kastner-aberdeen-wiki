> Presentation slides from Aberdeen Group's November 1, 2002 DCT webinar. Three deep-dive sections: Mobile Convergence Devices (handset economics, network operator gatekeepers, 3G timing), Media Servers (desired functionality, emerging product classes from HP Media Center/Intel Media PC/Sony Cocoon/STB accessories, Media Center requirements), and Home Networking (eleven contending technologies, longer-term architecture with consumer-configurable gateway/firewall/VoIP/DRM, 5 GHz spectrum of choice). ## Top observations

- TV video recorder and playback; music library (rip/burn, drive analog+digital players); photo and home-video library/authoring; import legacy content (videos/CDs/DVDs); commercial content rental/purchase; ability to access and play content anywhere in/around home and take with you `[ps=5]`
- Emerging architecture requires: consumer UI for configuration and security management; gateway/firewall/router/remote management; cordless phones/VoIP; multiple simultaneous channels of music, data, streaming video; digital transmission rights protection `[ps=5]`
- 'Digital Consumer Technology: The Revolution Has Started' — Nov 1 2002 webinar positioning DCT as a convergence opportunity with three concrete deep-dive areas `[ps=4]`
- Hot: Picture Messaging (MMS), flat-rate data plans. Not: non-wireless organizers, per-MB billing `[ps=4]`
- Key architectural trade-off: user selects episode → viewing happens on PC, or via network streaming to TV via home network — media server streams program to TV via network is the mass-market answer `[ps=4]`
- 5 GHz will be the spectrum of choice, BUT: QoS issues in 802.11a for streaming video; need better distances via second+third-generation silicon; need minimized transmit power at required link bandwidth to reduce interference; need automatic identification and minimization of interference sources `[ps=4]`
- Webinar correctly predicted PC makers entering handsets, 3G 3-year horizon, consolidation of North American carriers, streaming-to-TV architecture over disk-per-device PVR, 5 GHz Wi-Fi dominance, and mass-market media servers — high prescience consistent with Aberdeen's DCT practice foundational deliverables `[ps=4]`
- Reference design targeting OEM media PCs `[ps=3]`
- PVRs are prototypical but insufficient as media servers; mass-market assumption minimizes component duplication (e.g., disk drives in TiVo) `[ps=3]`
- Eleven contending standards: Bluetooth, 802.11b, 802.11a, 802.11g, Ultra Wide Band, Home PNA, HomePlug, Home RF, Ethernet, HDMI, X10 `[ps=3]`
- Mobile devices are highly personal items; there is no ideal convergence device — rejecting single-device convergence model `[ps=2]`
- Purpose-built media server from HP `[ps=2]`
- True 3G networks are still 3+ years from becoming a reality `[ps=1]`
- Custom consulting + retainer package including: presentation of summary trends and strategy review, continuous information bulletins/InSights, quarterly update via webcast, add-on competitive analysis, bundled consulting days, analyst access `[ps=0]`
- Integrated viewpoint (silicon to out-of-box experience); strategic and business-process perspective; deep technology understanding married to pragmatic consumer buying/usability understanding; DCT research and consultative analysis `[ps=0]`
- Peter Kastner, (617) 854-5221, kastner@aberdeen.com; download URL www.aberdeen.com/ab_company/researchareas/dctwebinar.htm `[ps=0]`
- Voice (toll/VoIP), data, content (MPEG-3 audio, MPEG-2/4 video, HDTV, HDCP, DTCP), gaming — mapped against 802.11a with DTCP, Home RF + 802.11a, HomePNA, HomePlug, Philips 1394-over-wireless, Proprietary, Bluetooth, 802.11a/b, Ethernet, HDMI, UWB `[ps=-1]`
- Broadband companies, point-product CE, game, PC/peripheral, display, software + new entrants + semiconductor IP/device arms merchants — identical to DCEIT v6 framework
- Building handsets is no longer black magic; PC makers increasingly competing with established phone makers; rapid innovation shortening product life cycle; shrinking margins require contract manufacturers
- Flat-rate data plans are here; focus on MMS and gaming applications; consolidation in North America imminent
- Network-connected PVR-style media server
- Many usage models: general-purpose PC + media; media-centric PC; closet rack-mount; next to TV/stereo; combinations — placement drives network topology
- Fair-use DRM; consumer user interface and applications; large mass store; CD/DVD red+blue read/write drive; encoding for audio/video legacy content; search engine for indexing and retrieval; multiple interlaced output streams; QoS management; wireless connectivity
- Multiple computing devices; LAN and WAN access; security, data management, maintenance, virus protection, firewall, caching/proxy server — all requirements of any business — with no professional IT department
