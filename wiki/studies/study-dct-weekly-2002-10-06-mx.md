# PC Deals Weekly Analysis: October 6, 2002

> Week of October 6 2002 PC Deals analysis. Major retail channel rotation after back-to-school season ends. eMachines, HP, and Sony deliver new models incorporating Intel August price cuts (nine weeks late). P3-based Celerons to 1.4 GHz now clearance; P4-based Celerons 1.8-2.0 GHz at low end. More Athlon XP options. HP finally has P4 above 2.2 GHz at retail; Compaq Presario brand appearing to exit consumer channel. Microsoft enters home networking hardware. Per-price-band winners $550-$1550+.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Major retail channel rotation week of October 6 2002 after back-to-school season; eMachines, HP, Sony deliver new models with Intel August price cuts — nine weeks after announcement
- Most machines above $550 include DVD-ROM and CD-RW; DVD burners still $300+ add-on not expected to come down until 2003
- Pentium III-based Celerons up to 1.4 GHz are now clearance items; low end now populated by Pentium 4-based Celerons at 1.8-2.0 GHz
- More Athlon XP configurations available; however Athlon XP 2400+ or better not reaching top-tier retail systems; AMD 2800+ recently announced but not expected in covered price points until 2003
- HP finally has P4 above 2.2 GHz at retail; Compaq Presario brand appears to be exiting consumer channel — all new HP retail machines bear HP corporate name, not Pavilion
- Sony's new lineup (PCV-RZxxx) priced $1500-$2000 online; well above competition; competition has caught up or surpassed Sony on media features; PCV-RXxxx models obsolescent
- Gateway seldom wins on value-price methodology; Kastner observes Gateway moving toward high-end niche with custom-loaded machines; 500X/700X come with LCD monitors as differentiator
- Dell 4500: category killer $900-$1600, infinitely configurable; top config P4 2.8 GHz + 512MB DDR just under $1400; Dell 8200 RDRAM equivalent costs $1669 — $270 RDRAM premium described as pure profit for Dell
- Microsoft enters home networking hardware market selling 802.11b WiFi access points and adapters; addresses poor consumer usability of competing reference designs; Windows XP WiFi support described as inadequate; Microsoft products have consumer-focused installation wizards
- eMachines wins $550 with new T1840; wins $750 with T2085 (Athlon 2000+ with DVD-RW+DVD); wins $900 with T2200 (Athlon XP 2200+, flat screen) at Circuit City — eMachines takes three lowest price categories
- HP 523n wins $1000 at BestBuy with PC+monitor+printer bundle at $950; HP 753n wins $1200 at BestBuy with printer
- Dell wins $1100 (P4 2.4 GHz + 512MB DDR + 19in monitor + 80GB); wins $1350 (P4 2.66 GHz at Dell Online); wins under $1400 (P4 2.8 GHz); wins $1550 and over-$1550 categories
- Alienware $1800 machine included as AMD high-end price-to-value indicator; AMD 2400+ or better not in top-tier mass-market retail systems
