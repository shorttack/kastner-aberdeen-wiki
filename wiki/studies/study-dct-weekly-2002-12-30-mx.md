# PC Deals Weekly Update: December 30, 2002

> Holiday 2002 PC sales yield lowest rate of growth in decades. Post-Christmas price cuts did not materialize as expected. HP 573n price jumped from $900 at Staples last week to $1,418 at HP Online. PCs are not flying off the shelves; retailers resist price decreases. Dell, eMachines, and Compaq are the best deals of the week. Gateway prices stagnant but impressive flat-screen monitor pricing.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Holiday 2002 PC sales yielded the lowest rate of growth in decades; PC Deals is not surprised given disappointing holiday deals especially from Dell
- Hypothesis that immediate post-Christmas flyers would reveal price cuts did not pan out; small electronics (DVD players) continue to sell well but PCs are not flying off shelves; retailers resist further price decreases
- P4 2.8 GHz machines taking noticeably longer to fall in price than 2.53 GHz did when that chip was second-best; P4 2.8 GHz is no longer the fastest Pentium 4
- HP 573n (Athlon 2600+, 512 MB): sold at Staples last week for $900; this week costs $1,418 at HP Online — the largest price hike recorded in PC Deals history
- Dell: $100 off all Dimension 8250 and 4550 models; CD-RW and hard drive upgrades on all models; Dimension 2350 has $30 price increase
- Gateway prices stagnant for past few weeks but Kastner continues to be impressed with Gateway's pricing on flat-screen monitors
- Circuit City Online features customer reviews on all products; Kastner recommends checking reviews at Circuit City Online, CNet, or Dealtime.com for unbiased critiques before purchase
- BestBuy is the last online retailer to offer free shipping on all items; Circuit City and others offer in-store pickup (negates shipping); most other sites charge shipping; BestBuy free shipping saves at least $20 on ground
- BestBuy's eMachines T2085 (end-of-season stock) and Circuit City's Compaq 6350US are great post-Christmas deals that retailers are trying to move as new models arrive
- Consumer hesitance to buy expensive items or replace 2-year-old machines will lead to a disappointing quarter for many manufacturers and retailers
