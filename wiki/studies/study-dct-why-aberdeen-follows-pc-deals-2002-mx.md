# Why Aberdeen Is Following Consumer PC Deals — DCT Practice Methodology

> Foundational methodology paper for Aberdeen's Digital Consumer Technology practice. Defines six tracked brands (Compaq, Dell, eMachines, Gateway, HP, Sony), an 8-factor price-value hierarchy (CPU speed/cache, memory, modem/NIC, HDD, monitor, OS, printer, software), and notes rebate complexity observations after 90 days of tracking. Explicitly excludes Apple (no head-to-head competition). Raises HP dual-brand strategy as open research question post-Compaq merger.


_Published 2002, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **dct**._


## Top observations

- Methodology factor 1: CPU speed and cache size; faster chip with greater cache wins within architecture; Pentium 4 at 1.8 GHz beats Celeron at 1.8 GHz due to greater cache `[ps=4]`
- Methodology factor 3: integrated 56K modem and 10/100 Ethernet should be standard; no need to use expansion slots for these functions `[ps=4]`
- Methodology factor 6: Windows XP Home adequate for most home users; XP Pro more valuable for power users, home office, and small business `[ps=4]`
- Methodology factor 7: creditable color printers at $50 and up; often bundled with PC and monitor to maximize margin while creating ink/paper customer; no one should buy a PC just for the printer `[ps=4]`
- Methodology factor 8: software bundle; CD-burner enabling software vs. productivity software like Microsoft Office; latter helps PC function as office PC `[ps=4]`
- Online stores adjust prices via changing rebates several times per week; retailers use advertising-driven weekly cycles; observed after 90 days of market tracking starting April 2002 `[ps=4]`
- Core research questions: which brand offers most PC value at a given price point; velocity of price/value changes; how quickly does new technology reach consumers; how promotions and rebates stimulate demand; end-game for obsolescent merchandise; HP dual-brand strategy post-Compaq merger `[ps=3]`
- Methodology factor 2: memory size and speed; 128MB significantly slower than 256MB; multimedia users want 512MB; DDR faster than SDRAM; RDRAM best for high-end Pentium 4s `[ps=3]`
- Methodology factor 4: hard drive: 7200 RPM faster than 5400 RPM and preferred; 40GB entry-level adequate; 80GB satisfies most; multimedia archivists may want more `[ps=3]`
- Methodology factor 5: 17" monitors under $100 in bundles; 19" upgrade provides 40% more pixel real estate; 19" flat-panel monitor is to die for; retail bundle deals often include monitor and printer `[ps=3]`
- Rebates complex; net price/value not easy to ascertain; multiple rebate coupons (six on the low side for store purchase); complicated fulfillment; rebate check from April 15 purchase still pending at time of writing `[ps=3]`
- Apple Computer not tracked because it has no head-to-head competition in the consumer market at the time; acknowledged as a major player `[ps=3]`
- Aberdeen DCT practice tracking six major US consumer brands: Compaq, Dell, eMachines, Gateway, HP, and Sony at manufacturer online stores and major retail channel partners; Apple explicitly excluded `[ps=2]`
- Open research question: how will HP's dual-brand strategy (HP and Compaq) play out in the consumer market after May 2002 merger `[ps=0]`
