# Dell Asset Recovery Services: Remove, Recycle, or Resell — An Executive White Paper

> Aberdeen executive white paper (underwritten by Dell) examining the business, data-security, and environmental case for proactive IT-asset disposition. Models total reclamation cost at $115-$387 per device and evaluates Dell's Asset Recovery Services ($49-$69/system) as a compelling alternative. Recommends treating disposal as an up-front TCO element rather than a sunk cost.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- Annually removing 3,000 PCs saves $1M/year in unnecessary storage and disposal costs.
- 6% to 10% per month
- cost/value, data security, environmental concerns
- $2.3M awarded to three women whose confidential mental-health records were not kept private (HIPAA Advisory Feb 2003).
- Used $159 PC held 2,000+ patient records incl. SSNs and medication lists (NY Times).
- Of 129 functional drives purchased from resellers/eBay, only 12% were properly sanitized; rest yielded 675 Word docs, 566 PPTs, 5,000 credit-card numbers, 9,500 emails.
- BSA (Sept 2003): improperly discarded software can expose both original owner and recipient to legal action with penalties in the thousands.
- 315 million old PCs ready to be junked by 2004 in the US.
- ~1 billion pounds of lead plus mercury, cadmium, PVC.
- 1999: 34,000 tons e-waste in Canadian landfills; 2005 projected: 67,000 tons.
- Only 11% of the 315M PCs rendered obsolete by 2004 will be recycled in the US.
- 70% of heavy metals in US garbage dumps come from electronics.
- Aberdeen expects legally acceptable level of e-waste tolerance will continue to tighten; companies may be held retroactively accountable.
- E-waste regulation tightened significantly around 2002-2003. The EU WEEE Directive (2002/96/EC) was issued in 2003 and required implementation by August 2004-2005. California enacted the Electronic Waste Recycling Act of 2003 (SB 20). This represented a major tightening of e-waste regulation, validating Aberdeen's prediction.
- 77% of IT executives purchase secondary-market equipment; 46% plan to increase spending in that area by at least 15%.
- Right time to dispose of a PC is ~36 months rather than 48 months.
- PC disposal/replacement window did not shorten dramatically to less than 3 years for corporate desktops by 2003. Enterprise PC refresh cycles remained at approximately 3-5 years through the 2000s. The prediction of shorter replacement windows was not broadly validated; corporate lifecycles stabilized around 4 years.
- $50 low to $126 average per disk cleansed (ex-large-scale-automated operations).
- $25 to $50 per device for de-manufacture and component resale.
- $12 low to $132 average per unit.
- $25 to $67 per device.
- $3 to $12 per device.
- $115 to $387 per device (sum of all components).
- $59 per system unit.
- $69 per system unit.
