# The Dollars and Sense of the New Electronic Commerce: Business-to-Business Electronic Commerce in Transition

> This 115-page Aberdeen Group report by analyst David Alschuler examines the transition in B2B electronic commerce from traditional EDI/EFT systems to internet-based commerce platforms. The study profiles 17 supplier organizations within an analytical framework of EC capabilities, covering the full technology spectrum from EDI VANs through emerging internet commerce platforms, and addresses the strategic implications for corporate management responsible for e-commerce, supply chain, and technology planning. It introduced the concept of Infinite Resource Planning as the promise of the new EC.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group (David Alschuler)**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Electronic Commerce is a market in transition with new organizations breaking new ground
- Traditional EDI suppliers have launched significant new initiatives reflecting different technical and business models
- It is a period of change — of opportunity and risk closely entwined
- 4 months of interviews with major EC suppliers and end users conducted starting March 1998
- 17 supplier organizations profiled
- Business process functionality as primary EC capability dimension
- Key technical standards as EC evaluation dimension
- Public/private key encryption as internet commerce security foundation
- Worldwide EDI VAN market (1996) quantified
- Worldwide EDI software and service revenues market growth estimates presented
- Hub-and-spoke EDI network described from both buyer and supplier perspectives
- Trading partner full systems integration as advanced EC capability
- Internet-based EC will become the dominant B2B commerce channel displacing traditional EDI
- Commerce One identified as new internet EC marketplace entrant with high growth potential
- Ariba identified as leading e-procurement platform with strong market position
- Sterling Commerce positioned as market leader in EDI VAN services
- Harbinger positioned as acquisitive EDI VAN transitioning to internet EC
- Premenos acquired by Harbinger for $230M in December 1997
- Commerce One filed for bankruptcy October 6 2004; patents auctioned for $15.5M
- Ariba acquired by SAP AG for $4.3 billion in 2012 as cloud procurement leader
- Internet-based B2B commerce (XML/AS2/web services) became dominant; EDI persists only in legacy healthcare/retail
- GEIS rebranded as GXS in 2000; sold by GE to Francisco Partners 2002; eventually acquired by OpenText 2014
- Sterling Commerce sold to IBM in 2010 for $1.4B; now IBM Sterling B2B Integrator
- Most ERP suppliers have launched significant EC initiatives reflecting different technical and business models
