# Data Knowledge: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary for the Data Knowledge and Complex Decision Support practice, covering data warehousing, data marts, OLAP, query/reporting/analysis tools, data mining, and Customer Relationship Management (CRM). The report frames these technologies as components of an enterprise Data Knowledge strategy and assesses their integration with CIS, ERP, and Electronic Commerce applications. Thirteen primary vendors receive detailed abstracts including NCR/Teradata, IBM, Oracle, Cognos, Business Objects, SAS, Arbor/Essbase, and MicroStrategy, plus a second tier of over a dozen additional suppliers.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- Implementing data mining is approximately 75% data preparation `[ps=5]`
- Most Data Knowledge tools analyze summary data in offline/batch fashion; real-time decision support slowly emerging `[ps=5]`
- Data warehouses can take well over a year to implement `[ps=4]`
- Microsoft Plato acquisition from Panorama will increase consolidation trend; many suppliers embracing Plato and planning partnerships despite uncertainty about what Plato actually is `[ps=4]`
- SQL Server 7.0 OLAP Services (1999) did indeed catalyze market consolidation; several pure-play OLAP vendors declined or were acquired `[ps=4]`
- Start-ups without true differentiation will fall on road to IPO or be acquired by predominant suppliers; consolidation becoming more common `[ps=4]`
- Arbor Software and Hyperion Software announced intentions to merge into Hyperion Solutions; targeting leadership in custom on-demand and packaged analytic applications `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen believes more integrated CIS-DSS solutions will be introduced in next 12-24 months `[ps=4]`
- Sophisticated Global 1000 organizations have spent past few years building Data Knowledge infrastructure and now looking for applications that leverage it `[ps=4]`
- IBM holds top-ranking position in Data Knowledge marketplace based on combined installed base in hardware software and services; DecisionEdge CRM for finance and insurance `[ps=3]`
- Approximately 500 installed sites; selected as tool of choice among information providers; DSS Broadcaster narrows information to web/email/pager subscribers `[ps=3]`
- Integrating ERP and Data Knowledge is at a relatively primitive stage in 1998; suppliers cannot view ERP as just another data source `[ps=3]`
- Executives view quick-install data marts (weeks) more favorably than data warehouses or slow 3-6 month data marts `[ps=3]`
- Big-ticket CRM systems and data warehouses affecting entire organization increasingly purchased by Chief Financial Officer `[ps=2]`
- $5.2 billion (includes hardware software and systems integration consulting) `[ps=0]`
- $20.6 billion projected `[ps=0]`
- $1 million to $1.5 million typical cost `[ps=0]`
- $50000 to $400000 depending on type `[ps=0]`
- Approximately $50000 for web server version; $1500 per component for full client and administration `[ps=0]`
- $250000 to $400000 for high-end scalable systems `[ps=0]`
- $200000 to $2.5 million depending on size and existing infrastructure `[ps=0]`
- Between two weeks and six months depending on type and application `[ps=0]`
- Cognos has installed more than 700000 seats of Impromptu and PowerPlay; tight resource-sharing linkage between client-server and web versions `[ps=0]`
- From current $1 billion to approximately $2.5 billion in 1999
- Approximately $40000 per server
