# e-Learning in the Enterprise

> This study analyzes e-Learning in the Enterprise using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 1999, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **benchmark**._


## Top observations

- In 1996, about 90% of the enterprise training opportunities used the traditional classroom method. `[ps=5]`
- Already more than 70% of corporations offer their employees computer- or Web-based training options. `[ps=4]`
- And 60% of institutions of higher education now offer online learning courses. `[ps=4]`
- The total costs of training include the up-front costs of developing the training and the ongoing costs of delivering the training, as well as travel-related expenses — with travel costs consuming up to 40% of the average corporate `[ps=3]`
- The worldwide market for e-Learning software, services, and content has moved from near zero in 1996 to the point where it should be worth well over $10 billion by 2002. e-Learning’s share will have about one-third of the training market by 2002, derived from the market share of TBT. `[ps=3]`
- In 1998, the traditional classroom method decreased to about 70% of training situations, whereas TBT provided about 25% of the other training opportunities. `[ps=2]`
