# Electronic Commerce Professional Services: 1998 Practice Summary

> A 1998 practice summary analyzing the market for professional services supporting electronic commerce deployment. The report defines four categories of EC professional services providers (OEMs, SSPs, IPSPs, EDI/VAN integrators), identifies key implementation challenges (skills shortages, integration complexity, standards immaturity), and profiles 11 major service providers including IBM, HP, Digital, Microsoft, Oracle, SAP, Cambridge Technology Partners, EDS, GEIS, Sterling Commerce, and Harbinger. Key Aberdeen finding: enterprises are turning to outside service providers because internal IS resources lack EC skills.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- $2.6 billion in 1996 to $220+ billion by 2001
- $8 billion in 1997 to $327 billion in 2002
- Less than 15% of users within an organization
- PC home penetration still less than 50%
- IS decision-makers receive hundreds of calls yearly from professional services providers; it is a buyers market
- $2000 to $5000 per day per consultant for strategy and process reengineering; $750 to $3000 per day for implementation and support
- One to two years away from one-stop shop EC implementation; today individual suppliers provide 80-90% of required functionality
- Product immaturity; inexperience at professional services level; lack of standards; legacy integration issues; fear of disclosure; cost barriers
- E-commerce systems should be considered to last at best only a few years; highly dynamic entity
- Enterprises with EDI origin generally quicker to understand internet EC due to legacy/systems integration experience
- Infrastructure skills foremost; then architectural design; then installation; then integration/customization; then EDI/security/benchmarking
- He who lingers is lost; companies hesitating to adopt full-blown EC strategy may find themselves trailing competitors
- Internet threatens to outflank highly profitable VANs; EDI/VAN integrators must embrace internet to survive
- ERP providers have the best way in to the enterprise for EC services
- 120000 professionals in Global Services; 10000 dedicated to E-business
- $6.2 billion
- $5.4 billion; 40% of company revenue
- 10000+ consultants in 55 countries; $5.6B in sales
- Running in 7000+ companies worldwide including Microsoft IBM and General Motors
- Joint venture with Intel (Pandesic LLC) announced August 1997 to bring SAP applications to mid-range and small enterprises
- 70% of new business internet-related in 1997; up from 50% in 1996
- E-commerce only 2% of revenues in 1997
- 2000 service professionals dedicated to E-commerce; only IBM claims more
- Number two EDI/VAN integrator; most progressive in moving to Internet/EDI; second in size only to GEIS
- Fewer than 1000 employees; baby among big three EDI/VAN integrators; lead in PC-based EDI
