# Why Aberdeen Group is Focusing on Enterprise IT Integration

> Dated/published version of the Aberdeen Enterprise IT Integration practice launch perspective. Kastner identifies six megatrends driving IT integration opportunity: (1) Programmer productivity stuck at 1970s levels — legacy spaghetti code; SOA/composite applications/BPM as solutions; best-in-class frees 15% of IT budget with lower maintenance costs. (2) SOA and Web Services will constitute majority of development by ~2010. (3) Deconstruction of processing at core and edge — datacenter as JBOR; SANs; blade servers; virtualization; grid computing. (4) Mobility is the norm — technically mature in 2006 but enterprises have tepid reactive strategy. (5) Real-Time Business Intelligence (RTBI) — gradual replumbing creates new opportunities; EII as augmentation to data warehouses. (6) Knowledge Worker productivity — collaboration, messaging, smart phones, laptops, enterprise portals, CRM/ERP improvements. Bottom line: act tactically but build strategically; focus on IT investment synergy. February 23, 2006 dateline confirms this as the formal published version of the perspective.


_Published 2006, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- Will constitute majority of development by turn of decade (~2010)
- ~10 years for significant dividends in lower IT lifecycle costs and faster more flexible application changes
- Best-in-class IT organizations free up 15% of IT budget with lower software maintenance costs vs average
- Microsoft discarded tens of millions of lines of code in 2004 to institute programming process change enabling lower lifecycle cost
- Programmer productivity stuck at 1970s level at many shops — legacy application spaghetti-code soaks up enormous talent for trivial gains
- Aberdeen's first framework in 1988 — spoke-node-ring (SNR) — foreshadowed client-server and LAN/WAN revolution
- The edge is gaining lots of intelligence — exemplified by growth in retail store and shop floor computing power
- Sun and other server suppliers at risk from Cisco angling to provide intelligent network service networks
- Technologies contributing to KW productivity: collaboration, messaging, smart phones, laptops, podcasting, conferencing, enterprise portals, project management and compliance, ERP, SFA, CRM
- Best-in-class organizations show positive ROI for laptops merely from a few minutes of email time gained while waiting for meetings to start
- Mostly large companies with RTBI experience consider business value generation so critical that the 'how' becomes a company secret — a competitive weapon
- EII is technology that promises to augment traditional data warehouse technology; data warehouses look at older information not where business is or will be
- Leading companies will translate lessons from scientific massively parallel computing into a grid for commercial computing; linked to real-time operational analysis and decision-making
- Mobility technology technically mature enough in 2006 but many enterprises have only tepid reactive strategy for generating business value
- IT investments must be synergistic — over time worth more than sum of individual projects; integration of IT with business processes is area ripe for improving efficiency
- Chip-maker focus on instructions-per-watt will allow greater density; better cooling technology lowering HVAC demands
- Operational tools to remotely monitor, virtually reconfigure, and deploy servers are needed to keep labor costs under control
- Enterprise WAN and related managed services may be an outsourcing candidate
