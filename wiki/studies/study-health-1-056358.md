# Healthcare: 1998 Industry Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 survey of the Healthcare Information Systems (HIS) market, covering integrated HIS vendors, independent software vendors, professional services firms, and systems suppliers. The report maps the three application tiers—business, clinical, and management information—and assesses the impact of managed care consolidation, client-server adoption, Internet/intranet deployment, and Year 2000 compliance on healthcare IT buyers. Brief vendor profiles are provided for fifteen named companies.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- Mandate to reduce costs while delivering quality care; managed care consolidation accelerating competition
- Advances in client-server technology allowing cost-effective HIS implementation; merging with legacy data access
- Majority of HIS are custom-developed; trend shifting toward packaged applications tailored to provider needs
- Typical healthcare provider IT budget ~4% of annual operating budget; majority of IT departments projecting staffing increases
- Most EMR solutions operate on departmental level vs. enterprise-wide; some enterprise-wide versions emerging; 'who owns the data' unresolved
- Aberdeen estimates over 70% of all physicians use the Internet for research
- Within 12-18 months, forward-looking health plans will provide online functionality for claims processing and payment
- Single electronic patient record shared across the enterprise will someday emerge as an operational and technical reality
- Significant progress made by suppliers with discipline-specific continuous speech dictation; IBM MedSpeak for Radiology cited
- Speech recognition technologies will soon become more commonplace in healthcare
- Advances in wireless technologies will become increasingly critical communication medium across the industry
- Like the industry it serves, HIS supplier groups are consolidating; start-up vendors using new technologies are emerging
- Proven leader in HIS; $245M revenue; strong growing services organization; identified integration as significant revenue opportunity
- 5500+ employees; 1997 revenues >$1.2B; comprehensive suite for hospitals, physician offices, home health, managed care; partnerships and acquisitions strategy
- Acquired Phamis July 1997; combined revenues >$251M; 1400 customers; historically strong in ambulatory care; merging inpatient/outpatient strategy
- 1200+ customers; 1997 software license revenues $193M; founded 1969; transitioned from proprietary minicomputer/mainframe to distributed environments
- 3000+ customers worldwide; 1997 revenues >$779M; 25+ year presence; Physician Enterprise Manager released Feb 1998; equity stake in Visteon
- Important HIS player; NT Server powerful and rapidly growing; Microsoft healthcare technology partner ecosystem expected to multiply
- 1997 revenues $25M (22% YoY growth); strong clinical data extraction; Oacis Passport web-based CIS launched early 1997; challenge: professional services partnerships
- Healthcare providers historically lagged other industries in IT adoption; conservative buying patterns; now accelerating investment
- Data warehouses provide foundation for decision support by integrating legacy and client-server data; typically terabyte-scale on high-end Unix
- Security and privacy are major concerns in healthcare IT
- Healthcare Y2K risk elevated due to life-threatening potential; dependent on business partners and payers having Y2K-compliant systems for electronic billing
- Applications must integrate with existing systems; intuitive interfaces requiring minimum training; phased implementation produces best results
- Healthcare consolidating vertically and horizontally into IDNs; combining disparate systems including many older legacy systems is major challenge
