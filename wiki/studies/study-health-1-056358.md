> Aberdeen Group's 1998 survey of the Healthcare Information Systems (HIS) market, covering integrated HIS vendors, independent software vendors, professional services firms, and systems suppliers. The report maps the three application tiers—business, clinical, and management information—and assesses the impact of managed care consolidation, client-server adoption, Internet/intranet deployment, and Year 2000 compliance on healthcare IT buyers. Brief vendor profiles are provided for fifteen named companies.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- Advances in wireless technologies will become increasingly critical communication medium across the industry `[ps=5]`
- Healthcare providers historically lagged other industries in IT adoption; conservative buying patterns; now accelerating investment `[ps=5]`
- Data warehouses provide foundation for decision support by integrating legacy and client-server data; typically terabyte-scale on high-end Unix `[ps=5]`
- Healthcare consolidating vertically and horizontally into IDNs; combining disparate systems including many older legacy systems is major challenge `[ps=5]`
- Majority of HIS are custom-developed; trend shifting toward packaged applications tailored to provider needs `[ps=4]`
- Typical healthcare provider IT budget ~4% of annual operating budget; majority of IT departments projecting staffing increases `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen estimates over 70% of all physicians use the Internet for research `[ps=4]`
- Single electronic patient record shared across the enterprise will someday emerge as an operational and technical reality `[ps=4]`
- Significant progress made by suppliers with discipline-specific continuous speech dictation; IBM MedSpeak for Radiology cited `[ps=4]`
- Like the industry it serves, HIS supplier groups are consolidating; start-up vendors using new technologies are emerging `[ps=4]`
- Proven leader in HIS; $245M revenue; strong growing services organization; identified integration as significant revenue opportunity `[ps=4]`
- Security and privacy are major concerns in healthcare IT `[ps=4]`
- Healthcare Y2K risk elevated due to life-threatening potential; dependent on business partners and payers having Y2K-compliant systems for electronic billing `[ps=4]`
- Mandate to reduce costs while delivering quality care; managed care consolidation accelerating competition `[ps=3]`
- Advances in client-server technology allowing cost-effective HIS implementation; merging with legacy data access `[ps=3]`
