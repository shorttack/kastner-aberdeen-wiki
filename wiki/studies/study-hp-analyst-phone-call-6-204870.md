> Transcript of a pre-summit analyst phone call in which HP sought Kastner feedback on corporate messaging, Adaptive Enterprise positioning, divisional execution (PSG, IPG, ESG, Services), and consumer/SMB/enterprise capabilities. Kastner delivers harsh ratings: 'low TCO' message not believable; Adaptive Enterprise too IT-facing; HP-Compaq brand confusion in retail; mobility late to market; strong in digital imaging and printing.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner (Aberdeen Group, Chief Research Officer)**, type **transcript**._


## Top observations

- HP's home networked video initiative (circa 2003) did not succeed as a major market. HP launched digital entertainment/media center products but the home networked video market was slow to develop. Mass-market home video networking was not realized until streaming services (Netflix, Hulu 2007-2010) and HDTV adoption. `[ps=5]`
- HP message of 'low total-cost-of-ownership provider' — not heard, not believable. `[ps=4]`
- Adaptive Enterprise is too IT-facing and too little business-executive-management-facing. `[ps=4]`
- Adaptive Enterprise is an ESG and Services initiative; PSG and IPG not tied in. `[ps=4]`
- Analysts want beef — 'too much vision and not enough what-can-I-do-and-how-can-I-do-it.' `[ps=4]`
- Neither HP Adaptive Enterprise nor IBM On Demand winning mindshare; On Demand 'too web-services-oriented and not enough infrastructure-metric-oriented.' `[ps=4]`
- ESG new management due to lack of profitability; PSG barely profitable; IPG very profitable and executing well (high-end printing could do more); consumer space doing best but not profitably. `[ps=4]`
- HP is a technology components company moving to technology solutions; least believable label is 'business solutions company.' `[ps=4]`
- HP's capability to measure and maintain IT-to-business value-chain linkage is spotty — not a core competency. `[ps=4]`
- HP heterogeneous integration is adequate but not world-leading; modest IBM-mainframe integration skills. `[ps=4]`
- 'Products that work seamlessly together' across all geographies/product lines is not believable; IPG in SMB doing good delivery/satisfaction. `[ps=4]`
- HP is not a full-spectrum camera company; no interchangeable-lens model; well-focused for consumers. `[ps=4]`
- Not yet the leader in connectivity/end-to-end processing ('radical simplicity'); Internet Home Alliance research showed more customer-faced interaction needed. `[ps=4]`
- HP consumer service and support only mediocre; echoed by big PC-magazine survey; 'HP's PCs break too often.' `[ps=4]`
- HP 'wears so many different hats'; Carly has different messages for consumer, IT exec, and enterprise exec audiences — of necessity. `[ps=4]`
- HP Services can handle individual projects but lacks capacity for Fortune 500 company transformation. `[ps=3]`
- HP was very late to market with Centrino-based notebooks; has products but has not earned stripes in 'Mobility as a lifestyle.' `[ps=3]`
- HP is a leader in digital imaging and photography. `[ps=3]`
- Too much HP business comes from installed base — implies trouble generating new customers. `[ps=3]`
- HP has not adequately differentiated Compaq and HP brands in retail channel; marketing dollars not effective; Dell ruthlessly focused. `[ps=3]`
- HP has Unix expertise and growing Linux share but is underplaying the Microsoft market. `[ps=2]`
- HP tries to buy channel partner loyalty — probably not enough. `[ps=2]`
- HP is dropping the ball by waiting for home networked video market to evolve — letting Sony and others set standards. `[ps=2]`
- Primary focus: PSG and IPG; as Chief Research Officer, covers HP as a company. `[ps=0]`
- HP corporate messages have been 'very simplistic' and not communicated or drilled down at AR/analyst-facing events. `[ps=0]`
