# Project eLiza Positioning and Messaging Workshop Report


## Short-horizon prescience (3-year / 5-year)

- **3-year verdict:** medium — 3y Rule A: mean=3.23 over 35 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> medium [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].
- **5-year verdict:** high — 5y Rule A: mean=3.57 over 35 usable obs (0 prefiltered, 0 pending) -> high [high>=3.5, medium>=2.0].

> Aberdeen Group consulting deliverable to IBM following the May 16, 2001 Somers positioning workshop for Project eLiza — IBM's self-managing, self-healing server architecture initiative. Provides a high-level computing vision and three key messages (Best Practices Automation, Simplification, Lower Cost) mapped to three stakeholder classes (LOB Executive, IT Management & Staff, Internal/External Users). Predicts 'computers managing computers' in five to ten years. Flag: IBM-internal confidentiality.


_Published 2001, author **Joyce Becknell, Gordon Haff, Peter Kastner (Aberdeen Group)**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- eLiza self-management is transparent to developers — third-party software will not require modification using a whole new API set. `[ps=4]`
- Computers are running the business in a fundamental way — without working computers, a business will largely cease to operate. `[ps=4]`
- The real-time enterprise has arrived — enabled by compute tools and decision support; just-in-time manufacturing requires end-to-end automated supply chains. `[ps=4]`
- Manual backup procedures are increasingly unable to deal with computer failures, even in non-e-Business contexts such as grocery chains; the computer system IS the business system. `[ps=4]`
- The computer system is the total instantiation of the business process; IT architecture implicitly defines time, place, and manner of employee/customer/supplier interaction. `[ps=4]`
- In five to ten years, one of the big paybacks will be computers managing computers and running business processes automatically; people taken out of the loop, focused on exceptions. `[ps=4]`
- Autonomic computing, AIOps, Kubernetes operators, cloud auto-scaling, and self-healing infrastructure realized this prediction in the 2010s-2020s, with further acceleration from ML-driven operations and AI agents in the 2020s. `[ps=4]`
- Knowledge-worker exception handling follows: discover exception, query cause, make decision, transact to change, report/collaborate; eLiza self-management should perform all steps subject to preset policies. `[ps=4]`
- Preset-policy pager modes: (1) problem fixed, (2) approve default procedure, (3) choose among actions, or (4) no notification needed — contrasted with low-bar 'wireless page that something is wrong'. `[ps=4]`
- Message 1: Best Practices Automation — leveraging IT and domain expertise of IBM and others to address skills, expertise, flexibility, technology, and heterogeneity for more effective business processes. `[ps=4]`
- Message 2: Simplification — rooting out complexity wherever found by limiting and directing human interaction to high-level tasks requiring human decision-making. `[ps=4]`
- Stakeholder class 1: Line-of-Business (LOB) Executive — responsive to benefits of others' experience, improved total customer experience, domain expertise, predictable variable cost, improved ROI on IT. `[ps=4]`
- Stakeholder class 2: IT Management & Staff (CIO, architects, developers, operations) — responsive to adaptable architectures, easier capacity planning, specialist-silo cut-through, lower lifecycle cost, reduced routine code. `[ps=4]`
- Stakeholder class 3: Internal & External Users/Customers — responsive to services that 'just work', proactive handling, minimal training, self-help, passed-through cost savings. `[ps=4]`
- Key part of process is defining what and when eLiza is; eLiza is ultimately things as well as a concept; does not exist today; first phase must come into being. `[ps=4]`
- Sub-text to eLiza theme: 'this is not business as usual' — CEO/CIO-level pitch matched by dramatic proof points (e.g., reducing management keystrokes so one operator can manage 10x more servers). `[ps=4]`
- Avoid positioning eLiza as 'great technology' (even though it is) — focus on business outcomes. `[ps=4]`
- eLiza message to developers should emphasize masking of complexity — eLiza is not about mastering a thousand more APIs; it acts as a 'super operating system' providing a reliable foundation. `[ps=4]`
- eLiza is about both e-Business and other businesses; assertion that e-Business is the harder general case may be true but will get lost in translation. `[ps=4]`
- Service providers can generally be thought of as being today where enterprises are going — from an IT architecture perspective — but enterprises are more heterogeneous with greater legacy integration. `[ps=4]`
- IBM needs to articulate proof points for specific areas of eLiza with strongest leadership positions; proof points are key to selling CxOs on ROI. `[ps=4]`
- Make sure 'eLiza is heterogeneous' message is not confused with 'eLiza is based on standards' — heterogeneous has great pull with executives; standards are ho-hum. `[ps=4]`
- Umbrella messaging opportunity: Pervasive Computing + Real-time Computing demands Managed Computing (i.e., eLiza). `[ps=4]`
- For 30 years, MIS science has not been allowed to optimize the enterprise — 'we've always done it this way' remains pervasive; automating a mess yields an automated mess. `[ps=4]`
- IBM is a leader in systems management today; eLiza positioning reflects starting from a position of strength rather than a standing start. `[ps=3]`
