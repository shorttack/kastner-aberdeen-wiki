# Consumer Adoption of Memory Cards: A Qualitative Study

> Aberdeen Group qualitative study commissioned by the Internet Home Alliance (IHA) to understand consumer adoption barriers and purchase intent for SD Memory Cards in 2003. Research found digital electronics market maturing rapidly with digital cameras as primary entry point. Key findings: consumers broadly aware of memory cards but unable to identify specific formats/brands; primary adoption barrier is lack of compatible equipment; SD card seen as versatile with high 'cool factor' after demonstration; market transition to SD expected to take a decade; preferred capacity 256MB-1GB; willingness to pay premium of 10-15% for SD capability. Covers retail channel expectations, communication strategies, and data transfer behaviors.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- More mature than pre-study conjecture; receptivity and future buying patterns maturing
- Virtually all participants (after desktop computers and printers)
- Only a few respondents have MP3 players
- Only a few respondents have LANs; wireless transfer limited to PDAs only
- Majority unable to identify type/brand of memory card they own
- Most users never remove memory card from camera; transfer via USB cable not card
- Small size/portability; capacity choice; reusability; improving prices
- Small size (loss risk); incompatible formats; price at high capacities
- Largely positive; high 'cool factor'; versatility as key differentiator
- Lack of compatible equipment; need to acquire SD-enabled devices
- A decade (approximately)
- 256 MB to 1 GB (where price not a consideration)
- Majority will wait to see if SD becomes industry standard
- Substantial proportion willing to pay 10-15% premium; notable proportion would not pay any premium
- Minor consideration initially; 'tie-breaker' between otherwise identical products
- Consumer electronics stores (Best Buy), computer stores, office superstores, warehouse clubs, mass merchants (Target, Wal-Mart), online; eventually pharmacies, grocery stores, toy stores
- Product demonstrations (in-store, continuous loop video, infomercials); mass media (TV, radio, print); online advertising; direct mail
- Identified as ultimate goal by multiple respondents; frustration with cable proliferation
- E-mail is a key behavioral driver; photos emailed to friends/family and printed locally
- SD is one contender but not the assured winner; card type not well understood by consumers
- Kazaa and P2P widely used by respondents under 35; downloaded to PC then burned to CD
- In family households, typically one tech-savvy adult or teen handles digital data transfer; non-family households all members participate
- Key perceived drawback: notably smaller than most currently used cards; risk of loss or being swallowed by children/pets
- Photos primarily for family/social sharing; minority also use for commercial purposes (eBay listings, rental property docs, freelance news)
- Photos on PC hard drive (some on CDs); music on CDs; video on VHS tapes; no one uses memory cards for permanent storage
