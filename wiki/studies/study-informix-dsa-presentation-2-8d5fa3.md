# Choosing The Right Markets and the Right Partners for Informix

> Aberdeen Group analysis of the optimal markets and channel partners for Informix. Covers industry trends (open systems, RAD, distributed computing, client-server), customer decision-making dynamics, CSS market characteristics with pricing tiers ($500-$150k/module by company size), and detailed channel partner assessments (VARs, ISVs, OEMs, SIs, Big 6). Includes vertical market ratings for telecomm, retail, banking, manufacturing, and state/local government. 67-slide deck used in Informix sales training.


_Published 1995, author **Peter S. Kastner / Aberdeen Group**, type **consulting-report**._


## Top observations

- 20%+ per year
- Steadily picking up steam at workgroup/department level
- <2% of server market
- NT Server clustering will emerge in 1996, thrive in 1997 (Compaq+Tandem; DEC)
- unknown
- $500 to $5,000 per module (companies <$25M revenue)
- $10,000 to $75,000 per module (companies <$250M revenue)
- $50,000 to $150,000 per module (companies >$251M revenue)
- SAP AG is enterprise CSS market leader
- Solution set (integrated vs best-of-breed); architecture (app-centric vs DB-centric); toolsets; channel; midrange OS
- Manufacturing & Distribution: 12 to 36 months (Fortune 500)
- Human Resources: 1 to 3 months (Fortune 1000)
- Client-Server implementations surging vs mainframe; 449 vs 210 planned
- Highly attractive — time to market beats price; many channel partners
- Good with 50% chance rain — price always important; low margins
- Good over time; fair in 1996 — Informix not broadly known
- Good short and long term — solutions-oriented; VARs and ISVs critical
- Fair — long selling cycle; bidding regulations; competition among partners
- Leads; programs; tiered coop marketing funding; senior account management; tech support access
- Price; switch incentives; best technology du jour; moral superiority
- Aligning IT and corporate goals (#1 issue for IT executives in 1995)
- SAP R/3 to Informix: large leverage factor for ISV partners

- Only in 1990s has Unix emerged as practical and reliable for commercial applications
