# Industry Analyst Relations at Spring IDF 2005 — Peter Kastner Delegate Confirmation Letter

> Intel Analyst Relations personalized schedule for Peter Kastner (Vericours, Inc., P.O. Box 3480, Westport MA) for Spring IDF 2005 at Moscone West, San Francisco, March 1-3. Features Craig Barrett opening keynote, multicore briefing, Digital Enterprise/Digital Office luncheon and keynote, Sean Maloney mobile briefing, Virtualization Technology 101 briefing, Flash Products Group briefing, TMG (Technology & Manufacturing) briefing, UWB/Wireless USB briefing at W Hotel, Justin Rattner R&D keynote, CE-ATA briefing, and CTO panel. Documents a complete analyst-access schedule six months before the Fall IDF 2005 event.


_Published 2005, author **Liz Ryon (Intel SAS-Industry Analyst Relations Group)**, type **employer-record**._


## Top observations

- Craig Barrett (then Intel CEO, 2005) personally delivered the Spring IDF 2005 opening keynote — his final year as CEO before transition to Chairman.
- Spring IDF 2005 scheduled a dedicated Multicore Briefing (Tue 10:45-11:45 AM), six months before Fall IDF's multi-core mega-briefing — showing Intel's consistent multi-core drumbeat throughout 2005.
- Intel delivered multicore processors in 2005 as predicted. Intel Pentium D (dual-core desktop) shipped May 26, 2005. Paxville MP (dual-core server Xeon) shipped November 1, 2005. Core Duo (Yonah, mobile) shipped January 2006. Multicore was in production by end-2005.
- Dedicated 'Virtualization Technology 101 - Usage Models & Benefits' briefing on Wednesday indicates Intel was still educating analysts on virtualization use cases in early 2005.
- Flash Products Group briefing at Spring IDF 2005 shows flash was still a strategic Intel business unit in early 2005 (later divested via IMFT JV with Micron in 2006, ultimately sold to Micron in 2012).
- TMG (Technology & Manufacturing Group) briefing — process node roadmap update delivered to analysts.
- Dedicated UWB / Wireless USB Briefing at W Hotel on Wednesday suggests Intel was bullish on Wireless USB at Spring IDF 2005.
- UWB (Ultra-Wideband) failed to achieve mass market adoption as predicted. The WiMedia Alliance IEEE 802.15.3a standardization process stalled; the task group disbanded January 2006. Wireless USB based on WiMedia saw limited consumer adoption. WiMedia Alliance dissolved in 2009. Wireless USB never achieved mass adoption, replaced by USB 3.0 and Wi-Fi.
- CE-ATA Briefing and Class on Thursday reflects Intel's push for a small-form-factor storage interface for consumer electronics; eventually superseded by eMMC and UFS.
- Closing CTO Panel on Thursday was a standing IDF format — gathering industry CTOs for joint Q&A moderated by Intel.
