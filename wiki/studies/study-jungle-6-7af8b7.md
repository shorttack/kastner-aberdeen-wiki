Reprises market size and supplier data from Chapters 1-5, then provides expanded competitive detail: vendor-by-vendor weakness analyses, RDBMS technology rating breakdowns (scalability, distributed data, open technology, development tools, other technologies, supplier solutions), ISV platform support data (IBM gaining Unix share at HP's expense in 1994), IBM RS/6000 + DB2 best-fit scenarios, and wrap-up on RDBMS market dynamics. 58-slide deck.

## Top observations

- Largest RDBMS supplier; on track for $3.6+ billion FY1996 `[ps=5]`
- Unix-server DBMS revenue now greater than mainframe revenues `[ps=4]`
- Poor beyond 4 processors; Navigation Server poor high-end MPP on AT&T 3600 `[ps=4]`
- IBM gained Unix ISV market share in 1994, usually at expense of HP `[ps=4]`
- DB2 for AIX 2 functionally much improved; Data Propagator for mainframe-RS6000; CICS/Ensina/Tuxedo OLTP; HACMP and SP2 scalability `[ps=4]`
- No Ingres people remain; CA force doesn't understand Unix; architecture at OnLine 6.0 (no parallel); OPENROAD incompatible with character 4GL users `[ps=4]`
- RDBMS on midrange server is the hot market; technology incredibly fast-moving; deemphasize speeds-and-feeds; emphasize long-term cost-of-ownership `[ps=4]`
- Good async replication in Data Propagator; no peer-peer replication `[ps=3]`
- Powersoft & Watcom among market leaders in development tools `[ps=3]`
- Clunky tools only work on Oracle; Oracle Objects low-end and incompatible with Developer/2000 `[ps=3]`
- Most aggressive in multimedia marketing; objects by 1997; serious about video on demand `[ps=3]`
- Modest data warehouses on SMP or SP2; products now in place; field experience over next year `[ps=3]`
- Worldwide support and service ... the SAP R/3 story; enterprises committing to DB2 across IBM platforms `[ps=3]`
- Open Client & Open Server best gateway breadth; most flexible `[ps=2]`
- OpenROAD is most mature OO CADE (computer-aided development environment) `[ps=2]`
- ~$20 billion worldwide `[ps=1]`
- CA-Ingres partnership with Fujitsu for first ORDBMS in 1996 `[ps=1]`

- 50%+ per year `[ps=0]`

- Second-largest RDBMS supplier `[ps=0]`
- Past 60-80% growth rate `[ps=0]`
