# Report of Peter S. Kastner: Intelligent Electronics Inc. v. Andersen Consulting LLP

> Expert witness report by Peter S. Kastner of Aberdeen Group in the litigation between Intelligent Electronics Inc. and Andersen Consulting LLP. Analyzes IE's IT strategy, Andersen's systems integration performance, AS/400 platform decisions, and the PC distribution industry dynamics of the mid-1990s. Concludes that Andersen performed its engagement responsibilities competently and that IE's business failures stemmed from strategic market positioning errors.


_Published 1999, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- IE's long-term viability was questionable regardless of IT outcome — the PC distribution industry was consolidating to 2-3 major players `[ps=5]`
- AS/400 was a viable platform for distribution in 1994 with strong RPG developer ecosystem and proven reliability `[ps=5]`
- AS/400 platform would remain viable for distribution applications well beyond IE's planning horizon `[ps=5]`
- IBM AS/400 (now IBM i) remains active and supported in 2025 — over 30 years after IE case; still widely used in distribution `[ps=5]`
- Client-server became the dominant enterprise architecture by late 1990s before evolving to web-based and then cloud architectures `[ps=5]`
- SAP became the dominant enterprise ERP system globally; evolved from R/3 to ECC to S/4HANA `[ps=5]`
- Tech Data merged with SYNNEX in 2021 to form TD SYNNEX — the other surviving major distributor `[ps=5]`
- PC distribution industry consolidating from 10+ players to 2-3 major full-line distributors; mid-tier players being squeezed by margin compression `[ps=5]`
- Enterprise systems integration in 1994 was inherently high-risk — industry failure rates for large IT transformations exceeded 50% `[ps=5]`
- RPG programming language had a mature but shrinking developer pool in 1994 — finding RPG talent was increasingly difficult `[ps=5]`
- IE's market share was declining as Ingram Micro and Tech Data gained share through scale advantages and lower margins `[ps=5]`
- Even with successful IT implementation IE would likely have been acquired or dissolved due to structural industry dynamics — IT was necessary but not sufficient for survival `[ps=5]`
- Option 2 (build new client-server system while maintaining legacy) was the best strategic choice among the three options presented `[ps=4]`
- MicroAge filed for bankruptcy in 2000 — another mid-tier PC distributor eliminated by consolidation `[ps=4]`
- HP 3000 was already in decline by 1994 — losing market share and developer support `[ps=4]`
- MSD upgrade (Issue 1) was a reasonable interim step — keeping legacy operational while building new system was standard practice `[ps=4]`
- Client-server architecture was the correct strategic direction for IE — replacing terminal-based AS/400 applications with modern interfaces `[ps=4]`
- IE revenue approximately $3 billion in 1993 — peak before decline `[ps=4]`
- SAP R/3 (Option 3) was evaluated but deemed too risky and expensive for IE — implementation timeline would exceed IE's survival window `[ps=4]`
- Ingram Micro was the largest PC distributor — exemplified the low-cost high-volume model that was winning `[ps=4]`
- Andersen's Application Software Factory (ASF) methodology was a reasonable development approach — standard for large SI engagements of the era `[ps=3]`
- Ingram Micro survived as a leading distributor until acquired by Platinum Equity in 2021 for $7.2B — validating the full-line distributor model `[ps=3]`
- Inacom filed for bankruptcy in 2001 — third mid-tier PC distributor eliminated `[ps=1]`
- HP announced end-of-life for HP 3000 in 2003; platform fully discontinued 2008 `[ps=1]`
- $3 billion revenue; third-largest US PC distributor behind Ingram Micro and Tech Data `[ps=0]`
