# Report of Peter S. Kastner: Intelligent Electronics Inc. v. Andersen Consulting LLP

> Expert witness report by Peter S. Kastner of Aberdeen Group in the litigation between Intelligent Electronics Inc. and Andersen Consulting LLP. Analyzes IE's IT strategy, Andersen's systems integration performance, AS/400 platform decisions, and the PC distribution industry dynamics of the mid-1990s. Concludes that Andersen performed its engagement responsibilities competently and that IE's business failures stemmed from strategic market positioning errors.


_Published 1999, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- $3 billion revenue; third-largest US PC distributor behind Ingram Micro and Tech Data
- Speed of IT implementation was critical — IE needed to transform IT within 12-18 months to survive competitive pressure
- IE positioned itself as leading-edge technology company — a reseller not just a distributor
- IE lacked internal IT competency to execute a major systems transformation without external help
- IE's fundamental strategic error was pursuing a reseller/value-added model rather than low-cost distributor model as industry consolidated
- Andersen Consulting performed competently on the IE/2000 engagement given the constraints imposed by IE
- IE owned the IE/2000 project — IE management made all key decisions including scope, timing, and technology choices
- Andersen's IT Strategy Report was an excellent piece of work — thorough, well-researched, and appropriate for IE's situation
- Option 2 (build new client-server system while maintaining legacy) was the best strategic choice among the three options presented
- IE's long-term viability was questionable regardless of IT outcome — the PC distribution industry was consolidating to 2-3 major players
- IE filed for bankruptcy in 1996 and was liquidated by 1997
- MicroAge filed for bankruptcy in 2000 — another mid-tier PC distributor eliminated by consolidation
- Inacom filed for bankruptcy in 2001 — third mid-tier PC distributor eliminated
- AS/400 was a viable platform for distribution in 1994 with strong RPG developer ecosystem and proven reliability
- AS/400 platform would remain viable for distribution applications well beyond IE's planning horizon
- IBM AS/400 (now IBM i) remains active and supported in 2025 — over 30 years after IE case; still widely used in distribution
- HP 3000 was already in decline by 1994 — losing market share and developer support
- HP announced end-of-life for HP 3000 in 2003; platform fully discontinued 2008
- MSD was IE's core distribution system — RPG-based on AS/400; functional but aging and difficult to extend
- MSD upgrade (Issue 1) was a reasonable interim step — keeping legacy operational while building new system was standard practice
- Andersen's resignation from the IE/2000 project was justified given IE's failure to meet commitments and changing project conditions
- IE repeatedly changed project scope and requirements — a key factor in IE/2000 difficulties
- Andersen's work product quality was comparable to industry standards for major SI firms based on Aberdeen's research across 7 major SI firms
- Andersen's staffing levels and expertise were appropriate for the IE/2000 engagement scope
- Andersen's Application Software Factory (ASF) methodology was a reasonable development approach — standard for large SI engagements of the era
