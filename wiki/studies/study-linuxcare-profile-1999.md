# Linuxcare, Inc. -- Enterprise Service and Support for All Things Linux

> An Aberdeen Group company profile of Linuxcare, Inc. published in November 1999, assessing the startup's viability as the first enterprise Linux services and support company. The paper evaluates Linuxcare's four service pillars (24x7 support training porting and certification), its key OEM contracts with Dell IBM and Sun, and its Kleiner Perkins Caulfield and Byers backing. Aberdeen concludes that a credible enterprise Linux services market exists and recommends Linuxcare be considered by IT executives seeking Linux as an alternative enterprise OS.


_Published 1999, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **case-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Founded 1998 as Linux consulting company; OEMs sought expertise; expanded to training and support
- Kleiner Perkins Caulfield and Byers chose Linuxcare as its first Linux-related investment
- Linux became dominant enterprise server OS; IBM acquired Red Hat for $34B in 2019; KPCB's early Linux thesis proved visionary
- Within the next year IT managers will have a Linux-based alternative in every major application area
- Oracle SAP IBM middleware all ported to Linux by 2003-2005; enterprise Linux became the dominant server platform; prediction proved accurate within 3-4 years not just 1
- IT departments using Linux for email file/print Web servers; increasingly for decision support caching storage DNS Web commerce
- Linux grew from ~25% enterprise server OS in 2003 to >70% by 2015; became THE dominant server OS
- 24x7 support + training (Linuxcare University) + porting/migration (Linuxcare Labs) + benchmarking/certification
- Red Hat built $3.4B annual revenue on exactly this model before IBM acquisition; SUSE and Canonical followed same playbook; market thesis proved correct
- Dell (back-end support for OptiPlex Dimension Precision workstations and PowerEdge Servers) + IBM (RS/6000) + Sun (Star Office) + TurboLinux (North American customers)
- Dell contracted Linuxcare for back-end Linux support on OptiPlex Dimension Precision workstations and PowerEdge Servers
- IBM contracted Linuxcare for Linux support on RS/6000 line
- Sun contracted Linuxcare for support of Star Office suite (acquired from Star Division)
- Alpha + Intel + MIPS + PowerPC + SPARC + StrongARM
- Bronze / Silver / Gold / Platinum
- Linux System Administration + Network Administration + Security + Linux Overview for NT Professionals + Extreme Samba + Linux for Executives
- All services can be private-branded (e.g. www.dell.linuxcare.com)
- Aberdeen convinced by extensive market research that credible enterprise Linux services market exists; Linuxcare proven viable source of Linux expertise
- Linuxcare pivoted to focus on network management (Definiti brand) then acquired by Levanta in 2004; faded as independent company but market thesis proved correct
- IT management has contracts for Unix and Windows NT support; same 24x7 global support question must be answered for Linux
- Growing headcount and placing QA metrics in place (scaling challenges)
- Andrew Tridgell (original Samba author) employed as Linux guru at Linuxcare
- ISVs have announced ports of major enterprise applications to Linux
- Red Hat built the dominant enterprise Linux business on Linuxcare's model; acquired by IBM for $34B in 2019
- Porting and migration assistance via Linuxcare Labs — distribution-neutral approach
