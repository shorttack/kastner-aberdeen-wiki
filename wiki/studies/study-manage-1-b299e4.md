# Managed Carrier Services: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering the managed carrier services market, analyzing how enterprises use telecom outsourcing and virtual private networks to reduce costs and improve network operations. The report profiles nine service providers including Ameritech, Bell Atlantic, IBM Global Services, and Infonet, and provides market sizing estimates projecting the managed services market will reach $36 billion by 2002. It documents enterprise attitudes toward outsourcing, service differentiator trends, and the emerging role of IP-based VPNs and TCP/IP in replacing traditional dedicated-circuit architectures.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- $563 billion
- $8.5 billion
- $36 billion
- $1.4 trillion (CAGR 19.5%)
- $2000-$5000 per month per connection
- 10:1 (access to backbone)
- 150+ countries
- Greater adoption predicted over next 5 years
- Early contracts widely considered failures
- Cost reduction plus improved functionality
- 25% of end users surveyed cited pay-as-you-go as deciding factor
- Facilities-based end-to-end providers have market advantage
- Many original experiences disappointing; second contracts written defensively
- Network Connectivity, Applications, Hardware, Remote Access, Network Security
- Voice VPN, Data VPN, IP-based VPN (intranet/extranet)
- Switched Virtual Circuits and bandwidth-on-demand identified as future trends
- Major network outsourcer taking complete control of network assets and personnel
- Global facilities-based data service provider with end-to-end network coverage
- Offers customer-configurable committed information rate (CIR) parameters
- Will Frame Relay and ATM prevail or will IP-based services win long term?
- Computer traffic typically uses about 10% of network capacity
- 1980: voice VPN; 1985: data VPN; 1988: outsourced networks; 1990s: managed services
- Global enterprises avoid coordinating last-mile with each local telco in every country
