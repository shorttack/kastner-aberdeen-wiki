# Management Skills Learned 1969–1979

> Peter S. Kastner reflects on the five core professional skills forged during his first decade in computing (1969–1979): defensive professionalism, pragmatic adaptation, fiduciary forensics, high-volume systems logistics, and resilience under chaos. Drawing on engagements at firms including ADL, Chase Manhattan, Houghton Mifflin, and Continental Forest Industries, he argues these 'foxhole' experiences created the analytical and personal temperament that drove his later career as an industry strategist.


_Published 2025, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **memoir**._


## Top observations

- First decade 1969-1979 described as apprenticeship in 'economic physics' of computing; defined by professional agency rather than management ladder
- 'Watergate' environment requiring meticulous personal record-keeping as forensic trail; project headed for litigation
- Maintaining journal and printouts as 'personal flight recorder' to document technical reality in litigation-bound projects
- Carrying police radio while auditing code in dangerous neighborhood; developed clinical vendor evaluation with situational awareness
- In Rio de Janeiro, pivoted from unaffordable hardware to 'bicycle-powered' batch system; directed translation of complex system into foreign language environment
- Designed 5-baud remote meter reading system respecting physical reality of rural party lines and utility regulation
- Vendor Support Manager at ADL; conducted OEM audits with Frank Hawthorne; developed C-suite financial literacy through technical lens
- Identified $80,000 pending liability that management had booked as revenue; skill of 'Radical Transparency' about broken accounting
- Predicted $12 million USM disaster nine months before it hit courts by recognizing IBM 4300 to DG Eclipse migration as violation of 'Simple Physics'
- USM Corporation litigation reached courts approximately 9 months after Kastner's architectural diagnosis
- Lead designer of securities subsystem; responsible for workflow bridge between 96-column and 80-column punched cards
- Securities subsystem logic required to be proven 'to the penny' by manual audit; army of workers reprocessed every day's work
- 2,000-pound paper rolls moving at lethal speeds; 'Order and Harmony' required as matter of physical safety
- Managing critical path of physical mass at paper mill; direct connection between system logic and factory floor safety
- Survived two hurricanes and a dismasting at sea; translated 'sailor's temperament' into calm-under-pressure baseline for all projects
- 104-degree heat in Rio de Janeiro; customs officer destroying hospital data tape; operated under extreme physical and logistical adversity
- By 1979: left code behind for 'Clean Sheet' operating at scale of entire industries; characterized as Market Planner and Analyst who had seen 'Stench of Failure' and 'Aura of Success'
- Machine time valued at $360/hour; far more valuable than human labor; drove mastery of I/O optimization
- COBOL and Assembler as foundation; dump reading in raw hexadecimal core memory rather than high-level debuggers
