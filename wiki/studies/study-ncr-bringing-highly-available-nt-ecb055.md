# NCR: Bringing Highly-Available NT to Transaction-Intensive Production Environments

> This 1997 Aberdeen Group vendor profile abstract documents Aberdeen's findings from in-depth interviews with IS managers who deployed NT servers, specifically comparing NCR's NT offerings against Compaq, Hewlett-Packard, and IBM. Aberdeen concludes that NCR's NT products are distinguishable in processing power, applications focus, and service/support availability, and recommends NCR for IS decision makers seeking High Availability Transaction Processing (HATP) servers.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group (James Gruener)**, type **vendor-profile**._


## Top observations

- Many NT servers ostensibly look alike (same fundamental hardware running Windows NT Server); IS decision makers finding tremendous differences in robustness
- Gathering detailed comparative information to determine best NT supplier can add weeks or months to selection process
- Conducted in-depth interviews with IS managers who deployed NT servers; compared NCR customers against users of alternative NT platforms
- Processing power — NCR distinguishable from Compaq HP and IBM
- Applications focus — NCR distinguishable in transaction-intensive application support
- Service/support availability — NCR distinguishable from competitors
- IS decision makers looking for leading-edge HATP servers would be well-served to evaluate NCR's NT services for transaction-intensive NT applications
- NCR exited commodity server market post-2000; split into two independent companies in 2023: NCR Voyix (digital commerce) and NCR Atleos (ATM/banking)
- Compaq acquired by Hewlett-Packard for $25 billion in 2002; brand discontinued; ProLiant server line absorbed into HP/HPE
- IBM sold x86/Netfinity server business to Lenovo in 2005 for $1.75 billion; IBM exited commodity server market
