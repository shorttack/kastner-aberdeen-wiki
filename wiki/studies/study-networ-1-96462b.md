> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering the enterprise networking market — including LAN/WAN technologies, hubs, routers, switches, and high-speed networking. The report profiles major networking suppliers including Cisco, 3Com, Bay Networks, and Cabletron, and identifies key market trends such as the transition from hubs to switches, the rise of Gigabit Ethernet, and the impact of Internet traffic on network design. It also addresses Year 2000 compliance concerns for network infrastructure.


_Published 1998, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **other-research**._


## Top observations

- Gigabit Ethernet is attractive alternative to ATM for Ethernet backbone users; ATM not accepted end-to-end `[ps=5]`
- ATM posted healthy growth in 1997 as WAN and LAN backbone but failed as end-to-end desktop technology `[ps=5]`
- VPNs using tunneling/security protocols turn public Internet into private WAN at fraction of private line costs `[ps=5]`
- Cisco profiled as dominant enterprise router and switch vendor; major internetworking supplier `[ps=5]`
- In excess of $24 billion; fastest growing IT segment `[ps=4]`
- Internet adoption inverted 80/20 traffic rule — now 80% of traffic crosses backbone; bursty and unpredictable `[ps=4]`
- SOHO is fastest growing segment; 90% of businesses worldwide; ~40% use Internet as part of business `[ps=4]`
- ~45 million US households with home computers; ~25% own more than one PC; nascent home networking market `[ps=4]`
- DWDM enables up to 1 terabit on single fiber; will remove WAN bottleneck; stimulate enterprise bandwidth demand `[ps=4]`
- Intel and Compaq making successful networking debuts; driven by high-volume/low-cost manufacturing `[ps=2]`
- Overall revenue growth not to exceed 15% due to price declines and Y2K resource diversion `[ps=1]`
- Verify actual adoption rates by 2000-2002. `[ps=0]`
- 3Com holds 40% NIC market share; leads Ethernet managed hubs and Fast Ethernet workgroup hubs `[ps=0]`
- xDSL technologies emerged; 128 Kbps IDSL to 7 Mbps ADSL; not yet widely deployed or geographically available `[ps=0]`
- Bay Networks profiled as major internetworking supplier; acquired by Nortel Networks `[ps=0]`
- Routing switches replacing routers in enterprise LAN core; ~10x capacity at ~1/10 cost
- Fast Ethernet adopted as primary upgrade from 10 Mbps; 10/100 NIC prices at 10 Mbps levels
- Wireless occupies niche; 802.11 ratification improves interoperability; bandwidth approaching 10 Mbps
- Workgroup switch prices dropped; switches replacing hubs; hubs survive as low-end SOHO/SME product
- Y2K compliance overlooked for network components; critical for hub/router/switch firmware; worst in SME
- Converged networks (voice/video/data) will not become widespread for several more years due to quality/uncertainty
- As network complexity increases more IT orgs consider outsourcing; particularly remote access
- Alteon profiled as high-speed networking newcomer focused on Gigabit Ethernet switches
