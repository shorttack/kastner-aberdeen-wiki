# Building Distributed Systems with Midrange Servers

> Aberdeen Group presents a comprehensive guide for IS executives on building enterprise distributed systems using midrange servers, covering enterprise requirements, server characteristics, operating systems, and distributed architectures. The study advocates the three-tier-plus topology and demonstrates how midrange servers surpass mainframes in I/O bandwidth, processor performance, and cost, delivering 50% IS cost reductions on average. Aberdeen profiles leading hardware suppliers (HP, NCR, Unisys, Compaq, DEC) and RDBMS vendors (Oracle, Sybase, Informix, Ingres, Software AG) as the foundation for distributed production systems.


_Published 1993, author **Peter S. Kastner, John Logan, Thomas Willmott**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Midrange servers exceed IBM ES/9000 in technological capability and systems reliability
- 1 to 1,000 active concurrent users; economical from single-employee brokerage to large datacenter
- Midrange: 20-32 MB/sec channel I/O; mainframe ESCOM: 9.5 MB/sec
- 50% average cost reduction in acquisition, maintenance, and support; application development up to 80% less costly
- RDBMS is an integrable part of midrange server; provides speed of app dev, portability, customization, departmental customization
- Midrange servers provide greater functionality in security, data integrity, availability than either mainframes or PC LANs
- Falls short of enterprise production requirements in 1993; may change within 2-3 years
- Midrange servers will move from TCP/IP to OSF DCE within next several years
- [UNVERIFIED]
- Dominant revenue supplier of midrange servers for new enterprise applications
- Market leader for multiuser Intel/Unix midrange servers
- $280 million per year in midrange servers based on SCO Unix
- Pioneer; standards to which others aspire; but unable to win major new accounts until Alpha/OpenVMS stabilizes
- New generation of transaction monitors for efficiently integrating OLTP apps, heterogeneous databases, and users
- USL (now Novell division) introducing new generation of transaction monitors to tie islands of disparate databases
- Three-tier-plus (client + distributed midrange + central production + analytical) is most successful distributed architecture
- Enterprises will implement client-server applications based on RDBMS-of-choice rather than any one manufacturer's midrange platform
- [UNVERIFIED]
- If IBM emulated Unisys A-Series strategy it could extend useful life of ES/9000 mainframes
- Today's midrange servers can be remotely backed up, applications propagated, batch scheduled, and crashes recovered without on-site operators
- Users connected to midrange server hub have interoperable access to mainframes, PC users, databases worldwide including public data networks
- Three classes of midrange servers will be chief building blocks: datacenter application servers, gateway servers, distributed production servers
- [UNVERIFIED]
- Surround strategy: mainframe as data repository while new OLTP and decision support apps developed on midrange; preserves investment and retrains staff
- Advanced servers based upon multiple microprocessor architectures will become standard server platforms of mid-to-late 1990s
