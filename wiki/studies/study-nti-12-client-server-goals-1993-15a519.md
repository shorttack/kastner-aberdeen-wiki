# Realistic Goals for Client-Server Computing

> Aberdeen Group defines and evaluates the client-server computing paradigm across five critical planning areas: client hardware options, scalable server architectures, legacy system roles, systems software transitions, and network infrastructure. The study covers current client-server implementations and traces the evolution to advanced designs including distributed relational and object-oriented databases, cascading server-to-server transactions, and intelligent high-speed networks. Aberdeen concludes that client-server drives the next-generation computing vision but warns that the installed base technology does not yet fully support the vision and that significant non-technical barriers—management vision, process reengineering, and training—must be overcome.


_Published 1993, author **Peter S. Kastner, John Logan, Thomas Willmott**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Advanced multiprocessor architecture servers will become standard platforms of mid-to-late 1990s `[ps=5]`
- Many new and innovative client types will emerge: miniaturized hand-held devices, scanners, pen-based, voice-actuated systems `[ps=5]`
- Industry consensus that client-server is vision of next-generation computing; installed base technology does not yet support the vision `[ps=4]`
- Nearly 40% of typical IT budget consumed by PCs, terminals, workstations, LANs, and client-related expenses `[ps=4]`
- Intel 486-based and RISC-based workstations capable of most demanding C-S applications available at low price points in 1993 `[ps=4]`
- Seven key dependencies: OO software, distributed database, robust microprocessors, pre-emptive multitasking OS, network capacity, systems/network management tools, applications software availability `[ps=4]`
- Four major non-technical barriers: management vision/leadership, process reengineering skills, human resources/training, financial stability of leading suppliers `[ps=4]`
- Rudimentary tools and components now available to begin client-server for decision support applications `[ps=4]`
- Dominant LAN OS market share; NetWare 4.0 moving to enterprise-wide directory services `[ps=3]`
- 100 Mbps performance; $2,000 per connection on fiber; CDDI experimenting for unshielded twisted pair `[ps=3]`
- Distributed relational databases employing business policy triggers plus increasing OO programming will characterize second half of decade `[ps=3]`
- Mainframes/minicomputers becoming hub systems: enterprise data repositories and communications processors; eventually devalued to zero `[ps=3]`
- Joint PowerPC development with IBM will move Macintosh interface to AIX and RISC chip platforms `[ps=2]`
- Custom development of production systems is only alternative today `[ps=1]`
