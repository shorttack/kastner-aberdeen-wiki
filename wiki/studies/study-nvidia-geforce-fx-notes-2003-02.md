# nVidia GeForce FX Product-Line Briefing Notes

> Briefing notes from an nVidia product-line meeting with Brian Burke and Todd Reddick covering the GeForce FX NV30/NV31/NV34 lineup — targets, process (TSMC 0.13 micron / 0.15 micron), memory (DDR1/DDR2), AGP 8x, DirectX 9 support, NV35 high-end future, and SKU positioning versus ATI Radeon 9500 and prior GeForce Ti 4200/MX 440 parts. Includes pricing of $99 (NV34) to $399 (NV30/5800) and April ship timing.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Very modular: mobile, low cost $99 (NV34) up through high-end
- Announced March 6; ships April
- Announced March 6; ships April
- Full DirectX 9 support across FX line
- High-end future; under May 11 NDA
- 20 GB/s — 2x NV30 (2nd gen compression/caching, 2nd gen pixel shader, AGP 8x, clocks ≥ NV30)
- 2x Ti4200; 4-pipe engine; 0.13 micron TSMC; AGP 8x; DDR1; lossless compression continues
- 1.5x ATI Radeon 9500
- $179 at 128MB — 'pretty aggressive'
- No OEMs to announce yet — PR later; production silicon rev A01
- Replaces GeForce Ti 4200 low end and MX; 2x GeForce 4 MX 440 64MB
- Full cinematix; 128-bit; vertex and pixel shaders; dual RAMDACs 350 MHz 2048x1356; MPEG decoder; DDR1; 0.15 micron TSMC; 128MB standard
- $99 price point
- Lacks compression and other high-end features
- End February 2003
- $399 — 'more than a 4600'; product name 5800
- Power, precision, programmability
- Vertex and pixel shaders across the line; 128-bit precision; hardware pixel/vertex shaders into mainstream (Dawn, Doom III)
- Will outperform GeForce 4 Ti 4600 at $399
