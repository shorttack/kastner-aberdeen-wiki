# PC Buying Guide Guest Column: 'Take Her Up to 2.4 GHz, Mr. Scott'

> Short Kastner guest column from InternetWeek (January 7, 2003), mirrored on Techno-News Blog. Kastner observes that the prior corporate-standard 2.0-GHz Pentium 4 has been displaced in just three months: Dell, HP, and IBM all added the 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 to the single-price lower Pentium 4 tier, so corporate IT managers ordering a 1.8-, 2.0-, 2.2-, or 2.4-GHz desktop all pay the same price at checkout. Short-form memoir-style commentary on the 2002-2003 CPU clock-speed race.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner (InternetWeek guest column, via Techno-News Blog)**, type **memoir**._


## Top observations

- For the past three months, a 2.0-GHz Pentium 4 microprocessor has been considered the standard for the corporate mainstream desktop, representing the best value in performance and price for the vast majority of enterprise workers.
- Dell, HP, and IBM recently added the 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 microprocessor to their single-price lower Pentium 4 price tier — corporate IT managers can order a 1.8-, 2.0-, 2.2-, or 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 desktop and pay the exact same price at checkout.
- Continuing the trend toward faster, smaller, and cheaper.
- Intel clock-speed race stalled at ~3.8 GHz due to thermal limits; Pentium 4 was supplanted by multi-core Core microarchitecture starting 2006; corporate standard shifted to clock-for-clock and core count rather than clock speed.
