# Powerful Network Management Made Easy

> Aberdeen Group compares Tivoli's TME 10 NetView 5.0 (on NT) against Hewlett-Packard's OpenView Network Node Manager (NNM) 5.0 for managing networks from NT servers. The study evaluates both products across four criteria: ease of use, ability to drill down to problems quickly, cost-effective management, and IP address management. Aberdeen strongly recommends Tivoli's TME 10 NetView for branch-office and distributed NT environments, citing its superior Windows-native GUI, Mid-Level Manager (agent) architecture, DHCP support, and SmartSets capability.


_Published 1997, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- HP leads network management market in both market share and mindshare; large installed base of hardware and software; worldwide presence; strong marketing
- Tivoli completely rewrote TME 10 NetView 5.0 to fully exploit Windows APIs; designed for branch office NT environments with Mid-Level Manager agent architecture
- Mid-Level Manager agents collect data on branch office devices and filter only relevant data up to centralized server; Central NetView + Regional NetView + MLM agents
- HP NNM requires full package licensed and deployed at each location to be monitored; processes all event data unfiltered; leads to wasted bandwidth and system delays
- TME 10 NetView was first network management product to adopt Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP); reduces false alarms from users turning off terminals
- Dynamically updated groups of nodes (e.g. 'down routers' 'all printers'); automatic addition when router goes down; improves exception reporting and proactive management
- HP NNM web browser is static text-only HTML format; requires third-party supplier separately; text-based table reports time-consuming to decipher
- NetView uses Netscape browser with combination of Java / JavaScript / HTML formats; view-only at publication time; full security/remote management planned by end 1998
- TME 10 NetView fully exploits Windows APIs; drop-down menus parallel Microsoft Office suite; reduces training time; NNM navigation time-consuming with excessive windows
- SAM revises multiple locations simultaneously with single button press; NNM requires manual updates at each location; SAM ships on Unix; usable from Unix to execute on NT
- HP reached success then lost momentum; competitors filled vacuum; complacency and lack of investment in product R&D caused NNM to fall behind in user-friendliness / efficiency / breadth
- HP recently announced addition of 200-300 new sales staff for OpenView — described as a small step but not addressing root problem
- TME 10 NetView advantage: Windows-native GUI; Office-parallel drop-down menus; drag-and-drop; sidebar navigation history; reduces training time and cost
- TME 10 NetView advantage: prebuilt report formats launchable with single click; dynamic event updates without user intervention; SmartSets for proactive exception reporting
- TME 10 NetView advantage: Regional NetView small footprint for branch offices; fraction of cost of full implementation; Mid-Level Managers reduce hardware/admin overhead
- TME 10 NetView advantage: first to adopt DHCP; reduces false alarms from powered-off devices; distinguishes blue (off) from red (broken)
- Integrated network and systems management is a 'two-horse race' between IBM Tivoli TME and Computer Associates CA-Unicenter at enterprise level; HP and Sun lead below enterprise level
- Released September 1997
- Aberdeen predicts NNM destined to fall further behind as Tivoli moves forward unless HP redirects commitment to customer needs
- Aberdeen highly recommends TME 10 NetView for managers evaluating cost-effective robust scalable NT management options; leadership role in market
- HP discontinued NNM version 6.4/6.41 effective December 31 2007; final version was 7.5x discontinued May 2010; product line sold to MicroFocus then OpenText
- TME 10 NetView evolved into IBM Tivoli NetView; Tivoli brand became dominant IBM systems management platform through 2000s; NetView for z/OS still active as of 2026 for mainframe management
- Hewlett-Packard split into HP Inc. and Hewlett Packard Enterprise in 2015; HP Software division (including OpenView heritage products) sold to MicroFocus in 2017 and then to OpenText in 2022
- TME 10 NetView supports: Unix AIX / Digital UX / Solaris / NT on Alpha and Intel
- HP NNM supports: Solaris / HP-UX / NT Server environments
