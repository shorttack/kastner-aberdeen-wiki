# Prime Computer — Industry Product Requirements Plan (Five-Year Plan, July 1981, COMPANY CONFIDENTIAL)

> Internal Prime Computer Inc. five-year Industry Product Requirements Plan dated July 15, 1981 (COMPANY CONFIDENTIAL), authored by Peter Kastner of Prime's Market Planning and Management Department of Marketing Development with input from Prime market managers (the 'and Others' coauthors). The plan synthesizes vertical-market plans for Construction Engineering, Power Utilities, Manufacturing Engineering, Manufacturing Production, Banking, Government, and Energy (Petroleum) into a single statement of common product requirements for Prime's first half-of-the-1980s strategy. Forecasts product revenue from planned markets growing from $117.7M in 1980 (44% of corporate goal) to $1,193.1M in 1985 (81% of corporate goal). Covers seven System Use Categories (Computational, Timesharing/Program Development, CAD/CAM, Office Automation, Transaction Processing, Query, Prime Information / Small Business) crossed against five system price bands ($30-49K Rabbit through $300K+ Prime 850). Identifies five priority near-term product gaps: programmable parallel-channel interface (HPC-4 / MPC-4 Special), floating-point precision, COBOL at full ANSI intermediate level, FORTRAN 77 compile/runtime performance, and Hyperchannel. Recommends a $10K-$30K personal computer with integral Primenet networking before mid-1980s. Identifies four 'attack' market opportunities: local-area networks of terminals, distributed processing including personal/departmental systems, office automation, and CAD/CAM. The plan defines the product strategy that Kastner — then in Prime Market Planning — used to influence Prime's 1981-1985 development cycle, and is the principal Kastner-authored artifact from his Prime Computer (1979-1981) employer era.


_Published 1981, author **Peter Kastner & Others — Prime Computer Inc., Market Planning and Management Department**, type **internal-strategic-plan**._


## Top observations

- 'Market Planning strongly believes that a very low end system, in a $10K-$30K price band, is needed before the mid-1980's' — networked, professional/managerial/engineering workstation `[ps=5]`
- Local-area networks of terminals and other devices; Distributed processing including personal/departmental systems; Office automation; CAD/CAM `[ps=5]`
- 'Lack of SNA limits Prime's applicability within many major banks.' `[ps=5]`
- 'The shortage of skilled programmers will continue at least until 1985. Programmer productivity aids and tools are readily marketable.' — Unix PWB cited as the model toolset. `[ps=5]`
- Prime Computer underwent J.H. Whitney leveraged buyout in 1989; spun off Computervision; remaining computer business wound down by 1992; brand discontinued; Computervision acquired by PTC in 1998. `[ps=5]`
- 'Gateway to industry standard local networks like IEEE and Ethernet' as Other Communications requirement. `[ps=5]`
- Computational, Timesharing/Program Development, CAD/CAM, Office Automation, Transaction Processing, Query (DB & Batch), Prime Information (small business) `[ps=4]`
- 'Stand-alone TP system sold to small and medium companies will require applications packages and support such that we recommend a channel of distribution strategy using system builders and dealers.' `[ps=4]`
- $8,000-$10,000 1984 entry level; full networking and Prime host access; benchmarked vs. Xerox 820 / AM Jacquard / AMX 90 / Apollo Domain / Three Rivers PERQ / Convergent `[ps=4]`
- 'Prime's excellent communications capabilities can be enhanced so that Prime provides a computer which translates one word processor's communications to what is understandable by another vendor's word processor, facilitating the movement of documents in the many organizations with mixed vendor's equipment. We recommend that Prime pursue this niche.' `[ps=4]`
- Direct sales to medium/large/major petroleum; system builders/dealers/joint marketers for small/medium; segments prioritized: production (oil & gas accounting), refining, exploration, R&D, OA. `[ps=4]`
- 'The potential exceeds 100,000 terminals' for OA in first-tier petroleum companies. `[ps=4]`
- 'The set of product requirements common to all markets is where most of Prime's product development attention and investment should be placed.' `[ps=3]`
- (1) Programmable parallel-channel interface (HPC-4 / MPC-4 Special); (2) Floating-point precision and consistency; (3) COBOL at full ANSI intermediate level; (4) FORTRAN 77 compile/runtime performance; (5) Hyperchannel interface `[ps=3]`
- 'The OA product has been plagued with several problems: stability, low word processing functionality, lack of networking and ease of use.' `[ps=3]`
- 'We usually only see one competitor when computational timesharing is called for, albeit that competitor is DEC's VAX' `[ps=3]`
- (1) Marketing & Development aligned with corporate strategies; (2) Prime can make-or-buy needed products; (3) Feature prioritization is iterative; (4) Market Planning lacks expertise/charter for full functional product specs; (5) Development timing & resource limits ignored; (6) Defining what is needed, not necessarily what is feasible. `[ps=3]`
- 'Prime offers price benefits over mainframes, functionality and communications benefits over stand-alone workstations, and performance benefits over networked micros.' `[ps=2]`
- 'Avoid competing with large mainframes for core application business. Niche market in bank administration, personal computing, timesharing and communications capabilities. OA can make us unique. Sell to large banks in money market cities in the U.S. and Europe.' `[ps=2]`
- 1. FORTRAN; 2. COBOL; 3. Pascal; 4. PL/I; 5. BASIC; 6. RPG; 7. C; 8. Ada; 9. APL et al. `[ps=2]`
- 'We do not foresee significant corporate revenue streams from TP at Fortune 1000-type firms before 1984' — slow but progressive growth from pilots `[ps=1]`
- Acquired by HP 1989; Domain workstation line wound down — Prime never effectively competed. `[ps=1]`
