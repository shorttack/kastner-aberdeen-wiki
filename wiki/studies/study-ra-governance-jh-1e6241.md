## Top observations

- Although 26% of all firms in the world are performing at best-in-class levels when it comes to managing security, less than 10% are operating governance programs that are at best-in-class levels. `[ps=5]`
- In addition, best-in-class organizations are delivering a unique Zen-like capability for their security programs: information is available to meet business needs and is being protected from negative consequences. `[ps=5]`
- Although 28% of all companies are operating security programs at best in class levels, Aberdeen research shows that less than 10% operate best-in-class security governance programs. `[ps=5]`
- More specifically, Aberdeen research shows that firms operating at best-in-class levels emphasize repeatable procedures, effective management of data and knowledge, an efficient and transparent organizational structure and strategy, and enabling automation technologies that assist with responses to business pressures. `[ps=5]`
- Furthermore, best-in-class firms place a strong emphasis on standards and policies to ensure everyone in the organization understands what’s expected of them, and the role everyone plays in improving security performance. `[ps=5]`
- Moreover, firms performing at best-in-class levels for governing security define their performance objectives and measurement metrics, continually measure themselves against these objectives, and update performance objectives to keep pace with changing business pressures. `[ps=5]`
- To achieve optimization, best-in-class firms are placing more emphasis on governance (Figure 1). `[ps=5]`
- Rather, almost all firms that are operating security programs on an ad-hoc basis are industry laggards, with financial loss rates 8 to 12 times higher than those of the organizations with best-in-class security programs. `[ps=5]`
- Moreover, the business pressures and strategic actions laggard firms take to relieve these pressures differ markedly from those in best-in-class firms. `[ps=5]`
- Best practices are also enabling best-in-class firms to reduce business cycles, respond to local market conditions more aggressively and timely, and manage their supply chains more efficiently. `[ps=5]`
- Five years ago, 50% of all firms employed multi-data-center approaches involving two, three, and sometimes four data centers spread out around the world. `[ps=5]`
- Because of the benefits — accelerating access for business purposes and minimizing downside risk from extended operations — many firms operating at best-in-class levels are implementing security governance programs that are automating testing, correlating results of testing, and analyzing results based on thresholds that exceed standards and acceptable risk levels. `[ps=5]`
- For companies operating at slightly different maturity levels, the practices being implemented for procedures, data and knowledge, the organizational structure, and enabling technologies are lower, at defined and ad-hoc levels (Figure 2). `[ps=5]`
- Aberdeen’s research clearly shows best-in-class firms are deploying and relying on more than one solution supplier and more than one enabling technology solution in each of three main areas: network security and infrastructure, information and access, and governance. `[ps=5]`
- Best-in-class firms in security governance practices are leveraging security programs for two distinct purposes: enabling business operations to function at full throttle, and mitigating business risk (Table 4). `[ps=5]`
