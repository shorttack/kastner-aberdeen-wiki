# The Procurement Outsourcing Benchmark Report

> This study analyzes The Procurement Outsourcing Benchmark Report using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2006, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Nearly 60% that outsource today are outsourcing no more than 10% of their procurement functions. `[ps=5]`
- The survey also found that Best in Class, or leading, organizations, that outsource are more likely to have a vice president or director leading a center-led procurement organization than Best in Class organizations that do _not_ outsource. `[ps=5]`
- In addition, Best in Class organizations are more likely to want to outsource more procurement functions than Industry Average and Laggard enterprises. `[ps=5]`
- And, the Best in Class are more likely to have detailed visibility into spend and knowledge of global supply markets. `[ps=5]`
- The primary reason: a perceived loss of control (Figure 1). `[ps=5]`
- • Most Best in Class enterprises that outsource procurement functions share the following characteristics: They’re led from the center by a VP or director and have better spend visibility and market knowledge than Best in Class enterprises that are _not_ planning to outsource. `[ps=5]`
- Those with more than 66% of spend controlled by the corporate procurement group and that controlled both strategic and non-strategic spend categories were placed in the **Best in Class** group. `[ps=5]`
- However, this latest survey found that Best in Class organizations that outsource are more likely to have a vice president or director leading a center-led procurement organization than Best in Class organizations that do _not_ outsource. `[ps=5]`
- But over the next two years, 27% ─ 33% of the Best in Class ─ _expect_ to access that technology, indicating the growing influence of On Demand technologies in the procurement arena. `[ps=5]`
- Most Best in Class companies have invested in “front end” technologies, such as reverse auction and e-sourcing negotiation applications; Industry Average and Laggard enterprises have done the same, though to a lesser degree. `[ps=5]`
- Headcount reduction is high on the lists of Best in Class and large organizations. `[ps=5]`
- Best in Class enterprises’ stronger emphasis on steering procurement professionals toward more strategic activities highlights their goal of using outsourcing as a way to help their procurement organizations think more strategically. `[ps=5]`
- However, most (52%) Best in Class firms have cited headcount reduction as a chief benefit from outsourcing, and it ranks as the highest expected benefit (64% of Best in Class) over the next two years. `[ps=5]`
- **Large organizations** make up most of the Best in Class (78%) in the survey pool; and `[ps=5]`
- **Large** and **Best in Class:** headcount reduction, lower transaction processing costs and fewer burdens, and pressures to refocus personnel on more strategic activities. `[ps=5]`
- Best in Class enterprises’ stronger emphasis on steering procurement professionals toward more strategic activities and on standardizing procurement procedures highlights their goal of using outsourcing as a way to help their procurement organizations think more strategically. `[ps=5]`
- In addition, Best in Class enterprises cite payment and spot buying and operational sourcing support as their most likely procurement processes to be outsourced, providing further evidence that they would favor outsourcing a tactical function that can allow their procurement organizations to focus on more strategic activities (Table 4). `[ps=5]`
- It’s also interesting to note that travel ─ a prime outsourcing target for several years ─ remains a top outsourcing priority among Industry Average and Laggard enterprises, while it doesn’t appear high on the priority lists of Best in Class companies. `[ps=5]`
- However, the amount of spend under procurement group management among organizations that are planning to outsource is a cause for concern (Figure 9). _**Aberdeen believes it critical that any enterprise looking to outsource a procurement function or process place as much spend as possible under the procurement group’s management in order to make the best informed decision on what may be a critical `[ps=5]`
- But the companies that outsource today, taken as a whole, plan to expand their outsourcing over the next year to about 21% of their procurement functions from 18% today. `[ps=4]`
- However, only 19% of enterprises that are planning to outsource have more than two-thirds of spend under procurement group management. `[ps=4]`
- Outsourcing within the procurement function is growing: Within a year, enterprises that are already outsourcing will have farmed out an average of 21% of their procurement activities, up from 18% today. `[ps=4]`
- Some 31% currently outsource some portion of their N procurement, while another 18% are planning to do so (Figure 2). `[ps=4]`
- But as Figure 3 shows, the companies that outsource today, taken as a whole, plan to expand their outsourcing over the next year to an average of about 21% of their procurement functions from 18% today. `[ps=4]`
