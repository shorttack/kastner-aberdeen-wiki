# The Supply Chain Innovator’s Technology Footprint

> This study analyzes The Supply Chain Innovator’s Technology Footprint using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2006, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- As Figure 2 shows, strivers are much more likely to focus on reducing costs with their supply chain initiatives, while innovators are more focused on delighting customers with tailored fulfillment capabilities, streamlining fulfillment across multiple channels (e.g., direct, indirect, e-commerce) and presenting a single face to the customer, and orchestrating activities across third-party manufact `[ps=5]`
- Companies prioritizing warehouse technology are looking for solutions that will let them flexibly support dynamic customer fulfillment requirements and will drive labor productivity (Figure 9). `[ps=5]`
- While cost control is still a strong desire, 72% of companies have other motivations that rank higher. `[ps=4]`
- While lowering supply chain operating costs is still important, 72% of companies have a different primary motivation for improving supply chain processes. `[ps=4]`
- While cost control is still a strong desire, 72% of companies have other motivations that rank higher, such as being better able to meet customers’ unique fulfillment requirements (Figure 1). `[ps=4]`
- Companies ranked inventory and demand management at the top of the list (Figure 4). `[ps=4]`
- Nearly three-quarters of companies looking to improve their demand management processes are prioritizing technology that supports specific product-level, customer, and channel forecasts (Figure 5). `[ps=4]`
- As Figure 7 illustrates, supply chain visibility remains the “motherhood and apple pie” goal of companies. `[ps=4]`
- However, Aberdeen estimates that fewer than 10% of all companies have robust supply chain visibility of their overseas orders and shipments. `[ps=4]`
- **Outsourced manufacturing management.** Some 54% of respondents prioritizing manufacturing improvement are seeking to enhance master scheduling across a combination of in-house and outsourced manufacturing. `[ps=4]`
- **Shop floor control.** While shop floor control scored low overall, 30% of innovators are prioritizing shop floor control that includes real-time dashboards. `[ps=4]`
- Some 60% of strivers say they lack the internal skills to use more advanced supply chain technology. `[ps=4]`
- As Figure 11 shows, innovators are about evenly split in seeking to develop supply chain innovations in partnership with a best-of-breed supply chain vendor, with their ERP vendor, or through internal technology development. `[ps=4]`
- To overcome a shortage of internal supply chain skills, 22% of respondents said that leveraging vendor staff to operate the supply chain solution would help. `[ps=4]`
- After a number of years of slowed investment in supply chain technology, respondents report that their technology budgets are now rising again but that there is no room for failure. “We are able to get funding for one part of our footprint and then we need to prove results, which creates support for the next investment step,” explains the manager of logistical systems and forecasting for Shaw Indu `[ps=3]`
- Multi-tier inventory optimization tops the priority list for companies (Figure 6). `[ps=3]`
- In addition, 57% of innovators say that RFID is a key innovation technology for their supply chain roadmap. `[ps=3]`
- Some 21% of respondents said that using on-demand options (also called “software as a service”) would help them overcome their lack of IT budget and resources. `[ps=3]`
- Companies such as Rich Products, a $2 billion food manufacturer, and Panasonic’s consumer electronics division are using managed services today from their supply chain technology vendors. `[ps=3]`
- These solutions are routinely helping companies eliminate 10%-30% of their inventory while maintaining or improving service levels. `[ps=3]`
- Constraint-based manufacturing planning and scheduling, long a mainstay of advanced planning and scheduling initiatives, remains at the top of the priority list (Figure 10). `[ps=2]`
- Fully 53% of mid-size companies plan to spend more on new supply chain technology in 2006 than they did in 2005. `[ps=0]`
- Two-thirds of large enterprises plan to spend $300,000 or more on new supply chain technology in 2006, and 35% plan to spend more than $1 million. `[ps=0]`
