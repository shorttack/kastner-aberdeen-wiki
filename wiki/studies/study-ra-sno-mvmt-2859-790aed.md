# The Service Network Optimization Benchmark Report

> This study analyzes The Service Network Optimization Benchmark Report using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 1988, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Fully 78% of best-in-class companies reported that **technology** would be a critical enabler of better service network management. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-class companies cite the need for **greater geographic service coverage** , **profitability mandates** from executives, and rising **field service costs** as the chief drivers of their service chain outsourcing activities. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 61% of companies polled in a recent Aberdeen study said field service is currently a strategic operation with revenue and profit goals in place, while another 17% said field service, while not currently a strategic operation, would be so in the future. `[ps=4]`
- Firms committed to outsourcing are proceeding with caution given that respondents indicate that only approximately 30% of their overall services functions would be outsourced over the course of the next 18 months. `[ps=4]`
- To chart and manage service network relationships and performance, 78% of best-in-class OEMs said they are assigning service oversight responsibilities to senior-level executives. `[ps=4]`
- Fully 78% of best-in-class companies also reported that technology would be a critical enabler of better service network management. `[ps=4]`
- But almost one-third of surveyed companies said they planned to leverage third-party logistics providers (3PLs) in the near future for such activities as service parts management, reverse logistics, and depot management (Figure 5). `[ps=4]`
- urveyed companies’ service network management capabilities fell into one of three maturity categories – Laggard, Industry Average, or Best in Class – based on a S weighted assessment of their performance in three metrics: SLA compliance; serviceable asset uptime; and profitability of service operation, as a percentage of service revenues. `[ps=4]`

- To capture all the potential benefits of outsourcing service, best-in-class OEMs are leveraging a broad spectrum of enabling technologies. `[ps=4]`
- Fully 89% of leading companies reported that they currently use or plan to use ERP or service management systems, while 77% indicated that they use or plan to use warranty and contract management systems to manage their service networks (Figure 9). `[ps=4]`
- Fully 89% of best-in-class companies said they are looking for specialized field service expertise from their network partners. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-class OEMs will constantly be changing their product mixes and service requirements, so be proactive in maintaining current skill sets among your field force. `[ps=4]`
- s best-in-class OEMs continue to lean harder on their service organizations for profits, revenues, and competitive advantage, they are opportunistically seeking A service chain performance spikes by outsourcing parts of their service operations. `[ps=3]`
- As 67% of best-in-class companies would agree, surveying existing customers on service quality and product performance can provide invaluable insight into opportunities to leverage service network partners for higher levels of service performance. `[ps=3]`
- ERP, service management, contract/warranty management, and call/order management systems are the technology solutions most commonly used by best-in-class companies for service network management. `[ps=3]`
- 38% of outsourcing OEMs support **asset uptimes** of greater than 95%. `[ps=0]`
- 57% of companies Aberdeen surveyed indicated that they currently outsource or plan to outsource field-service labor, ranking first among all service functions. `[ps=0]`
- 29% of firms indicate that they plan to make use of third-party logistics providers (3PLs) to outsource some aspect of the service chain, while 32% already do. `[ps=0]`
