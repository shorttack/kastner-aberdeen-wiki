# The Supplier Enablement Benchmark Report Enterprises Shift Their Procurement Activities to the Web

> This study analyzes The Supplier Enablement Benchmark Report Enterprises Shift Their Procurement Activities to the Web using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2006, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Our survey uncovered several challenges companies face in bringing new suppliers aboard, chief among them being that many suppliers lack the budget, infrastructure, or resources to support and sustain electronic enablement initiatives (Figure 2). `[ps=4]`
- • Most Best in Class enterprises mandate that suppliers adopt the buying company’s pre- `[ps=4]`
- Most Best in Class enterprises mandate that suppliers adopt the buying company’s prescribed electronic collaboration and communication methods. `[ps=4]`
- Most, in fact, continue to rely on a mix of the three predominant supplier enablement methods, even more so than in 2004 (Figure 4): `[ps=4]`
- To find which companies are leading the way in supplier enablement (i.e., the Best in Class), we asked respondents for the percentage of all transactions received or issued electronically. `[ps=4]`
- They share more information electronically with suppliers than Industry Average and Laggard firms share, especially critical transaction information such as purchase orders and payment status (Figure 5). `[ps=4]`
- Best in Class companies are also ahead of the curve on technology adoption for supplier enablement, especially in their use of catalog management tools, and EDI to support messaging, transactions, and collaboration. `[ps=4]`
- Phone, fax, and e-mail may be easiest to use, but among Industry Average and Best in Class companies, Internet-based methods of communicating and doing business with suppliers are taking hold, especially Internet-based messaging, which includes electronic data interchange (EDI-INT AS1 or AS2 protocols), EDI over secure FTP, and XML or Web services. `[ps=4]`
- While only 43% of companies in our survey (50% among Best in Class) are using Web-based supplier portals, only 18% have no plans to use them within the next two years. `[ps=4]`
- Large companies (at least $1 billion in annual revenue) are the widest users of traditional EDI, accounting for 19% of their communications with suppliers. `[ps=4]`
- In this survey, Aberdeen determined Best in Class companies by the percentage of all transactions that are issued and received electronically. `[ps=3]`
- Best in Class companies have 65% to 100% issued and received electronically; Industry Average enterprises, 6% to 44%, and Laggards, 5% or fewer. `[ps=3]`
- Companies landing in the Best in Class category (the top 20% of our respondent pool) have at least 65% of their transactions transmitted electronically, while Laggard companies (the bottom 20%) have no more than 5%. `[ps=3]`
- Organizing a supplier enablement strategy goes hand in glove with process among Best in Class companies, which take a more collaborative approach. `[ps=3]`
- Although most communication and transactions with suppliers are conducted via the offline methods ─ phone, fax, and e-mail ─ Best in Class companies lead the way in the percentage of transactions received electronically. `[ps=3]`
- In addition, some, but not most, of the Best in Class are more willing to share even some of their more sensitive information, such as manufacturing plans and release schedules (Figure 6). `[ps=3]`
- Laggards rely most heavily on phones, fax communications, and e-mail (Figure 7). `[ps=3]`
- This reinforces our view that enterprises should use more carrots instead of sticks when it comes to electronic supplier enablement (Figure 8). `[ps=3]`
- Nearly 20% of respondents say they outsource some aspect of supplier enablement, and that is expected to double within two years. `[ps=3]`
- Here are some key findings: `[ps=0]`
- All the Best in Class enterprises described in this report have had supplier enablement programs in place for at least 12 months. `[ps=0]`
- This was cited by 48% of our survey base, and by an alarming 62% from industries focusing on supply risk management. `[ps=0]`
