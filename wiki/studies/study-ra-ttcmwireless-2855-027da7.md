# The Wireless Costs and Performance Benchmark Report

> This study analyzes The Wireless Costs and Performance Benchmark Report using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


## Top observations

- An overwhelming 87% of these fleet operators say their investments in mobile technology have resulted in customer service improvements. `[ps=5]`
- Notably, though, 67% of the Best in Class update a centrally managed inventory when an employee leaves. `[ps=5]`
- Track and report on key KPIs so that management has visibility into the results of wireless cost management initiatives (Figure 7). `[ps=5]`
- Leading companies, or what Aberdeen calls the Best in Class, stand out from the others by standardizing and aligning wireless cost management across their companies, and using fully automated, company-wide systems. `[ps=4]`
- So, all told, more than 50% of companies rely on their suppliers to _tell_ them what they have, which raises the “fox in the henhouse” approach Aberdeen has alluded to in previous TTCM research. `[ps=4]`
- So, it’s no surprise that the issue of costs is the dominant pressure enterprises face in managing what they spend on wireless technology (Figure 1). `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 76% of survey respondents had at least some wireless devices whose contracts were in the employees’ names and whose monthly expenses were reimbursed via T&E. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 49% of respondents cited “employee resistance to previously unfettered choices” as one of their greatest challenges in managing wireless costs. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 37% of survey respondents say adoption of new wireless devices and services provides an ideal juncture to get processes and costs under control. `[ps=4]`
- 61% of all companies surveyed (and 75% of top-performing, or what Aberdeen calls Best in Class enterprises) have developed and implemented formal, written wireless policies; `[ps=4]`
- 37% (42% of the Best in Class) secured executive management’s support. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 46% of respondents report that they have no formal limits in place for wireless services consumption (Figure 3). `[ps=4]`
- Many Best in Class companies realize the importance of limits and enforcement – and they get tough. `[ps=4]`
- Half have formal management reporting for out-of-policy consumption, and 25% of the Best in Class deny employee reimbursement for out-of-policy usage. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 38% of respondents noted that one of their top three challenges to wireless cost management was that payment of wireless expenses via T&E reimbursement makes it hard to enforce policy and find violations. `[ps=4]`
- Best in Class companies realize the pitfalls of having wireless contracts held by employees who then claim reimbursement via T&E. `[ps=4]`
- Eighty-two percent of the Best in Class have the large majority (76% to 100%) of their wireless device contracts in the company’s name. `[ps=4]`
- Best in Class enterprises stand out from others by standardizing and aligning wireless cost management across their companies, and using fully automated, company-wide systems. `[ps=4]`
- Best in Class enterprises stand out from the rest of the survey pool in four areas: process, organization, knowledge, and technology (Table 3). `[ps=4]`

- Best in Class companies have two main characteristics here: a high degree of participation by all four “stakeholder” departments across the wireless cost management process and a clear owner to drive and coordinate across these functional areas. `[ps=4]`
- Best in Class companies clearly distinguish themselves in the area of knowledge. `[ps=4]`
- Only 8% of BiC companies identified lack of wireless industry knowledge and expertise as a problem, compared with more than 40% of Industry Average companies and Laggards. `[ps=4]`
- This is clear from the fact that 47% of respondents have total telecom cost management (TTCM) systems for landline telecom, but 21% cite their systems’ inadequacy in managing wireless spend is one of their top three challenges. `[ps=4]`
