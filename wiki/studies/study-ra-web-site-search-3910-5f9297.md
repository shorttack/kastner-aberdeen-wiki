## Top observations

- Web site search is a minimum competitive requirement for online commerce as indicated by the 96% of eCommerce web sites that currently have or will have a search tool in place in the next twenty-four months. `[ps=5]`
- The payoff in the eyes of 83% of leading online companies is a positive return on investment (ROI) from their web site search applications. `[ps=4]`
- • Search tools convert shoppers to buyers at conversion rates of 5% or higher for 60% of companies that track this metric. `[ps=4]`
- 62% of Best-in-Class companies continually fine-tune search to satisfy customers and meet their financial goals. `[ps=4]`
- Although search is a ubiquitous technology, producing accurate and relevant results remains a top pressure for 78% of survey respondents who seek to deliver search tools to enhance the customer experience. `[ps=4]`
- can be accomplished to some degree by internal Search Engine Optimization (SEO) techniques, which are used by 58% of all respondents to produce better results both internally and externally. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-Class companies follow this up by offering collaborative filtering in the form of related searches or similar products purchased by others to influence a buyer’s purchasing decision. `[ps=4]`
- Additionally, learning more about customers through analytics and personalizing the results for unique customers or customer segments ranked as top strategies for Best-in-Class merchants. `[ps=4]`
- 51% of Laggards dedicate very little time if any at all, to managing search applications. 17% of Best-in-Class have a full time employee dedicated to this task. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-Class responds by making changes based on actions derived through search analytics – following what customers do, not what they say. `[ps=4]`
- Further, only 8% of Best-in-Class neglect the data and do not use it in any way as compared to the 35% of Laggards that do not use search data to benefit their organizations. `[ps=4]`
- Currently, only 18% of Laggards are using a faceted search structure, but this is likely to change as 44% all respondents plan to implant this taxonomy in the next 12 to 24 months. `[ps=4]`
- While 41% of average and leading merchants are learning from collective behavior, predictive merchandising is currently used by 26% of Best-in-Class merchants. `[ps=4]`
- When asked about processes used to merchandise to customers, Aberdeen found that Best-in-Class companies predominately use search analytics to learn about customer be- `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-Class companies use search technologies that foster a customer centric online experience such as tolerance for mis-spelled words, thesaurus/synonyms and natural language processing. `[ps=4]`
- Although process automation is a necessary component to search tool optimization, 48% of Best-in-Class use a combined system of automation and manual adjustments. `[ps=4]`
- • Analytics derived from search are valuable. 72% of Best-in-Class use this data to improve SEO practices and 64% use analytics data to influence marketing campaigns. `[ps=4]`
