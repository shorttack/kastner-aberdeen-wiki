## Top observations

- Study participants that track more than 80% of their domestic shipments were _**twice as likely**_ as their peers to have an on-time delivery rate of 95% or higher. `[ps=4]`
- Moreover, 77% of companies seeking to improve _global_ supply chain processes say they plan to invest in improving their supply chain visibility technology over the next two years (For information on how companies plan to extend their global visibility technology, read the _Global Supply Chain Benchmark Report_ ; June 2006). `[ps=4]`
- **On-demand Software.** Also called “software as a service,” on-demand TMS solutions are being adopted by companies with freight spends that range from $3 million to $100 million and much more. `[ps=4]`
- In fact, 74% of companies say they would at least consider using an ondemand solution for their domestic TMS, and 68% say they would consider it for international transportation management ( _The Software as a Service Buyer’s Guide_ ; August, 2006). `[ps=4]`
- Enterprises with international freight spend above $15 million should be evaluating how international transportation management solutions can deliver savings and improved budget accuracy for their organizations. `[ps=4]`
- Figure 1 illustrates these companies’ top priorities. `[ps=0]`
- Visibility research conducted in July by Aberdeen shows that 44% of companies rely today on spreadsheets for at least part of their domestic shipment tracking. `[ps=0]`
- For instance, Cube Route, an on-demand fleet routing and scheduling vendor, serves clients as small as a $10 million bakery and as large as a multibillion dollar home improvement retailer. `[ps=0]`
- On-demand TMS vendor LeanLogistics, for instance, is providing managed services to P&G to help the consumer goods giant identify and take action on shipment anomalies, helping P&G increase on-time delivery on certain lanes by an average of 3 percentage points – from 94% to 97%. `[ps=0]`
