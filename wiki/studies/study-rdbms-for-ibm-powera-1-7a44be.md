# Power Academy RDBMS Sales Training

> A comprehensive RDBMS competitive intelligence training deck prepared by Aberdeen Group for IBM's Power Academy in January 1996. The document provides detailed competitive analysis of six major RDBMS vendors (Computer Associates/Ingres, IBM DB2, Informix, Oracle, Sybase, Microsoft, Progress, Red Brick, and Software AG) across dimensions including scalability, distributed technology, open technology, development tools, other technologies, and supplier value-add. Financial data, market share, strengths, weaknesses, and IBM-specific selling tips are included.


_Published 1996, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **benchmark**._


## Top observations

- $2.74 billion
- 22.2% — best in database industry
- Client-server 30% of business; remainder mainframe
- Behind Oracle, Sybase, and Informix; not much of market factor outside installed base; hard to sell as number four
- No parallel/scalable architecture; no MPP; CA sales force does not understand Unix well
- Typically bundled sell with IBM hardware/services; future: add non-IBM platforms, match independents in RDBMS technology, scale SMP to MPP SP2, better DB2 version coordination
- Perception is DB2/6000 is not world-class; must shatter perceptions of limited scalability; limited cross-platform support
- Build best-in-class image with best price/performance; make SP market leader for mission-critical/data warehousing; DB2/6000 v2 new functionality; innovative data mining query tools
- $469 million, up 33% from 1993; last 12 months at $568 million
- $4.3 billion, up from $1.5B in 1994
- Up 207% in last 52 weeks
- Third-largest RDBMS supplier; fastest growing; service revenues 25% vs Oracle 52%
- Good scalability to 8 processors on SMP; many TPC-Cs; now on SP2 and loosely-coupled HW with version 8
- No-consulting policy slowed large enterprise growth; late with desktop/workgroup; behind in replication; slow to build direct sales force
- $2.4 billion, up 40% from 1993
- $18.7 billion, up 100% from 1994
- On track for $3.6+ billion in revenues in fiscal 1996
- Confirmed — Oracle FY1997 revenue reached $5.7B, then $8.8B by FY1999; growth trajectory was accurate
- Oracle 7.3 is still performance champion; good scalability story from desktop to SP2 & ES/9000; broad gateways; tons of services
- Oracle 8 with OO technology not until 1997
- Confirmed — Oracle 8 released June 1997, consistent with the projected timeline
- RDBMS engine looks dated; no 2nd gen client-server tools; service/consulting drive makes ISV partnering tough; unfocused across Hollywood/multimedia/TV set-tops
- $826 million, a 71% increase from 1993
- $2.4 billion (July 28 1995)
- Momentum is downwards but could recover in 1996; 1980s architecture running out of steam; very high sales force turnover in 1995
