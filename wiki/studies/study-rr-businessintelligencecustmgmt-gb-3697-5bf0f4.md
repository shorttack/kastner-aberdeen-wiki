## Top observations

- Best-in-Class retailers lead the way when it comes to managing customer product interaction: 70% of these organizations track new product adoption; 100% track promotion participation; and 91% of these retailers track product preferences/affinity. `[ps=4]`
- Other top uses of BI in customer management included sales analysis, identification and targeting specific households as potential customers, and prediction of spending patterns (Figure 1). `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-Class retailers are on the cutting edge of quantifying customer behaviors: 73% of these organizations measure customer conversion, and 100% of Best-in-Class retailers measure retention and acquisition. `[ps=4]`
- • Best-in-Class retailers also lead the way when it comes to managing a customer’s product interaction: 70% of these organizations track new product adoption, 100% track promotion participation, and 91% of these retailers track product preferences/affinity. `[ps=4]`
- Lack of data management centralization was the top challenge associated with business intelligence in customer management (according to 49% of respondents), and data cleansing projects to improve data quality was the top response to existing challenges (according to 61% of respondents). `[ps=4]`
- In addition, in all three metrics, Best-in-Class were much more likely then either of the previous two groups to use such metrics in their business process. `[ps=4]`
- ## **Best in Class Keep their C-Level Executives Invested in Business Intelligence** `[ps=4]`
- In the original Aberdeen Group business intelligence benchmark report, it was reported that 58% of average performers, and 70% of Best-in-Class performers noted that their BI endeavors were driven by top management, including C-level executives. `[ps=4]`
- Indeed, Best-in-Class C-level executives may be using Corporate Performance Dashboards, in addition to and augmented by operational BI, to monitor customer activity. `[ps=4]`
- Survey respondents in each of the three ComA petitive Framework categories – Laggard, Industry Average, or Best in Class — exhibited different characteristics in five key categories: _**process**_ (consistency across the enterprise); _**organization**_ (corporate focus/philosophy, level of collaboration among stakeholders); _**knowledge**_ (visibility into and timing of results); _**technology**_  `[ps=4]`
- While Best-in-Class retailers were focused on enterprise-related BI functionality (100% of responses), a majority of both average (81%) and laggard responders (100%) focused on departmental-based BI data. `[ps=4]`
- Surprisingly, customer retention was indicated as a top success measurement only by 33% of respondents. `[ps=4]`
- Industry norm retailers must take this to heart, as only 57% of these executives use enterprisewide business intelligence (compared with a full 100% of Best-in-Class retailers). `[ps=4]`
- But for Best in Class retailers, the focus of BI should not be limited to these specific niches of the enterprise. `[ps=4]`
- Best-in-Class respondents indicated they measure customer management data at the enterprise level. `[ps=4]`
