# Safeway Damages Report: Expert Opinion on Consonus Data Center Incident

> Expert damages opinion by Aberdeen Group (Hugh Bishop with Peter Kastner) analyzing the impact of a February 2002 fire suppression system discharge at the Consonus data center on Safeway Inc.'s 40-node Teradata data warehouse system. Assesses hardware damage valuation, business interruption costs, data recovery efforts, and the strategic importance of Teradata to Safeway's retail operations.


_Published 2003, author **Hugh Bishop with Peter Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- $1.226 billion Teradata division revenue
- Approximately 700 Teradata systems installed worldwide; ~100 new systems per year
- 40-node Teradata system; grown from original 28 nodes (1997) to 34 (2000) to 40 (2002)
- $16.7 million total Teradata hardware investment ($10.2M original + $6.5M expansions)
- 25+ applications in 3 classes: Class 1 (static/ad-hoc); Class 2 (daily batch); Class 3 (ODS real-time)
- FM-200 fire suppression system discharged on February 22 2002 at Consonus Portland data center
- 144+ hours of complete system downtime following the fire suppression discharge
- 111 hours of degraded performance after initial recovery
- 359.5 person-hours of IT staff time for incident recovery
- Multiple applications required partial or complete rewrites after data corruption from the incident
- Planned application rollouts delayed by the incident — opportunity cost beyond direct damages
- Risk of permanent loss of data confidence — users may not trust data warehouse outputs after corruption event
- Risk of undetected data loss — some corrupted data may not be identified until much later
- Business interruption during recovery — grocery retail operations depend on daily/weekly data cycles
- Risk of losing NCR maintenance coverage — incident may have voided warranty or maintenance terms
- Inability to evolve the system during recovery period — platform frozen while competitors advanced
- $1,311,542.76 in direct recovery costs (labor; hardware repair/replacement; emergency services)
- $11,742,608.18 in direct replacement costs (new hardware; new software; migration; installation)
- $2,939,903.00 in business interruption damages (lost productivity; delayed projects; opportunity cost)
- $15,994,053.94 total damages claimed (direct recovery + direct replacement + business interruption)
- Safeway migrated from Informix/Sun to Teradata due to Informix performance limitations and Sun hardware constraints
- Informix acquired by IBM in 2001 for $1B — validating Safeway's decision to migrate away
- Sun Microsystems acquired by Oracle in 2010 for $7.4B — SPARC platform eventually discontinued
- Teradata spun off from NCR as independent company in 2007; became publicly traded on NYSE
- Safeway acquired by Albertsons/Cerberus Capital Management in 2015 for $9.4B
