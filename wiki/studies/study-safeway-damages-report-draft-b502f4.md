# Safeway Damages Report: Expert Opinion on Consonus Data Center Incident

> Expert damages opinion by Aberdeen Group (Hugh Bishop with Peter Kastner) analyzing the impact of a February 2002 fire suppression system discharge at the Consonus data center on Safeway Inc.'s 40-node Teradata data warehouse system. Assesses hardware damage valuation, business interruption costs, data recovery efforts, and the strategic importance of Teradata to Safeway's retail operations.


_Published 2003, author **Hugh Bishop with Peter Kastner**, type **expert-report**._


## Top observations

- Teradata spun off from NCR as independent company in 2007; became publicly traded on NYSE `[ps=5]`
- Teradata remains active as independent company in 2025; shifted to cloud (Teradata Vantage) but faces competition from Snowflake and Databricks `[ps=5]`
- Planned application rollouts delayed by the incident — opportunity cost beyond direct damages `[ps=4]`
- Risk of permanent loss of data confidence — users may not trust data warehouse outputs after corruption event `[ps=4]`
- Inability to evolve the system during recovery period — platform frozen while competitors advanced `[ps=4]`
- Sun Microsystems acquired by Oracle in 2010 for $7.4B — SPARC platform eventually discontinued `[ps=4]`
- Used Teradata equipment had limited resale market — replacement cost significantly exceeds residual value `[ps=4]`
- Safeway was building an Operational Data Store (Class 3 applications) for real-time retail data — most advanced tier of DW deployment `[ps=4]`
- Data warehouse is a strategic asset — loss or degradation would permanently impair Safeway's competitive position in grocery retail `[ps=4]`
- Damages calculated using three-tier methodology: direct recovery costs; direct replacement costs; business interruption costs — standard approach for IT disaster claims `[ps=4]`
- Expert opinion: full replacement preferred over repair due to data integrity concerns and risk of latent hardware damage from fire suppression chemicals `[ps=3]`
- $1.226 billion Teradata division revenue `[ps=0]`
- Approximately 700 Teradata systems installed worldwide; ~100 new systems per year `[ps=0]`
- $16.7 million total Teradata hardware investment ($10.2M original + $6.5M expansions) `[ps=0]`
- FM-200 fire suppression system discharged on February 22 2002 at Consonus Portland data center `[ps=0]`
- 144+ hours of complete system downtime following the fire suppression discharge `[ps=0]`
- 111 hours of degraded performance after initial recovery `[ps=0]`
- 359.5 person-hours of IT staff time for incident recovery `[ps=0]`
- Multiple applications required partial or complete rewrites after data corruption from the incident `[ps=0]`
- $1,311,542.76 in direct recovery costs (labor; hardware repair/replacement; emergency services) `[ps=0]`
- $11,742,608.18 in direct replacement costs (new hardware; new software; migration; installation) `[ps=0]`
- $2,939,903.00 in business interruption damages (lost productivity; delayed projects; opportunity cost) `[ps=0]`
- Safeway's Teradata implementation was among the more sophisticated in grocery retail — 25+ applications across 3 tiers `[ps=0]`
- Trickle feed was a custom Safeway application for real-time data loading into the ODS layer `[ps=0]`
- $1,680,000 in damages attributed to CRM S&T application — largest single application damage claim `[ps=0]`
