# SAP, Sybase Face Off

> InformationWeek news story (Jan 29 1996, Issue #564) on a feud between SAP AG and Sybase Inc. over database support for SAP's R/3 ERP system. Sybase had promised in October 1995 that SAP R/3 would support its SQL Server System 11 database, but the support does not appear imminent: Mike Regan (GM applications support, Sybase Emeryville) says SAP decides the timing; Paul Wahl (EVP international marketing, SAP Walldorf) says the ball is in Sybase's court. The dispute centers on row-level locking, which Sybase System 10/11 does not support; SAP R/3 requires it for satisfactory multi-user performance. An anonymous former Sybase source describes row-level locking as 'a deal-breaker' for SAP. Peter Kastner, VP and analyst at Aberdeen Group Inc. in Boston, comments: 'There is no doubt Sybase is missing a huge amount of business with SAP. Just look at what a great business SAP has been for Informix.'


_Published 1996, author **Doug Bartholomew, InformationWeek (Issue #564)**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- SAP R/3 ASE support eventually arrived but Sybase was never a major share-holder of the SAP database installed base. SAP customers continued to split between Oracle, IBM DB2, SQL Server, and later SAP HANA. `[ps=5]`
- SAP AG acquired Sybase in 2010 for approximately $5.8 billion, integrating Sybase ASE / IQ / Replication Server / mobile platform into SAP's product line — ironic outcome to the 1996 database-certification feud. `[ps=5]`
- There is no doubt Sybase is missing a huge amount of business with SAP. Just look at what a great business SAP has been for Informix. `[ps=4]`
- They have not proven to SAP that Sybase 11 can perform to the level we would need for R/3 to be made available on Sybase. `[ps=3]`
- Informix Software was acquired by IBM in 2001 for approximately $1 billion — consistent with Kastner"s implied view that the entire non-Oracle RDBMS tier was losing share. `[ps=3]`
- Sybase's not supporting row-level locking was a deal-breaker for SAP. There was a lot of hope at Sybase that they would be able to do some sort of patch to enable SAP to work with their database, but it did not happen.
