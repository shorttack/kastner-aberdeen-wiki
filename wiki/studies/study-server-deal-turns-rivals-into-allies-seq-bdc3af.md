# Server Deal Turns Rivals Into Allies: Sequent to Resell NCR WorldMark

> InformationWeek news (Jan 27 1997, Issue #615) on a three-year reseller agreement under which Sequent Computer Systems (Beaverton OR) will resell NCR WorldMark 4300 midrange Pentium Pro servers. Analysts estimate the deal at $50M over 12-18 months. Andre Dahan (SVP Sales/Marketing, Sequent) explains: as customers move to Baan, Oracle, and SAP applications there is a clear separation of database vs application tiers, driving demand for NT servers around Sequent's high-end boxes. Sequent had bundled Dell and Compaq PC servers previously. Mark Hurd (VP Marketing, NCR Dayton OH) — the future HP/Oracle CEO — says the 'unusual deal between traditional rivals' benefits large accounts strengthening NT infrastructure, with continued competition at high end; NCR (recently spun off by AT&T) focuses on data warehousing and high-end banking/telecom/retail transaction processing. Both vendors explicitly aim to 'beat the RISC vendors in the market' via Intel Pentium Pro and Windows NT. Peter Kastner, group VP with The Aberdeen Group (Boston), comments: 'The deal makes sense for both vendors and customers. It lets Sequent leverage R&D, while NCR gains channel opportunities for its products. There is no downside for Sequent customers because the NCR boxes do tough work in tough places. It is also an indication that Sequent is serious about a broad line of products and is not cutting corners for an increasingly commodity marketplace.' WorldMark 4300 scaled to 4 Pentium Pro CPUs with 8-CPU support targeted Q2 1997.


_Published 1997, author **Martin J. Garvey, InformationWeek (Issue #615)**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- The deal makes sense for both vendors and customers. It lets Sequent leverage R&D, while NCR gains channel opportunities for its products.
- There is no downside for Sequent customers because the NCR boxes do tough work in tough places. It is also an indication that Sequent is serious about a broad line of products and is not cutting corners for an increasingly commodity marketplace.
- We are both trying to advance the Intel architecture, and both of us are out to beat the RISC vendors in the market.
- As people move to Baan, Oracle, and SAP applications, there is a clear separation of tasks between the database and the application, causing increasing demand for NT servers around our own high-end servers.
- WorldMark 4300 scales up to 4 Pentium Pro processors at launch; NCR expected 8-processor support by second quarter 1997.
- Analysts estimate the Sequent-NCR reseller deal to be worth $50 million over 12 to 18 months.
- IBM acquired Sequent Computer Systems in July 1999 for approximately $810 million — validating Kastner"s 1997 flag that Sequent needed to broaden its product line or risk being consumed by the commodity market.
- NCR largely exited the general-purpose server-systems business by mid-2000s, refocusing on financial-services/retail ATMs and point-of-sale systems plus Teradata (spun off as independent company 2007).
- Mark Hurd rose from NCR VP Marketing (1997) to NCR CEO (2003), to HP CEO (2005-2010), to Oracle co-CEO (2014-2019). Deceased October 2019. This InformationWeek article is an early primary source on Hurd.
- By approximately 2010 Intel x86/x64 decisively displaced RISC (Sun SPARC, HP PA-RISC, DEC Alpha) in most commercial server markets; IBM POWER retained niche high-performance-computing/AIX installed base; ARM later re-entered the server market.
