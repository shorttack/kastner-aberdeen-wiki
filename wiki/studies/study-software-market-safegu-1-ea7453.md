# Software Market Dynamics

> This presentation by Peter S. Kastner, Vice President at Aberdeen Group, surveys mid-1990s software market dynamics across five domains: operating systems, database management, client-server application development, client-server application solutions, and enterprise information systems management. Kastner argues that software is evolving faster than typical enterprises can absorb change, and identifies key market transitions and investment opportunities in areas such as RDBMS growth, Windows NT ascendancy, and EISM consolidation.


_Published 1995, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- Software is changing slower than hardware advances but faster than the typical company can assimilate change
- Windows 95 will gain rapid acceptance by 1996 after a rocky start
- Confirmed — Windows 95 achieved massive adoption; became dominant consumer/business OS
- NT Advanced Server sweeps up from workgroup toward enterprise; ISVs eyeing NT as next step
- Confirmed — Windows NT (evolved to 2000/XP/Server) swept enterprise market by early 2000s
- IBM OS/2 Warp missed the window. Can never recover.
- Not confirmed — OS/2 Warp never recovered; IBM discontinued OS/2 in 2001
- Mature market; variants capable of single enterprise-size applications
- Old proprietary OS (MVS, VMS) do not matter for new platform decisions
- 9 RDBMS licenses for every 10 multiuser Unix servers sold
- Relational DBMS is hot-hot-hot; existing players get richer
- Pure ODBMS window has closed — relegated to a niche
- Confirmed — OODBMS remained niche; object-relational features absorbed into mainstream RDBMS (PostgreSQL, Oracle, DB2)
- Data warehousing is reaching mainstream
- Gateways to data sources vs. ODBC as competing approaches
- Client-server will evolve to a distributed computing model by year 2000
- Confirmed — client-server evolved to n-tier, web, SOA, then cloud/microservices
- Next big market move is object-oriented development; products in place but ORB infrastructure missing
- OO development will be very hard for IS to learn
- Barriers to entry for Financials, HR, and high-end manufacturing (SAP) are huge
- 1995 sees mainstream availability of wide variety of C-S vertical apps
- Windows NT is now a common option for C-S application solutions
- EISM consolidates: network management, systems management, operations management, database management
- Distributed systems are rapidly causing administrative chaos; huge interest in alleviating IS pain
- Software integration services compensate for IS inability to choose/implement
