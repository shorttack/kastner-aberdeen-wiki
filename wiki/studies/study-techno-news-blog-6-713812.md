# PC Buying Guide Guest Column: 'Take Her Up to 2.4 GHz, Mr. Scott'

> Ray Schroeder's Techno-News Blog (Jan 7 2003) reblog of Peter Kastner's InternetWeek PC buying-guide guest column titled 'Take Her Up to 2.4 GHz, Mr. Scott.' Kastner, writing in his InternetWeek column voice, argues that the 2.0-GHz Pentium 4 has been the corporate-mainstream standard for the past three months but is now being displaced: Dell, HP, and IBM have added 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 to their single-price lower-Pentium-4 price tier, meaning corporate IT managers can order a desktop with a 1.8-, 2.0-, 2.2-, or 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 for the exact same price at checkout. Classic Kastner practitioner-focused PC-procurement advice: take the faster chip, it's free. The Techno-News Blog is the preservation channel — Ray Schroeder (University of Illinois at Springfield OTEL editor) reblogged the Kastner column with attribution and source reference.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner, InternetWeek (reblogged by Ray Schroeder, Techno-News Blog, University of Illinois at Springfield)**, type **authored-column**._


## Top observations

- For the past three months, a 2.0-GHz Pentium 4 microprocessor has been considered the standard for the corporate mainstream desktop, representing the best value in performance and price for the vast majority of enterprise workers. - InternetWeek was shuttered by CMP Media in 2003 as part of a consolidation of CMP's enterprise-IT publications around InformationWeek and related brands — the original URL for this Kastner column no longer resolves; the Techno-News Blog reblog is one of the few preserved copies.
- Intel released Core 2 Duo (Conroe) in July 2006, ending the Pentium 4 NetBurst era Kastner was writing in; clock-speed-as-primary-spec gave way to multi-core performance metrics, ending the simple 'take the faster Pentium 4 for free' procurement logic this column exemplified.
