# AMD's Semprons Spare Athlon from Low-End Market

> TechNewsWorld news article (Jun 8 2004, 10:24 AM PT) by Jay Lyman on AMD's launch of the Sempron CPU brand to replace Athlon XP in the low-end desktop market — freeing the Athlon brand to compete exclusively in the mainstream/premium segment. Peter Kastner, chief research officer at Aberdeen Group, provides the global-market framing: 'There might not be increasing budget market demand in the United States, but in other parts of the world such as Thailand or Malaysia, the so-called white-box market represents 40 percent of available sales... To compete with the very low-end systems, AMD needs a mature and inexpensive processor because cost is more important in that market.' Insight64 analyst Dean McCarron also comments on AMD's split-branding strategy to clearly separate mainstream-willing-to-pay-more from price-sensitive low-cost markets.


_Published 2004, author **Jay Lyman, TechNewsWorld**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- AMD launched the Sempron CPU brand July 2004 (one month after this article's publication), placing Sempron in the low-end segment previously held by Athlon XP and freeing the Athlon brand to compete in mainstream/premium alongside Athlon 64. `[ps=5]`
- There might not be increasing budget market demand in the United States, but in other parts of the world such as Thailand or Malaysia, the so-called white-box market represents 40 percent of available sales. `[ps=4]`
- To compete with the very low-end systems, AMD needs a mature and inexpensive processor because cost is more important in that market. `[ps=4]`
- Insight 64 analyst Dean McCarron frames AMD's split-branding strategy as clearly separating the mainstream segment where people are willing to pay more from the low-cost market where products are purchased on price. `[ps=4]`
- AMD gradually retired the Sempron brand through 2013, replaced by Athlon low-end and later Ryzen 3; the Sempron-vs-Celeron low-end duopoly Kastner described ended as the overall low-end-desktop segment shrank. `[ps=4]`
