# Apple's Power Mac G5 Hits the Street

> TechNewsWorld article (Aug 18 2003, Jay Lyman) on Apple's shipment of the Power Mac G5 — the first personal computer with 64-bit processing and a 1-GHz front-side bus, billed as 'world's fastest personal computer.' IBM-Apple PowerPC G5 (PowerPC 970). Configurations: 1.6 GHz at $2,000, 1.8 GHz at $2,400, dual 2.0 GHz at $3,000. Apple reports 100,000+ pre-orders since June 23 introduction. Aberdeen chief research officer Peter Kastner provides two of the article's most-quoted lines: 'For a new chip, this is about as good as it gets. The performance improvements are so evident that only a few minutes in the store demo area will convince many buyers that it's time to upgrade.' And: 'The G5 is a hot box and one which for the first time in several years gives Mac owners some bragging rights versus their industry-standard brethren.' Kastner calls the G5 'better, faster, cheaper, smaller' for Apple and predicts graphics designers will see vast improvements from 64-bit-optimized code. IDC's Alan Promisel: 'It's definitely an architecture the commercial IT managers are keeping an eye on.'


_Published 2003, author **Jay Lyman, TechNewsWorld**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- For a new chip, this is about as good as it gets. The performance improvements are so evident that only a few minutes in the store demo area will convince many buyers that it's time to upgrade.
- The G5 is a hot box and one which for the first time in several years gives Mac owners some bragging rights versus their industry-standard brethren.
- The G5 processor, after months and years of speculation and anticipation, finally delivers a better, faster, cheaper, smaller solution for Apple.
- Apple Power Mac G5 models: 1.6 GHz (256 MB 333 MHz DDR, $2,000), 1.8 GHz (512 MB 400 MHz DDR, $2,400), dual 2.0 GHz ($3,000 later this month). First PC with 64-bit processing and 1 GHz front-side bus; 6.4 GB/s memory bandwidth; 16 GB/s in dual. 100,000+ pre-orders since Jun 23 introduction.
- IDC's Alan Promisel: 'While the G5 is aimed at Apple's core audience — high-end users, gamers and audio-visual producers — the 64-bit desktop computer is being closely watched. It's definitely an architecture the commercial IT managers are keeping an eye on.'
- Graphics designers and image manipulators will see vast improvements in their ability to apply filters because the code has been optimized for 64-bit computation. Customers in search of simulation as close to reality as possible will be willing to pay the G5's higher prices.
- IBM failed to deliver a 3 GHz PowerPC G5 within 12 months as promised at the Jun 2003 introduction; G5 topped out at 2.7 GHz desktop (2005) and never shipped in laptop form due to heat/power — directly prompting Steve Jobs' WWDC Jun 2005 Intel-transition announcement.
- Apple completed PowerPC-to-Intel transition by Aug 2006 (32 months after this article). Mac Pro with Intel Xeon replaced Power Mac G5 Aug 2006; partial validation of Kastner's 2003 enthusiasm (G5 did deliver the 2003 performance bump) but his 'bragging rights for the first time in several years' proved fleeting.
