# Sun Unveils UltraSPARC IV Processor

> TechNewsWorld article (Oct 13 2003, Jay Lyman) reporting Sun Microsystems' UltraSPARC IV unveiling at the 16th annual Microprocessor Forum in San Jose. The dual-threaded chip (two UltraSPARC III cores, on-chip memory controller supporting 16 GB DRAM, 8 MB L2 tags per core) was positioned as Sun's 'Throughput Computing' answer to Intel Itanium and IBM Power. Aberdeen Group research director Peter Kastner is a central skeptical-but-defensive voice: 'To Sun's installed base, UltraSPARC IV has to be competitive. It doesn't have to be world-beating,' and it would be 'foolish for Sun to exit processors' given 20 years of investment. Gartner VP Martin Reynolds argues the opposite — that Sun should exit chips. Harlan McGhan (Sun) previews 90nm with Texas Instruments and an 8-core/32-thread future chip.


_Published 2003, author **Jay Lyman, TechNewsWorld**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- To Sun's installed base, UltraSPARC IV has to be competitive. It doesn't have to be world-beating.
- Sun's customers want UltraSPARC IV to be successful so they can continue to feel comfortable investing in Sun and in Sun's real strength, which is Solaris.
- The big picture is Sun has more to gain at this time by delivering a world-beating UltraSPARC IV than by abandoning 20 years of work in chip research.
- Certainly, the message is Sun should reconsider what it's doing with processors. Sun's value doesn't lie in the processor anymore. It lies in the robust systems and infrastructure they're capable of providing.
- 2x performance via multithreading (two UltraSPARC III cores on single die)
- more radical chip multithreading design by 2006, which may increase throughput of today's UltraSPARC IIIi by as many as 15 times
- Sun UltraSPARC T1 (Niagara) shipped Nov 2005 with 8 cores, 4 threads/core = 32 threads; design target partially met a year earlier than predicted
- begin shipping in systems during the first half of 2004
- praised the additional cache Sun built into the new UltraSPARC IV, Reynolds said Sun's multithreading technology is still untested in practice
- Intel's Xeon processor is taking a toll on all competitors; the large server market where Sun has historically played well is suffering from the increasing share of lower-cost servers
- a processor with eight cores on a single-chip die. That's a single chip that can actually execute 32 threads in parallel
- Oracle confirmed SPARC M8 (2017) as its final SPARC CPU and canceled its M8 successor; effectively ended SPARC roadmap
- Oracle completed acquisition of Sun Microsystems for $7.4B on 2010-01-27
