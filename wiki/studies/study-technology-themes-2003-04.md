# IT Technology Themes and Trends

> A collection of Aberdeen Group research themes and trends for 2003-2004 authored by Peter S. Kastner, covering twelve strategic IT topics including IT deflation and per-processor software licensing, tiered storage and information lifecycle management, consumer empowerment via Internet, the extinction of best-of-breed IT, Sarbanes-Oxley compliance costs, and real-time consumer tracking. Each theme is framed as an Aberdeen research offering with a pithy headline and analytical summary. The document serves as both a market intelligence snapshot and a business development vehicle for Aberdeen consulting engagements.


_Published 2003, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- Software licensing shifted from per-processor to subscription/SaaS driven partly by server consolidation via virtualization; VMware's virtual CPU model further disrupted per-processor licensing `[ps=5]`
- Savvy internet marketers tracking every click; real-time analytics programs sifting marketing wheat from chaff; anonymous web behavior predicting real-world behavior `[ps=5]`
- SOX compliance drove massive IT investment in financial controls GRC systems and audit management software; spawned entire software category `[ps=5]`
- RFID adopted in logistics and warehouse management (Wal-Mart mandate 2005) but consumer retail tracking happened via web/mobile not physical RFID tags `[ps=5]`
- VMware ESX/vSphere drove dramatic server consolidation 2006-2015; server count reduced while capacity grew; per-processor licensing crisis intensified as Kastner predicted `[ps=5]`
- Consumers demanding power to do it themselves specifically in-store checkout; kiosks near point-of-sale and information on demand `[ps=5]`
- Enterprise storage decisions once simple (GB per disk); now complicated by compliance new data types and ILM policy-based tiers `[ps=4]`
- New thinking about pools of storage controlled by ILM policy opening doors to lower-cost storage devices and architectures `[ps=4]`
- Tiered storage became universal; ILM embedded in all major storage platforms (NetApp EMC IBM); cloud tiering (AWS S3 Glacier) extended the model `[ps=4]`
- 30% annual server technology deflation; today's servers have fewer processors than predecessors creating real problem for per-processor software licenses `[ps=4]`
- At least 1/3 of US population considers themselves IT savvy; another 1/3 at least competent — driving retail IT spending on automation CRM and analytics `[ps=4]`
- Automated replenishment RFID tags new process designs enhanced parcel tracking partially or totally attributed to rise of modern consumer `[ps=4]`
- Billions invested in enterprise management for inward-facing value chain; extended value chains complicate partner management; new technologies allow partner-system management without impinging on partner prerogatives `[ps=4]`
- IT buyers realizing not every data byte has same value to enterprise and that value changes over time — core ILM thesis `[ps=4]`
- Great ROI only relevant if technology addresses executive's top 3-5 pain points; executive committee demands high-ROI projects implementing in weeks not months `[ps=4]`
- Largest firms own dozens of redundant overlapping software pieces; smart CIOs/CFOs rationalizing IT purchases; remote data centers being consolidated `[ps=4]`
- Loyalty must be bought repeatedly; requires opt-in customer affinity programs backed by decision-support-backed promotion systems `[ps=4]`
- Not long ago multi-billion dollar technology club contained just a handful of firms; newer colossal firms possess astronomical R&D budgets dwarfing competitors `[ps=4]`
- Compliance costs so corrosive to earnings that some firms reverting to privately held status `[ps=4]`
- AWS S3/Glacier Intelligent-Tiering Azure blob tiers Google Nearline extended Kastner's pools-of-storage model to cloud; ILM policies now automated by ML `[ps=4]`
- CIOs see cost of integrating best-of-breed applications from different vendors as more expensive than single-vendor; CFOs prefer pre-integrated; colossal firms with astronomical R&D budgets dominating `[ps=3]`
- Oracle acquired PeopleSoft (2004) Siebel (2005) BEA (2008) Sun (2010); SAP acquired Business Objects Sybase Ariba; Salesforce became mega-vendor; best-of-breed largely absorbed or marginalized `[ps=3]`
- WiFi scares executives fearful of security support and high initial cost; fewer than 30% corporate laptops; corridor cruisers the target use case `[ps=3]`
- WiFi became ubiquitous in enterprise; security concerns resolved via WPA2/802.1X; laptop penetration exceeded 50% by 2010 driven by tablet and smartphone co-adoption `[ps=3]`
- Minimum several hundred thousand to several million dollars annually per affected business `[ps=0]`
