# The Business Value in IT Outsourcing

> Aberdeen research brief announcing a benchmark study on IT outsourcing business value, to be published late June 2006. Identifies five key drivers for outsourcing (core competency focus, cost savings, resource constraints, time-to-market, culture wars); describes a maturity model for outsourcing outcomes; classifies enterprises into three tiers: misaligned (~30%), sub-optimal (50-67%), and optimal (5-10%) outsourcing engagements. Author is Peter Kastner, VP Enterprise Integration Research.


_Published 2006, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **white-paper**._


## Top observations

- 1. Core competency focus; 2. Cost savings; 3. IT resource constraints; 4. Time-to-market; 5. Culture wars
- Many enterprises have never done a serious make-or-buy outsourcing decision on any IT segment
- Up to 30% of IT outsourcing engagements
- 50-67% of IT outsourcing contracts running at sub-optimal levels
- 5-10% of multi-million dollar IT outsourcing agreements driving all anticipated business value
- 56% measure ROI after every application change
- 25% measure after each upgrade; 25% sometimes; 50% never
- 0% after each upgrade; 10% sometimes; 20%+ never
- Over 2,000 companies surveyed on IT in past two years
- Poor scope understanding; inaccurate internal metrics; business changes over time away from contract terms
- Cannot name a single company that outsourced IT without learning painful lessons
- SLAs, Governance, Preparation, Transition, Operations, Change Management, Pricing, Conflict Resolution, Metrics
- Misjudging ability to manage outsourcer; poor SLA metrics; confusing low cost with value; inflexible contract language
- Global 5000 companies across all industries, all sizes
- 50-67% of contracts sub-optimal; only 5-10% fully optimal
- [UNVERIFIED]
- Ranges from simple app outsourcing (e-mail) to full BPO of major processes (general ledger, accounts payable)
- High; prior research indicates widespread dissatisfaction with outsourcing
