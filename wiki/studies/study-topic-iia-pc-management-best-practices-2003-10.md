# PC Management Best Practices: A Study of the Total Cost of Operation, Risk, Security, and Audit

> IIA Research Foundation rough-draft report (Oct 7, 2003) authored by Mark Salamasick (University of Texas at Dallas) and Charles Le Grand (IIA AVP Technology Practices). Based on 221 GAIN flash-survey responses plus the July 2003 IIA Technology Audit Forum where Peter Kastner of Aberdeen Group was keynote speaker. Organized around the COSO Internal Control Integrated Framework and SAC model; covers business risk and insurance, Sarbanes-Oxley/HIPAA/GLBA compliance, information security and appropriate use, business continuity, software management, and PC TCO. Cites Kastner's research extensively on PC fleet TCO ($8k-$9k per seat), 20-25% cost reduction through best practices, 70-80% of TCO in operations, 40 million desktops running outdated OSes, and standardized images.


_Published 2003, author **Mark Salamasick, Charles Le Grand**, type **topic-analysis**._


## Top observations

- 221 responses as of 2003-07-16
- 2003-07-24 through 2003-07-25
- 8000-9000 TCO per system typical enterprise
- 20-25% cost reduction from PC best practices
- 70-80% of PC TCO in operations; only 20-30% in acquisition/deployment
- $4000 per user annual savings (~50% reduction) from automated PC mgmt tools
- 40 million+ desktops running outdated operating systems
- Microsoft ending Windows 98 support in 2003
- Microsoft ending Windows NT 4.0 support in 2003
- 93% respondents have overall security policy for PCs
- only 33% considered policies/practices tightly controlled
- less than 30% believed organization employed virus-protection best practices
- only 21% had performed wireless cost analysis
- only 30% addressed wireless in PC policy/procedures
- only 58% automatically deliver software patches
- 85% deliver patches as needed rather than on fixed schedule
- over 80% include PC in overall enterprise risk assessment
- approximately 50% differentiate PC risk by applications/processes
- more than 50% replace PCs 'as needed' or on 4+ year cycle
- 32% replace OS 'as needed'; 54% have no OS upgrade criteria
- 40% back up PC data in central location
- 47% have full image backed up for PC recovery
- 92% have controls to enforce corporate standards; only 8% tightly enforced
- 76,404 security incidents reported first half 2003 (vs 82,094 total 2002)
- 56% increase 2001 to 2002
