# Translating Early Skills and Experiences into Marketing and Industry Analysis

> Peter S. Kastner traces his professional arc from Machine Architect (1969–1979) through Market Planner (1979–1988) to Industry Strategist at Aberdeen Group (1988–2007), arguing that each phase translated technical 'scars' into progressively higher-level analytical tools. The memoir identifies the forensic foundation, capitalist's perspective, and 'Simple Physics' mindset as the three compounding assets that enabled cross-industry prediction at scale.


_Published 2025, author **Peter S. Kastner**, type **memoir**._


## Top observations

- Trajectory 1969-2007 described as continuous evolution from 'Machine Architect' to 'Industry Strategist'; technical scars of mainframe era became predictive tools of analyst years
- Machine time at $360/hour; far more valuable than human labor; every bit and cycle had to be justified; drove mastery of I/O optimization and dump reading
- Ability to identify system 'thrashing' or 'Unmitigated Software Mess' nine months before failure; honed at ADL and Chase Manhattan
- Designing 'bicycle-powered' systems in Rio and rural 5-baud networks in Wisconsin respecting physical and economic constraints
- At Prime Computer, technical mastery translated into Market Planning and strategic 'Go/No-Go' Logic; shift from fixing one engine to planning entire factory
- Years of trading Wang stock options and riding market cycles informed transition from technical work to market planning
- ADL fixed-price turnkey failures became primary asset; understanding liability of unrecognized accruals and OEM contract 'billback' traps enabled creation of flexible software pricing models
- Intuition of Moore's Law enabled move toward distributed departmental minicomputers like Prime 750 just as mainframe's price/performance monopoly was collapsing
- 'Simple Physics' mindset used to judge entire industry; engineering baseline from 1969 provided clear view of which architectures would genuinely scale vs 'making it up as they go'
- Ability to detect 'Unmitigated Software Messes' at corporate level; analyzing company health through technical 'Order and Harmony' not just spreadsheets
- $12 million litigation at USM/Emhart referenced as formative example of forensic auditing applied at Aberdeen Group level
- Houghton Mifflin environment referenced as formative example of reading corporate health signals
- Knowing more than 'locals' due to breadth of cross-industry auditing; COBOL and Assembler background turned into predictive tool for global technology market
- Learning from Sandy Kurzig at ASK; more flexible, profitable software pricing models derived from understanding OEM contract risks
- Technical ability to intuit Moore's Law as tool for strategic technology timing decisions; informed distributed computing move
- Mastery of COBOL and Assembler; I/O optimization; dump reading in raw hexadecimal; foundation that later became industry prediction tool
