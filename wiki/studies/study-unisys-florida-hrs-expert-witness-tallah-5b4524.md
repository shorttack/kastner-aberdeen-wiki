> Peter S. Kastner recalls his expert-witness role in the Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS) bid protest — Unisys vs. IBM/EDS — in the early 1990s. Kastner went through a box of computer paper to find the single IBM performance estimate sheet that proved the proposed mainframe plus DB2 architecture could not support the required terminal counts and average response time, and testified in the Florida Senate Chamber in Tallahassee. Although Unisys lost the bid, the IBM/EDS implementation failed catastrophically — a $200 million fiasco involving system crashes during food stamp distribution, national records for error rates, and EDS suing for $46 million in unpaid bills. The fallout helped motivate Congress to pass two pieces of Senator William Cohen's IT-procurement reform legislation — the Information Technology Management Reform Act and the Federal Acquisition Reform Act (jointly the Clinger-Cohen Act of 1996) — which remain in force today. era: 1990s; mood: reflective; trigger: Perplexity research session January 2026 on government IT procurement disasters

- Congress eventually passed two pieces of Cohen legislation that stand to this day `[ps=5]`
- Combines Information Technology Management Reform Act and Federal Acquisition Reform Act; sponsored by Sen. William Cohen (R-ME) and Rep. William Clinger (R-PA); established CIO requirements, capital planning, performance-based IT management; still in force 2026 `[ps=5]`
- FBI Virtual Case File abandoned April 2005 after $170 million spent; 318-page Aerospace Corp report found system incomplete, inadequate, essentially unusable under real-world conditions `[ps=5]`
- Government procurement relied almost exclusively on waterfall development methods; requirements frozen before development began; catastrophic for software in rapidly changing technology environments `[ps=5]`
- Established CIO requirements, capital planning, and performance-based management in federal IT; active 30 years after passage `[ps=5]`
- Response times eventually improved to 3-4 seconds after heroic effort and schedule changes — still slow for interactive casework `[ps=4]`
- IBM/EDS strategy leaned on 'no one ever got fired for buying IBM' reputation; promised mainframe power and DB2 maturity could handle volume `[ps=4]`
- FAILED: response times initially in minutes; system crashed during food stamp distribution; CPU at 95-100% capacity; national food stamp error records set `[ps=4]`
- IBM posted losses exceeding $8 billion in 1993 as mainframe-centric business model collapsed `[ps=4]`
- Denver DIA fully automated baggage system: 16-month opening delay, approximately $600 million cost; opened February 1995 using manual baggage handling instead `[ps=4]`

- The 'no one ever got fired for buying IBM' safe-bet narrative was becoming dangerously obsolete advice as IBM architectural strengths increasingly misaligned with emerging requirements `[ps=4]`
