# Unisys vs. Florida HRS — Expert Witness Memoir (Senate Chamber Tallahassee, c.1990-1991)

> Peter S. Kastner recalls his expert-witness role in the Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS) bid protest — Unisys vs. IBM/EDS — in the early 1990s. Kastner went through a box of computer paper to find the single IBM performance estimate sheet that proved the proposed mainframe plus DB2 architecture could not support the required terminal counts and average response time, and testified in the Florida Senate Chamber in Tallahassee. Although Unisys lost the bid, the IBM/EDS implementation failed catastrophically — a $200 million fiasco involving system crashes during food stamp distribution, national records for error rates, and EDS suing for $46 million in unpaid bills. The fallout helped motivate Congress to pass two pieces of Senator William Cohen's IT-procurement reform legislation — the Information Technology Management Reform Act and the Federal Acquisition Reform Act (jointly the Clinger-Cohen Act of 1996) — which remain in force today. era: 1990s; mood: reflective; trigger: Perplexity research session January 2026 on government IT procurement disasters

## Top observations

- Testified as expert witness in a hearing in the Florida Senate Chamber, Tallahassee
- Unisys lost the bid despite Kastner's testimony
- IBM and Unisys both failed miserably in the $200M fiasco (Kastner's characterization)
- Congress eventually passed two pieces of Cohen legislation that stand to this day
- Tipped over the dominoes — Kastner's own metaphor for his role in setting the cascade in motion
- $104 million contract to Electronic Data Systems (EDS) Federal Corp. in 1989 to design and maintain the FLORIDA System welfare computer
- Touted as first of its kind in the world; massive centralized computer system to determine eligibility and issue welfare benefits across entire state
- By 1990 project scope changed dramatically to a massive centralized system operated out of Tallahassee, creating the architectural bottleneck
- System crashed during the April 1992 food stamp distribution cycle
- System crashed again in June 1992 after HRS took over operations from EDS
- System response times initially measured in minutes — completely unacceptable for interactive welfare processing
- Florida set national records for error rates in issuing food stamps; more than $200 million in food stamp errors including overpayments and underpayments
- 200,000 ineligible Medicaid recipients on state rolls; $28 million in improper Medicaid payments
- EDS sued Florida HRS for $46 million in unpaid bills; HRS countersued that EDS had failed to deliver
- By fall 1994 CPU was operating at 95-100% capacity during business hours vs. industry average of 65%; staff worked nights and Saturdays to reduce load
- Response times eventually improved to 3-4 seconds after heroic effort and schedule changes — still slow for interactive casework
- Two former HRS officials indicted in 1993
- Unisys proposed more distributed open architecture using BNA and DCA peer-to-peer systems arguing centralized DB2 processing would create bottleneck effect in state-wide distributed network
- IBM/EDS strategy leaned on 'no one ever got fired for buying IBM' reputation; promised mainframe power and DB2 maturity could handle volume
- FAILED: response times initially in minutes; system crashed during food stamp distribution; CPU at 95-100% capacity; national food stamp error records set
- IBM performance estimate sheet proved mainframe + DB2 architecture could not support required terminal counts and average response time
- CORRECT: response times in minutes initially; CPU at 95-100%; system crashed during food stamp distribution — exactly the failure mode Kastner predicted
- Unisys formed from 1986 merger of Burroughs Corporation and Sperry UNIVAC
