# Unisys vs. Florida HRS — Expert Witness Memoir (Senate Chamber Tallahassee, c.1990-1991)

> Peter S. Kastner recalls his expert-witness role in the Florida Department of Health and Rehabilitative Services (HRS) bid protest — Unisys vs. IBM/EDS — in the early 1990s. Kastner went through a box of computer paper to find the single IBM performance estimate sheet that proved the proposed mainframe plus DB2 architecture could not support the required terminal counts and average response time, and testified in the Florida Senate Chamber in Tallahassee. Although Unisys lost the bid, the IBM/EDS implementation failed catastrophically — a $200 million fiasco involving system crashes during food stamp distribution, national records for error rates, and EDS suing for $46 million in unpaid bills. The fallout helped motivate Congress to pass two pieces of Senator William Cohen's IT-procurement reform legislation — the Information Technology Management Reform Act and the Federal Acquisition Reform Act (jointly the Clinger-Cohen Act of 1996) — which remain in force today. era: 1990s; mood: reflective; trigger: Perplexity research session January 2026 on government IT procurement disasters

## Top observations

- Combines Information Technology Management Reform Act and Federal Acquisition Reform Act; sponsored by Sen. William Cohen (R-ME) and Rep. William Clinger (R-PA); established CIO requirements, capital planning, performance-based IT management; still in force 2026 `[ps=5]`
- Government procurement relied almost exclusively on waterfall development methods; requirements frozen before development began; catastrophic for software in rapidly changing technology environments `[ps=5]`
- Established CIO requirements, capital planning, and performance-based management in federal IT; active 30 years after passage `[ps=5]`
- Response times eventually improved to 3-4 seconds after heroic effort and schedule changes — still slow for interactive casework `[ps=4]`
- IBM posted losses exceeding $8 billion in 1993 as mainframe-centric business model collapsed `[ps=4]`
- Empirical benchmarking; architecture-suitability-for-use-case; total cost of ownership (TCO); vendor lock-in risk assessment `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen focused on technical architecture evaluations rather than market positioning; Gartner did Magic Quadrant; IDC did market share statistics; Aberdeen did deep-dive technical assessments `[ps=4]`
- Conflict between traditional mainframe architectures and emerging demands of distributed high-performance systems in early 1990s; state stuck with underperforming system because switching mid-stream is politically and financially devastating `[ps=4]`
- System response times initially measured in minutes — completely unacceptable for interactive welfare processing `[ps=3]`
- Unisys proposed more distributed open architecture using BNA and DCA peer-to-peer systems arguing centralized DB2 processing would create bottleneck effect in state-wide distributed network `[ps=3]`
- FoxMeyer Drug ($5B pharmaceutical distributor) went bankrupt 1996 after SAP R/3 ERP implementation; SAP designed for manufacturers not wholesalers; system processed only 10,000 orders/night vs 420,000 on mainframe `[ps=3]`
- Every transaction in centralized architecture incurs: network round-trip to Tallahassee + mainframe processing time + DB2 query processing + return network transmission `[ps=3]`
- Whether single mainframe in Tallahassee could scale to handle peak load across 1000+ sites simultaneously was a fundamental red flag in the IBM/EDS proposal `[ps=2]`
- Went through a box of computer paper to find the single IBM performance estimate sheet that proved the mainframe + DB2 architecture could not support the required terminal counts and average response time `[ps=0]`
- Unisys lost the bid despite Kastner's testimony `[ps=0]`
- IBM and Unisys both failed miserably in the $200M fiasco (Kastner's characterization) `[ps=0]`
- Tipped over the dominoes — Kastner's own metaphor for his role in setting the cascade in motion `[ps=0]`
- By 1990 project scope changed dramatically to a massive centralized system operated out of Tallahassee, creating the architectural bottleneck `[ps=0]`
- System crashed during the April 1992 food stamp distribution cycle `[ps=0]`
- Florida set national records for error rates in issuing food stamps; more than $200 million in food stamp errors including overpayments and underpayments `[ps=0]`
- 200,000 ineligible Medicaid recipients on state rolls; $28 million in improper Medicaid payments `[ps=0]`
- EDS sued Florida HRS for $46 million in unpaid bills; HRS countersued that EDS had failed to deliver `[ps=0]`
- By fall 1994 CPU was operating at 95-100% capacity during business hours vs. industry average of 65%; staff worked nights and Saturdays to reduce load `[ps=0]`
