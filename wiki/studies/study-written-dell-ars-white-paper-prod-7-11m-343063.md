## Top observations

- Old IT equipment can’t be thrown in the dumpster any longer; it must be properly disposed of, at a cost of $387 and up per PC if you do it yourself. `[ps=4]`
- We estimate disposal costs at over 25% of the initial PC acquisition cost and climbing. `[ps=4]`
- Organizations that dispose of 3,000 PCs or other IT devices annually can save more than $1 million a year in unnecessary storage and disposal costs with an asset recovery program. `[ps=4]`
- The critical business process change that can literally be worth $500 to $800 per IT asset is simple: identify and retire underutilized IT assets. `[ps=4]`
- Without a proper plan, companies will spend $1.9 million annually to dispose of 5,000 IT devices and still expose IT to public scrutiny by not recycling. `[ps=4]`
- The disposal plans discussed above can save an enterprise more than $1 million to $10 million annually while making your organization a poster child of environmental concern. `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen has determined that the costs for cleansing disks can range from $185 to a low of $16, with the low estimate based on a significant capital investment to automate the process and support parallel processing, a combination cost justified only with large volumes like those attained by Dell. `[ps=3]`
- A large enterprise will pay approximately $126 per drive to do it. `[ps=3]`
- Anything left over is broken up and refined back into a raw material and sold on the commodities market or directly back to manufacturers (see Figure 1). `[ps=3]`
- Add $30 for the packing and moving plus the use of the Dell ARS service costs the enterprise a total of $79. `[ps=3]`
- A service such as Dell’s ARS saves $1.54 million and eliminates an e-waste headache. `[ps=3]`
- For $49 per unit, Dell will pick up and recycle the e-waste, regardless of manufacturer. `[ps=3]`
- Doing disk cleansing is a major expense at an average cost of $126 per disk. `[ps=3]`
- Storage and market value depreciation of a 36-month old Pentium III PC for one year costs about $368. `[ps=2]`
- Putting this in terms of purchase price, a typical enterprise PC and monitor costs $1,200, so the $387 disposal cost represents 32% of the original cost — a percentage that only increases rapidly as organizations continue to drive the cost of new PCs towards $1,000. `[ps=2]`
- ## _Identity cleansing $2_ `[ps=0]`
- ## _Data cleansing $126_ `[ps=0]`
- ## _Packing/moving $132_ `[ps=0]`
- When a PC unit, including the CRT, is packed and shipped to a regional office for processing and then packed and shipped again to a disposal partner, Aberdeen estimates the cost for labor, transportation, and material to be as high as $198. `[ps=0]`
- If multiple assets intended for recycling are placed on a pallet and shrink wrapped for pick-up by the disposal partner — the mechanism recommended for Dell ARS customers — the cost could be as low as $12 per device. `[ps=0]`
- The average cost is $132 per unit for do-it-yourself, which covers material and handling associated with moving twenty PCs and monitors with four printers from multiple cubes to a processing area where they are packed, and then trucked to a regional recycler. `[ps=0]`
- The challenge for smaller recyclers is made clear in Figure 1. `[ps=0]`
- Depending on the scale and management of the operation, refurbishment and shipping may be as low as $28 and high as $67 per device. `[ps=0]`
- Compare these $28 to $67 costs to the whole Dell ARS program, which charges a flat rate of $69 including identity and disk data cleansing, and you can see the economic benefits of ARS. `[ps=0]`
