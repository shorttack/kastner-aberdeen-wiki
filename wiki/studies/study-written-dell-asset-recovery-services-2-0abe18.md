# Dell Asset Recovery Services: a Trustworthy Partner for Eliminating Aging IT Assets (possible title: Remove, Resell, Recycle)

> This study analyzes Dell Asset Recovery Services: a Trustworthy Partner for Eliminating Aging IT Assets (possible title: Remove, Resell, Recycle) using Aberdeen Group research material and prepared extraction artifacts.


_Published 2003, author **Aberdeen Group**, type **market-study**._


## Top observations

- Old IT equipment can’t be thrown in the dumpster any longer; it must be properly disposed of, at a cost of $387 (these types of numbers through the document are the ones that we would like to see footnoted or explained how derived) and up per PC if you do it yourself. `[ps=4]`
- The critical business process change that can literally be worth $500 to $800 (footnote where/how this comes from) per IT asset is simple: identify and retire underutilized IT assets. `[ps=4]`
- _Identity cleansing $2 (where do these numbers come from, what work goes into this?)_ IT assets must have all enterprise identification removed in preparation for asset transfer to the disposal partner. `[ps=4]`
- material and sold on the commodities market or di-rectly back to manufacturers (see Figure 1). `[ps=4]`
- A service such as Dell’s ARS saves $1.54 million and eliminates an e-waste headache. `[ps=4]`
- Without a proper plan, companies will spend $1.9 million annually to dispose of 5,000 IT devices and still expose IT (the business, not IT) to public scrutiny by not recycling. `[ps=4]`
- Organizations that dispose of 3,000 PCs or other IT devices annually can save more than $1 million a (per) year in unnecessary storage and disposal costs with an as-set recovery program. `[ps=3]`
- Aberdeen has determined that the costs for cleansing disks can range from $185 to a low of $16, with the low estimate based on a significant capital investment to automate the process and support par-allel processing, a combination cost justified only with large volumes like those attained by Dell. `[ps=3]`
- For $49 per unit, Dell will pick up and recycle the e-waste, regardless of manufac-turer. `[ps=3]`
- The disposal plans discussed above can save an enterprise more than $1 million to $10 million annu-ally while making your organization a poster child (*another example of the tone issue) of environmental concern. `[ps=3]`
- We estimate disposal costs at over 25% of the initial PC acquisition cost and climbing. `[ps=2]`
- Putting this in terms of purchase price, a typical enterprise PC and monitor costs $1,200, so the $387 disposal cost represents 32% of the original cost — a percent-age that only increases rapidly as organizations continue to drive the cost of new PCs towards $1,000. `[ps=2]`
- Doing disk cleansing is a major expense at an average cost of $126 per disk. `[ps=2]`
- Storage and market value depreciation of a 36-month old Pentium III PC for one year costs about $368. `[ps=0]`
- ## _Data cleansing $126_ `[ps=0]`
- A large enterprise will pay approximately $126 per drive to do it. (Is there a way to tighten the preceding four paragraphs??? `[ps=0]`
- ## _Packing/moving $132_ `[ps=0]`
- When a PC unit, including the CRT, is packed and shipped to a regional office for processing and then packed and shipped again to a disposal partner, Aberdeen es-timates the cost for labor, transportation, and material to be as high as $198. `[ps=0]`
- If multiple assets intended for recycling are placed on a pallet and shrink wrapped for pick-up by the disposal partner — the mechanism recommended for Dell ARS customers — the cost could be as low as $12 per device. `[ps=0]`
- The average cost is $132 per unit for do-it-yourself, which covers material and handling associated with moving twenty PCs and monitors with four printers from multiple cubes to a proc-essing area where they are packed, and then trucked to a regional recycler. `[ps=0]`
- The challenge for smaller recyclers is made clear in Figure 1. `[ps=0]`
- Depending on the scale and management of the operation, refur-bishment and shipping may be as low as $28 and high as $67 per device. `[ps=0]`
- Compare these $28 to $67 costs to the whole Dell ARS program, which charges a flat rate of $69 including identity and disk data cleansing, and you can see the economic benefits of ARS. (*This is a very confusing comparison) `[ps=0]`
