- Storage and market value depreciation of a 36-month old Pentium III PC for one year costs about $368. `[ps=4]`
- Old IT equipment can’t be thrown in the dumpster any longer; it must be properly disposed of, at a cost of $387 (these types of numbers through the document are the ones that we would like to see footnoted or explained how derived) and up per PC if you do it yourself. `[ps=4]`
- We estimate disposal costs at over 25% of the initial PC acquisition cost and climbing. `[ps=4]`
- Organizations that dispose of 3,000 PCs or other IT devices annually can save more than $1 million a (per) year in unnecessary storage and disposal costs with an as-set recovery program. `[ps=4]`
- The critical business process change that can literally be worth $500 to $800 (footnote where/how this comes from) per IT asset is simple: identify and retire underutilized IT assets. `[ps=4]`
- Aberdeen has determined that the costs for cleansing disks can range from $185 to a low of $16, with the low estimate based on a significant capital investment to automate the process and support par-allel processing, a combination cost justified only with large volumes like those attained by Dell. `[ps=4]`
- When a PC unit, including the CRT, is packed and shipped to a regional office for processing and then packed and shipped again to a disposal partner, Aberdeen es-timates the cost for labor, transportation, and material to be as high as $198. `[ps=4]`
- If multiple assets intended for recycling are placed on a pallet and shrink wrapped for pick-up by the disposal partner — the mechanism recommended for Dell ARS customers — the cost could be as low as $12 per device. `[ps=4]`
- The average cost is $132 per unit for do-it-yourself, which covers material and handling associated with moving twenty PCs and monitors with four printers from multiple cubes to a proc-essing area where they are packed, and then trucked to a regional recycler. `[ps=4]`
- Depending on the scale and management of the operation, refur-bishment and shipping may be as low as $28 and high as $67 per device. `[ps=4]`
- Using the numbers above, it costs approximately $387 or more for the enterprise on its own to properly eliminate one electronic asset without engaging a partner’s help. `[ps=4]`
- A service such as Dell’s ARS saves $1.54 million and eliminates an e-waste headache. `[ps=4]`
- Putting this in terms of purchase price, a typical enterprise PC and monitor costs $1,200, so the $387 disposal cost represents 32% of the original cost — a percent-age that only increases rapidly as organizations continue to drive the cost of new PCs towards $1,000. `[ps=4]`
- For $49 per unit, Dell will pick up and recycle the e-waste, regardless of manufac-turer. `[ps=4]`
- Without a proper plan, companies will spend $1.9 million annually to dispose of 5,000 IT devices and still expose IT (the business, not IT) to public scrutiny by not recycling. `[ps=4]`
- The disposal plans discussed above can save an enterprise more than $1 million to $10 million annu-ally while making your organization a poster child (*another example of the tone issue) of environmental concern. `[ps=4]`
- Doing disk cleansing is a major expense at an average cost of $126 per disk. `[ps=4]`
