# Tech Watching Is Fast Becoming an Industry to Watch (Wall Street Journal, March 19, 1998)

> William M. Bulkeley writes in the Wall Street Journal (March 19, 1998, page B7) that U.S. computer-industry research firms' revenue will rise ~28% in 1998 to $3 billion (Gartner Group forecast). Profiles the major firms in a 'Who's Who' table: Aberdeen (35 analysts, marketing consulting/training, buyer=computer makers), Forrester (75, trend-spotting), Gartner Group (550, the giant), Giga Group (75), International Data (135), Intelliquest Info (market research), Meta Group (111), Yankee Group (100, telecom). Quotes Marsha Haugen (HP market research manager, $4M/year spend), Rick O'Coin (Aetna team leader), Robert Paquin (L.L. Bean VP information services, Meta client), John W. Harmon (GAO CIO, $100K/year Gartner), Deon Babiuk (IPL Energy Calgary, cut $60K Gartner spend), Leonard Tenner (Hewitt Associates CIO), Sherri Wolf (Adams Harkness Hill analyst: '80% recurring revenue, 15-20% pretax operating margins'), and George Colony (Forrester president: 'McKinsey-like consultants are selling time — they're like dentists'). 1997 revenue figures: Gartner $511.2M (+30%), Meta $51.2M (+38%), Forrester $40.4M (+62%).


_Published 1998, author **William M. Bulkeley (The Wall Street Journal)**, type **news-article**._


## Top observations

- U.S. computer-industry research firms' revenue to rise ~28% in 1998 to $3 billion
- $511.2M 1997 revenue, +30% YoY; 550 analysts; Stamford CT
- $51.2M 1997 revenue, +38% YoY; 111 analysts; Stamford CT
- $40.4M 1997 revenue, +62% YoY; 75 analysts; Cambridge MA
- 35 analysts/researchers; positioned as 'marketing consulting, training'; main buyer = computer makers
- 135 analysts; 'Best market numbers, building consulting'; main buyer = computer makers, Wall St.
- 75 analysts; 'Internet delivery, sharp analysis'; main buyer = CIOs and below
- 100 analysts; 'Opinionated telecom experts'; main buyer = telecom/network buyers
- 75 analysts; 'Trend-spotting'; main buyer = CIOs, marketers
- The giant; tries to cover all IT issues; main buyer = everyone in IT
- 80% recurring revenue; 15-20% pretax operating margins — 'very attractive business model'
- Traditional consultants like McKinsey 'are only selling their time — they're like dentists'; Forrester boosts profit via publishing/conference leverage
- HP computer division $4M/year analyst subscriptions; salespeople pressure vendors ('pay your basic ransom')
- If Hewitt had followed Meta/Gartner advice to replace IBM mainframes with distributed Unix 5 years ago, we couldn't do what we're doing today
- You're buying expertise that it doesn't make sense to have full time on staff
- Cut $60,000/year Gartner subscription; gathers equivalent content from the Internet
- In 1986 a Gartner analyst predicted 4 companies (IBM, AT&T, Fujitsu, Hitachi) would dominate the computer universe by 1996 — only IBM remains a player
- Gideon Gartner left and set up Giga Group in 1995; competes with his former firm
- Worldwide sales of IT computers/software to reach $835 billion in 1998, +10%
