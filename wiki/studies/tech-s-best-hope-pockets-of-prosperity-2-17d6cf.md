# Tech's Best Hope: Pockets of Prosperity

> BusinessWeek Online feature (March 15 2002) by Alex Salkever analyzing which tech segments will lead the post-dot-com recovery despite continuing telecom weakness. Peter Kastner, chief research officer at Aberdeen Group, provides the core Aberdeen 2002 forecast: total tech-sector revenues (hardware, software, IT services, excluding heavy telecom) will grow from $446.1 billion (2001) to $465.3 billion (2002), a 4.3% increase. Aberdeen projects IT services growing 5.6% ($173.9B -> $183.7B), software 6.7% ($93.7B -> $100B), and hardware only 1.7% ($178.4B -> $181.4B). Kastner calls the telecom-equipment segment 'an industry in nuclear winter.' He highlights Dell's two-hour inventory posture (supply-chain visibility) and IBM/EDS backlogs for ERP/CRM integration work at customers rolling out PeopleSoft, SAP, and Siebel. Merrill Lynch survey of 100 CIOs: most shifted spending-recovery expectations from 2002 into early 2003.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — Aberdeen 2002 total $465B: Aberdeen projects total tech-sector revenues (hardware, software, IT services, excluding heavy telecom) will grow from $446.1 billion (2001) to $465.3 billion (2002), a 4.3% increase.
- **2002** — Telecom nuclear winter: [Telecom-equipment suppliers like Lucent, Nortel, Cisco] remain far from optimistic. It's an industry in nuclear winter.
- **2002** — IT services 5.6% growth: Aberdeen projects IT services revenues growing from $173.9 billion (2001) to $183.7 billion (2002), a 5.6% increase.
- **2002** — Software 6.7% growth: Aberdeen projects software revenues climbing from $93.7 billion (2001) to $100 billion (2002), a 6.7% increase — fastest among major tech segments.
- **2002** — Hardware 1.7% growth: Aberdeen projects hardware revenues growing only from $178.4 billion (2001) to $181.4 billion (2002), a 1.7% increase — slowest among major tech segments.
- **2002** — Dell 2-hour inventory: Dell keeps two hours of inventory. Therefore, if Dell's supplier's supplier has a plant fire, you want to know about that, because this could back up the assembly line tomorrow.
- **2002** — IBM/EDS integrator backlogs: Kastner observes big systems integrators like IBM and EDS enjoy growing backlogs of work for ERP/CRM installations (PeopleSoft, SAP, Siebel).
- **2002** — CIO survey delays: Mar 11 Merrill Lynch survey of 100 chief information officers at major companies found them shifting their expectations of a tech-spending recovery out of 2002 and into early 2003.
- **2009** — Nortel bankrupt: Nortel Networks filed for bankruptcy Jan 14 2009 — the culmination of the telecom 'nuclear winter' Kastner diagnosed in 2002; assets sold piecemeal to Ericsson (wireless), Ciena (Metro Ethernet), Avaya (enterprise), Genband.
- **2006** — Lucent into Alcatel: Lucent Technologies merged with Alcatel Dec 1 2006 forming Alcatel-Lucent — Nokia acquired the combined entity in 2016 for €15.6B.
- **2005** — Oracle acquires PeopleSoft: Oracle completed its $10.3B hostile takeover of PeopleSoft on Jan 7 2005 after an 18-month battle — one of many 2005-2006 enterprise-software consolidations.
- **2006** — Oracle acquires Siebel: Oracle acquired Siebel Systems for $5.85B on Jan 31 2006 — consolidating the Kastner-era CRM-integration backlog business into Oracle Fusion Applications.
