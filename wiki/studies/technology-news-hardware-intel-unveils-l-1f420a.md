# Intel Unveils Low-Cost Celeron M While AMD Releases Mobile Athlon 64

> TechNewsWorld article (Jan 6 2004, Jay Lyman) on parallel announcements: Intel's Celeron M value mobile processor (1.3/1.2/0.8 GHz ULV; $134/$107/$161 in 1,000-unit quantities) to compete with AMD at the low end while differentiating from Pentium M; and AMD's Mobile Athlon 64 — the industry's only Windows-compatible 64-bit mobile processor for mainstream notebooks, supported by 60+ manufacturers including HP, eMachines, Fujitsu. Aberdeen chief research officer Peter Kastner delivers the canonical mobile-segment read: 'Both companies are rightly attacking the mobile PC space, which is the fastest-growing portion of the industry.' And: '2003 proved that laptop sales are price elastic in that $800 to $1,000 laptops are flying off the shelves.' Kastner rejects the 'dumbing down' framing of Celeron M: it's 'simply a low-cost, high-performance microprocessor designed for the wireless world' that 'will be quickly incorporated into value-oriented, under-$1,000 machines.' He calls Mobile Athlon 64 'another respin of AMD's long-term effort to increase laptop market share.' Mercury Research's Dean McCarron frames 2004 as likely 'one of the more exciting years in microprocessors because of all the new products and the positioning.'

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2004** — Mobile fastest-growing: Both companies are rightly attacking the mobile PC space, which is the fastest-growing portion of the industry.
- **2003** — $800-$1000 laptops price-elastic: 2003 proved that laptop sales are price elastic in that $800 to $1,000 laptops are flying off the shelves.
- **2004** — Not a dumbing down: The Celeron M is not a 'dumbing down'; Intel's chip is simply a low-cost, high-performance microprocessor designed for the wireless world. It will be quickly incorporated into value-oriented, under-$1,000 machines.
- **2004** — Mobile Athlon 64 another respin: AMD's Mobile Athlon 64, which is aimed at the same notebook market, is another respin of AMD's long-term effort to increase laptop market share.
- **2004** — Exciting year in microprocessors: Mercury Research president Dean McCarron: 2004 is probably going to be one of the more exciting years in microprocessors because of all the new products and the positioning.
- **2004** — Celeron M pricing: Intel Celeron M pricing: 1.3 GHz $134, 1.2 GHz $107, 800 MHz ULV $161 (all in 1,000-unit quantities). 0.13-micron process; value-vs-Pentium-M differentiation.
- **2004** — Mobile Athlon 64 60+ OEM support: AMD Mobile Athlon 64: industry's only Windows-compatible 64-bit mobile processor for mainstream notebooks; 60+ manufacturers and system builders supporting, including HP, eMachines, Fujitsu.
- **2010** — Sub-$1000 laptops dominate: Sub-$1,000 laptop share grew from ~25% in 2003 to ~60% by 2010 per Gartner/IDC — strongly validating Kastner's 2004 price-elasticity thesis. Netbooks 2007-2011, sub-$500 Chromebooks 2011+, budget Windows laptops became the bulk of PC unit sales.
