# IT Technology Themes and Trends

> A collection of Aberdeen Group research themes and trends for 2003-2004 authored by Peter S. Kastner, covering twelve strategic IT topics including IT deflation and per-processor software licensing, tiered storage and information lifecycle management, consumer empowerment via Internet, the extinction of best-of-breed IT, Sarbanes-Oxley compliance costs, and real-time consumer tracking. Each theme is framed as an Aberdeen research offering with a pithy headline and analytical summary. The document serves as both a market intelligence snapshot and a business development vehicle for Aberdeen consulting engagements.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2003** — Storage market theme: IT deflation in storage: Enterprise storage decisions once simple (GB per disk); now complicated by compliance new data types and ILM policy-based tiers
- **2003** — Pools of storage with ILM policy — adoption prediction: New thinking about pools of storage controlled by ILM policy opening doors to lower-cost storage devices and architectures
- **2015** — Tiered storage / ILM adoption — actual outcome: Tiered storage became universal; ILM embedded in all major storage platforms (NetApp EMC IBM); cloud tiering (AWS S3 Glacier) extended the model
- **2003** — Storage budget growth drivers: Storage budgets skyrocketing due to compliance issues data replication for real-time/DSS and PC/project data proliferation
- **2003** — IT deflation — per-processor software licensing crisis: 30% annual server technology deflation; today's servers have fewer processors than predecessors creating real problem for per-processor software licenses
- **2015** — Per-processor licensing crisis — actual outcome: Software licensing shifted from per-processor to subscription/SaaS driven partly by server consolidation via virtualization; VMware's virtual CPU model further disrupted per-processor licensing
- **2003** — Server deflation rate: ~30% annual technology deflation for servers; IT buyers replacing older boxes with more powerful machines at same or lower prices
- **2003** — Consumer empowerment theme: IT-empowered customer: At least 1/3 of US population considers themselves IT savvy; another 1/3 at least competent — driving retail IT spending on automation CRM and analytics
- **2003** — Real-time consumer tracking prediction: Savvy internet marketers tracking every click; real-time analytics programs sifting marketing wheat from chaff; anonymous web behavior predicting real-world behavior
- **2015** — Real-time consumer tracking — actual outcome: Digital advertising and real-time behavioral analytics became a multi-hundred-billion-dollar industry; Google Facebook Amazon built empires on exactly this model
- **2003** — Privacy concern about consumer tracking: Consumers have less privacy on web than ever; hue and cry of privacy advocates muted — but will consumers put up with electronic minder tracking every click?
- **2003** — Best-of-breed extinction prediction: CIOs see cost of integrating best-of-breed applications from different vendors as more expensive than single-vendor; CFOs prefer pre-integrated; colossal firms with astronomical R&D budgets dominating
- **2012** — Best-of-breed extinction — actual outcome: Oracle acquired PeopleSoft (2004) Siebel (2005) BEA (2008) Sun (2010); SAP acquired Business Objects Sybase Ariba; Salesforce became mega-vendor; best-of-breed largely absorbed or marginalized
- **2003** — Sarbanes-Oxley compliance cost range: Minimum several hundred thousand to several million dollars annually per affected business
- **2007** — Sarbanes-Oxley IT compliance cost — actual outcome: SOX compliance drove massive IT investment in financial controls GRC systems and audit management software; spawned entire software category
- **2003** — SOX effect on business-to-business transactions: Beyond internal changes how will Sarbanes-Oxley affect B2B transactions and business processes?
- **2003** — Consumer as real driver of channel master success: Dell and Wal-Mart's success driven by the modern consumer — educated online interconnected shopping machine forcing change on virtually every firm
- **2003** — RFID adoption prediction: Automated replenishment RFID tags new process designs enhanced parcel tracking partially or totally attributed to rise of modern consumer
- **2015** — RFID adoption — actual outcome: RFID adopted in logistics and warehouse management (Wal-Mart mandate 2005) but consumer retail tracking happened via web/mobile not physical RFID tags
- **2003** — WiFi enterprise adoption barriers: WiFi scares executives fearful of security support and high initial cost; fewer than 30% corporate laptops; corridor cruisers the target use case
- **2010** — WiFi enterprise adoption — actual outcome: WiFi became ubiquitous in enterprise; security concerns resolved via WPA2/802.1X; laptop penetration exceeded 50% by 2010 driven by tablet and smartphone co-adoption
- **2003** — Partner system management via web services: Billions invested in enterprise management for inward-facing value chain; extended value chains complicate partner management; new technologies allow partner-system management without impinging on partner prerogatives
- **2003** — Storage value differentiation thesis: IT buyers realizing not every data byte has same value to enterprise and that value changes over time — core ILM thesis
- **2003** — IT value redefinition by executives: Great ROI only relevant if technology addresses executive's top 3-5 pain points; executive committee demands high-ROI projects implementing in weeks not months
- **2003** — IT over-buying and rationalization trend: Largest firms own dozens of redundant overlapping software pieces; smart CIOs/CFOs rationalizing IT purchases; remote data centers being consolidated
