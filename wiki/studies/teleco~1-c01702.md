# Telecommunications: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering the full telecommunications landscape: deregulation, CLEC/ILEC dynamics, the shift from TDM to packet (IP/ATM) networks, managed services, telecom hardware/software vendors, and OSS/billing. The document profiles managed-services providers and analyzes the competitive forces reshaping carriers, equipment vendors, and ISPs as voice and data networks begin to converge. Key findings include the $670 billion global telecom market size, the growth of IP services alongside dominance of private-line revenues, and the challenge of providing PSTN-grade reliability on packet networks.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — Global telecom industry size: $670 billion worldwide (75% services / 25% hardware+software)
- **1998** — U.S. Private Line revenues: $10 billion+
- **1998** — U.S. Frame Relay revenues: $2.5 billion
- **1998** — U.S. IP services revenues: $300 million
- **1994** — World information industry total: $1.4 trillion (ITU)
- **1998** — Large enterprise managed services adoption: Over 80% employ some form of managed telecom service
- **1998** — Managed Services market size by 2002: $36 billion (2.6% of projected $1.4T telecom services)
- **1998** — Actual managed services market 2002 outcome: Placeholder for Phase 3 verification
- **1998** — U.S. carrier count growth rate: 10% per year; interconnects growing exponentially
- **1998** — North American carrier interconnects by 2002: Over 2 million (up from 200,000 in 1997)
- **1998** — PSTN reliability vs public IP networks: PSTN: 99.9999% (53 min/year); public IP: ~95% (36.5 hr/month)
- **1998** — Internet share of world telecom traffic by 2000: 50% (per UUNet VP Alan Taffel)
- **1998** — IP telephony feasibility status: Technically feasible but back-office (billing/provisioning/care) gaps remain
- **1998** — IP VPN enterprise readiness: Not yet a reality; no public QoS; enterprises distrust public IP for traffic
- **1998** — Traditional switch vendor strategic position: At crossroads; CO software upgrade revenue model threatened by IP/open platforms
- **1998** — Traditional switch vendor business model shift: Annual CO software upgrades disappearing; replaced by third-vendor OSS on open platforms
- **1998** — Dense WDM (DWDM) commercial viability status: Not yet viable; hardware prototypes only; field trials within one year
- **1997** — WTO Basic Telecom Agreement impact: Signed November 1997; eliminated bilateral accounting rates; opened foreign competition in basic services
- **1998** — European telecom competition: Trade barriers removed January 1 1998; ISPs and CLECs posing serious threat to incumbent PTTs
- **1998** — Asia-Pacific telecom market structure: Largely oligopolistic; financial crisis halted new investment
- **1998** — IBM Global Services managed networks position: Formidable competitor; all competitors challenged to overcome IBM's dominant services position
- **1998** — Ameritech regional managed services strategy: Solidly positioned for regional enterprise needs; leverages IBM for data; focuses on voice core
- **1998** — Bell Atlantic post-NYNEX merger position: Strengthened by NYNEX integration; 14 east coast states; All@Once managed services since 1995
- **1998** — Equant global network services position: Powerhouse in global data; extensive seamless network via SITA PTT partnerships
- **1998** — Cost advantage of IP vs circuit-switched networks: Commonly assumed cheaper but incorrect; packet networks face same operational cost drivers
