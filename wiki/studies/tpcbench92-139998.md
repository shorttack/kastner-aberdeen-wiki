# TPC Benchmarks: Users Benefit From Competition (1992 Presentation Notes)

> Outline and notes for a 1992 Aberdeen Group presentation on TPC benchmarks covering TPC-A and TPC-B standards and their role as the primary vehicle for commercial system price/performance competition. Per-supplier analysis covers Digital (VAX), HP, IBM (RS/6000, AS/400, mainframes), Bull, Data General, and Sun, with specific data points including HP's Jan-1990 cost of $36.50/TPS-A falling to DEC's $7.69/TPS-A by March 1992, and Digital VAX 4000-300 improving price-performance by 50% over 18 months.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1988** — TPC founding: Founded 1988 with Aberdeen principal help; independent supplier-sponsored benchmarking standards body
- **1992** — TPC benchmark suite as of 1992: TPC-A (TP), TPC-B (database), TPC-C (coming - WIC)
- **1992** — TPC-A standard configuration requirements: 5-year LCS; 10 user terminals per TPS; costs grow as users grow (terminals + history storage); FDC required
- **1992** — TPC strategic role: Primary vehicle for commercial system price and performance competition
- **1992** — TPC competitive dynamic: King for a day: weekly jockeying for best position; beware of out-of-date information
- **1990** — HP TPC-A price/performance (January 1990): 38.2 TPS at $36.50/TPS-A; $1.39M system
- **1992** — DEC TPC-A price/performance (March 1992): 27.9 TPS at $7.69/TPS-A; $214K system (27 months after HP Jan-1990 result)
- **1992** — Q1-1992 TPC-A average price/performance: Averaging $9K per TPS-A; Digital VAX 6000 best price/performer in 10-200 TPS range
- **1992** — Digital VAX 4000-300 price/performance improvement: $31.90/TPS-A in October 1990 to $10.71/TPS-A in March 1992; 50% improvement in 18 months
- **1992** — DEC TPC-A pricing strategy: Aggressive pricing to TPC p/p; cost per TPS stable as performance rises; rationalizes VAX pricing; VAX 6640 actually lower cost than 6410
- **1992** — HP TPC-A competitive positioning: Growing from timesharing to OLTP contender; maturing Unix and ALLBASE/SQL; rationalizing HP/UX vs. MPE pricing
- **1992** — IBM RS/6000 550 TPC-A improvement (January 1992): 18% p/p improvement due to new models; 59% better performance due to maturing AIX
- **1992** — IBM AS/400 x70 series TPC-A improvement: 83% p/p improvement from B to D to E70 series (April 1991 to February 1992) at $17.59/TPS; not flat - more performance costs more per unit
- **1992** — IBM mainframe TPC-A absence: IBM mainframes missing in action; IBM said 'not appropriate' but Unisys ran one; TPC forbids estimation; IBM has enough problems selling S/390s
- **1992** — Unisys mainframe TPC participation: Unisys ran a mainframe-class system on TPC despite IBM's 'not appropriate' stance
- **1992** — Sun SPARC/Sybase TPC participation: Sun SPARC with Sybase SQL Server participating in TPC competition
- **1992** — Buyer TPC-A guidance: TPC-A is valid p and p/p compare; enough results for valid sample; ask for results; write into RFPs; hire auditor; use latest results
- **1992** — TPC competition benefit to buyers: Buyers will benefit from supplier competition; competition breeds better price and performance
- **1992** — Supplier TPC optimization forecast: Suppliers are aggressively optimizing price and performance
- **1993** — Supplier TPC optimization - outcome: unknown
- **1992** — TPC-C status: Coming benchmark (TPC-C WIC) announced but not yet published
