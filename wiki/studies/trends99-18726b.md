# IT Trends for 1999 -- Internal Kastner/Logan Analysis

> This internal Aberdeen Group memo captures a dialogue between John R. Logan (JRL) and Peter S. Kastner (PK) on IT trends for 1999. Logan proposes 8 trends and Kastner responds with critiques, refinements, and additional topics. Key themes include Y2K preparedness, Windows 2000 adoption risk, Intel PC refresh cycle, EAI approaches, ISP market evolution, application integration, e-commerce development methodologies, and security. Kastner also adds three original trend topics: security ownership, objects in 1999, and ISP utility computing.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1999** — EAI approaches: Crossworlds/Oberon: Logan: data warehouse best path; Kastner: data warehouse only good for past business; key is buy vs build; ERP vendors dark horse
- **1999** — Windows 2000 enterprise adoption in 1999: Will not be quickly adopted by large enterprises in 1999; Y2K issues, delayed functionality, requires both server and desktop for proper operation -- likely one of Microsoft's flops at end of 1999
- **2000** — Windows 2000 enterprise adoption actual: unknown
- **1999** — Office 2000 delay and impact: Office 2000 delayed beyond expected Q1 1999; represents 40% of Microsoft profits; key feature: move in/out of HTML for Intranet posting
- **1999** — Intel PC refresh cycle 1999: Intel will have a banner year; SME replacing PCs and LAN servers in mid-1999 for Y2K + Windows 95/DOS replacement; buying frenzy in 3Q99
- **1999** — Intel 1999 banner year actual: unknown
- **1999** — #1 companies gaining market share: SAP, Oracle, Cisco, Compaq (NT servers), Sun, IBM, Dell will increase shares in their specific areas of dominance in 1999; not year for new entrant
- **1999** — Market share concentration 1999 actual: unknown
- **1999** — Sun enterprise data center ascendancy: Sun's ascendancy is inevitable; championing Java; beacon of intellectual ideas for web-based data center applications; HP has higher-end servers, IBM better services
- **2005** — Sun enterprise ascendancy actual: unknown
- **1999** — Y2K preparedness level: JRL very pessimistic; Y2K bugs found in supposedly corrected code; testing not succeeding; interfaces with trading partners not being tested; Y2K-compliant EDI not tested at application level
- **2000** — Y2K actual impact: unknown
- **1999** — Y2K contingency planning approach: Business units cut off from IS support; must use LAN + Office 97 + NT/Unix; three approaches: a) VBA + Office97 + Access + Outlook; b) Lotus Notes + Office97; c) Intranet toolset
- **1999** — Post-Y2K web application development: After fixing Y2K, enterprises will use improved methodologies to develop web-based apps; roll-your-own e-commerce required for years; Y2K best-practice firms better positioned
- **1999** — Security ownership problem: Security used to be one mainframe program; TCP/IP opens enormous holes; rampant complexity in distributed systems; unsolved whether LOB managers or CIO owns security
- **1999** — 1999 as Year of the Object: After 10 years, OO development mostly in supplier software products not IT apps; in 1999, leading ERP suppliers and IBM San Francisco project opening new era of objects for practical business use
- **1999** — ISP and IP telephony development: ISPs: are they just toll-takers or sustainable business? Consolidation, hot niches, key products; will 1999 be year of IP telephony?
- **2003** — IP telephony year of breakthrough: unknown
- **1999** — G2000 telecom switching: AT&T, WorldCom/MCI, Sprint, British Telecom, Cable and Wireless cutting each other's throats for overarching enterprise contracts; users worried about quality of service
- **1999** — Document purpose: Internal collated list for HP sales training project and Joe's speech in the Bahamas; dual-audience memo
