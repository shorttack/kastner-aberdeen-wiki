# Utilities: 1998 Industry Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering information technology for electric and natural gas utilities facing deregulation. The document defines three practice pillars — Distribution (billing/CIS/call centers/meter networks), Asset Management (SCADA/EMS/ERP/GIS), and Transactions (energy trading/risk management/ISO systems) — and profiles 12 vendors. Key findings include that U.S. gas and electric utilities represent $300B in revenues; the last major U.S. monopoly being deregulated; and that conservative IT buying behavior will coexist with urgent spending on billing, CIS, and customer-facing systems.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — U.S. gas and electric utility revenues: $300 billion+
- **1998** — Deregulation analogy: Utilities going same route as banking telecommunications and transportation
- **1998** — Utility IT buying behavior: Conservative; many will wait until forced by deregulation; some will then outsource
- **1998** — Customer information systems investment urgency: Essential for marketers and distributors; regulated utilities with archaic systems must aggressively compete
- **1998** — Meter data networks strategic role: Provide new distribution mechanism feeding back-end billing and operational support; strategic IT asset
- **1998** — ERP adoption trajectory for utilities: Utilities will gradually integrate existing specialized apps over networks using new infrastructures and ERP
- **1998** — SCADA market status in U.S.: Mature; new versions of controls systems will link to heterogeneous environments for ERP purposes
- **1998** — Electronic transactions for utilities: Will ultimately become essential; moving from paper to e-commerce with real-time transactions
- **1998** — Utility Y2K exposure: Real-time operational systems (EMS) raise most dire Y2K possibilities; driving system replacement
- **1998** — Open systems transition driver: Cost control needs from deregulation plus customizable app suites incentivize shift from proprietary
- **1998** — Utility telecom ventures strategic rationale: Make sense because utilities have facilities and expertise giving head start in telecom business
- **1998** — ABB Systems Control market position: Strong global presence; unique position to support large facilities engineering contracts with IT
- **1998** — Andersen Consulting utility position: Well-positioned to sell/integrate software; strategic consultant for restructuring decisions
- **1998** — IBM Utility and Energy Services scale: 1500+ people worldwide; ~$2 billion revenues; large component of utility data center outsourcing
- **1998** — SAP utility customer base: 350+ utility customers worldwide; SAP Utilities 1998 release features purchasing accounting asset management HR
- **1998** — J.D. Edwards utility group status: Growing very rapidly; best for AS/400 users; well-suited for mid-sized T&D companies
- **1998** — Smallworld GIS utility adoption: Increasingly popular; playing important role in integration and data conversion projects involving multiple suppliers
- **1998** — Supplier success factors for utility IT: Must leverage existing industry competencies; offer integration/outsourcing; provide pre-packaged object-based solutions
- **1998** — Power marketer and ISO emergence: Utilities selling generation assets; ISOs overseeing regional power markets; next-hour trading
- **1998** — IBM-Siemens transmission trading partnership: Partnered to market transmission trading service for utilities; IBM is lead integrator for Western Exchange
