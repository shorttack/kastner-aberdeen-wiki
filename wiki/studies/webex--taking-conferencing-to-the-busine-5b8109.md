# WebEx: Taking Conferencing to the Business Communications Summit

> Aberdeen Group vendor profile of WebEx Communications, analyzing its emergence as leader in high-end Web conferencing. Documents WebEx's third-generation Mediatone infrastructure and private WAN architecture as key differentiators versus competitors. Covers the five-level Aberdeen model for real-time business communications, new Training Center service, multimedia capabilities (UCF, Access Anywhere, multipoint video), and the market shift away from face-to-face meetings accelerated by the 2001 recession and post-9/11 travel concerns. At April 2002, WebEx had 5,000+ customers growing at 800+ per quarter.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **2002** — WebEx market position: Clear leader in high-end Web conferencing
- **2002** — WebEx customer count: 5,000+ customers
- **2002** — WebEx customer growth rate: 800+ customers per quarter (virus-like, word-of-mouth-induced)
- **1999** — Early Web conferencing competitors: ~40 companies competing to deliver shared PowerPoint presentations over Internet in 1999
- **2002** — WebEx key differentiator: Private conferencing switched network on top of Internet; utility-grade QoS and security
- **2002** — Mediatone capabilities: 5 new capabilities: UCF, multi-document sharing, Access Anywhere, multipoint video, PDA support
- **2002** — UCF PowerPoint animation support: Dramatically enhances WebEx competitive position; most critical/demanded capability by end-users
- **2002** — Face-to-face meeting trend: Clear trend away from face-to-face meetings, especially large internal meetings and training
- **2002** — WebEx brand adoption indicator: Noun becoming verb: 'Let's WebEx'
- **2002** — WebEx Training Center capabilities: Full training schedule/registration; breakout sessions; testing/scoring; SCORM support; re-broadcast
- **2002** — Aberdeen 5-level communications model: Face-to-face > Telephone > Audio Conference > Video Conference > Web Conference (hierarchy)
- **2002** — Video conferencing limitations: Fixed location; high capital cost; B2B video over Internet blocked by firewall security; requires ISDN lines
- **2002** — WebEx vs Internet QoS: WebEx provides utility-grade QoS; Internet provides none; competitors' erratic response times can be catastrophic
- **2002** — WebEx optimal use cases: Media-rich: software demos, training, project management, staff meetings, remote support, sales/marketing presentations, seminars, partner briefings, customer service
- **2002** — WebEx strategic trajectory: Positioned to become dominant online meeting standard; making online meetings as straightforward as telephone calls
- **2007** — WebEx market outcome - actual: Acquired by Cisco Systems in 2007 for approximately $3.2 billion; market leadership validated
- **2002** — Competitor architecture assessment: Database-centric store-and-forward approach led to many failures; cannot match Mediatone capabilities
- **2002** — VoIP security/QoS limitations: Internet VoIP suffers all security and privacy flaws of Internet; biggest Web conferencing drawback
- **2002** — Corporate training market attractiveness: Very easy business case; presenters spend more time at jobs, less on airplanes; training has longer shelf life via recording/rebroadcast
- **2002** — DHTML PowerPoint limitation: Extremely unpredictable; significant limitations; not adopted by market
- **2002** — Multipoint video via browser: Power of multipoint video conferencing with just browser + webcam; also supports Polycam cameras and video camcorders
- **2002** — Market shift trigger: 2001 recession + post-9/11 travel uncertainty = tectonic market-shifting earthquake away from face-to-face meetings
