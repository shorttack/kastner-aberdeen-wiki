# Technical Workstations: 1998 Practice Summary

> Aberdeen Group's 1998 practice summary covering standalone Unix and Windows NT technical workstations used for CAD/MCAD/CAE/DCC/animation/scientific visualization and decision-support. The document profiles market leaders (HP Sun IBM SGI for Unix; HP Compaq Dell Digital IBM Intergraph for NT) and provides key findings including NT growing at 30%+ annually, Unix maintaining high-end dominance for 4-5 more years, and the emerging use of workstations as data visualization decision-support tools. HP is identified as the overall combined-platform market leader for 1997.

## Key observations (top 25)

- **1998** — RISC Unix workstation market trajectory: Flat-to-decreased growth over next several years as NT-on-Intel gains in processor and graphics performance
- **1998** — NT workstation market growth rate: Greater than 30% growth rate; continuing at ~30% well into next millennium
- **1998** — NT workstation price range: $2,000 (low-end) to ~$20,000; median $5,000-$7,500
- **1998** — Unix vs NT performance parity: NT benchmarked equal to midrange Unix at one-third the acquisition cost
- **1998** — 64-bit NT workstation availability: 2000 at earliest; ensures Unix dominance at high end for next 3 years
- **1997** — HP overall workstation market leadership: Overall technical workstation market leader 1997 (combined Unix+NT units)
- **1997** — Sun Unix workstation market share: #1 Unix workstation seller by greater than 2:1 margin over HP (#2 Unix)
- **1997** — IBM RS/6000 Unix market position: #3 unit sales; quality AIX and IBM Global Services positive; PowerPC performance issues
- **1997** — SGI Unix workstation performance: Declining market share; financial and management turmoil; undercut by Sun HP and NT-on-Intel
- **1997** — Compaq NT workstation market position: #2 NT position achieved in only 16 months; strategy: right price for low-end and mid-range enterprise
- **1997** — Digital Equipment NT workstation differentiator: #4 NT; Alpha maintained 10-50% performance lead over Intel for floating-point NT workstations
- **1997** — Dell NT workstation market rise: #3 NT within only 5 months of first product launch (Workstation 400)
- **1997** — Intergraph NT workstation position: #5 total NT unit volume; strong 3D graphics but loss of share due to lack of mid-level product focus
- **1997** — HP NT workstation market leadership: #1 NT workstation market by edging out Compaq; VISUALIZE rendering on Kayak differentiator
- **1997** — IBM IntelliStation NT launch result: #6 NT position in 1997 after March launch; significant progress on ISV relationships and qualified apps
- **1998** — Workstation channel model: Majority through indirect channels (HP Sun IBM Compaq SGI Intergraph); direct model (Dell Gateway Micron) gaining
- **1998** — Technical workstation decision support opportunity: Major new market emerging; Unix has early edge on statistical visualization; NT has long-term advantage via desktop app breadth
- **1998** — Supplier success factors for workstations: Platform performance; applications capture (qualified ISVs); service and support; connectivity
- **1998** — Windows NT Y2K compliance status: NT 4.0 Workstation compliant with minor issues; made fully compliant via Service Pack 3 and free QFE fixes
- **1998** — Parametric Technology ISV importance: Market-leading ISV for CAD/MCAD; applications capture for PTC determines platform purchasing decisions
