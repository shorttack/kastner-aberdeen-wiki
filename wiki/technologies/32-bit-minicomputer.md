# 32-bit minicomputer

> Democratized compute access by 1972; economically displaced by x86 servers; proprietary margins funded x86 development that displaced them.


## Top observations

- By the time Kastner left PHI in 1972, minicomputers were democratizing compute access. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Minicomputer was not technically obsolete when x86 servers displaced it; it was economically uncompetitive compared to commodity hardware manufactured at scale. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- 20-40 percent growth predicted for terminals, superminis, 32-bit minis in OEM and end-user CDP markets `[ps=4]` — [[study-stratus-corporate-overview-1982-11-a7e367]]
- Proprietary minicomputer margins funded the x86 development that displaced minicomputers — a direct illustration of Pattern 2. `[ps=2]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- By the time Kastner joined Prime in 1979, compute was escaping the glass house. — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
