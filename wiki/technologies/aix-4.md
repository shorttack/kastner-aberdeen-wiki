# IBM AIX 4

> Launched fall 1994; introduced SMP, VRMF versioning, JFS; successor versions AIX 5L, 6, 7 remain active through AIX 7.3


## Top observations

- AIX 4 is future-driven by SMP; RS/6000 user base did not initially have SMP hardware — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Production-grade, flexible Unix OS for enterprise line-of-business applications — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Small business users blocked by VAR applications not yet migrated to AIX 4; ISVs also suffered version-3 fatigue — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Majority of users feared AIX 4 would repeat AIX 3 patch frustration — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Aberdeen believes AIX users still on 3.2.5 by end of 1996 will be doing a disservice to their enterprises — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- AIX 4 became the dominant AIX version through the late 1990s; IBM released AIX 5L in 2001 evolving the platform further — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Aberdeen: reward-to-risk ratio so great that RS/6000 customers should move to AIX 4 as fast as they can in 1996 — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- IBM cannot force VARs who suffered version-3 frustration to upgrade until they are very confident in AIX 4 stability — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- Aberdeen SMP Efficiency Rating introduced in Figure 1; specific numeric values not visible in text extraction — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
- 1996 RDBMS vendors expect SMP and AIX 4 compatibility as table-stakes for enterprise deployments — [[study-aberdeen-1996-ibm-aix-4-not-your-fathers-325]]
