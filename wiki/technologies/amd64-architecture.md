# AMD64 / x86-64 architecture (Opteron/Athlon 64)

> AMD 64-bit extension of x86 (Opteron Apr 2003, Athlon 64 Sep 2003); Intel adopted as Intel 64 (EM64T) 2004; became dominant 64-bit desktop/server ISA


## Top observations

- Very few consumer desktops can take advantage of 64-bit. Intel has to drop the other shoe and deem that the broad, mass market for consumers is ready for 64-bit. I predict that will not be this year. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-future-tech-microsoft-la-625f49]]
- AMD64/x86-64 became the dominant desktop and server ISA: Windows XP x64 Edition shipped Apr 2005, Intel adopted EM64T 2004, AMD64 displaced IA-64 in server market by 2006 — fully validating the 2003 inflection. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-beats-apple-5399ec]]
- Intel officially announced EM64T (later Intel 64) Feb 2004 and shipped production Nocona-core Xeon with EM64T Jun 2004, expanded through 2004-2005 — exactly matching Kastner's 'later this year' prediction. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-s-opteron-a-ecf5a9]]
- That means users really aren't getting any benefit out of 64-bit instructions on Athlon or Apple. Neither Apple nor AMD users are truly taking advantage of 64-bit computing at this point, largely because of the lack of applications optimized for the technology. `[ps=4]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-beats-apple-5399ec]]
- Tune in a year from now, and then we'll have a better idea of how these chips run on real-world, 64-bit applications. `[ps=0]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-beats-apple-5399ec]]
