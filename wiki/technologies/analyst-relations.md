# Analyst Relations (AR) Practice

> The structured practice of managing relationships between technology vendors and industry analysts; Aberdeen training formalizes the AR protocol from the analyst side.


## Top observations

- Experienced Aberdeen analysts have been burned: positive and negative analysis, only negative printed; quotes taken out of context; confidential info printed `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- There is a thin line between when suppliers are getting to know Aberdeen/analyst before acquiring services and when they are looking for free advice continuously `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- PR representative: gets paid to schedule meetings; gets to keep client if analyst says nice things; may act as court reporter or take active role `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Trade weeklies have a noon Thursday deadline; expect calls on Wednesday; product announcements are typically on Tuesdays `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- The reporters are frequently looking for Aberdeen bias — especially if your analysis differs from theirs `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Report the announcement; quote analyst opinion; quote customer impact; summarize editor opinion `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- The analyst is in charge of the meeting; the supplier gave them 1-2 hours of valuable analyst time `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Ask at any time: What are the three major points you want me to take away from this meeting? `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Aberdeen described to investment community as fast growing and profitable; privately held; moving towards IPO as soon as possible `[ps=2]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Aberdeen analysts dress formally because they are also business people and consultants; overdressed better than underdressed `[ps=2]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- This is the start of a nuclear war between IBM and DEC `[ps=1]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Never say or imply that Aberdeen will write positive comments if a supplier becomes a client — that's extortion `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Investors Business Daily does not like to quote individual analysts; Business Week quotes analysts on a relatively rare basis `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Keep the session interactive; base comments on 'Aberdeen research shows...' where possible `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Do not assume the presenter prepared the presentation; politely but pointedly state the Aberdeen view; use company not 'you' for negative comments `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Call prospect and say: 'The press has been asking me about your announcement and this is what I have been telling them' `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Call prospect at 3:00 PM on Friday: 'I am planning my schedule for next month and have limited time. Do you think I should schedule start of this project?' `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Target audience: supplier executives who do not yet know Aberdeen or whose knowledge comes from intermediaries (PR firms, press quotes) — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Try to relax them at the start by getting off the subject; talk family, hobbies, travel; say something nice; ease tension — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
- Aberdeen web site gets approximately 40,000 hits per month, mostly from users — [[study-aberdeen-training-day-2-representing-abe-38ef4b]]
