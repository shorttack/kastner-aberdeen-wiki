# Apple/IBM PowerPC G5 (PowerPC 970)

> IBM PowerPC 970 (64-bit, POWER4-derived) shipped in Apple Power Mac G5 Aug 2003; never reached 3 GHz; replaced by Intel transition 2006


## Top observations

- IBM failed to deliver a 3 GHz PowerPC G5 within 12 months as promised at the Jun 2003 introduction; G5 topped out at 2.7 GHz desktop (2005) and never shipped in laptop form due to heat/power — directly prompting Steve Jobs' WWDC Jun 2005 Intel-transition announcement. `[ps=5]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- Apple announced Intel transition WWDC Jun 2005, shipped first Intel Macs Jan 2006, completed transition by Aug 2006 — validating Kastner's implicit skepticism that PowerPC G5 would retain a unique performance position. Power Mac Pro with Intel Xeon shipped Aug 2006. `[ps=4]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-amd-beats-apple-5399ec]]
- Apple completed PowerPC-to-Intel transition by Aug 2006 (32 months after this article). Mac Pro with Intel Xeon replaced Power Mac G5 Aug 2006; partial validation of Kastner's 2003 enthusiasm (G5 did deliver the 2003 performance bump) but his 'bragging rights for the first time in several years' proved fleeting. `[ps=4]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- For a new chip, this is about as good as it gets. The performance improvements are so evident that only a few minutes in the store demo area will convince many buyers that it's time to upgrade. `[ps=3]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- The G5 is a hot box and one which for the first time in several years gives Mac owners some bragging rights versus their industry-standard brethren. `[ps=3]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- Graphics designers and image manipulators will see vast improvements in their ability to apply filters because the code has been optimized for 64-bit computation. Customers in search of simulation as close to reality as possible will be willing to pay the G5's higher prices. `[ps=3]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- The G5 processor, after months and years of speculation and anticipation, finally delivers a better, faster, cheaper, smaller solution for Apple. `[ps=2]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- IDC's Alan Promisel: 'While the G5 is aimed at Apple's core audience — high-end users, gamers and audio-visual producers — the 64-bit desktop computer is being closely watched. It's definitely an architecture the commercial IT managers are keeping an eye on.' `[ps=2]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
- Apple Power Mac G5 models: 1.6 GHz (256 MB 333 MHz DDR, $2,000), 1.8 GHz (512 MB 400 MHz DDR, $2,400), dual 2.0 GHz ($3,000 later this month). First PC with 64-bit processing and 1 GHz front-side bus; 6.4 GB/s memory bandwidth; 16 GB/s in dual. 100,000+ pre-orders since Jun 23 introduction. `[ps=0]` — [[study-technology-news-hardware-apple-s-power-m-91980c]]
