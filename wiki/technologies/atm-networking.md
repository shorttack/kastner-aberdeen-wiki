# ATM (Asynchronous Transfer Mode) Networking

> Virtual circuit switching alternative to IP; Aberdeen cited as too complex for campus environments vs. Bay's switch-node approach.


## Summary




## Top observations

- ATM migration generally too complex for campus network environments and not always relevant `[ps=5]` — [[study-1997-bay-networks--switch-node-routing-s-4d9885]]
- ATM failed to achieve campus dominance; IP-based switching (Gigabit Ethernet) became campus backbone standard by 2000 `[ps=5]` — [[study-1997-bay-networks--switch-node-routing-s-4d9885]]
- ATM posted healthy growth in 1997 as WAN and LAN backbone but failed as end-to-end desktop technology `[ps=5]` — [[study-networ~1-96462b]]
- ATM was deployed as backbone in telco networks while frame relay persisted as edge access through early 2000s; both eventually superseded by MPLS/IP, not one replacing the other as Aberdeen predicted `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-frame-relay-access-devices]]
- Will accelerate complex high-volume applications `[ps=4]` — [[study-nti-2-open-systems-1dd3af]]
- Cell-relay ATM for high-throughput T3+ real-time apps being developed to integrate with frame relay base; ATM targets integrated voice/video/data `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-frame-relay-access-devices]]
- Cheap, infinite network bandwidth with ATM by 2001 `[ps=2]` — [[study-1996-sequent-38f0b1]]
- ATM largely displaced by Gigabit Ethernet for LAN; retained niche in telecom WANs — [[study-1996-sequent-38f0b1]]
