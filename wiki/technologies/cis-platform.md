# Customer Interaction Software (CIS)

> Aberdeen's term for what became CRM software; encompassed sales, service, helpdesk, field service.


## Top observations

- Literally hundreds of CIS suppliers in the market in 1996; study profiles over 40 — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Second Aberdeen CIS report; first published May 1995; 1996 edition priced at US$695 — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Three audiences: (1) business executives choosing CIS, (2) IS executives implementing CIS, (3) CIS suppliers analyzing market position — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Issue tracking, escalation, knowledge base for internal IT and external customer support — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Dispatch, scheduling, parts management, on-site service tracking — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Outbound/inbound calling campaigns, lead management, scripting — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Profiled CIS supplier; strong in IT helpdesk/service management; Action Request System product — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Profiled CIS supplier; focused on customer support and CRM; later acquired by Nortel for $2.1B in 1998 — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Companies rushing to install CIS systems cannot afford to make mistakes. Market filled with hundreds of suppliers — only a few will be around over the long term — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Growth and profit-oriented companies gaining market share through coordinated sales tactics and superior customer service via CIS — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Most of hundreds of CIS suppliers will not survive long-term; only a few will remain to support users; buyers must choose right the first time — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Siebel Systems became dominant CRM vendor with ~$1.5B revenue before Oracle acquisition for $5.8B (2006); SAP CRM and Salesforce.com (2000) also emerged; hundreds of small vendors were consolidated or disappeared — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
- Over 40 CIS software suppliers profiled; 5 systems integrators profiled; 3 hardware/platform suppliers profiled — [[study-aberdeen-1996-managing-customers-next-gen-helpdesk]]
