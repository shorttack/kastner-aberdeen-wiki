## Top observations

- Kastner's expert witness / Intel advisory period (2008-2015) coincided with cloud infrastructure and mobile-first transformation. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-appendix-career-timeline]]
- Cloud, SaaS, and AI are genuine transitions that will encounter same organizational inertia, misaligned incentives, gap between architecture and enterprise willingness to reorganize. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-epilogue-argument-with-reality]]
- Cloud infrastructure failed to come from established vendors like Dell, HP, and IBM as Kastner expected. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Infrastructure migrated to shared utility services managed by Amazon, Microsoft, Google — not to enterprise-owned commodity hardware as would have been a reasonable extrapolation of earlier trends. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Cloud infrastructure is one of the intermediate automation layers preceding AI inference in the full automation progression. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- The infrastructure will move faster than the institution, as it always has. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-epilogue-argument-with-reality]]
- AI inference on commodity hardware; foundation models available to any developer with a credit card. Constraint is not whether machine can do the thing but whether organization can govern it. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-epilogue-argument-with-reality]]
- Big hype trend; Salesforce.com as leading example `[ps=3]` — [[study-2010-intel-ia-continuum-wp-1-2-fc5653]]
- Emergence of public cloud as dominant enterprise infrastructure: understood compute commoditization but did not anticipate infrastructure migrating to shared utility services managed by Amazon, Microsoft, Google rather than enterprise-owned commodity hardware. `[ps=3]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- The Internet boom funded the cloud infrastructure that commoditized enterprise servers, following Pattern 2. — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Most of the time, Web applications hosted in the cloud work OK; the limiter is network bandwidth, which affects speed and reliability (e.g., data lost in flight). Plus you have to trust your online cloud service to backup and secure your data. — [[study-washington-times-kellner-google-chrome-o-02e6a9]]
