# Corporate desktop PC / business PC

> Enterprise business desktop computers; Dell OptiPlex, HP Compaq, IBM/Lenovo ThinkCentre; commodity hardware with multi-year refresh cycles


## Top observations

- Kastner cites Dell as the canonical example: unit shipments up almost 30% year-over-year, but revenues up only 18% — the revenue treadmill deflation compels hardware vendors onto. `[ps=5]` — [[study-tech-investor-deflation-the-dropping-dol-c85a61]]
- About 43% of desktops and 54% of laptops are replaced with a philosophy of 'when the need is justified', 'when they break', or 'when a new application requires it'. `[ps=4]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Those who replace PCs on a catch-as-catch-can basis also experience higher costs of ownership, according to my research, due to higher support costs. The TCO gap will widen further as the benefits of stable image platforms are not attained by this class. `[ps=4]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- The schedule is 36 months (desktops), converging with ~42 months for laptops. `[ps=3]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Industries with the stingiest Yankees are healthcare and retail. Manufacturing is around the average, and financial services and - gasp - governments are least likely to wait for something to go wrong. `[ps=3]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Global corporate PC spending recovered strongly 2004-2007; 3-year replacement cycle became the de facto corporate standard through the 2010s. `[ps=3]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- The tech industry is inherently deflationary — therefore more resilient than most sectors to macro deflation. `[ps=3]` — [[study-tech-investor-deflation-the-dropping-dol-c85a61]]
- By Peter Kastner, the Aberdeen Group (InternetWeek); EVP and Chief Research Officer for the Consumer Digital Technology Practice at Aberdeen Group. `[ps=0]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Aberdeen/InternetWeek survey completed by over 250 readers managing about 750,000 business PCs; top-10 respondents manage over 550,000 users. `[ps=0]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Six deals of 10,000+ PCs totaling 70,000 PCs were done in the last twelve months. `[ps=0]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Three companies are now buying 30,000 PCs each and another handful of big orders bring the forward-looking total to 160,000. `[ps=0]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Corporate IT managers can order a desktop machine with a 1.8-, 2.0-, 2.2-, or 2.4-GHz Pentium 4 microprocessor and pay the exact same price at checkout. — [[study-techno-news-blog-6-713812]]
