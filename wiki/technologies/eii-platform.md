# Enterprise Information Integration (EII)

> Leaves data sources in place; real-time ETL; cross-data-source querying without copying; creates global metadata repository; distinct from EAI and ETL; market <$200M in 2003


## Top observations

- EII market less than $200 million; 80% growth projected for 2003 — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Business Objects EII revenue estimate: $15 million (2003 projected) — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Venetica EII revenue estimate: $7-10 million (2003 projected) — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Attunity, XAware, Nimble Technologies: each $4-7 million EII revenue estimate (2003 projected) — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- EII projected to grow to $250M+ market within 2 years if major hardware/database suppliers successfully enter market — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- EII to become key technology in $7.5 billion market as incorporated in portal, EAI, BI, and BPI solutions — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Database administration costs now dominate TCO of applications below 500-user level and continue to increase in importance for all sizes — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Slight web site disadvantage can cause 10-20% of installed base to walk away even in early e-commerce era — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- For every $1 spent on EII software licenses, $1.25 to $1.50 spent on professional services for implementation — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Business benefit of EII increases exponentially with successive integration of each related application and database — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Most M&A activity over next 2 years will center on larger infrastructure solution providers acquiring newcomer EII suppliers — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- IBM and BEA quickest to respond to EII opportunity; Oracle and Microsoft have not shown full appreciation of EII potential — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Distinct EII market exists, beginning 'high-end user per-project' stage; driven by need to rationalize multiple database access to cut admin costs and leverage cross-database proprietary information — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- No single supplier dominates EII market; most EII suppliers are small companies 1-4 years old; market awareness began 2001 — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Sybase open veneer provides common SQL front-end to its own and other relational databases; lets developers write to common SQL; does not allow cross-supplier transactions — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- EII visibility grown dramatically over last year, especially due to IBM's announcement of Information Integrator products — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Six enabling technologies for EII in 2003: application servers, Java, XML, database administration technologies, enterprise portals, and Web services — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- Aberdeen EII Technology Segmentation Model: EAI technology (30+ vendors), Database technology (IBM/Microsoft/Oracle/Sybase), XML messaging (20+ vendors), EII solution (Attunity/Business Objects/Ipedo/XAware/MetaMatrix/Venetica/BEA/Nimble/IBM) — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- EII typically lacks persistence technology; combination of EII and ODS is better than either separately for data-store size and performance; EII/mid-tier ODS enables 'have it your way' architecture — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
- IT buyers should assess existing information aggregation tools, supplement where necessary, create long-range plan to use EII as ongoing effort to amass metadata about all enterprise data sources — [[study-aberdeen-eii-2003-ea3f1a]]
