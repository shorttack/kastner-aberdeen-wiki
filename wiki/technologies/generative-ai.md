# Generative AI

> Kastner's 2026 assessment: doing to knowledge work what the minicomputer did to batch processing in the 1970s. Impact on workers positive and negative. Adoption primarily a 2030s story.


## Top observations

- As enterprises integrate AI inference engines with operational systems in 2026, the integration tax is being collected again on a new layer. The pattern is unchanged. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- In 2026: machine inference abundant and cheap; human judgment and creativity the scarce resource. Jobs resisting automation are those requiring contextual judgment, relationship management, navigating ambiguity. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Jobs resisting automation are not those requiring most education or most physical skill — they are those requiring most contextual judgment, relationship management, and ability to navigate ambiguity with incomplete information. `[ps=5]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- Generative AI in 2026 is doing to knowledge work what the minicomputer did to batch processing in the 1970s: making a previously scarce resource suddenly abundant and disruptive. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- AI inference is handling the next layer of paper — summarization, pattern recognition, first-draft generation, routine analysis. The question is: what should we do with the time automation frees? `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- The impact of generative AI on workers is positive and negative — the same pattern as prior compute-democratization waves. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- The same economic inversion that replaced expensive scarce compute with cheap abundant compute is now replacing expensive knowledge-work tasks with cheap AI inference, making human contextual judgment the new scarce resource. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
- AI adoption across large and small enterprises is much more a 2030s story than a 2020s story. `[ps=2]` — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
