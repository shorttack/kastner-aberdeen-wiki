# IBM mainframe (S/370 / S/390 / zSeries)

> The proposed centralized architecture; IBM ES/9000 series mainframe family in use circa 1990-1992. Peak CPU hit 95-100% under production load. Reused from cache.


## Top observations

- Primary platform requiring remediation due to COBOL date handling — [[study-1998-y2k-report-f512fb]]
- If MIS is the competitive advantage of the 1990s, mainframes do not provide a differentiator. New mainframes are not good long-term investment. — [[study-aberdeen-megatrends-mfa-cs-mis-1993-05-98fc87]]
- HP heterogeneous integration is adequate but not world-leading; modest IBM-mainframe integration skills. — [[study-hp-analyst-phone-call-6-204870]]
- Rewrite — write application from scratch on Web server — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Migrate and upgrade — move mainframe app to web server then web-enable it — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Upgrade in place — web-enable mainframe application while keeping it on mainframe — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Paradoxically positive — made it easier to keep and web-enable mainframe apps rather than replace them — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Migration or rewriting results in applications that are less scalable and robust than original mainframe apps — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- IBM mainframe (z/OS) remains in active production at major banks and government agencies nearly 30 years after study — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Senior IS managers should reconsider rewriting or migrating mainframe apps; use new mainframe-software tools to web-enable in place — [[study-sirius-web-enablement-b81ea8]]
- Went through a box of computer paper to find the single IBM performance estimate sheet that proved the mainframe + DB2 architecture could not support the required terminal counts and average response time — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- By 1990 project scope changed dramatically to a massive centralized system operated out of Tallahassee, creating the architectural bottleneck — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- System crashed again in June 1992 after HRS took over operations from EDS — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- System response times initially measured in minutes — completely unacceptable for interactive welfare processing — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- By fall 1994 CPU was operating at 95-100% capacity during business hours vs. industry average of 65%; staff worked nights and Saturdays to reduce load — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- Response times eventually improved to 3-4 seconds after heroic effort and schedule changes — still slow for interactive casework — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
- Whether single mainframe in Tallahassee could scale to handle peak load across 1000+ sites simultaneously was a fundamental red flag in the IBM/EDS proposal — [[study-unisys-florida-hrs-expert-witness-tallah-5b4524]]
