# IBM System/36

> Installed base declined from ~87,000 sites (1992) to ~42,000 sites (1996); declining at 20%+ per year; hardware parts increasingly unavailable


## Top observations

- Installed base declining at a rate in excess of 20% per year; even third-parties finding support less profitable `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Majority of System/36 owners prefer NOT to migrate due to high satisfaction with stable platform; yet universally say 'it is time to move' due to external support ecosystem collapse `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Y2K compliance deadline proved real; System/36 installed base dropped to near zero by 2000; IBM's 90%+ migration rate to AS/400 solutions validated both the urgency and Aberdeen's migration recommendation `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Most System/36 applications will become dysfunctional well before January 1, 2000 due to two-digit year fields and forecasting date ranges `[ps=2]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Declined from approximately 87,000 sites (1992) to projected 42,000 sites (1996) — decline of more than 50% in four years `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Over 90% of System/36 sites that migrated in 1995 moved to AS/400 solutions — mainly the Advanced 36 `[ps=0]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- Most System/36 applications will become dysfunctional well before January 1, 2000 — forecasting and planning use future dates starting years ahead; SSP Year 2000 compliant apps very hard to find — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
- System/36 hardware design and operating system underlying architecture is over 20 years old; has natural limitations compared to more modern systems — [[study-aberdeen-1996-evaluating-system36-migration-strategies]]
