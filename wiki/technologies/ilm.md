# Information Lifecycle Management (ILM)

> Central business concept: data moves through lifecycle stages; Pools of Storage framed as the economic best-practice implementation of ILM; ILM later became core enterprise data management concept


## Summary




## Top observations

- IT organizations need to focus on information life cycle and how content progressively moves through storage tiers as a function of value and access requirements `[ps=5]` — [[study-04-mid-line-storage-v2-whitepaper-7db9bc]]
- As data amount increases frequency of access to particular piece drops; compliance with new regulations will further reinforce this tendency `[ps=5]` — [[study-04-mid-line-storage-v2-whitepaper-7db9bc]]
- More copies being made of content to ensure faster access and better data protection `[ps=5]` — [[study-04-mid-line-storage-v2-whitepaper-7db9bc]]
- Manages 'content' through different stages of its life using policy-driven software automation; assigns data to least expensive storage device without compromising service `[ps=5]` — [[study-09-maxtor-workshop-prep-guide-a218dc]]
- ILM is a next-decade initiative; no single vendor can deliver on ILM vision anytime soon `[ps=5]` — [[study-10-maxtor-workshop-02-09-eod-53f22b]]
- Different application access patterns justify different storage tiers with distinct cost-performance profiles `[ps=5]` — [[study-15-classification-key-applications-55b6be]]
- Three data types: Structured; Semi-Structured; Unstructured — each with percentage allocation `[ps=5]` — [[study-18-maxtor-strategy-event-template-faeb40]]
- Big concept short on solutions — no one can deliver on the ILM vision anytime soon. `[ps=5]` — [[study-aberdeen-preso-for-emc-final-storage-5-d855b8]]
- Immediate services opportunity around ILM despite long product roadmap. `[ps=5]` — [[study-aberdeen-preso-for-emc-final-storage-5-d855b8]]
- Ageing (value and use of content change as content ages); Freezing (content changes from dynamic to fixed); Accumulation (data grows; access per piece drops); Redundancy (more copies for faster access and data protection) `[ps=5]` — [[study-midline-storage-white-paper-jun2003]]
- At least 20% of enterprise data fits the definition of inactive/seldom-used online storage appropriate for high-capacity/low-cost ATA `[ps=5]` — [[study-midline-storage-white-paper-jun2003]]
- Content changes from dynamic to fixed as updates cease — becoming read-only and therefore fixed content `[ps=4]` — [[study-04-mid-line-storage-v2-whitepaper-7db9bc]]
- IT organizations must think about storage from a quality of service (QoS) perspective — not all applications require the same service levels `[ps=4]` — [[study-04-mid-line-storage-v2-whitepaper-7db9bc]]
- Lack of effective information management software and concurrent lack of information policies will be the major inhibitor for aggressive adoption of tiered storage `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- 1.ILM 2.Pools of Storage 3.Disk Drives; strategy is to drive buying discussion to Pools of Storage in 2004 while nodding to ILM `[ps=4]` — [[study-10-maxtor-workshop-02-09-eod-53f22b]]
- Driver 6: Staging data on devices based on an information lifecycle plan. `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-preso-for-emc-final-storage-5-d855b8]]
- The 'decade-long battle' for ILM leadership was won not by a single vendor but by cloud storage economics. EMC, IBM, and HP competed through the 2000s, but by 2010-2015 cloud object storage (AWS S3, Azure Blob) disrupted traditional tiered storage ILM frameworks. The battle outcome was ultimately displacement rather than vendor consolidation. `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-preso-for-emc-final-storage-5-d855b8]]
- ILM definition is too vague, but vagueness gives ability to morph the concept over time as products mature. `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-preso-for-emc-final-storage-5-d855b8]]
- IT must adopt QoS perspective; not all applications require same service levels; create formal or informal SLAs; long-term: storage utility as part of information utility `[ps=4]` — [[study-midline-storage-white-paper-jun2003]]
- Enterprises that manage ILM effectively will: cut disk storage costs; balance performance to need; focus IT administrator resources; achieve greater scalability and flexibility `[ps=4]` — [[study-midline-storage-white-paper-jun2003]]
