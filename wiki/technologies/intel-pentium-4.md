# Intel Pentium 4 (2.4 GHz)

> NetBurst microarchitecture CPU; high clock speeds 1.4-3.8 GHz; last of the Pentium brand before Core 2 Duo (2006)


## Summary




## Top observations

- Desktop MHz goes from 2.2 to 3.6 in 18 months `[ps=5]` — [[study-dct-segment-priorities-matrix-2003]]
- Pentium 4 reached 3.6 GHz with the Prescott F-series / Pentium 4 560 launched June 21 2004 (3.6 GHz) — within the anticipated timeline; NetBurst hit its 3.8 GHz ceiling with P4 570J by November 2004 before thermal limits forced the architectural pivot to Core. `[ps=5]` — [[study-dct-segment-priorities-matrix-2003]]
- Judging by Intel's buoyant Q3 guidance and capacity-driven memory price increases in the past couple of months, it's fair to say the buying drought of the past couple of years is fading away. `[ps=5]` — [[study-pc-replacement-survey-one-size-fits-none-73e2e0]]
- Intel cancelled the 4GHz Pentium 4 roadmap and pivoted to the Core microarchitecture (Yonah 2006, Conroe 2006), confirming Kastner's diagnosis that the clock-speed race had hit a wall. `[ps=5]` — [[study-power-macs-won-t-hit-3ghz-this-year-anal-73ad0f]]
- P4 2.4 GHz falling to same $900 category as P4 1.8/2.0 GHz in January 2003, driven by November 3.06 GHz HT P4 introduction; P4 1.8 GHz beginning to disappear; P4 2.0 GHz remains strong option `[ps=4]` — [[study-dct-intel-processor-prices-2003-01-mx]]
- Kastner predicts 'end is near for the 2.0 GHz processor' because with HT launch Intel now produces three differently structured P4 processor families; P4 2.4 and 2.0 GHz cannot occupy same price category long `[ps=4]` — [[study-dct-intel-processor-prices-2003-01-mx]]

- G5 is legitimate competitor to 32-bit workstations; Intel will close gap by turning up GHz crank `[ps=3]` — [[study-dct-apple-powermac-g5-2003-06-mx]]
