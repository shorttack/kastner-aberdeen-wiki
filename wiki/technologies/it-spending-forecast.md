# IT-spending forecast (annual industry outlook)

> Annual quantified IT-spending growth forecasts from IDC, Gartner, Forrester, Aberdeen; multi-billion-dollar industry around global tech-budget forecasting


## Top observations

- Aberdeen Group is predicting a global expansion in information technology spending in the 3%-4% range — back-end loaded in 2003 due to Q1 concerns about the economy and war. — [[study-remarks-of-peter-kastner-to-the-massachu-96335b]]
- Global IT spending grew roughly 3-4% in 2003 (Gartner reported ~4%; IDC ~3%), matching Aberdeen/Kastner forecast. — [[study-remarks-of-peter-kastner-to-the-massachu-96335b]]
- Competing analysts project closer to 3% 2003 IT-spending growth — half the IDC forecast — reflecting continued industry skepticism about the pace of post-dot-com recovery. — [[study-tech-forecasts-idc-keeps-score-114e08]]
- Worldwide IT spending grew approximately 5-6% in 2003 per subsequent IDC 2004 data — broadly validating the IDC forecast discussed here, though well below pre-dot-com trend. — [[study-tech-forecasts-idc-keeps-score-114e08]]
- Aberdeen projects total tech-sector revenues (hardware, software, IT services, excluding heavy telecom) will grow from $446.1 billion (2001) to $465.3 billion (2002), a 4.3% increase. — [[study-tech-s-best-hope-pockets-of-prosperity-2-17d6cf]]
- Aberdeen projects IT services revenues growing from $173.9 billion (2001) to $183.7 billion (2002), a 5.6% increase. — [[study-tech-s-best-hope-pockets-of-prosperity-2-17d6cf]]
- Aberdeen projects software revenues climbing from $93.7 billion (2001) to $100 billion (2002), a 6.7% increase — fastest among major tech segments. — [[study-tech-s-best-hope-pockets-of-prosperity-2-17d6cf]]
- Aberdeen projects hardware revenues growing only from $178.4 billion (2001) to $181.4 billion (2002), a 1.7% increase — slowest among major tech segments. — [[study-tech-s-best-hope-pockets-of-prosperity-2-17d6cf]]
