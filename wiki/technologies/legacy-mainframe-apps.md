# Legacy COBOL/Mainframe Applications

> 35% of companies use legacy mainframe for critical business processes; Industry Average 39% vs BIC 24% vs Laggard 34%


## Top observations

- Most legacy mainframe apps use two-digit dates and are not Y2K safe `[ps=5]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-year-2000-transition-opportunity-reconstruction]]
- Large and pervasive (100B LOC); hard to detect (multiple languages, missing documentation/source code); time-consuming to fix; interdependence makes parallel fixing difficult `[ps=5]` — [[study-aberdeen-1997-picks-right-tools-year-2000]]
- Up to 80% of application inventory may be shelfware `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1996-year-2000-transition-opportunity-reconstruction]]
- Minimum 100 billion lines of code to check worldwide `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1997-picks-right-tools-year-2000]]
- Over 1,000 person-years to manually check the 200 million lines of code in a typical large enterprise `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1997-picks-right-tools-year-2000]]
- Y2K represents a once-in-generation technology crisis requiring massive sustained effort; tool selection critical to outcome `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-1997-picks-right-tools-year-2000]]
- Worldwide Y2K spending totaled approximately $100 billion ($365 per US resident); spending peaked 1998-1999; transition on 1/1/2000 passed without major systemic failures; Aberdeen's scale estimates proved directionally accurate `[ps=3]` — [[study-aberdeen-1997-picks-right-tools-year-2000]]
