# Mail-in rebate (physical paper)

> $10B+ annual industry 2002; consumer-hostile breakage-dependent model


## Summary




## Top observations

- Best Buy eliminated mail-in rebates effective early 2008, fulfilling the 2-year phase-out pledge made in this 2005 article `[ps=5]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- Major retailers (Staples, Best Buy) shifted to instant or easy-rebate programs 2005-2008; mail-in rebates declined from $10B (2002) to ~$5B (2008) and continued falling `[ps=5]` — [[study-rebate-check-recordonline-van-vlack-d7d600]]
- Do manufacturers hope consumers will forget about redeeming the rebate? Absolutely. A lot of people do forget about it. `[ps=4]` — [[study-cuna-win-rebate-runaround-2004-9da530]]
- The dirty little secret of the industry has been that 40% of consumers don't file the rebate and that 20% bungle the paperwork `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-debate-smartmoney-grant-80650c]]
- Outright fraud is less common nowadays for mail-in rebates... the FTC goes after companies that don't send out rebates in a timely manner or fail to deliver on them at all `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-debate-smartmoney-grant-80650c]]
- Mail-in rebates collapsed from $10B annually (2002) to <$500M by early 2020s per industry trackers; replaced by instant rebates, cashback cards, gift cards, and online-activation codes `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-debate-smartmoney-grant-80650c]]
- Peter S. Kastner, director of consulting firm Vericours Inc., estimates fully 40% of all rebates never get redeemed because consumers fail to apply for them or their applications are rejected — translating into more than $2 billion extra revenue for retailers and suppliers each year `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- Best Buy announced in this article it plans to phase out mail-in rebates within 2 years `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- FTC increased enforcement on rebate advertising; staff guidance on 'after rebate' pricing disclosure followed CT AG-style investigations nationally 2006-2010 `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- Article frames mail-in rebates as under regulator pressure and facing retailer retreat; implicit prediction of decline `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- By 2010 most major US CE retailers had eliminated traditional mail-in rebates in favor of instant-rebate pricing or online-claim systems; TCA/Parago/Young America rebate-processor sector contracted substantially 2008-2014 `[ps=4]` — [[study-great-rebate-runaround-businessweek-grow-00f459]]
- fully 40% of all rebates never get redeemed because consumers fail to apply for them or their applications are rejected `[ps=4]` — [[study-palm-pre-199-really-299-cloudave-a0a25f]]
- By 2013, US wireless carriers transitioned to installment device-payment plans (T-Mobile JUMP, Verizon EDGE, AT&T Next, Sprint iPhone for Life) replacing rebate models `[ps=4]` — [[study-palm-pre-199-really-299-cloudave-a0a25f]]
- 60 percent of all consumers entitled to a rebate never get the refund. Manufacturers see increased sales, retailers get more sales at full price, and even the government benefits — it collects more sales tax because the tax levy is computed on higher, full-retail prices. `[ps=4]` — [[study-rebate-check-recordonline-van-vlack-d7d600]]
- Rebates motivate people to buy at full price, and many consumers never send in their rebates for redemption. Manufacturers have the better of two worlds when they use rebates. `[ps=4]` — [[study-rebate-check-recordonline-van-vlack-d7d600]]
- Rebates are a marketing game... the game is rigged `[ps=4]` — [[study-rebate-check-recordonline-van-vlack-d7d600]]
- 40% don't file the rebate and 20% bungle the paperwork — 60% total breakage (SmartMoney 2006) `[ps=4]` — [[study-rebate-check-recordonline-van-vlack-d7d600]]
- Only 40% of consumers submit rebates they are entitled to, 40% submit the rebate and successfully get a check, and 20% have problems `[ps=3]` — [[study-cuna-win-rebate-runaround-2004-9da530]]
- The shadier ones are actually making it increasingly harder for shoppers to redeem their rebate offers, says Edgar Dworsky... A common ploy? Ever shortening mail-in deadlines. Sometimes you'll have just 14 days from time of purchase. `[ps=3]` — [[study-great-rebate-debate-smartmoney-grant-80650c]]
