# Digital Multia (Universal Client)

> Compact all-in-one enterprise desktop; photo-album footprint; discontinued ~1997


## Top observations

- turn-key universal enterprise client desktop; NOT positioned as a personal computer — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- $500–$800 above comparable PC desktop (depending on options) — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- approximately the size of a family photo album — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- supports both Alpha 64-bit RISC and Intel Pentium 100 models — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- ZIF socket design allows processor upgrades as new architectures become available — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- 256 KB standard, upgradeable to 512 KB — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- TCP/IP, IPX/SPX, DECnet, LAT, NetBEUI, X Serial Line Protocol; NFS in X Server option — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS admins can remotely configure desktops, control application availability, enable/disable hardware (e.g., floppy), schedule software updates, distribute software — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS management and support costs will decrease; fewer remote site administrators required — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- End-user efficiency increases because systems are made more available through central IS management — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- Digital is the only computer supplier that has listened to and acted on IS enterprise client requirements — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- Organizations requiring turn-key access to multiple server types: multiple transports, diverse apps (Windows, Unix X, VT, 3270), and centralized IS configuration management — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- Single desktop device provides simultaneous access to mainframes, Unix servers, PC servers, and the World Wide Web via icon clicks — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- DIY assembly of equivalent third-party emulators and protocols would be less stable and less robust than integrated Multia — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS administrators can remotely enable or disable floppy drive; important for government and financial security — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- Administrators can remotely manage downed desktops, obtain system configuration info, schedule software updates, and perform software distribution — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- Multia enables IS and end-user organizations to stop working at cross purposes — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS management challenge: hardware and software inventory redundancy — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS management challenge: Tower-of-Babel inter-company communications from lack of standardization — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
- IS management challenge: compromised corporate data security from lax distributed computing data management — [[study-aberdeen-1995-digital-multia]]
