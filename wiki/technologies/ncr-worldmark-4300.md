# NCR WorldMark 4300 midrange server

> Pentium Pro 4-way (8-way target Q2 1997) midrange Windows NT server; NCR Dayton


## Top observations

- NCR largely exited the general-purpose server-systems business by mid-2000s, refocusing on financial-services/retail ATMs and point-of-sale systems plus Teradata (spun off as independent company 2007). `[ps=5]` — [[study-server-deal-turns-rivals-into-allies-seq-bdc3af]]
- The deal makes sense for both vendors and customers. It lets Sequent leverage R&D, while NCR gains channel opportunities for its products. `[ps=2]` — [[study-server-deal-turns-rivals-into-allies-seq-bdc3af]]
- There is no downside for Sequent customers because the NCR boxes do tough work in tough places. It is also an indication that Sequent is serious about a broad line of products and is not cutting corners for an increasingly commodity marketplace. `[ps=2]` — [[study-server-deal-turns-rivals-into-allies-seq-bdc3af]]
- WorldMark 4300 scales up to 4 Pentium Pro processors at launch; NCR expected 8-processor support by second quarter 1997. `[ps=0]` — [[study-server-deal-turns-rivals-into-allies-seq-bdc3af]]
- Analysts estimate the Sequent-NCR reseller deal to be worth $50 million over 12 to 18 months. `[ps=0]` — [[study-server-deal-turns-rivals-into-allies-seq-bdc3af]]
