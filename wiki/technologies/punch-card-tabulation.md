# IBM Punch-Card Tabulating Equipment

> Electromechanical sorters, collators, and accumulators programmed via plugboard wiring. Used at Cornell's Hotel Ezra Cornell weekend. Could not perform conditional logic flexibly enough to automate hotel accounting.


## Top observations

- Upper-class accounting team used donated IBM punch-card tabulating equipment to automate Hotel Ezra Cornell (HEC) weekend books. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- Programmed by physical wiring on a plugboard — patching cables like an old telephone switchboard — not software. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- Could sort, count, and add 80-column card data but could not perform conditional logic (if-then category assignment) with sufficient flexibility via plugboard wiring. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- 'Our eyes were bigger than what the technology could deliver.' Team conceded defeat; fell back on paper receipts and human bookkeepers. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- Punch-card tabulators could not do conditional logic at scale. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- Several months of effort produced an elegant design and an infeasible implementation — the tabulator could not execute required conditional logic. — [[study-volume-1-ch01-waiting-for-automation-1960-1969]]
- The answer arrived in stages: punch-card tabulation, batch processing, OLTP, relational databases, client-server, the web, mobile computing, cloud infrastructure, AI inference. — [[study-volume-1-ch10-the-long-view-1966-2026]]
