# Relational Database Management Systems (RDBMS)

> Identified by Kastner as the architectural force that eventually made Prime INFORMATION’s non-relational, proprietary model a strategic liability; lock-in became a prison when relational databases grew through the mid-1980s.


## Top observations

- Ingres's fate is the clearest lesson of the jungle: in enterprise software, technical superiority is necessary but nowhere near sufficient to survive; the market rewarded Oracle's salesforce over Ingres's engineering. `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- At a BMC Software luncheon at the Park Lane Hotel in London, the first question from UK journalists was 'What proof do you have that the relational model will win out in the market?' — 'Gotta love UK journalists.' `[ps=5]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The database is not a feature of an application but the foundation of an enterprise's information architecture — the central inversion of the whole career. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The database was the workload that decided which hardware won; buyers chose the machine that ran their Oracle or SQL Server fastest per dollar — hardware served the database, not the other way around. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The great enterprise applications of the 1990s (ERP, financials, early CRM) carried no database of their own; this made database vendors the silent partners of the entire applications boom and made database performance an applications problem. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The job was to prod enterprise IS executives past the belief that relational was fine for ad-hoc queries but too slow for real transaction processing, and convert them to database-platform-first purchasing. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The database story and the transaction-processing story are the same story told from two ends: OLTP taught how to measure, and the RDBMS gave something worth measuring. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- 'When I was right, I was right about the direction of the river, not the fate of any particular boat' — right about technology arcs, wrong or agnostic about which vendor would ride them. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The 1993 Report Card inversion — choose the database platform and let it dictate the hardware — is called 'the truest call of my career.' `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The migrated dot-com's new HP 9000/Oracle system went in smoothly and everyone was ecstatic; the following year the dot-com went bust anyway — 'such was the late 1990s Internet land grab.' `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- IBM's own research lab invented the relational model and SQL, then IBM spent roughly fifteen years as a follower in the market it had created — the great paradox of the relational era. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- up to 5x cheaper per transaction `[ps=4]` — [[study-aberdeen-open-oltp-enterprise-managers-k-3fc536]]
- When relational database movement gathered force in mid-1980s, Prime’s non-relational, non-standard application ecosystem had nowhere to migrate. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch04-prime-computer-1979-1981]]
- Prime INFORMATION’s non-relational, non-standard Pick foundation had nowhere to migrate when relational database movement gathered force in mid-1980s. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-ch04-prime-computer-1979-1981]]
- Predicted data analytics would become a strategic capability—correct, but later than forecast; organizational resistance to cross-functional data integration deeper than models captured. `[ps=4]` — [[study-volume-1-epilogue-argument-with-reality]]
- The only honest way to talk about databases was to measure them — TPS, dollars per transaction, price/performance under audited, repeatable load; everything else in the database business was marketing. `[ps=3]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- Five structural calls landed: relational as enterprise foundation (1993); universal/object-relational servers; parallel-scalability breaking the downsizing logjam; x86 killing proprietary big iron by 2003; OLTP going open off the mainframe. `[ps=3]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- The relational database won its argument so completely that the industry stopped having it; that total victory is precisely why the database decade fades from memory, and exactly why it deserves this record. `[ps=3]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
- Ended that same London afternoon at Speaker's Corner shouting about the doom of Y2K, calling the experience cathartic. `[ps=1]` — [[study-2026-kastner-database-decade-rdbms]]
