# SAP HANA

> In-memory database SAP built to replace third-party RDBMS beneath R/3-successor products


## Top observations

- The arc closes where the archive itself did not expect: SAP walked away from every third-party database to build HANA — the one move Kastner explicitly advised against in 2001. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- 2026 rebuttal: Kastner knew in-memory DBs ran 10-100x faster than disk-bound RDBMS even in 2001, but at ~$1/MB ($16,000 for 16GB on a Compaq 4-way) there wasn't enough affordable memory to run Fortune 1000 workloads in-memory yet. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- The real mistake was not the in-memory cost math itself but failing to anticipate the competitive/frictional advantage SAP would gain by replacing Oracle's cost with margin flowing to SAP's own HANA — SAP was not usually early to innovate, but it was with HANA. `[ps=4]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
- HANA has no first-party study in the archive; the in-memory pivot is visible only as retrospective outcome observations embedded inside older studies, leaving this arc closed on secondary rather than primary evidence. `[ps=0]` — [[study-2026-kastner-sap-r3-hana-longitudinal]]
