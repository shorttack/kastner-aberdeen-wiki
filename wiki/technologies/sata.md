# Serial ATA (SATA)

> Primary technology subject of report; 7200 RPM lower performance lower reliability than SCSI/FC but 1/2 to 1/3 TCO savings


## Top observations

- Capacity-oriented technology will be embedded within arrays as a class of capacity managed through current tools `[ps=5]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Over time capacity-oriented storage shipments will reach a quarter or more of the storage array terabytes shipped `[ps=5]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Most receptive applications are (1) mirrors and snapshots for data protection and (2) repositories for fixed content `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Raw survey results suggest 14% of all storage dollars in 2004 would be on SATA/high-capacity low-cost storage `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Total internal storage revenue: $6.8B in 2003 flat through 2007 (0.4% CAGR); TB growth: 364K TB (2003) to 1.29M TB (2007) at 37.2% CAGR; $/GB declining from $19 to $5 `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Market is moving now and can grow toward 50% of new capacity (per executive summary heading) `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Biggest market is for classes of capacity within storage arrays currently being sold; only minority want separate array products `[ps=4]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- There is no place in the datacenter for lower availability at the application level simply because the storage uses capacity-oriented technology `[ps=3]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Capacity-oriented disk storage poses little near or medium term threat to tape; in fact larger disk capacity means more data to transfer to tape for archiving `[ps=2]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
- Internal ATA storage revenue: $283M (2003) growing to $313M (2007) at 2.5% CAGR; share 4.2% to 4.5% `[ps=1]` — [[study-05-idc-sata-report-0872b8]]
