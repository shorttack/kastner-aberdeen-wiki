# Storage Pyramid Framework

> Four-tier model: RAM-based/FC-SCSI/ATA disk/tape; precursor to Pools of Storage framework.


## Top observations

- Ageing (value/use change as content ages); Freezing (changes from dynamic to fixed); Accumulation (very little old data discarded); Redundancy (more and more copies made) `[ps=5]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Content access follows Zipf's Law: frequency of access follows power law distribution from most to least frequently accessed `[ps=5]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Some analysts say tape is dead; Aberdeen position: there is a place for everything and everything in its place; tape meets streaming data demand `[ps=5]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- RAM-related (memory/solid state/disk cache); High Performance Disk (FC/SCSI); Cost-Effective Disk (ATA); Tape `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Key to change is not only price but impact upon IS processes skill sets and organizational structure `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Data: bits forming bit stream; Information: organized bits a person can recognize; Content: information used for some purpose (decision-making understanding enjoyment) `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Structured (database); Semi-structured (text documents); Unstructured (bitmaps) `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Structured: Sort; Semi-structured: Search; Unstructured: Sense `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Content (along with its distribution) is the long-term competitive differentiator; only one company owns its customer and product history data `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Conception/birth (read/write limited); Youth (high read access); Middle age (infrequent access); Old age (flatlined usage) `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Not all content even of same type ages the same; medical image quickly goes to middle age; video may have longer youth; much content is in middle age `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- At pennies per megabyte it may not be cost effective to have individuals clean out regularly; policy-driven cleaning effective only in limited ways `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Physical disk failure (RAID); Logical disk failure (point-in-time copy/backup); Catastrophic site failure (remote mirroring); Unexpected archive demands (offsite archiving) `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Versioning (user copies); Test copy (PIT copy/tape); Historical analysis (data warehouse); Online production copy (broadcast/cached) `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- On-site/Off-site by Online/Nearline/Offline; RAM-based: youth; High-perf disk: youth/middle age; ATA disk: middle age to old age `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- High-performance: uncompleted end-user tasks/WIP/currently retrieved files/high response time frequency; Cost-effective: completed tasks/older messages/not currently required files `[ps=4]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- Even if all four levels cost the same: FC/SCSI would prevail over ATA; RAM-based would prevail over hard disk if scaling issues addressed; hard disk replaces tape only if portability solved `[ps=3]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
- RAM disk: higher cost/higher capacity/faster/inflexible; FC/SCSI disk: medium cost-capacity-speed; ATA disk: lower cost/higher capacity; Tape: lowest cost/highest capacity/slowest/flexible `[ps=3]` — [[study-11-storage-hierarchy-presentation-e84d53]]
