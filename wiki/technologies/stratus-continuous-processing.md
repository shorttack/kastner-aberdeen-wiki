# Stratus Continuous Processing

> Hardware pair-and-spare fault-tolerant architecture; differentiator vs Tandem's NonStop software approach


## Top observations

- Stratus is 'distant second-place competitor' to Tandem in OLTP fault tolerance — [[study-informationweek-tolerant-debitcredit-198-4f3b26]]
- 98.5% reliability standard means computer goes down once every two weeks on statistical average — 'unthinkable for modern manufacturing' — [[study-stratus-computer-is-down-ad-reprints-c19-903ebf]]
- 'Designed not to fail; not once every two weeks, or once every 200 weeks, or once every 2,000 weeks' — [[study-stratus-computer-is-down-ad-reprints-c19-903ebf]]
- Stratus claim: hardware-based fault tolerance from chip-level redundancy adds 'a mere fraction to our cost, and absolutely nothing to your purchase price' — [[study-stratus-computer-is-down-ad-reprints-c19-903ebf]]
- Stratus's hardware fault-tolerant architecture shipped continuously from early 1980s into modern ftServer line and ztC Edge — among the longest-lived commercial fault-tolerant designs — [[study-stratus-computer-is-down-ad-reprints-c19-903ebf]]
- Founded May 1980 to design/manufacture/market supermini-class fault-tolerant computers for OLTP — [[study-stratus-corporate-overview-1982-11-a7e367]]
- Document dates to Kastner's Stratus employer era (1981-1985); reflects positioning that Kastner helped articulate as marketing/sales support staff — [[study-stratus-corporate-overview-1982-11-a7e367]]
- Stratus survived as independent vendor through 2014; later as Stratus Technologies under Penguin Solutions — among very few 1980s minicomputer-era companies still extant — [[study-stratus-corporate-overview-1982-11-a7e367]]
