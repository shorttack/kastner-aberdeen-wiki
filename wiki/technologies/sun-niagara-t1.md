# Sun UltraSPARC T1 (Niagara)

> Chip multithreading processor; up to 8 cores x 8 threads; ~70 W


## Top observations

- Sun pitched Niagara as recapture strategy for users who switched to low-cost Windows/Linux x86 via multithreading and power consumption advantages. `[ps=5]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- Oracle acquired Sun in January 2010; SPARC T-series continued under Oracle through T8 (2017) but never reclaimed x64 market share; by 2017 Oracle laid off the Solaris/SPARC core team. `[ps=5]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- With today's big WSJ story on computer power consumption, Sun's eight cores in a 70-watt envelope is the right recipe. But wouldn't this have been market-turning, say, mid-2002? `[ps=4]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- Niagara is a big help to Sun's SPARC base, but I doubt it will shake the market's penchant for x64-based servers, in Sun's case, AMD Opterons. `[ps=4]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- UltraSPARC T1 (Niagara) servers will be available with 4, 6, or 8 cores; each core capable of 8 threads; maximum server power consumption 70 watts. `[ps=3]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- I recall being briefed on the Niagara roadmap circa 2000. `[ps=0]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
- IBM circulated a poison-pen FUD gram criticizing Niagara launch timing as an attempt to buy momentum with an early product release. `[ps=0]` — [[study-niagara-sun-s-palaver-starter-and-diy-mp-158f17]]
