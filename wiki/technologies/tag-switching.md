# Tag Switching (Cisco)

> Cisco's proprietary approach to combining Layer 2 switching with Layer 3 routing using simple tags; submitted to IETF; evolved into MPLS (Multi-Protocol Label Switching); RFC 3031 published 2001; Tag Switching itself replaced by MPLS standard but was the direct precursor.


## Top observations

- Cisco's Tag Switching evolved into MPLS (Multi-Protocol Label Switching); MPLS RFC 3031 ratified January 2001; MPLS became the dominant carrier backbone and VPN protocol; still fundamental to carrier networks as of 2026 `[ps=5]` — [[study-1997-cabletron-and-ipsilon--linking-secu-adba48]]
- Tag Switching evolved into MPLS (RFC 3031, January 2001); MPLS became fundamental to carrier backbone networks and VPNs; still essential to carrier infrastructure as of 2026; Tag Switching itself obsolete but its successor dominant `[ps=5]` — [[study-1997-cisco-s-core-products-business-inte-bd8697]]
- Cisco's Tag Switching proposed to IETF; direct alternative to Ipsilon's IP Switching approach `[ps=4]` — [[study-1997-cabletron-and-ipsilon--linking-secu-adba48]]
- Combines Layer 2 switching performance with Layer 3 routing scalability; eliminates connection set-up congestion; pre-establishes accelerated paths; enables ATM switch participation in Layer 3 routing `[ps=4]` — [[study-1997-cisco-s-core-products-business-inte-bd8697]]
- Tag Switching promises significant features for internet and enterprise scaling; submitted to IETF; uncertainty about whether it will achieve IOS-level industry-standard enthusiasm `[ps=3]` — [[study-1997-cisco-s-core-products-business-inte-bd8697]]
