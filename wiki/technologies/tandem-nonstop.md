# Tandem NonStop fault-tolerant systems

> Fault-tolerant transaction-processing computer line (Himalaya series); acquired by Compaq 1997, then HP 2001; still sold by HPE as NonStop systems


## Top observations

- ~50% of product revenues from financial sector — [[study-1991-encore-and-metadata-47f414]]
- Migrating from proprietary to MIPS Risc/Unix; transition incomplete during planning period — [[study-1991-encore-and-metadata-47f414]]
- $20,800 per TPS using internal Tandem figures with configurations more comparable to Tolerant — [[study-informationweek-tolerant-debitcredit-198-4f3b26]]
- Tandem is OLTP fault-tolerant 'market leader' in 1986 — [[study-informationweek-tolerant-debitcredit-198-4f3b26]]
- [Pieper's] background at Unix Systems Labs and Software AG means he knows open systems and production computing requirements. — [[study-tandem-opens-up-e020ed]]
- Pieper says the key to Tandem's long-term success is to build partnerships that let its advanced software run on other manufacturers' computers — exploiting NonStop fault-tolerant capabilities and ServerNet technology in Unix and Windows NT environments. — [[study-tandem-opens-up-e020ed]]
- HPE NonStop systems (the Tandem NonStop line) continue in 2025 as a niche high-end fault-tolerant platform for banking, telecom, stock exchanges — now on x86 hardware rather than proprietary NonStop silicon; a durable but narrow survivor of the 1996 pivot. — [[study-tandem-opens-up-e020ed]]
