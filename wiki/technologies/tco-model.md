# Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Modeling

> Aberdeen's TCO calculators incorporated hardware, software licensing, implementation services, maintenance, training, and operational overhead over 3-5 year horizons; became Aberdeen's most commercially potent tool; challenged conventional sticker-price procurement logic.


## Top observations

- TCO calculators for major technology categories became Aberdeen's most commercially potent tool; incorporated hardware, software, implementation, maintenance, training over 3-5 year horizons — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- TCO models positioned Aberdeen's advisory work as quantitatively rigorous rather than qualitatively opinionated; both vendor challenge and endorsement proved the models were landing. — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- Platform $1M cheaper to license might cost $3M more to implement, $2M more to staff, $1.5M more to maintain: net $5.5M more expensive over 5 years. — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- TCO models only as good as their assumptions; vendor disagreements about TCO outcomes were usually disagreements about specific assumptions, creating valuable client conversations. — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- TCO analysis created a defensible methodology for choosing on five-year economics rather than initial price; positioned Aberdeen's advisory as quantitatively rigorous. — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
- Aberdeen was rigorous about documenting and making assumptions visible; conversations about assumptions were often more valuable to clients than the final number. — [[study-volume-1-ch08-aberdeen-go-go-years-1998-2006]]
