# TME 10 NetView Version 5.0 (NT)

> Released September 1997; fully rewrote to exploit Windows APIs; later became IBM Tivoli NetView; still lives as IBM Tivoli NetView for z/OS in mainframe form


## Top observations

- Tivoli completely rewrote TME 10 NetView 5.0 to fully exploit Windows APIs; designed for branch office NT environments with Mid-Level Manager agent architecture — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- Mid-Level Manager agents collect data on branch office devices and filter only relevant data up to centralized server; Central NetView + Regional NetView + MLM agents — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView was first network management product to adopt Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP); reduces false alarms from users turning off terminals — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- Dynamically updated groups of nodes (e.g. 'down routers' 'all printers'); automatic addition when router goes down; improves exception reporting and proactive management — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- NetView uses Netscape browser with combination of Java / JavaScript / HTML formats; view-only at publication time; full security/remote management planned by end 1998 — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView fully exploits Windows APIs; drop-down menus parallel Microsoft Office suite; reduces training time; NNM navigation time-consuming with excessive windows — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- SAM revises multiple locations simultaneously with single button press; NNM requires manual updates at each location; SAM ships on Unix; usable from Unix to execute on NT — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView advantage: Windows-native GUI; Office-parallel drop-down menus; drag-and-drop; sidebar navigation history; reduces training time and cost — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView advantage: prebuilt report formats launchable with single click; dynamic event updates without user intervention; SmartSets for proactive exception reporting — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView advantage: Regional NetView small footprint for branch offices; fraction of cost of full implementation; Mid-Level Managers reduce hardware/admin overhead — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView advantage: first to adopt DHCP; reduces false alarms from powered-off devices; distinguishes blue (off) from red (broken) — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- Integrated network and systems management is a 'two-horse race' between IBM Tivoli TME and Computer Associates CA-Unicenter at enterprise level; HP and Sun lead below enterprise level — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- Released September 1997 — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- Aberdeen highly recommends TME 10 NetView for managers evaluating cost-effective robust scalable NT management options; leadership role in market — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- TME 10 NetView evolved into IBM Tivoli NetView; Tivoli brand became dominant IBM systems management platform through 2000s; NetView for z/OS still active as of 2026 for mainframe management — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
- IBM acquisition prior to v5.0 brought additional technical resources and expertise providing catalyst for significant enhancements from TME 10 NetView version 4.1 — [[study-powerful-network-management-4cb125]]
