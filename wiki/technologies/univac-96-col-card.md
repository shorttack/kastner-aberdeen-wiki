# Univac 96-Column Punch Card

> 96-column format used by Chase Manhattan; incompatible with IBM 80-column cards; required field explosion during migration.


## Top observations

- Rockefeller’s holdings were so large that the standard dividend field could not contain the numbers after column-count reduction; team spent hours hunting for one more column. `[ps=0]` — [[study-volume-1-ch03-physics-of-consulting-1972-1979]]
- Chase Manhattan trust operations relied on Univac 96-column card processing; Univac exit from unit-record business in 1972 forced replacement — [[study-adl-commercial-engagements-kastner-1972-1979-b8b001]]
- Univac 96-column cards vs. IBM 80-column: every Chase field designed for 96 columns had to be exploded into multiple 80-column IBM cards, rebuilding every field boundary. — [[study-volume-1-ch03-physics-of-consulting-1972-1979]]
