# Y2K (Year 2000) Remediation

> Largest enterprise IT initiative of the 1990s


## Top observations

- No catastrophic Y2K failures on 1/1/2000 attributable to major enterprises that followed the recommended remediation timeline; enterprises that started late (post-1998) reported higher cost overruns and near-misses. `[ps=5]` — [[study-ca-y2k-cfo-executive-exchange-1997-c59ca4]]
- Renovate / Replace / Outsource / Retire — four options for each Y2K-affected application. `[ps=4]` — [[study-ca-y2k-cfo-executive-exchange-1997-c59ca4]]
- Majority of US enterprises are acting as though they have 2-3 years to fix Y2K, but true deadline is 15 months earlier; 'if you are not working on the problem today, you may have already failed'. `[ps=4]` — [[study-ca-y2k-cfo-executive-exchange-1997-c59ca4]]
- Arguing the world is unprepared for Y2K is whistling in the dark. This topic needs scenarios with depth: when will IS realize the problem; will remediation be in 2000 or 1999; what pressure/event will produce IS epiphany; how can IS test ISV applications; will testing+correcting make tangible difference vs. fail-and-remediate-in-2000? `[ps=4]` — [[study-kastner-jrl-trends-1999-pk-responses-f4c5a4]]
- Customers, suppliers, and competitors may be in trouble; existing value chains have every probability of experiencing broken links; opportunity to move into competitors' territory if they fail. `[ps=2]` — [[study-ca-y2k-cfo-executive-exchange-1997-c59ca4]]
- Agree and have formally told Intel that this is our scenario. `[ps=0]` — [[study-kastner-jrl-trends-1999-pk-responses-f4c5a4]]
- To be Y2K-safe by 1/1/2000: Y2K-safe applications in place by 9/30/98 for 5 quarters of testing/cutover; 12-month renovation/replacement program started by 9/30/97. — [[study-ca-y2k-cfo-executive-exchange-1997-c59ca4]]
