# Epilogue: The Argument with Reality

> Kastner reflects on fifty years in computing, observing that every technical breakthrough—from timesharing to the web to AI—has been slowed by organizational inertia, misaligned incentives, and the stubbornly human character of institutions. He offers an honest accounting of what Aberdeen got right (open systems, client-server, relational databases) and what it missed (the speed of the web, the depth of organizational resistance to data integration, and the culture-destroying effects of growth capital). The epilogue concludes that the fundamental constraint in computing has always been not the machine but the human organization around it.

- **2026** — Check-sorting automation achieved: Half-million Federal Reserve employees who touched paper nightly replaced by electronic clearing settling in seconds.
- **2026** — Airline physical ticket elimination: Airlines no longer print physical tickets.
- **2026** — Restaurant reconciliation modernization: Restaurant reconciliation runs on a tablet.
- **2026** — Core memoir thesis: The deeper lesson of fifty years is not what machines accomplished but what remained stubbornly, persistently, irreducibly human.
- **2026** — Universal pattern of technology adoption: Genuine technical breakthrough followed by organizational resistance, economic friction, and human misalignment making adoption slower, messier, more expensive than engineers predicted.
- **2026** — Why mainframe fell to minicomputer: Mainframe fell not because minicomputer engineering was better but because economics inverted and glass-house culture could not adapt quickly enough.
- **2026** — Why proprietary minicomputer fell to Unix: Margin compression was mathematically inevitable; no vendor found a durable strategy against commodity hardware. Open standards obvious to many by 1985.
- **2026** — Stratus niche defense mechanism: Fault-tolerant niche defended not by patents but by difficulty of making 'uptime' viscerally real to a buying committee and by the 'pull the board' demonstration.
- **2026** — Cloud/SaaS/AI will follow historical adoption pattern: Cloud, SaaS, and AI are genuine transitions that will encounter same organizational inertia, misaligned incentives, gap between architecture and enterprise willingness to reorganize.
- **2026** — Infrastructure vs. institution speed differential: The infrastructure will move faster than the institution, as it always has.
- **2026** — Aberdeen 1990s research correct forecasts: Prescient on: open systems transition, client-server persistence, relational database dominance, analytics democratization, integration complexity acceleration.
- **2026** — Web adoption speed underestimated: The web moved faster than any analyst predicted.
